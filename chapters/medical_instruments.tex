\medterm{Aseptic Technique}-- A set of practices used to prevent introduction of microorganisms into a susceptible site, sterile field, device, or specimen. It includes hand hygiene, appropriate barriers, skin antisepsis, and maintenance of sterile technique.

\medterm{Sterile Field}-- A designated area maintained free of viable microorganisms during an invasive procedure. Only sterile equipment and materials may contact the field or the sterile portions of instruments.

\medterm{Surgical Safety Checklist}-- A structured checklist used at defined points before anesthesia, before incision, and before leaving the operating room to verify patient identity, procedure, site, consent, equipment, specimens, and team communication.

\medterm{Instrument Sterilization}-- A validated process that destroys all forms of microbial life, including bacterial spores, on or within a medical instrument. Common methods include steam under pressure, ethylene oxide, hydrogen-peroxide plasma, and dry heat, selected according to device material and design.

\medterm{Disinfection}-- A process that eliminates many or all pathogenic microorganisms on inanimate objects, but not necessarily bacterial spores. It differs from sterilization and is classified as low-, intermediate-, or high-level according to the spectrum of activity.

\medterm{High-Level Disinfection}-- Disinfection that inactivates all microorganisms except high numbers of bacterial spores. It is commonly used for heat-sensitive semicritical equipment such as some flexible endoscopes after meticulous cleaning.

\medterm{Central Venous Catheter (CVC)}-- A catheter whose tip terminates in a central vein, usually the lower superior vena cava or cavoatrial junction. It provides reliable access for vasoactive drugs, irritating infusions, hemodynamic monitoring, and prolonged therapy, but may cause bloodstream infection, thrombosis, or mechanical complications.

\medterm{Arterial Catheter}-- A catheter placed in an artery, commonly the radial artery, for continuous blood-pressure monitoring and repeated arterial blood sampling. Complications include hemorrhage, thrombosis, infection, and distal ischemia.

\medterm{Biopsy Needle}-- A needle designed to obtain cells or tissue for cytologic or histopathologic examination. Fine-needle aspiration retrieves cells, whereas core biopsy obtains a tissue cylinder that preserves architecture.

\medterm{Ultrasound Transducer}-- A device that emits and receives high-frequency sound waves to generate diagnostic images or guide procedures. Linear, curvilinear, phased-array, and endocavitary transducers are selected according to depth and anatomic application.

\medterm{Video Laryngoscope}-- An airway device with a camera near the blade tip that displays the laryngeal view on a monitor. It may improve visualization during tracheal intubation, particularly when direct laryngoscopy is difficult, but does not eliminate aspiration or airway-trauma risk.

\medterm{Supraglottic Airway Device}-- An airway device positioned above the glottis to maintain oxygenation and ventilation without passing through the vocal cords. It is used for selected anesthetized or unconscious patients and is not a definitive substitute for a cuffed tracheal tube when aspiration protection is required.

\medterm{Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) Analyzer}-- An analyzer that measures arterial pH, partial pressures of oxygen and carbon dioxide, and usually bicarbonate or calculated acid-base variables. It is used to assess ventilation, oxygenation, and metabolic or respiratory acid-base disorders.

\medterm{Point-of-Care Testing (POCT)}-- Diagnostic testing performed near the patient rather than in a central laboratory. POCT can shorten turnaround time but requires validated devices, quality control, operator training, and appropriate interpretation.

\medterm{Electrosurgical Unit (ESU)}-- A generator that delivers high-frequency electrical energy through an active electrode to cut tissue or achieve hemostasis. Monopolar and bipolar configurations differ in current pathway and safety considerations; burns, unintended tissue injury, and interference with implanted devices are possible complications.

\medterm{Smoke Evacuator}-- A device that captures and filters airborne surgical plume generated by electrosurgery, laser use, or ultrasonic energy. Evacuation reduces exposure to particulate matter, toxic gases, and potentially infectious biologic material.

\medterm{Sharps Injury}-- A percutaneous or mucocutaneous injury caused by a needle or other sharp instrument that may expose a person to blood or body fluids. Immediate wound care, incident reporting, risk assessment, and pathogen-specific post-exposure management are required.
