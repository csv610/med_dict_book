\chapter{Dentistry}

\medterm{Agnathia}-- A rare congenital absence or severe underdevelopment of the mandible, maxilla, or both. It is usually part of a craniofacial developmental disorder and may cause profound facial deformity, airway obstruction, feeding difficulty, and impaired dentition. Management requires coordinated neonatal airway and nutritional support followed by multidisciplinary craniofacial, dental, and reconstructive care.

\medterm{Abfraction}-- Loss of tooth structure at cervical area from biomechanical loading. Controversial etiology. May contribute to sensitivity. Treatment: restoration, occlusal adjustment.

\medterm{Abrasion}-- Mechanical wear from foreign objects. Causes: aggressive brushing, toothpick use, nail-biting. Management: modify behavior, restore defects.

\medterm{Active Caries}-- Caries progressing. Soft, chalky, light-colored. Requires treatment.

\medterm{Acute Caries}-- Rapidly progressing caries. Soft, light-colored cavity. Common in children, xerostomia.

\medterm{Aesthetic Dentistry}-- Focus on appearance of teeth and smile. Veneers, bonding, whitening, crowns.

\medterm{Air Abrasion}-- Minimally invasive caries removal using abrasive particles. Alternative to drilling for small lesions.

\medterm{Air Polishing}-- Removal of stains using air-abrasive device. Glycine or erythritol powder.

\medterm{Alveolar Bone}-- Bone surrounding and supporting tooth roots. Resorbs with tooth loss. Maintained by function. Bone grafting may be needed for implants.

\medterm{Amalgam}-- Restorative material composed of mercury alloyed with silver, tin, copper, and other metals. Properties: strong, durable, cost-effective. Concerns: mercury toxicity debates, aesthetic limitations. Placement: requires cavity preparation, condensation, carving. Removal: sectioning, cooling to prevent mercury vapor.

\medterm{Ameloblastoma}-- Locally aggressive odontogenic tumor. Multicystic radiolucency in posterior mandible. High recurrence rate. Treatment: surgical resection with margins.

\medterm{Amelogenesis Imperfecta}-- Hereditary enamel defect. Affects all teeth. Types: hypoplastic, hypomaturation, hypocalcified.

\medterm{Ankyloglossia (Tongue-Tie)}-- Short or thick lingual frenulum restricting tongue movement. May cause breastfeeding difficulties, speech problems, oral hygiene issues. Treatment: frenotomy (simple release) or frenuloplasty.

\medterm{Ankylosis}-- Fusion of tooth root to bone. No periodontal ligament. Tooth appears submerged. Treated: extraction or orthodontic intrusion.

\medterm{Anodontia}-- Complete absence of teeth. Rare, associated with ectodermal dysplasia.

\medterm{Apicoectomy}-- Surgical removal of root tip and periapical lesion. Indication: failed root canal therapy.

\medterm{Arrested Caries}-- A carious lesion that is no longer progressing, often because the surface has become hard, smooth, and cleansable. Arrested lesions may be monitored and remineralized; treatment depends on activity, cavitation, cleansability, and patient risk.

\medterm{Attachment (Dental)}-- Retentive device connecting denture to implant or tooth. Precision attachments preferred over bar attachments.

\medterm{Atypical Facial Pain}-- Chronic facial pain without identifiable cause. Psychological factors may contribute.

\medterm{Black Stain}-- Extrinsic staining along gumline. Associated with poor hygiene. Polishing removes.

\medterm{Black Triangle}-- Triangular space between teeth at gumline. Caused by papilla loss, tooth shape, bone level. Treatment: orthodontics, restorative, papilla reconstruction.
\medterm{Bohn Nodules}-- Small white cysts on gingiva of newborns. Epithelial remnants. Resolve spontaneously.

\medterm{Bone Graft (Dental)}-- Material placed to augment alveolar bone. Types: autograft, allograft, xenograft, alloplast. Used for implants, ridge preservation.

\medterm{Bony Exostosis}-- A benign localized bony protuberance, including torus palatinus of the palate and torus mandibularis of the mandible. Treatment is usually unnecessary unless the lesion causes trauma, interferes with speech or prosthesis, or requires diagnostic clarification.

\medterm{Bridge (Dental)}-- Fixed prosthetic replacing missing teeth. Retained by adjacent teeth (abutments). Requires preparation of abutments.

\medterm{Brown Stain}-- Extrinsic staining from chromogenic bacteria, diet, tobacco. Removed by prophylaxis.

\medterm{Bruxism}-- Grinding or clenching teeth, often during sleep. Causes: stress, occlusion, sleep disorders. Consequences: tooth wear, fractures, muscle pain, TMJ disorders. Treatment: occlusal guard, stress management, behavior modification.

\medterm{Burn (Oral)}-- Thermal or chemical injury to oral mucosa. Food burn common. Treatment: symptomatic, healing usually rapid.

\medterm{Calculus (Tartar)}-- Mineralized dental plaque. Cannot be removed by brushing. Requires professional scaling. Retains plaque, promotes inflammation.

\medterm{Calculus Removal}-- Professional cleaning to remove hardened plaque. Scaling and root planing. Ultrasonic and hand instruments.

\medterm{Caries (Dental)}-- A biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven, multifactorial disease causing net demineralization of enamel, dentin, or cementum. Risk is influenced by fermentable-carbohydrate frequency, fluoride exposure, saliva, oral hygiene, tooth anatomy, and previous disease. Management may include remineralization, preventive care, minimally invasive treatment, or restoration according to lesion activity and cavitation.

\medterm{Caries Risk Assessment}-- Evaluation of individual caries susceptibility. Factors: diet, hygiene, fluoride exposure, saliva, previous caries. Guides prevention strategies.

\medterm{Cemento-Enamel Junction}-- Junction between enamel and cementum. Three relationships: butt junction, enamel overlapping cementum, cementum overlapping enamel.

\medterm{Chewing}-- Mastication of food. Teeth, muscles, joints coordinated. Difficulties: dental pain, missing teeth, TMJ disorder.

\medterm{Chronic Caries}-- Slowly progressing caries. Hard, dark-colored cavity. Common in adults.

\medterm{Cleft Lip and Palate}-- Congenital facial anomaly with gap or opening in lip and/or palate. Incidence: 1 in 700 births. Causes: genetic, environmental (maternal smoking, certain medications). Treatment: multidisciplinary surgical repair, speech therapy, orthodontics, psychological support.

\medterm{Cobalt-Chromium}-- Base metal alloy for cast restorations. Strong, corrosion-resistant. Used for partial dentures, crowns.

\medterm{Complete Denture}-- Removable prosthesis replacing all teeth in arch. Maxillary and mandibular. Stability challenging in edentulous patients.

\medterm{Composite Resin}-- Tooth-colored restorative material. Bis-GMA or UDMA resin matrix with silica filler. Adhesive bonding. Esthetic but less durable than ceramic.

\medterm{Concrescence}-- Union of adjacent teeth by cementum only. Usually posterior teeth.

\medterm{Copper}-- Component of dental amalgam and some alloys. Increases strength and corrosion resistance.

\medterm{Coronal Caries}-- Caries on clinical crown. Smooth surface or pits/fissures.

\medterm{Cracked Tooth Syndrome}-- Incomplete fracture of tooth causing pain on biting. Diagnosis difficult. Treatment: crown, root canal if pulpal involvement.

\medterm{Crown (Dental)}-- Cap covering damaged tooth. Materials: metal, porcelain-fused-to-metal, all-ceramic, zirconia. Indications: large caries, fractured tooth, root canal treated tooth.

\medterm{Crown Lengthening}-- Surgical procedure exposing more tooth structure. Involves gingivectomy and bone removal. For restorative or aesthetic purposes.

\medterm{Cusp Fracture}-- Breakage of tooth cusp. Often due to large restoration weakening tooth. Treatment: crown or onlay.

\medterm{Dens Evaginatus}-- Extra cusp or tubercle on tooth. Premolar most common. May contain pulp, fracture risk.

\medterm{Dens Invaginatus}-- Enamel-dentin invagination into pulp. Retains debris, high caries risk. Often mandibular lateral incisor.

\medterm{Dental Age}-- Dental development stage used for forensic age estimation in children. Teeth develop at predictable times.

\medterm{Dental Anesthesia}-- Techniques for pain control in dentistry. Local anesthesia (lidocaine, articaine), nitrous oxide, conscious sedation, general anesthesia.

\medterm{Dental Ceramic}-- Firing ceramic material for crowns and veneers. Feldspathic, leucite-reinforced, lithium disilicate, zirconia.

\medterm{Dental Plaque}-- Biofilm of bacteria on tooth surfaces. Initiated by acquired pellicle. Maturation leads to disease if not removed. Disrupted by mechanical cleaning.

\medterm{Dental Radiography}-- Imaging techniques for dental diagnosis: bitewing (caries, bone levels), periapical (root, periapical tissues), panoramic (full mouth overview), CBCT (3D imaging). Radiation dose: dental radiography low exposure. ALARA principle applies.

\medterm{Dental Restorative Materials}-- Materials used to restore teeth: amalgam, composite resin, glass ionomer, ceramic, gold alloy. Selection based on location, aesthetics, strength, cost, patient factors. Composite: tooth-colored, adhesive, conservative preparation. Ceramic: excellent aesthetics, brittle, expensive. Gold: durable, biocompatible, aesthetic concern.

\medterm{Dental Sealant}-- Thin protective coating applied to chewing surfaces of molars. Prevents caries by sealing pits and fissures.

\medterm{Dental Traumatology}-- Management of dental injuries. Types: enamel fractures, enamel-dentin fractures, pulp exposure, root fractures, avulsion. Immediate care critical for avulsed teeth.

\medterm{Dentin}-- Calcified tissue beneath enamel forming bulk of tooth. Contains dentinal tubules with fluid and processes. Sensitive to stimuli when exposed. Reparative dentin formed in response to irritation.

\medterm{Dentinogenesis Imperfecta}-- Hereditary dentin defect. Teeth appear opalescent, blue-gray or amber. Weak teeth, wear rapidly. Associated with osteogenesis imperfecta.

\medterm{Direct Veneer}-- Composite restoration placed chairside for aesthetic improvement. Conservative alternative to laboratory veneer.

\medterm{Distraction Osteogenesis}-- Surgical bone lengthening by gradual distraction. Used for jaw deformities, trauma.

\medterm{Dry Socket (Alveolar Osteitis)}-- A painful postoperative complication of tooth extraction, most commonly occurring after the removal of mandibular third molars (wisdom teeth). It is caused by the premature dissolution or loss of the blood clot in the extraction socket, exposing the underlying alveolar bone and nerve endings to the oral cavity. Symptoms include severe, radiating pain developing 1 to 4 days post-extraction, foul odor, and a visibly empty socket. Risk factors include smoking, oral contraceptive use, poor postoperative hygiene, and traumatic extraction. Treatment is symptomatic, involving socket irrigation and placement of a palliative medicated dressing.

\medterm{Enamel}-- Hard, highly mineralized outer layer of tooth crown. Avascular, aneural, cannot regenerate. Enamel hypoplasia causes defects. Dental caries begins in enamel. Fluoride strengthens enamel.

\medterm{Enamel Hypoplasia}-- Defective enamel quantity due to systemic disturbance during development. Pits, grooves, or thin enamel.

\medterm{Enamel Pearl}-- Small mound of enamel on root surface. Usually bifurcation area of molars. May interfere with periodontal attachment.

\medterm{Endodontic Therapy (Root Canal)}-- Treatment of dental pulp disease or necrosis. Indications: irreversible pulpitis, pulp necrosis, periapical abscess. Procedure: access cavity, locate canals, clean and shape, obturate with gutta-percha, seal. Success rate: >90%. Alternative: extraction.

\medterm{Epstein Pearls}-- Small white cysts on palate of newborn. Keratin cysts. Resolve spontaneously.

\medterm{Erosion (Tooth)}-- Chemical wear without bacterial involvement. Causes: acidic foods/drinks, gastric acid (GERD, bulimia), environmental acids. Management: dietary modification, fluoride, restoration.

\medterm{Eruption Cyst}-- Fluid-filled swelling over erupting tooth. Blue or purple color. Resolves spontaneously.

\medterm{Erythroplakia}-- Red patch with high malignancy risk. More concerning than leukoplakia. Biopsy essential.

\medterm{External Resorption}-- Loss of tooth structure from outside. Inflammatory, replacement, surface, cervical resorption. Treatment depends on type and extent.

\medterm{Facial Pain}-- Pain in face. Dental causes must be ruled out. Trigeminal neuralgia, TMJ disorder, sinusitis, neuropathic pain.

\medterm{Flossing}-- Interdental cleaning with dental floss. Removes plaque between teeth. Essential for periodontal health.

\medterm{Fluoride}-- Mineral that strengthens tooth enamel and inhibits bacterial acid production. Sources: water fluoridation (0.7 ppm optimal), toothpaste, professional applications. Mechanism: incorporates into hydroxyapatite forming fluorapatite (more acid-resistant), promotes remineralization, inhibits bacterial enzymes. Topical application most effective.

\medterm{Fluoride Varnish}-- Concentrated fluoride application professional use. Reduces caries by 30-40%. Applied 2-4 times yearly for high-risk patients.

\medterm{Fluorosis}-- Enamel hypomineralization from excess fluoride during development. Mild: white spots. Severe: brown stains, pitting. Prevention: monitor fluoride intake in children.

\medterm{Forensic Odontology}-- Application of dental knowledge to legal matters. Identification through dental records, bite mark analysis, age estimation.

\medterm{Frenectomy}-- Surgical removal or repositioning of frenulum. Indications: diastema, gingival recession, tongue-tie, interference with denture.

\medterm{Fusion}-- Union of two tooth buds. Results in large tooth with reduced tooth count.

\medterm{Gemination}-- Attempted division of single tooth bud. Results in large tooth with bifid crown, normal tooth count.

\medterm{Geriatric Dentistry}-- Dental care for elderly. Management of age-related changes, polypharmacy effects, xerostomia, root caries, periodontal disease, oral cancer screening.

\medterm{Gingival Hyperplasia}-- An abnormal overgrowth of gingival tissue around the teeth, which can impair plaque control, increase the risk of periodontal disease, and affect aesthetics. It can be caused by chronic inflammatory conditions (associated with poor oral hygiene) or systemic factors. A classic cause is drug-induced gingival hyperplasia, associated with anticonvulsants (phenytoin), immunosuppressants (cyclosporine), and calcium channel blockers (nifedipine). Treatment involves improved oral hygiene, surgical removal of excess tissue (gingivectomy), or substituting the offending medication.

\medterm{Gingivectomy}-- Surgical removal of gingival tissue. Indications: gingival hyperplasia, cosmetic recontouring, access to caries.

\medterm{Gingivitis}-- Inflammation of gums without bone loss. Caused by dental plaque accumulation. Signs: redness, swelling, bleeding on probing. Reversible with improved oral hygiene and professional cleaning. If untreated, progresses to periodontitis.

\medterm{Gingivoplasty}-- Surgical reshaping of gingiva. Often combined with gingivectomy.

\medterm{Glass Ionomer}-- Restorative material releasing fluoride. Bonds to tooth structure. Used for pediatric dentistry, cervical lesions, bases.

\medterm{Gold Alloy}-- Noble metal alloy for dental restorations. Excellent biocompatibility, durability, marginal adaptation. Expensive.

\medterm{Graft (Periodontal)}-- Tissue used to cover exposed root or augment gingiva. Connective tissue graft (gold standard), free gingival graft, allograft, xenograft.

\medterm{Guided Tissue Regeneration}-- Periodontal procedure using barrier membrane to promote regeneration of bone, cementum, periodontal ligament.

\medterm{Gummy Smile}-- Excessive gingival display when smiling. Caused by hypermobile lip, vertical maxillary excess, altered passive eruption. Treatment: surgery, Botox, orthodontics.

\medterm{Halitosis}-- Bad breath. Causes: oral (80-90%), nasal, sinus, pulmonary, gastrointestinal, systemic. Treatment: improve oral hygiene, tongue cleaning, treat cause.

\medterm{Hemangioma}-- Benign vascular tumor. Port-wine stain (capillary malformation), strawberry hemangioma. Treatment: observation, laser, surgery.

\medterm{Hypodontia}-- Congenital absence of one or more teeth. Common: third molars, maxillary lateral incisors, second premolars.

\medterm{Impacted Tooth}-- Tooth unable to erupt into functional position. Most common: mandibular third molars, maxillary canines. May cause pathology: cyst, root resorption, caries. Treatment: monitoring or extraction.

\medterm{Implant (Dental)}-- Titanium fixture placed in jawbone to support prosthetic tooth. Osseointegration occurs over 3-6 months. Indications: single tooth replacement, partial edentulism, complete edentulism (implant-supported denture). Success rate: >95% at 10 years. Complications: peri-implantitis, mechanical failure, aesthetic issues.

\medterm{Implant-Supported Prosthesis}-- Prosthetic tooth supported by dental implants. Fixed or removable. Superior to conventional dentures.

\medterm{Indirect Veneer}-- Ceramic restoration fabricated in lab. Porcelain or composite. Aesthetic enhancement.

\medterm{Interdental Brush}-- Small brush for cleaning between teeth. Used where spaces exist.

\medterm{Internal Resorption}-- Loss of dentin from pulp cavity inward. Pink spot may be visible. Treatment: root canal therapy.

\medterm{Laser Dentistry}-- Use of lasers for various procedures: caries detection, soft tissue surgery, whitening, periodontal therapy.

\medterm{Leukoplakia}-- White patch that cannot be scraped off. Potentially premalignant. Smoking-related. Biopsy indicated.

\medterm{Ludwig's Angina}-- A rapidly progressive, life-threatening cellulitis of the submandibular, sublingual, and submental spaces, typically originating from an odontogenic infection of the lower second or third molars. It is characterized by bilateral firm swelling of the floor of the mouth, elevation and posterior displacement of the tongue, dysphagia, dysphonia ("hot potato" voice), and stridor. The primary danger is airway obstruction. Treatment requires urgent airway management (often awake fiberoptic intubation or tracheostomy), intravenous antibiotics, and surgical decompression and drainage.

\medterm{Macrodontia}-- Abnormally large teeth. Microdontia: abnormally small teeth.

\medterm{Malocclusion}-- Misalignment of teeth or incorrect relation between dental arches. Types: Class I (normal molar relationship, crowding), Class II (retrognathic mandible), Class III (prognathic mandible).

\medterm{Maxillofacial Prosthesis}-- Artificial replacement of maxillofacial structures. Obturator for cleft palate or surgical defect. Ocular, nasal, auricular prostheses.

\medterm{Mercury}-- Liquid metal combining with other metals to form amalgam. Concerns about toxicity, proper handling essential.

\medterm{Mouthwash}-- Antimicrobial or cosmetic liquid for oral rinsing. Chlorhexidine (prescription), essential oils, cetylpyridinium chloride.

\medterm{Mucocele}-- Mucus extravasation cyst of minor salivary gland. Lower lip most common. Treatment: excision.

\medterm{Neuropathic Pain}-- Nerve-related facial pain. Burning, tingling, electric shock quality. Treatment: anticonvulsants, antidepressants.

\medterm{Nickel-Chromium}-- Base metal alloy for crowns. Less expensive than noble metals. Nickel allergy concern.

\medterm{Nutrition}-- Oral role in nutrition. Chewing efficiency affects food selection. Edentulism associated with poor nutrition.

\medterm{Occlusal Guard}-- Appliance protecting teeth from attrition. Night guard for bruxism. Sports mouthguard.

\medterm{Odontogenic Keratocyst}-- Aggressive cyst with high recurrence. Associated with nevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (Gorlin syndrome). Treatment: enucleation, resection, or decompression.

\medterm{Odontoma}-- Hamartomatous dental tissue mass. Compound (multiple tooth-like structures) or complex (amorphous calcified mass). Treatment: surgical removal.

\medterm{Oral Cancer}-- Malignancy of oral cavity, including lips, tongue, floor of mouth, buccal mucosa, palate, tonsils, oropharynx. Major risk factors: tobacco use, alcohol consumption, human papillomavirus (HPV, especially p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer). Presentation: persistent ulcer, leukoplakia, erythroplakia, mass, pain, difficulty swallowing. Diagnosis: biopsy. Treatment: surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, depending on stage.

\medterm{Oral Hygiene}-- Practices maintaining oral health: brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, flossing daily, mouthwash (optional), regular dental visits. Proper brushing technique: soft brush, 45-degree angle to gumline, gentle circular motions. Interdental cleaning essential for plaque removal.

\medterm{Oral Lichen Planus}-- Chronic inflammatory condition. Reticular (lace-like white lines), erosive (painful ulcers). Associated with hepatitis C. Malignant transformation rare but possible.

\medterm{Oral Medicine}-- Specialty diagnosing oral mucosal diseases, dental causes of facial pain, oral manifestations of systemic disease.

\medterm{Oral Pathology}-- Study of diseases affecting oral tissues. Includes neoplasms, cysts, inflammatory conditions, developmental anomalies.

\medterm{Oral Radiology}-- Specialty of producing and interpreting images of oral and maxillofacial region.

\medterm{Oral Surgery}-- Surgical procedures in oral and maxillofacial region. Extractions, implant placement, biopsies, cyst removal, corrective jaw surgery.

\medterm{Oral Ulcer}-- Ulceration of oral mucosa. Aphthous ulcer (canker sore) most common. Traumatic, viral, autoimmune causes.

\medterm{Orthodontics}-- Specialty of dental management of malocclusion and dental-facial discrepancies. Appliances: fixed braces, removable appliances, clear aligners. Treatment indications: crowding, spacing, overbite, underbite, crossbite, open bite, midline discrepancy. Treatment duration: 12-24 months typically. Retention phase essential for stability.

\medterm{Orthognathic Surgery}-- Corrective jaw surgery for skeletal malocclusion. Le Fort I, Bilateral Sagittal Split Osteotomy. Combined with orthodontics.

\medterm{Overdenture}-- Removable denture supported by implants or natural teeth. Preservation of alveolar bone, improved stability.

\medterm{Partial Denture}-- Removable prosthesis replacing some teeth. Metal framework or acrylic. Requires remaining teeth for support.

\medterm{Pediatric Dentistry}-- Specialty of dental care for children. Manages primary dentition, growth, behavior. Preventive focus. Special needs adaptation.

\medterm{Periapical Abscess}-- An acute or chronic localized collection of pus at the apex of a tooth's root, arising from pulpal infection and necrosis (usually due to untreated dental caries or trauma). Symptoms include severe, localized, throbbing pain, sensitivity to percussion and temperature, tooth mobility, and localized facial swelling or a sinus tract ("gum boil"). Diagnosis is confirmed by dental radiography showing a radiolucency at the root apex. Treatment requires drainage (via root canal therapy or tooth extraction) and occasionally antibiotics if systemic symptoms are present.

\medterm{Periodontal Flap Surgery}-- Surgical access for deep debridement. Flap reflected, calculus removed, bone contoured, flap repositioned.

\medterm{Periodontal Ligament}-- Connective tissue attaching tooth to bone. Contains collagen fibers, cells, blood vessels, nerves. Provides proprioception, shock absorption. Expanded in bruxism.

\medterm{Periodontitis}-- Inflammatory disease destroying supporting structures of teeth (periodontal ligament, alveolar bone). Caused by dysbiotic plaque biofilm with host immune response. Clinical signs: pocket formation, attachment loss, bone loss, tooth mobility, recession. Staging and grading guide treatment. Therapy: scaling and root planing, periodontal surgery, splinting, extraction. Prevention: oral hygiene, regular professional care.

\medterm{Polish (Dental)}-- Smoothing restorative surfaces to reduce plaque accumulation. Essential for longevity.

\medterm{Preventive Dentistry}-- Practice of preventing dental disease. Methods: oral hygiene instruction, fluoride, sealants, diet counseling, regular check-ups.

\medterm{Prophylaxis}-- Preventive dental cleaning. Removal of plaque, stain, calculus above gumline.

\medterm{Prosthodontics}-- Dental specialty concerned with replacement of missing teeth and restoration of oral function. Types: fixed (crowns, bridges, implants), removable (partial dentures, complete dentures), maxillofacial prosthetics (ocular, nasal, auricular). Goals: aesthetics, function, phonetics, preservation of remaining structures.

\medterm{Pulp}-- Soft connective tissue central to tooth containing blood vessels, nerves, connective tissue. Responds to injury with inflammation, necrosis, calcification. Root canal therapy removes infected pulp.

\medterm{Pulp Stone}-- Calcified mass in pulp chamber or root canal. May be free, attached, or embedded. Incidental finding.

\medterm{Pulpectomy}-- Complete removal of pulp from primary tooth. Followed by resorbable filling material.

\medterm{Pulpotomy}-- Removal of coronal pulp in primary tooth with reversible inflammation. Maintains radicular pulp vitality.

\medterm{Rampant Caries}-- Widespread severe caries affecting multiple surfaces. Associated with sugary drinks, xerostomia, bulimia.

\medterm{Recurrent Caries}-- Caries under or around restoration. Due to microleakage, marginal breakdown.

\medterm{Restorative Dentistry}-- Specialty of restoring damaged teeth. Procedures: fillings, inlays, onlays, crowns, bridges, implants. Goals: function, aesthetics, durability.

\medterm{Ridge Preservation}-- Socket grafting after extraction to maintain bone volume. Reduces ridge resorption.

\medterm{Root Caries}-- Caries on exposed root surfaces. Common in elderly with recession. Fluoride effective.

\medterm{Root Fracture}-- Vertical or horizontal fracture of root. Prognosis depends on location and direction. Vertical root fractures typically require extraction.

\medterm{Saliva}-- Secretion of salivary glands (~1-1.5 L/day). Functions: lubrication, digestion (amylase), buffering, antimicrobial, remineralization. Xerostomia (dry mouth) increases caries risk. Causes: medications, Sjogren syndrome, radiation, dehydration.

\medterm{Sandpaper Tooth}-- Rough enamel surface retains plaque. May indicate early demineralization or fluorosis.

\medterm{Sedation Dentistry}-- Pharmacological techniques for anxiety management: nitrous oxide (laughing gas), oral sedation, IV sedation, general anesthesia. Indications: dental phobia, special needs, prolonged procedures. Monitoring required for deeper sedation.

\medterm{Sialadenitis}-- Inflammation of salivary gland. Acute (bacterial) or chronic (recurrent). Treatment: antibiotics, hydration, gland massage, surgery if chronic.

\medterm{Sialolithiasis}-- Salivary gland stone. Submandibular gland most common (Wharton duct). Pain with meals. Treatment: removal, hydration, sialogogues.

\medterm{Sialorrhea}-- Excessive salivation. Neurological causes, medications, dental causes. Treatment: hydration, glycopyrrolate, botulinum toxin, surgery.

\medterm{Silver Diamine Fluoride}-- Liquid that arrests caries and stains cavity black. Used in young children, special needs patients.

\medterm{Sinus Lift}-- Procedure to increase bone height in posterior maxilla. Subperiosteal or transalveolar approach. Graft material placed.

\medterm{Sleep Apnea (Dental)}-- Oral appliance for obstructive sleep apnea. Mandibular advancement device. Indications: mild to moderate OSA, CPAP intolerance.

\medterm{Sleep Bruxism}-- Grinding or clenching teeth, often during sleep. See \hyperlink{Bruxism}{bruxism}.

\medterm{Smile Design}-- Planning smile based on facial features, gum display, tooth proportions. Digital smile design software available.

\medterm{Speech}-- Phonation and articulation. Teeth, tongue, lips, palate involved. Dental prostheses may affect speech initially.

\medterm{Splint (Dental)}-- Fixation of mobile teeth or fractured tooth. Wire and composite, orthodontic bracket splint.

\medterm{Stainless Steel}-- Alloy for preformed crowns, orthodontic bands, instruments. Corrosion-resistant.

\medterm{Stomatitis}-- Inflammation of oral mucosa. Causes: infection, trauma, allergy, systemic disease.

\medterm{Supernumerary Teeth}-- Extra teeth beyond normal complement. Most common: mesiodens between maxillary centrals. May cause impaction or malocclusion.

\medterm{Swallowing}-- Deglutition. Oral, pharyngeal, esophageal phases. Dental issues can affect oral phase.

\medterm{Taurodontism}-- Enlarged pulp chamber, short roots. Molars most affected. Associated with genetic conditions.

\medterm{Temporomandibular Joint Disorder (TMD)}-- Pain and dysfunction of TMJ and masticatory muscles. Symptoms: jaw pain, clicking, limited opening, headache, ear pain. Etiology: multifactorial (bruxism, stress, trauma, occlusion). Treatment: conservative (reassurance, self-care, splint, NSAIDs), physical therapy, rare surgery.

\medterm{Temporomandibular Joint Replacement}-- Total joint replacement for severe TMJ pathology. Indications: ankylosis, severe degenerative disease, failed previous surgery.

\medterm{Tetracycline Stain}-- Intrinsic discoloration from tetracycline antibiotics during tooth development. Gray or brown bands. Treatment: bleaching, veneers, crowns.

\medterm{Tin}-- Component of dental amalgam. Reduces strength, increases creep.

\medterm{Titanium}-- Biocompatible metal for implants and frameworks. Osseointegration capability.

\medterm{Tongue Cleaning}-- Removal of tongue coating using scraper or brush. Reduces bad breath.

\medterm{Tooth Eruption}-- Process by which tooth moves from intraosseous position to functional occlusion. Primary (deciduous) dentition: 20 teeth, eruption 6-30 months. Permanent dentition: 32 teeth (including third molars), eruption 6-21 years. Delayed eruption may indicate systemic conditions, local obstruction, or normal variant. Impacted teeth: most commonly third molars, maxillary canines.

\medterm{Tooth Sensitivity}-- Pain in response to thermal, sweet, acidic, or tactile stimulus. Caused by dentin exposure (recession, erosion, abrasion, abfraction). Management: desensitizing toothpaste, fluoride varnish, restorative coverage, gum graft for recession.

\medterm{Tooth Whiteness}-- Aesthetic concern driving bleaching. Natural shade varies. Over-bleaching causes sensitivity and translucency.

\medterm{Toothache}-- Common dental symptom indicating underlying pathology. Causes: dental caries, pulpitis (reversible or irreversible), periapical abscess, cracked tooth, periodontal disease, sinusitis (referred pain). Evaluation: history, clinical exam, radiographs. Management: treat underlying cause, analgesics, antibiotics if infection present.

\medterm{Turner Hypoplasia}-- Enamel defect on permanent tooth from infection of overlying primary tooth. Localized hypoplasia.

\medterm{Vascular Malformation}-- Abnormal blood vessel development. Arteriovenous malformation, venous malformation, lymphatic malformation.

\medterm{Vital Tooth Bleaching}-- Whitening of natural teeth with peroxide gel. In-office or take-home trays. Effect temporary.

\medterm{Water Flosser}-- Device using water stream to clean between teeth. Adjunct to brushing and flossing.

\medterm{Xerostomia}-- See saliva section.

\medterm{Zirconia}-- Strong ceramic material for dental restorations. High flexural strength. Can be monolithic or layered with porcelain.

\medterm{Aphthous Ulcer}-- A painful, shallow, non-keratinized oral mucosal ulcer with a gray-white base and erythematous halo. Minor ulcers usually heal spontaneously within two weeks; recurrent, severe, or persistent ulcers require assessment for systemic disease, nutritional deficiency, trauma, or neoplasia.

\medterm{Avulsed Tooth}-- A tooth completely displaced from its socket by trauma. It is a dental emergency; management depends on patient age, extraoral dry time, storage medium, root development, and periodontal-ligament viability, and may require urgent replantation by a dental professional.

\medterm{Bitewing Radiograph}-- An intraoral radiograph showing the crowns of the maxillary and mandibular posterior teeth and the alveolar crest. It is particularly useful for detecting interproximal caries and assessing crestal bone levels.

\medterm{Cementum}-- A mineralized connective tissue covering the tooth root. It provides attachment for periodontal-ligament fibers and is thinner near the cemento-enamel junction and thicker toward the root apex.

\medterm{Cone-Beam Computed Tomography}-- Three-dimensional dental imaging using a cone-shaped X-ray beam and a rotating detector. It provides high-resolution views of teeth and maxillofacial bone with generally less radiation than conventional medical CT, but should be used only when the additional information affects care.

\medterm{Dental Extraction}-- Removal of a tooth from its alveolar socket. Indications include unrestorable disease, advanced periodontal destruction, impaction, orthodontic treatment, or selected prosthodontic needs; risks include bleeding, infection, nerve injury, fracture, and alveolar osteitis.

\medterm{Dental Pulpitis}-- Inflammation of the dental pulp caused by caries, trauma, restorative procedures, cracks, or other irritation. Reversible pulpitis produces brief provoked pain, whereas irreversible pulpitis causes spontaneous or lingering pain and may require pulpal treatment or extraction.

\medterm{Dental Pulp Necrosis}-- Death of the dental pulp, usually following untreated inflammation, trauma, or loss of blood supply. The tooth generally fails sensibility testing and may develop apical periodontitis or a periapical abscess.

\medterm{DMFT Index}-- An epidemiologic index recording the number of permanent teeth that are decayed, missing because of caries, or filled. It estimates cumulative caries experience but does not measure current lesion activity or distinguish all causes of tooth loss.

\medterm{Edentulism}-- Complete or partial absence of natural teeth. It can impair mastication, speech, nutrition, facial support, and quality of life and may be managed with removable, fixed, or implant-supported prostheses.

\medterm{Gingival Recession}-- Apical displacement of the gingival margin from the cemento-enamel junction, exposing the root surface. Causes include periodontal inflammation, traumatic toothbrushing, thin periodontal tissue, tooth position, and iatrogenic factors; it may produce sensitivity and root-caries risk.

\medterm{Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block}-- A regional anesthetic injection near the mandibular foramen to anesthetize the inferior alveolar nerve before it enters the mandibular canal. It usually anesthetizes mandibular teeth on that side; the lingual nerve is often anesthetized incidentally.

\medterm{Interproximal Caries}-- Dental caries occurring on the contact surfaces between adjacent teeth. It may be difficult to detect clinically and is commonly assessed with bitewing radiographs when imaging is indicated.

\medterm{Oral Candidiasis}-- Infection or overgrowth of Candida species on the oral mucosa, often presenting as removable white plaques, erythematous patches, or angular cheilitis. Risk factors include antibiotics, inhaled corticosteroids, immunosuppression, diabetes, xerostomia, and dentures.

\medterm{Oral Mucosa}-- The moist stratified squamous epithelial lining of the oral cavity. It includes masticatory, lining, and specialized mucosa and provides barrier protection, sensation, lubrication, and sites for minor salivary glands.

\medterm{Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma}-- A malignant neoplasm arising from oral stratified squamous epithelium. Persistent induration, ulceration, red or white patches, unexplained bleeding, pain, or neck mass requires evaluation and biopsy; staging incorporates tumor, nodal, and metastatic findings.

\medterm{Panoramic Radiograph}-- An extraoral radiograph displaying both jaws, teeth, temporomandibular regions, and surrounding structures in one image. It is useful for surveying dentition, development, impaction, and gross osseous pathology but has less fine detail than intraoral radiographs.

\medterm{Periodontal Pocket}-- A pathologically deepened gingival sulcus caused by apical migration of the junctional epithelium and loss of periodontal attachment. Pocket depth and clinical attachment level are measured with a periodontal probe and interpreted with bleeding, inflammation, and radiographic bone loss.

\medterm{Periodontal Abscess}-- A localized accumulation of pus within periodontal tissues, commonly associated with a periodontal pocket, foreign material, or impaired drainage. It may cause pain, swelling, suppuration, tooth mobility, and systemic symptoms and requires urgent dental assessment.

\medterm{Pericoronitis}-- Inflammation of the soft tissue around the crown of a partially erupted tooth, most often a mandibular third molar. Food retention and bacterial accumulation may cause pain, swelling, trismus, fever, or spread into deeper spaces.

\medterm{Periapical Granuloma}-- A chronic inflammatory lesion at the apex of a tooth, usually resulting from pulpal necrosis and persistent apical infection. It may be asymptomatic and is diagnosed through clinical, radiographic, and sometimes histopathologic assessment.

\medterm{Replantation of an Avulsed Tooth}-- Replacement of an avulsed permanent tooth into its socket after trauma, followed by flexible stabilization and endodontic and periodontal follow-up when indicated. Prognosis depends strongly on extraoral dry time, storage conditions, root maturity, and contamination.

\medterm{Scaling and Root Planing}-- Nonsurgical periodontal instrumentation that removes supra- and subgingival deposits and smooths contaminated root surfaces. It reduces inflammation and pocket depth in appropriate patients but does not regenerate lost periodontal attachment by itself.

\medterm{Tooth}-- A mineralized organ composed primarily of enamel, dentin, cementum, and dental pulp. Teeth cut and grind food, contribute to speech and facial support, and are anchored in alveolar bone by the periodontal ligament.

\medterm{Tooth Luxation}-- Traumatic displacement of a tooth without complete avulsion. Concussion and subluxation involve periodontal injury with little or no displacement, whereas extrusive, lateral, and intrusive luxation involve increasingly marked displacement and require prompt dental evaluation.

\medterm{Tooth Replantation}-- See Replantation of an Avulsed Tooth.

\medterm{Tooth Sensibility Testing}-- Clinical testing of a tooth's response to thermal, electric, or dentin stimulation. It assesses neural response rather than directly measuring pulp blood flow, so results must be interpreted with history, examination, and imaging.

\medterm{Orthodontic Bracket}-- A bonded or banded attachment that transmits forces from an orthodontic archwire to a tooth. Bracket position and wire sequence guide controlled tooth movement.

\medterm{Orthodontic Retainer}-- A removable or fixed appliance used after active orthodontic treatment to maintain tooth position while periodontal and alveolar tissues remodel. Retention is often needed long term because relapse can occur.

\medterm{Osseointegration}-- Direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant under appropriate conditions. It depends on implant stability, surface characteristics, bone quality, healing, and control of infection and occlusal forces.

\medterm{Aphthous Stomatitis}-- See \hyperlink{Aphthous Ulcer}{Aphthous Ulcer}.
