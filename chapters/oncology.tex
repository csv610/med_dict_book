\chapter{Oncology}

\medterm{Adjuvant Therapy}-- Additional treatment after primary treatment to reduce recurrence risk. Examples: chemotherapy after surgery.

\medterm{Advance Care Planning}-- Discussion of future healthcare preferences. See \hyperlink{Geriatrics}{geriatrics}.

\medterm{Anaplasia}-- Lack of differentiation. Feature of malignancy.

\medterm{Anemia (Cancer)}-- Low red blood cells. Due to cancer, chemotherapy, chronic disease. Treatment: transfusion, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents.

\medterm{Angiogenesis}-- Formation of new blood vessels. Tumors induce angiogenesis for growth. Targeted by anti-angiogenic drugs (bevacizumab).

\medterm{Apoptosis}-- Programmed cell death. Dysregulated in cancer (avoiding death). Pro-apoptotic genes: BAX, P53. Anti-apoptotic: BCL-2.

\medterm{Benign Tumor}-- Non-cancerous growth, slow-growing, well-differentiated, localized, does not invade or metastasize. Examples: lipoma, fibroma, adenoma. May cause problems by compression or hormone secretion.

\medterm{Biomarker}-- Measurable indicator of disease. Cancer biomarkers: diagnostic, prognostic, predictive.

\medterm{Biomarker Testing}-- Analysis of DNA/RNA for mutations, fusions, expression. See \hyperlink{Molecular Testing}{molecular testing}.

\medterm{Biopsy}-- Tissue sampling for diagnosis. Types: needle biopsy, excisional biopsy, incisional biopsy, endoscopic biopsy. Essential for cancer diagnosis and treatment planning.

\medterm{Bladder Cancer}-- Urothelial carcinoma. Risk factors: smoking, occupational exposures.

\medterm{Bone Metastasis}-- Common in breast, prostate, lung, thyroid, kidney. Pain, fractures, hypercalcemia. Treatment: radiation, bisphosphonates, surgery.

\medterm{Brain Metastasis}-- Common in lung, breast, melanoma. Symptoms: headache, seizures, focal deficits. Treatment: surgery, radiation, stereotactic radiosurgery.

\medterm{Breast Cancer}-- Most common cancer in women. Risk factors: age, genetics, hormones, lifestyle. Screening: mammography. Treatment: surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy.

\medterm{Brenner Tumor}-- Rare ovarian epithelial neoplasm (1-2\% of ovarian tumors) composed of nests of transitional (urothelial-like) epithelial cells embedded in a dense fibromatous stroma. Most are unilateral, small ($<$ 2 cm), benign (90\%) and incidental; borderline (proliferative) and malignant variants are uncommon. Often asymptomatic and discovered incidentally at imaging or surgery for other reasons. Histology: characteristic "coffee-bean" nuclear grooves. Epidemiology: typically 5th-6th decade. Treatment: surgical excision (cystectomy or oophorectomy); malignant variants require full staging with total hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, omentectomy, and lymph node sampling. Prognosis is excellent for benign Brenner tumors; malignant variants are rare but may behave aggressively.

\medterm{Cachexia}-- Wasting syndrome in cancer patients. Features: weight loss, muscle atrophy, anorexia, fatigue. Mediated by inflammatory cytokines. Difficult to treat, affects prognosis.

\medterm{Cancer Genetics}-- Inherited cancer syndromes. Examples: BRCA1/2 (breast, ovarian), Lynch syndrome (colon), Li-Fraumeni (various).

\medterm{Cancer Registry}-- System for collecting cancer data. Used for epidemiology, quality improvement, research.

\medterm{Cancer Staging}-- Classification of cancer extent. TNM system: T (tumor size/extent), N (nodal involvement), M (metastasis). Stages: 0 (in situ), I (early), II-III (advanced), IV (metastatic). Guides treatment and prognosis.

\medterm{Cancer Survivor}-- Person living with cancer from diagnosis through end of life and beyond.

\medterm{CAR T-Cell}-- Chimeric antigen receptor T-cells. Engineered immune cells targeting cancer.

\medterm{Carcinogen}-- Substance causing cancer. Examples: tobacco, asbestos, UV radiation, HPV.

\medterm{Carcinoma}-- Malignant tumor of epithelial origin. Types: squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, basal cell carcinoma, transitional cell carcinoma. Most common cancer type (~80-90% of malignancies). Examples: lung carcinoma, breast carcinoma, colon carcinoma.

\medterm{Carcinoma in Situ}-- Full-thickness dysplasia without invasion through basement membrane. Stage 0 cancer.

\medterm{Checkpoint Inhibitor}-- Drug blocking immune checkpoints. Examples: pembrolizumab (PD-1), ipilimumab (CTLA-4).

\medterm{Checkpoints}-- Immune regulatory molecules. Cancer exploits checkpoints. Inhibitors block checkpoints.

\medterm{Chemotherapy}-- Use of cytotoxic drugs to treat cancer. Mechanisms: DNA damage, microtubule inhibition, antimetabolite action. Common agents: alkylating agents, anthracyclines, taxanes, platins, antimetabolites. Side effects: myelosuppression, nausea, alopecia.

\medterm{Chordoma}-- Slow-growing, locally aggressive malignant bone tumor originating from embryonic remnants of the notochord along the axial skeleton. Three anatomic locations: (1) sacrococcygeal ($\sim 50\%$ of cases, most common), (2) spheno-occipital/clival ($\sim 35\%$, base of skull), (3) vertebral (cervical $\gg$ thoracolumbar, $\sim 15\%$). Pathology hallmark: physaliphorous cells (large cells with bubbly vacuolated, mucin-rich cytoplasm) arranged in cords/lobules with myxoid stroma; immunohistochemistry positive for S100, brachyury (nuclear T-box transcription factor, characteristic; diagnostic — encoded by T gene, also drives notochord development), CK8/18/19, EMA. Clinical: insidious pain at site, mass effect — sacral pain/numbness/bladder-bowel dysfunction (sacral), cranial nerve palsies (clival), myelopathy/spinal cord compression (vertebral). Diagnosis: MRI for soft-tissue extent, CT for bone detail; biopsy for confirmation. Treatment: en bloc wide surgical excision with negative margins (gold standard, often challenging) followed by high-dose proton beam or carbon ion radiotherapy (tumor is relatively radioresistant — conventional photon doses insufficient; proton therapy enables dose escalation to 74 Gy with safer organ-at-risk). Chemotherapy ineffective; targeted therapy under investigation (brachyury vaccine, EGFR inhibitors for skull base). Prognosis: high rate of local recurrence ($> 50\%$); 5-year survival $\sim 68\%$, 10-year $\sim 40\%$; distant metastases in 30-40\%.

\medterm{Clinical Research}-- Research study testing new cancer treatments. See \hyperlink{Clinical Trial}{clinical trial}.

\medterm{Clinical Trial}-- Research study testing new cancer treatments. Phases: I (safety), II (efficacy), III (comparison), IV (post-marketing).

\medterm{Colorectal Cancer}-- Third most common cancer. Screening: colonoscopy. Prevention: polyp removal. Treatment: surgery, chemotherapy, radiation.

\medterm{Combination Therapy}-- Using multiple treatments together. Improves outcomes, reduces resistance.

\medterm{Comorbidity}-- Presence of one or more additional conditions co-occurring with a primary disease. See general.

\medterm{Complete Response}-- All detectable cancer disappears.

\medterm{CT Scan}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Cytogenetics}-- Study of chromosomes. Detects abnormalities in cancer.

\medterm{Cytology}-- Examination of individual cells. Examples: Pap smear, fine needle aspiration.

\medterm{Differentiation}-- Degree of cellular differentiation. See \hyperlink{Grade}{grade}.

\medterm{Dysplasia}-- Abnormal cell growth and differentiation. Precancerous lesion. Graded mild, moderate, severe.

\medterm{Early Detection}-- Testing asymptomatic individuals for disease. See \hyperlink{Screening}{screening}.

\medterm{End-of-Life Care}-- See hospice, palliative care.

\medterm{Evidence-Based Medicine}-- Treatment based on best available evidence. Guidelines from NCCN, ASCO, ESMO.

\medterm{Fatigue (Cancer)}-- Most common cancer symptom. Mult-factorial: tumor, treatment, anemia, psychological. Management: exercise, energy conservation, treat underlying causes.

\medterm{Flow Cytometry}-- Analysis of cells by flow. Used for leukemia/lymphoma classification.

\medterm{Grade}-- Degree of cellular differentiation. Grade 1: well-differentiated. Grade 2: moderately differentiated. Grade 3: poorly differentiated. Grade 4: undifferentiated. Higher grade = more aggressive.

\medterm{HER2-Positive}-- Cancer overexpressing HER2 protein. Treated with trastuzumab.

\medterm{Hereditary Cancer}-- Cancer caused by inherited genetic mutations. Genetic counseling recommended.

\medterm{Histology}-- Microscopic examination of tissue.

\medterm{Hormone Therapy}-- Treatment blocking hormone effects in hormone-sensitive cancers. Examples: tamoxifen (breast cancer), leuprolide (prostate cancer), aromatase inhibitors.

\medterm{Hormone-Receptor Positive}-- Cancer expressing hormone receptors (ER+, PR+, AR+). Treated with hormone therapy.

\medterm{Hospice}-- End-of-life care for patients with <6 month prognosis. Comfort-focused.

\medterm{Hypercalcemia of Malignancy}-- Elevated calcium from bone metastasis or PTHrP production. Symptoms: confusion, constipation, polyuria. Treatment: hydration, bisphosphonates, calcitonin.

\medterm{Hyperplasia}-- Increased cell number. Can be physiological or pathological.

\medterm{Hypertrophy}-- Increased cell size. Physiological (muscle) or pathological.

\medterm{Imaging}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Immune Evasion}-- Cancer mechanisms to avoid immune detection. Examples: PD-L1 expression, antigen loss.

\medterm{Immunohistochemistry}-- Use of antibodies to detect antigens in tissue. Used for cancer diagnosis and subtyping.

\medterm{Immunotherapy}-- Treatment using immune system to fight cancer. Types: checkpoint inhibitors (PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA-4), CAR T-cell therapy, cytokines, cancer vaccines. Examples: pembrolizumab, nivolumab, ipilimumab, axicabtagene ciloleucel.

\medterm{In Situ Carcinoma}-- Full-thickness dysplasia without invasion through basement membrane. See \hyperlink{Carcinoma in Situ}{carcinoma in situ}.

\medterm{Informed Consent}-- Patient understanding and agreement to treatment or research participation.

\medterm{Kidney Cancer}-- Renal cell carcinoma. Risk factors: smoking, obesity, hypertension.

\medterm{Late Effects}-- Health problems occurring months to years after cancer treatment. Cardiac, pulmonary, endocrine, secondary cancers.

\medterm{Leukemia}-- Malignant proliferation of hematopoietic cells in bone marrow. Types: acute lymphoblastic (ALL), acute myeloid (AML), chronic lymphocytic (CLL), chronic myeloid (CML). Acute: rapid onset, immature cells (blasts). Chronic: slower progression, mature cells.


\medterm{Liquid Biopsy}-- Detection of circulating tumor DNA or cells in blood. Minimally invasive monitoring.

\medterm{Liver Cancer}-- Hepatocellular carcinoma, often in cirrhosis. Risk factors: hepatitis, alcohol, aflatoxin.

\medterm{Local Invasion}-- Tumor growth into surrounding tissues. Distinguishes malignant from benign.

\medterm{Lung Cancer}-- Leading cancer death worldwide. Types: small cell, non-small cell. Risk factors: smoking, radon, asbestos.

\medterm{Lymph Node Dissection}-- Removal of lymph nodes for staging or treatment. Sentinel node biopsy less invasive alternative.

\medterm{Lymphoma}-- Malignant tumor of lymphocytes. Types: Hodgkin lymphoma (Reed-Sternberg cells), non-Hodgkin lymphoma (diverse subtypes). Hodgkin: more common in young and older adults, predictable spread. NHL: more common overall, variable behavior.


\medterm{Maintenance Therapy}-- Long-term treatment to prevent recurrence. Examples: hormonal therapy, targeted therapy.

\medterm{Malignancy}-- See cancer.

\medterm{Malignant Tumor}-- Cancerous growth, invasive, can metastasize, poorly differentiated. Requires aggressive treatment.

\medterm{Melanoma}-- Most dangerous skin cancer. Risk factors: UV exposure, fair skin, moles. Early detection improves outcomes.

\medterm{Metaplasia}-- Change from one cell type to another. Often adaptive. Can be precancerous.

\medterm{Metastasis}-- Spread of cancer cells from primary site to distant locations. Routes: lymphatic, hematogenous, direct extension, transcoelomic. Common sites: lungs, liver, bone, brain. Distant metastasis indicates Stage IV disease.


\medterm{Metastatic Cascade}-- Sequence of events in metastasis: local invasion, intravasation, circulation, extravasation, colonization. Many cells die during process.

\medterm{Molecular Testing}-- Analysis of DNA/RNA for mutations, fusions, expression. Guides targeted therapy.

\medterm{Monoclonal Antibody}-- Laboratory-produced antibody targeting specific antigen. Examples: rituximab, trastuzumab, bevacizumab.

\medterm{MRI}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Multidisciplinary Team}-- Cancer care involves surgeons, medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, pathologists, radiologists, nurses, social workers.

\medterm{Mutation}-- Permanent change in DNA sequence. See genetics.

\medterm{Myeloma}-- Multiple myeloma: plasma cell malignancy. Features: bone pain, anemia, renal failure, hypercalcemia.

\medterm{Neoadjuvant Therapy}-- Treatment before primary treatment to shrink tumor. Examples: chemotherapy before surgery.

\medterm{Neoplasm}-- Abnormal growth of tissue, may be benign or malignant. Benign neoplasms: localized, slow-growing, do not metastasize. Malignant neoplasms (cancer): invasive, metastasize, can be fatal. Classification: carcinoma (epithelial origin), sarcoma (mesenchymal origin), leukemia (hematopoietic), lymphoma (lymphoid).

\medterm{Neutropenia}-- Low neutrophil count. Common side effect of chemotherapy. Infection risk. Growth factors may help.

\medterm{Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer}-- Most common lung cancer type. Includes adenocarcinoma, squamous cell, large cell.

\medterm{Nutrition (Oncology)}-- Cancer and cancer treatment affect nutrition. Malnutrition common. Dietitian referral recommended.

\medterm{Oncogene}-- Gene that can cause cancer when mutated or overexpressed. Normal version: proto-oncogene. Examples: RAS, MYC, HER2, BCR-ABL. Drive cell proliferation.

\medterm{Overall Survival}-- Time from treatment to death from any cause.

\medterm{Pain Management}-- Emergency pain control: NSAIDs, opioids, ketamine, lidocaine. See emergency medicine.

\medterm{Palliative Care}-- Care focusing on symptom relief and quality of life. Can be provided with curative treatment.

\medterm{Pancreatic Cancer}-- Aggressive cancer, often diagnosed late. Risk factors: smoking, chronic pancreatitis, genetics.

\medterm{Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndrome}-- Neurologic symptoms from immune response to cancer. Examples: Lambert-Eaton, cerebellar degeneration.

\medterm{Paraneoplastic Syndrome}-- Symptoms caused by cancer but not by tumor mass or metastasis. Mechanisms: hormone production, immune cross-reaction. Examples: SIADH, Cushing syndrome, Lambert-Eaton myasthenia, hypercalcemia.

\medterm{Partial Response}-- Significant tumor reduction but not complete.

\medterm{Pathology}-- Study of disease. Cancer pathology: diagnosis, grading, staging, molecular characterization.

\medterm{Pediatric Cancer}-- Cancers in children. Common: leukemia, brain tumors, lymphoma, neuroblastoma, Wilms tumor. Often curable.

\medterm{Personalized Medicine}-- Treatment tailored to individual tumor characteristics. See \hyperlink{Precision Medicine}{precision medicine}.

\medterm{PET Scan}-- See \hyperlink{Nuclear Medicine}{nuclear medicine}.

\medterm{Precision Medicine}-- Treatment tailored to individual tumor characteristics. Requires molecular profiling.

\medterm{Prevention}-- Preventing cancer development. See \hyperlink{Primary Prevention}{primary prevention}.

\medterm{Primary Prevention}-- Preventing cancer development. Strategies: smoking cessation, vaccination (HPV, hepatitis B), sun protection, healthy diet, exercise.

\medterm{Primary Tumor}-- Original cancer site. Secondary: metastatic sites.

\medterm{Prognosis}-- Predicted outcome of cancer. Factors: stage, grade, molecular features, patient status. Survivability statistics.

\medterm{Progression}-- Cancer growth or spread despite treatment.

\medterm{Prostate Cancer}-- Common cancer in men. Screening: PSA, DRE. Most indolent, active surveillance often appropriate.

\medterm{Proto-oncogene}-- Normal gene that can become oncogene when mutated.

\medterm{Psychosocial Support}-- Cancer affects mental health. Depression, anxiety common. Support groups, counseling beneficial.

\medterm{Quality of Life}-- Perception of well-being including physical, psychological, social, spiritual domains. See \hyperlink{Geriatrics}{geriatrics}.

\medterm{Radiation Therapy}-- Use of ionizing radiation to kill cancer cells. Types: external beam, brachytherapy, stereotactic. Mechanisms: DNA damage, cell death. Side effects: fatigue, skin reactions, site-specific effects.

\medterm{Recurrence}-- Return of cancer after treatment. Can be local, regional, or distant.

\medterm{Recurrence-Free Survival}-- Time without cancer return after treatment.

\medterm{Refractory}-- Cancer not responding to treatment. May require alternative approaches.

\medterm{Relapse}-- Return of cancer after treatment. See \hyperlink{Recurrence}{recurrence}.

\medterm{Remission}-- Decrease or disappearance of cancer signs. Complete remission: no detectable disease. Partial remission: significant reduction but not complete.


\medterm{Sarcoma}-- Malignant tumor of mesenchymal origin (bone, cartilage, fat, muscle, blood vessels). Types: osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, liposarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma. Less common than carcinomas (~1% of adult cancers).


\medterm{Screening}-- Detection of cancer in asymptomatic individuals. Methods: mammography (breast), colonoscopy (colon), Pap smear (cervix), PSA (prostate), low-dose CT (lung). Balance benefits vs. harms.

\medterm{Screening Guidelines}-- Testing asymptomatic individuals for disease. See \hyperlink{Screening}{screening}.

\medterm{Second Malignancy}-- New cancer after initial cancer treatment. Risk from radiation, chemotherapy.

\medterm{Secondary Prevention}-- Early detection through screening.

\medterm{Secondary Tumor}-- Spread of cancer cells from primary site to distant locations. See \hyperlink{Metastasis}{metastasis}.

\medterm{Sentinel Lymph Node}-- First lymph node draining tumor site. Biopsy assesses nodal spread.

\medterm{Sequential Therapy}-- Treatments given in sequence. Used when combination not suitable.

\medterm{Skin Cancer}-- Most common cancer. Types: basal cell, squamous cell, melanoma. Prevention: sun protection. Early detection critical.

\medterm{Small Cell Lung Cancer}-- Aggressive lung cancer, strongly associated with smoking. Early metastasis, responds to chemotherapy.

\medterm{Somatic Mutation}-- Acquired genetic change in cancer cells. Not inherited.

\medterm{Spinal Cord Compression}-- Oncologic emergency. Back pain, weakness, sensory loss, bowel/bladder dysfunction. MRI diagnostic. Treatment: steroids, radiation, surgery.

\medterm{Stable Disease}-- Cancer neither growing nor shrinking significantly.

\medterm{Stage I}-- Early cancer, localized. Good prognosis.

\medterm{Stage II}-- Localized but larger or with local spread.

\medterm{Stage III}-- Regional spread to lymph nodes or nearby structures.

\medterm{Stage IV}-- Distant metastasis. Poor prognosis.

\medterm{Superior Vena Cava Syndrome}-- Obstruction of SVC by tumor. Facial swelling, dyspnea, dilated neck veins. Emergency: radiation, stenting.

\medterm{Supportive Care}-- Management of symptoms and side effects. Improves quality of life.

\medterm{Surgery}-- Surgical removal of tumor. Curative when complete resection possible. Palliative for symptom relief.

\medterm{Survival Rate}-- Percentage of patients surviving for specified time after diagnosis. 5-year survival commonly used.

\medterm{Survivorship}-- Care for cancer survivors. Addresses late effects, surveillance, quality of life.

\medterm{Survivorship Care Plan}-- Document summarizing treatment, follow-up, late effects. Provided to cancer survivors.

\medterm{Targeted Therapy}-- Drugs targeting specific molecular abnormalities in cancer. Examples: imatinib (BCR-ABL), trastuzumab (HER2), rituximab (CD20), tyrosine kinase inhibitors. Requires molecular testing.

\medterm{Terminology}-- Cancer terms: neoplasm, tumor, carcinoma, sarcoma, leukemia, lymphoma, metastasis, remission, recurrence.

\medterm{Tertiary Prevention}-- Reducing complications from established cancer.

\medterm{Thrombocytopenia}-- Low platelet count. Bleeding risk. May require transfusion.

\medterm{Thyroid Cancer}-- Usually indolent. Types: papillary, follicular, medullary, anaplastic.

\medterm{TNM Staging}-- Classification of cancer extent. See \hyperlink{Cancer Staging}{cancer staging}.

\medterm{Triple-Negative Breast Cancer}-- Breast cancer lacking ER, PR, HER2. Aggressive, limited treatment options.

\medterm{Tumor}-- Abnormal mass of tissue, may be benign or malignant. Tumors classified by tissue of origin. Terminology: -oma usually benign, but exceptions (melanoma, lymphoma, seminoma are malignant).

\medterm{Tumor Lysis Syndrome}-- Metabolic emergency from rapid cell death. Hyperuricemia, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia. Prevention: hydration, allopurinol, rasburicase.

\medterm{Tumor Marker}-- Substance produced by cancer or body in response to cancer. Used for diagnosis, monitoring, prognosis. Examples: PSA (prostate), CA-125 (ovarian), CEA (colon), AFP (liver), CA 19-9 (pancreas).

\medterm{Tumor Markers}-- Substance produced by cancer or body in response to cancer. See \hyperlink{Tumor Marker}{tumor marker}.

\medterm{Tumor Microenvironment}-- Surrounding tissue influencing tumor growth. Includes immune cells, blood vessels, fibroblasts, signaling molecules.

\medterm{Tumor Suppressor}-- Gene that prevents uncontrolled cell growth. See \hyperlink{Tumor Suppressor Gene}{tumor suppressor gene}.

\medterm{Tumor Suppressor Gene}-- Gene that prevents uncontrolled cell growth. Examples: TP53, RB1, BRCA1, BRCA2, APC. Loss of function contributes to cancer.

\medterm{Ultrasound}-- Imaging using high-frequency sound waves (2-18 MHz). See radiology.

\medterm{Vaccine}-- Preventive or therapeutic vaccination. Preventive: HPV vaccine, hepatitis B vaccine. Therapeutic: sipuleucel-T (prostate cancer).

\medterm{Performance Status}-- A standardized measure of a patient’s functional ability and activity level, commonly recorded with ECOG or Karnofsky scales. It helps estimate prognosis, determine treatment eligibility, and quantify change during cancer care.

\medterm{Minimal Residual Disease (MRD)}-- The small number of malignant cells that remain after treatment and are below detection by conventional clinical examination or routine microscopy. Sensitive flow-cytometric, molecular, or imaging methods may detect MRD and refine relapse-risk assessment.

\medterm{Measurable Residual Disease}-- The burden of cancer remaining after therapy that can be quantified using a validated assay. The term is increasingly preferred to minimal residual disease because residual disease may be clinically important even when not minimal.

\medterm{Germline Mutation}-- A genetic variant present in the egg or sperm and therefore present in essentially every cell of the individual. Pathogenic germline variants may confer inherited cancer susceptibility and can have implications for relatives and treatment.


\medterm{Tumor Heterogeneity}-- The presence of genetically, epigenetically, or phenotypically distinct subpopulations within the same tumor or between primary and metastatic sites. Heterogeneity can influence prognosis, sampling, treatment response, and resistance.

\medterm{Clonal Evolution}-- The selection and expansion of tumor-cell subclones with biologic or treatment-related advantages over time. It contributes to progression, metastasis, and acquired resistance to anticancer therapy.

\medterm{ECOG Performance Status}-- A five-point scale describing functional impairment, from 0 for fully active to 5 for death. It is widely used in clinical trials, prognostic assessment, and decisions about systemic anticancer treatment.

\medterm{Febrile Neutropenia}-- Fever in a patient with a sufficiently low absolute neutrophil count to create a high risk of serious infection, generally defined as a single temperature of at least 38.3°C or sustained temperature of at least 38.0°C with ANC below 500/\(\mu\)L or expected to fall below that level. It requires urgent evaluation and empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics.

\medterm{Oncologic Emergency}-- A cancer-related condition that threatens life, organ function, or neurologic integrity and requires urgent treatment. Examples include malignant spinal-cord compression, superior vena cava syndrome, tumor lysis syndrome, hypercalcemia, airway obstruction, and febrile neutropenia.

\medterm{Dose-Limiting Toxicity (DLT)}-- An adverse effect of an anticancer treatment severe enough to prevent further dose escalation or require dose reduction according to a predefined protocol. DLTs are used in early-phase clinical trials to estimate a tolerable dose.

\medterm{Adverse Event (AE)}-- Any untoward medical occurrence during or after a treatment or intervention, whether or not it is considered causally related to that treatment. Severity, seriousness, expectedness, and relatedness are assessed separately.

\medterm{Immune-Related Adverse Event (irAE)}-- An inflammatory or autoimmune toxicity caused by immune checkpoint blockade or another immune-activating cancer treatment. It may affect skin, bowel, liver, endocrine organs, lungs, heart, kidneys, or nervous system and often requires treatment-specific immunosuppression.

\medterm{Chemotherapy Extravasation}-- Leakage of an intravenous anticancer drug into surrounding tissue. Vesicant extravasation can cause severe local injury, so infusion must stop immediately while the catheter is managed and drug-specific antidote and surgical guidance are obtained.

\medterm{Cancer-Associated Thrombosis}-- Venous or arterial thrombosis occurring in the setting of active malignancy or its treatment. Tumor biology, immobilization, surgery, central venous devices, and systemic therapy contribute to risk; anticoagulation is individualized according to bleeding risk and cancer factors.

\medterm{Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB)}-- The number of somatic mutations per unit of coding DNA in a tumor specimen. TMB may correlate with production of neoantigens and response to some immune checkpoint inhibitors, but interpretation requires a validated assay and disease-specific context.

\medterm{Microsatellite Instability (MSI)}-- A molecular pattern caused by deficient DNA mismatch-repair function, resulting in length changes in short repetitive DNA sequences. MSI-high tumors may have increased mutation burden and can be eligible for particular immunotherapies.
\medterm{Adenocarcinoma}-- A malignant tumor arising from glandular epithelial tissue, forming gland-like structures. The most common type of carcinoma, occurring in organs such as the colon, breast, lung, pancreas, and prostate.
\medterm{Angiosarcoma}-- A rare, aggressive malignant tumor of blood vessel or lymphatic endothelial cells. Common sites include skin, breast, liver, and bone. Treatment typically involves wide surgical excision with chemotherapy and radiation.
\medterm{Blastic plasmacytoid dendritic-cell neoplasm}-- A rare, aggressive hematologic malignancy derived from plasmacytoid dendritic cells. It often involves skin, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and central nervous system, with a poor prognosis.
\medterm{Carcinoma en cuirasse}-- An unusual form of breast cancer characterized by diffuse, firm, indurated skin that resembles armor. It results from extensive dermal lymphatic invasion by tumor cells.
\medterm{Chondroblastic osteosarcoma}-- A variant of osteosarcoma in which the malignant cells produce a cartilaginous matrix. It typically affects the long bones of adolescents and young adults, with a predilection for the metaphyseal regions.
\medterm{Conjunctival squamous-cell carcinoma}-- A malignant tumor of the conjunctival epithelium, typically arising from the limbal area. It is associated with UV radiation exposure and can invade locally if not treated promptly.
\medterm{Cutaneous metastases}-- Secondary deposits of cancer in the skin, originating from a primary tumor elsewhere in the body. They may present as nodules, plaques, or ulcers, and often indicate advanced or recurrent disease.
\medterm{Extranodal natural killer T-cell lymphoma}-- An aggressive lymphoma arising from natural killer cells, most commonly involving the nasal cavity and upper aerodigestive tract. It is strongly associated with Epstein-Barr virus infection.
\medterm{Intraabdominal cancer}-- A broad term for malignancies arising within the abdominal cavity, including cancers of the colon, stomach, pancreas, liver, ovaries, and peritoneum. Presentation varies widely depending on the specific organ involved.
\medterm{Invasive ductal carcinoma}-- The most common type of breast cancer, characterized by malignant cells that have broken through the duct wall and invaded surrounding breast tissue. It can metastasize via lymphatic or hematogenous spread.
\medterm{Leukemia cutis}-- Infiltration of the skin by leukemic cells, presenting as papules, nodules, or plaques. It is more common in acute myeloid leukemia and indicates a poorer prognosis.
\medterm{Metastatic adenocarcinoma}-- An adenocarcinoma that has spread from its primary site to distant organs, commonly the liver, lungs, bones, or brain. The prognosis and treatment depend on the primary tumor origin and the extent of metastatic disease.
\medterm{Metastatic inguinal lymphadenopathy}-- Enlargement of inguinal lymph nodes due to metastatic cancer cells, often from primary tumors of the lower extremities, pelvis, or anorectal region. It may indicate advanced disease requiring systemic therapy.
\medterm{Metastatic lung cancer}-- Lung cancer that has spread beyond the lungs to other organs, such as the brain, bones, liver, or adrenal glands. It represents advanced stage disease with limited treatment options.
\medterm{Orbital metastasis}-- Secondary cancer deposits in the orbit, most commonly from breast, lung, or prostate primaries. Symptoms include proptosis, diplopia, pain, and visual impairment.
\medterm{Pituitary tumor}-- A neoplasm arising from the pituitary gland, either functioning (hormone-secreting) or non-functioning. It can cause visual field defects, headaches, and hormonal imbalances depending on size and secretory status.
\medterm{Plasmacytoma}-- A solitary mass of neoplastic plasma cells, which may occur in bone (solitary plasmacytoma of bone) or soft tissue (extramedullary plasmacytoma). It can progress to multiple myeloma.
\medterm{Primary adrenal lymphoma}-- A rare lymphoma arising primarily in the adrenal gland, most commonly diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. It often presents bilaterally and may cause adrenal insufficiency.
\medterm{Radiation-induced angiosarcoma}-- A rare, aggressive soft tissue sarcoma arising in previously irradiated tissue, typically with a long latency period. It carries a poor prognosis due to local recurrence and metastatic potential.
\medterm{Spindle-cell sarcoma}-- A group of sarcomas characterized by elongated, spindle-shaped malignant cells. It includes entities like leiomyosarcoma, fibrosarcoma, and malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor, with variable behavior.
\medterm{Tonsillar cancer}-- A malignancy arising from the palatine tonsils, most commonly squamous cell carcinoma associated with HPV infection. It often presents with throat pain, otalgia, and cervical lymphadenopathy.
\medterm{Urachal mucinous cystic tumor}-- A rare tumor arising from the urachal remnant between the bladder dome and umbilicus. It may be benign or malignant, with mucinous histology, and requires surgical excision.
\medterm{Verrucous carcinoma}-- A low-grade, well-differentiated variant of squamous cell carcinoma with a warty, exophytic appearance. It is locally invasive but rarely metastasizes, commonly affecting the oral cavity, skin, or genitalia.
\medterm{Nephroblastoma}-- Wilms tumor, a malignant embryonal renal tumor of childhood that commonly presents as an asymptomatic abdominal mass.
\medterm{Oncocytoma}-- A usually benign epithelial tumor, commonly arising in the kidney and composed of cells with abundant eosinophilic mitochondria-rich cytoplasm.
\medterm{Rhabdomyosarcoma}-- A malignant tumor showing skeletal-muscle differentiation, occurring primarily in children and commonly involving the head and neck, genitourinary tract, or extremities.
\medterm{Seminoma}-- A malignant germ-cell tumor of the testis, typically presenting as a painless testicular mass and highly sensitive to radiation and chemotherapy.
\medterm{Sertoli-cell tumor}-- A sex-cord stromal tumor that may produce androgens or estrogens and can cause endocrine manifestations.
\medterm{Yolk-sac tumor}-- A malignant germ-cell tumor that commonly occurs in children and produces alpha-fetoprotein.
\medterm{Adenocarcinoma of the colon}-- A malignant gland-forming epithelial tumor of the colon, commonly presenting with altered bowel habits, occult bleeding, anemia, or an obstructing mass.
\medterm{Cervical carcinoma}-- A malignant tumor of the uterine cervix, most commonly associated with persistent high-risk human papillomavirus infection.
\medterm{Cerebral tumor}-- A neoplasm arising within or involving the brain, producing symptoms according to its location, growth rate, and effect on intracranial pressure.

\medterm{Advanced Cancer}-- Cancer that has grown beyond its original site. It may be locally advanced when it has invaded nearby tissues or metastatic when it has spread to distant organs.

\medterm{Aggressive Cancer}-- A cancer that grows, invades, or spreads rapidly and may require prompt diagnosis and treatment. Aggressiveness depends on tumour biology, grade, stage, and response to therapy.

\medterm{Active Surveillance}-- Close, structured monitoring of a cancer or precancerous condition without immediate treatment. It includes scheduled examinations, laboratory tests, imaging, or biopsies, with treatment started if evidence of progression develops.

\medterm{Cancer Cell}-- A cell that has acquired genetic and epigenetic abnormalities allowing uncontrolled growth, impaired normal differentiation, resistance to cell death, invasion of surrounding tissues, or spread to distant sites.

\medterm{Cancer Care Team}-- The multidisciplinary group of health professionals involved in cancer care, which may include oncologists, surgeons, radiologists, pathologists, nurses, pharmacists, rehabilitation specialists, psychologists, and palliative-care professionals.

\medterm{Cancer Surveillance}-- The systematic, ongoing collection and analysis of information about cancer incidence, stage, treatment, survival, recurrence, and mortality for patient follow-up, research, and public-health planning.

\medterm{Cancer Susceptibility Genes}-- Genes in which inherited pathogenic variants increase a person’s risk of developing one or more cancers. Examples include BRCA1, BRCA2, APC, and mismatch-repair genes; a susceptibility variant does not guarantee that cancer will develop.

\medterm{Adenoma}-- A benign tumour arising from glandular or gland-like epithelial tissue. Some adenomas, particularly certain colorectal adenomas, can develop dysplasia and progress to carcinoma.

\medterm{Adenomatous Polyp}-- A benign gland-forming polyp, most commonly in the colon, that may contain dysplasia and can progress through the adenoma--carcinoma sequence. Tubular, villous, and tubulovillous types are recognized.

\medterm{Cancer Vaccine}-- A vaccine intended either to prevent cancer-causing infections or to stimulate an immune response against cancer cells. Preventive examples target oncogenic infections, while therapeutic vaccines are designed for selected established cancers.

\medterm{Progression-Free Survival}-- The length of time from a defined starting point, such as diagnosis or treatment, until cancer progression or death from any cause, whichever occurs first.

\medterm{Disease-Free Survival}-- The length of time after treatment during which a patient remains alive without detectable evidence of the disease. The precise definition depends on the cancer and the study protocol.

\medterm{Oncologist}-- A physician who specializes in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer. Includes medical oncologists (systemic therapy), radiation oncologists (radiotherapy), and surgical oncologists (operative management).

\medterm{Glioma}-- A tumor arising from glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, ependymal cells) in the brain or spinal cord. Includes astrocytomas, oligodendrogliomas, and ependymomas; behavior ranges from low-grade to highly aggressive (glioblastoma).

\medterm{Astrocytoma}-- A glioma arising from star-shaped cells called astrocytes. Graded I-IV by the WHO; grade IV (glioblastoma) is the most aggressive and common primary malignant brain tumor in adults.

\medterm{Oligodendroglioma}-- A slow-growing glioma arising from oligodendrocytes, most often in the cerebral hemispheres. Associated with IDH mutation and 1p/19q codeletion, which confer a better prognosis and chemosensitivity.

\medterm{Ependymoma}-- A glioma arising from the ependymal cells lining the ventricles and spinal canal; more common in children and frequently associated with hydrocephalus.

\medterm{Medulloblastoma}-- A fast-growing embryonal tumor of the cerebellum and the most common malignant brain tumor of childhood. Treated with surgical resection, craniospinal radiation, and chemotherapy.

\medterm{Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)}-- Primary liver cancer arising from hepatocytes. Risk factors: cirrhosis, chronic hepatitis B/C, alcohol, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. Surveillance with ultrasound and AFP; treatment includes resection, transplantation, ablation, TACE, and systemic therapy.

\medterm{Hepatoblastoma}-- A malignant embryonal liver tumor occurring almost exclusively in infants and young children, associated with elevated AFP and prematurity.

\medterm{Fibrosarcoma}-- A malignant tumor of fibroblasts, a soft tissue sarcoma of adulthood or infancy, arising in deep soft tissues, bones, or paranasal sinuses.

\medterm{Leiomyosarcoma}-- A malignant tumor of smooth muscle, most often arising in the uterus, retroperitoneum, or gastrointestinal tract.

\medterm{Liposarcoma}-- A malignant tumor of fat cells, usually arising in the deep soft tissues of the thigh or retroperitoneum; well-differentiated, myxoid, and pleomorphic subtypes are recognized.

\medterm{Hemangiosarcoma}-- A malignant tumor of vascular endothelial cells, an aggressive sarcoma that may arise in the skin, liver, or bone.

\medterm{Malignant Mesothelioma}-- A cancer of the mesothelial lining of the pleura (most common), peritoneum, or pericardium. Strongly associated with asbestos exposure, presenting with effusion, chest pain, and dyspnea; prognosis is generally poor.

\medterm{Soft Tissue Sarcoma}-- A heterogeneous group of cancers arising in muscle, fat, fibrous tissue, blood vessels, and other connective tissues, accounting for about 1\% of adult cancers. Treatment: wide surgical excision with or without radiation and chemotherapy.

\medterm{Synovial Sarcoma}-- A soft tissue sarcoma typically arising near joints of the extremities in young adults, characterized by the t(X;18) chromosomal translocation.

\medterm{Thymoma}-- A tumor of thymic epithelial cells, associated with myasthenia gravis, pure red cell aplasia, and hypogammaglobulinemia. Malignant behavior is determined by capsular invasion and stage.

\medterm{Adenoid Cystic Carcinoma}-- A rare malignant tumor of glandular tissue, most commonly of the salivary glands, characterized by slow growth, perineural spread, and late recurrence.

\medterm{Adrenocortical Carcinoma (ACC)}-- A rare malignant tumor of the adrenal cortex that may secrete cortisol, androgens, or aldosterone or be non-functional. Often large at diagnosis; treatment is en bloc resection, with mitotane for advanced disease.

\medterm{Small Cell Carcinoma}-- A high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma, most often of the lung, strongly associated with smoking. Highly responsive initially to chemotherapy and radiation but prone to rapid recurrence and early brain metastases.

\medterm{Signet Ring Cell Carcinoma}-- A poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma in which intracellular mucin displaces the nucleus to the periphery, most often of the stomach or colon; associated with an aggressive course and diffuse growth pattern.

\medterm{Germinoma}-- A germ-cell tumor most commonly arising in the pineal or suprasellar region, highly sensitive to radiation and chemotherapy. Ovarian (dysgerminoma) and testicular (seminoma) counterparts exist.

\medterm{Carcinosarcoma}-- A malignant tumor containing both carcinomatous and sarcomatous elements, most commonly of the uterus (malignant mixed Müllerian tumor), with an aggressive course.

\medterm{Paraganglioma}-- A neuroendocrine tumor arising from extra-adrenal paraganglia, related to pheochromocytoma; may secrete catecholamines. Associations with hereditary syndromes (SDH mutations, VHL, NF1) warrant genetic counseling.

\medterm{Hamartoma}-- A benign growth composed of a disorganized mixture of tissue elements normally present at that site, e.g., pulmonary hamartoma, Peutz-Jeghers polyps, and PTEN-related (Cowden) lesions.

\medterm{Histiocytosis}-- A group of rare disorders of abnormal histiocyte (macrophage/dendritic cell) proliferation, including Langerhans cell histiocytosis, Erdheim-Chester disease, and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.

\medterm{Lymphadenectomy}-- Surgical removal of lymph nodes, either sentinel or regional, for staging or therapeutic purposes; examples include axillary, inguinal, cervical, and retroperitoneal lymph node dissection.

\medterm{Omentectomy}-- Removal of part or all of the greater omentum, commonly performed during staging and cytoreduction for ovarian and other intra-abdominal cancers.

\medterm{Salpingo-oophorectomy}-- Surgical removal of one or both fallopian tubes and ovaries. Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy treats and stages ovarian cancer and is used as risk-reducing surgery in BRCA mutation carriers.

\medterm{Orchiectomy}-- Surgical removal of one or both testicles. Radical inguinal orchiectomy is diagnostic and therapeutic for testicular cancer; bilateral orchiectomy is a hormonal therapy for prostate cancer.

\medterm{Pancreatectomy}-- Removal of all (total) or part of the pancreas. Pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple) is used for pancreatic head and periampullary tumors; distal pancreatectomy for body and tail lesions.

\medterm{Hepatectomy}-- Surgical removal of part or all of the liver for primary or metastatic tumors, requiring an adequate future liver remnant; portal vein embolization can hypertrophy the remnant before resection.

\medterm{Metastasectomy}-- Surgical removal of one or more metastases, which may be curative in selected oligometastatic disease, e.g., colorectal liver and lung metastases and sarcoma pulmonary metastases.

\medterm{Debulking}-- Surgical removal of as much tumor as possible when complete resection is not feasible, aiming to reduce tumor burden (cytoreduction) and improve response to subsequent therapy; central to ovarian cancer management.

\medterm{Radioembolization}-- Intra-arterial delivery of radioactive microspheres (yttrium-90) to liver tumors through the hepatic artery, used for hepatocellular carcinoma and liver metastases.

\medterm{Transarterial Chemoembolization (TACE)}-- A procedure in which chemotherapy and embolizing particles are infused selectively into tumor-feeding hepatic arteries, combining regional chemotherapy with ischemic tumor necrosis; used for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma.

\medterm{Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy (SBRT)}-- A highly precise form of external radiation delivering high-dose ablative treatment in few fractions, used for early-stage lung cancer and selected liver, pancreatic, spinal, and kidney tumors.

\medterm{Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT)}-- A computer-optimized form of three-dimensional radiation therapy that shapes and modulates the radiation dose to conform tightly to the target while sparing adjacent normal tissue.

\medterm{Hyperthermic Intraperitoneal Chemotherapy (HIPEC)}-- Heated chemotherapy circulated through the peritoneal cavity during surgery, following cytoreductive surgery, for peritoneal carcinomatosis from appendiceal, colorectal, and ovarian cancers and peritoneal mesothelioma.

\medterm{Pleurectomy}-- Surgical removal of part or all of the pleura, used in the treatment of malignant pleural mesothelioma and recurrent malignant pleural effusion.

\medterm{Pneumonectomy}-- Removal of an entire lung, most often for centrally located lung cancer; carries higher operative morbidity and mortality than lobectomy and requires preserved contralateral lung function.

\medterm{Micrometastasis}-- A small deposit of cancer cells that has spread from the primary tumor but is not detectable clinically or on standard imaging, e.g., isolated tumor cells in a sentinel lymph node; relevant to staging and prognosis.

\medterm{Myelosuppression}-- Reduced bone marrow production of red cells, white cells, and platelets, causing anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia; a common dose-limiting toxicity of chemotherapy and radiation that increases infection and bleeding risk.

\medterm{Mucositis}-- Inflammation and ulceration of the oral or gastrointestinal mucosa caused by chemotherapy or radiation, causing pain, dysphagia, and poor intake and predisposing to local infection.

\medterm{RECIST}-- Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, standardized imaging-based criteria that categorize response to anticancer therapy as complete response, partial response, stable disease, or progressive disease.

\medterm{Circulating Tumor DNA (ctDNA)}-- Fragments of tumor-derived DNA detectable in blood, enabling molecular profiling, treatment monitoring, and detection of minimal residual disease without tissue biopsy (liquid biopsy).

\medterm{Mismatch Repair Deficiency (dMMR)}-- Loss of DNA mismatch-repair function that produces microsatellite instability and a high tumor mutational burden. Seen in Lynch syndrome and some sporadic colorectal, endometrial, and other cancers, and associated with responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors.

\medterm{Anal Cancer}-- Malignant tumor of the anal canal or margin, most commonly squamous cell carcinoma associated with high-risk human papillomavirus infection. Presents with bleeding, pain, or a mass; primary treatment is chemoradiation, with surgery reserved for residual or recurrent disease.

\medterm{Esophageal Cancer}-- Malignant tumor of the esophagus. Squamous cell carcinoma predominates in the upper and middle third (smoking, alcohol); adenocarcinoma of the lower third arises from Barrett esophagus. Presents with progressive dysphagia and weight loss; treatment depends on stage and includes surgery, chemoradiation, and systemic therapy.

\medterm{Head and Neck Cancer}-- Cancers arising in the oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, sinonasal tract, and salivary glands, most commonly squamous cell carcinoma. Risk factors include tobacco, alcohol, and HPV (oropharyngeal). Treatment involves surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy, often combined to preserve function.

\medterm{Oral Cavity Cancer}-- Squamous cell carcinoma of the lip, tongue, buccal mucosa, floor of mouth, or palate, associated with tobacco, alcohol, and betel quid use. Treatment is primarily surgical resection, with adjuvant radiation or chemoradiation for adverse features.

\medterm{Salivary Gland Cancer}-- A rare malignancy most often arising in the parotid gland. Histologic types include mucoepidermoid carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, and acinic cell carcinoma; facial nerve preservation is a key surgical consideration.

\medterm{Vaginal Cancer}-- A rare gynecologic malignancy, most commonly squamous cell carcinoma linked to human papillomavirus. Treatment depends on stage and includes surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy.

\medterm{Fallopian Tube Cancer}-- A rare tumor histologically similar to high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma. Presents with a pelvic mass, pain, or watery discharge; staging and treatment follow the principles of ovarian cancer.

\medterm{Germ Cell Tumor}-- A tumor arising from germ cells (precursors of sperm and eggs), including seminoma, dysgerminoma, teratoma, yolk sac tumor, embryonal carcinoma, and choriocarcinoma. Occurs in the testis, ovary, and extragonadal sites (mediastinum, retroperitoneum, pineal); many are highly curable with platinum-based chemotherapy.

\medterm{Anaplastic Thyroid Cancer}-- A rare, rapidly growing, and highly aggressive thyroid cancer that often presents as a large fixed neck mass with dysphagia or airway compromise. Prognosis is poor; management is usually palliative.

\medterm{Desmoid Tumor}-- A locally aggressive but non-metastasizing fibrous tumor (fibromatosis), associated with familial adenomatous polyposis. Treatment includes surgery, radiation, and systemic therapy; recurrence after resection is common.

\medterm{Phyllodes Tumor}-- A breast tumor composed of both stromal and epithelial elements, ranging from benign through borderline to malignant. Typically a large, rapidly growing mass; treatment is surgical excision with clear margins.

\medterm{Mycosis Fungoides}-- The most common cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, presenting with progressive patches, plaques, and tumors on the skin with an indolent course. Staging and therapy depend on skin involvement and extracutaneous spread.

\medterm{Sézary Syndrome}-- The leukemic variant of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, characterized by erythroderma, lymphadenopathy, pruritus, and circulating atypical lymphocytes (Sézary cells).

\medterm{Hemangioblastoma}-- A benign, well-circumscribed vascular tumor of the central nervous system, most commonly the cerebellum, often cystic with a mural nodule. Associated with von Hippel-Lindau disease; treated by surgical resection.

\medterm{Invasive Lobular Carcinoma}-- A breast cancer characterized by discobesive cells infiltrating in single-file lines. More frequently bilateral and multifocal than ductal carcinoma and harder to detect on imaging; usually hormone receptor positive.

\medterm{Rhabdoid Tumor}-- A highly aggressive malignant tumor of infancy, occurring in the kidney (malignant rhabdoid tumor) or brain (atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor), characterized by loss of the SMARCB1 (INI1) gene.

\medterm{Granulosa Cell Tumor}-- An estrogen-secreting ovarian sex cord-stromal tumor that can cause precocious puberty, endometrial hyperplasia, or carcinoma. Generally low-grade with a tendency to late recurrence.

\medterm{Leydig Cell Tumor}-- A testicular sex cord-stromal tumor that secretes testosterone or estrogen, causing precocious puberty or gynecomastia; most are benign and treated by orchidectomy.

\medterm{Choroid Plexus Tumor}-- A papillary neoplasm of the choroid plexus, occurring most often in children, and frequently presenting with hydrocephalus from excessive cerebrospinal fluid production.

\medterm{Pineal Gland Tumor}-- A tumor of the pineal region, including germinoma, pineocytoma, and pineoblastoma. Presents with Parinaud syndrome, hydrocephalus, and raised intracranial pressure; germinomas are highly radiosensitive.

\medterm{Punch Biopsy}-- A small, full-thickness circular biopsy obtained with a rotating trephine, used to diagnose skin and soft-tissue lesions. Suture closure may be needed.

\medterm{Shave Biopsy}-- Removal of a raised skin lesion by horizontal slicing with a scalpel at the level of the dermis; used for exophytic lesions such as seborrheic keratoses and superficial basal cell carcinomas.

\medterm{Mohs Surgery}-- A micrographically controlled technique in which skin cancer is excised in thin layers and examined margin-by-margin, achieving high cure rates for basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma while sparing normal tissue.

\medterm{Photodynamic Therapy (PDT)}-- A treatment that combines a photosensitizing agent with light to generate reactive oxygen species and destroy cancer cells, used for actinic keratosis, superficial skin cancers, and selected visceral tumors.

\medterm{Whole-Brain Radiation Therapy (WBRT)}-- Radiation directed at the entire brain, used for multiple brain metastases, leptomeningeal disease, and prophylactic cranial irradiation in small cell lung cancer and acute lymphoblastic leukemia.

\medterm{Intrathecal Chemotherapy}-- Administration of chemotherapy directly into the cerebrospinal fluid via lumbar puncture or an Ommaya reservoir, used for leptomeningeal metastasis and central nervous system prophylaxis in leukemias and lymphomas.

\medterm{Fertility Preservation}-- Measures taken before gonadotoxic cancer therapy to preserve reproductive potential, including embryo or oocyte cryopreservation, sperm banking, and ovarian or testicular tissue cryopreservation.

\medterm{Cytoreductive Surgery}-- Surgical removal of as much visible tumor as possible to reduce disease burden, a cornerstone of ovarian cancer management and combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy for peritoneal carcinomatosis.

\medterm{Tumor Grade}-- A histologic measure of how much tumor cells resemble normal tissue; low-grade (well-differentiated) tumors behave less aggressively than high-grade (poorly differentiated) tumors. Grading is a prognostic, not staging, feature.

\medterm{Tumor Burden}-- The total amount of cancer in the body, estimated from the number, size, and extent of tumor deposits; higher burden is generally associated with worse prognosis.

\medterm{Progressive Disease}-- Worsening of cancer during or after treatment, defined per RECIST as growth of target lesions, appearance of new lesions, or unequivocal progression of non-target lesions.

\medterm{Objective Response Rate (ORR)}-- The proportion of patients whose tumor shrinks by a predefined amount (complete or partial response) during treatment; a common efficacy endpoint in oncology trials.

\medterm{Event-Free Survival}-- The time from randomization or treatment start to a specified event such as disease progression, relapse, or death from any cause.

\medterm{Relapse-Free Survival}-- The time from completion of treatment to recurrence of the cancer or death from any cause.

\medterm{Metastasis-Free Survival}-- The time from treatment until the development of distant metastasis or death; an important endpoint in adjuvant therapy trials.

\medterm{Median Survival}-- The time at which 50\% of patients have died; a common measure of prognosis in advanced cancer.

\medterm{Five-Year Survival Rate}-- The proportion of patients alive five years after diagnosis or treatment; a standard benchmark for comparing cancer outcomes.

\medterm{Chemoprevention}-- The use of drugs or natural agents to prevent, delay, or reverse cancer development, e.g., tamoxifen or raloxifene in high-risk breast cancer, HPV and hepatitis B vaccination, and aspirin in colorectal cancer.

\medterm{Chemoradiation}-- The concurrent combination of chemotherapy and radiation therapy, used to treat locoregionally advanced cancers such as anal, cervical, head and neck, esophageal, and limited-stage small cell lung cancer.

\medterm{Endocrine Therapy}-- Systemic treatment that manipulates hormones or their receptors in hormone-sensitive cancers, including tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone agonists, and antiandrogens.

\medterm{Systemic Therapy}-- Treatment that reaches the whole body, including chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, as opposed to local therapies such as surgery and radiation.

\medterm{Salvage Therapy}-- Treatment given for cancer that has not responded to or has recurred after initial (first-line) therapy.

\medterm{Induction Therapy}-- Intensive initial treatment aimed at achieving remission, especially in acute leukemias.

\medterm{Consolidation Therapy}-- Additional treatment given after remission to eliminate residual disease and reduce the risk of relapse.

\medterm{Oligometastasis}-- A limited number of metastatic deposits, often defined as up to five, amenable to aggressive local treatment (surgery, stereotactic radiation, or ablation) with the potential for prolonged control.

\medterm{Leptomeningeal Metastasis}-- Spread of cancer to the leptomeninges and cerebrospinal fluid (carcinomatous meningitis), causing multifocal neurologic deficits; managed with intrathecal chemotherapy and involved-field radiation.

\medterm{Cancer of Unknown Primary (CUP)}-- Metastatic cancer in which the primary site cannot be identified despite investigation. Workup includes histology, immunohistochemistry, and molecular profiling to guide treatment.

\medterm{Malignant Ascites}-- Intraperitoneal fluid accumulation caused by peritoneal tumor spread, most often ovarian or gastrointestinal cancer. Management includes therapeutic paracentesis and, in selected cases, intraperitoneal chemotherapy or shunt placement.

\medterm{Malignant Pleural Effusion}-- Pleural fluid accumulation from pleural or metastatic disease, causing dyspnea. Managed with thoracentesis, pleurodesis, or an indwelling pleural catheter.

\medterm{Tumor Board}-- A multidisciplinary conference of oncologists, surgeons, radiologists, and pathologists who review individual cancer cases to coordinate diagnosis and treatment planning.

\medterm{Watchful Waiting}-- Conservative observation of a cancer without immediate treatment, reserving therapy for symptom onset or progression; used in low-risk prostate cancer and indolent lymphomas.

\medterm{Genetic Counselor}-- A health professional who evaluates inherited cancer risk, arranges and interprets genetic testing, and provides counseling to patients and families, including cascade testing of relatives.

\medterm{Adoptive Cell Therapy}-- A personalized cellular immunotherapy in which a patient's own lymphocytes are expanded or engineered ex vivo and reinfused to attack the tumor; examples include tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) therapy and chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy.

\medterm{Oncolytic Virus}-- A genetically modified virus that selectively replicates in and destroys cancer cells while sparing normal tissue, e.g., talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC) for melanoma.

\medterm{Pseudomyxoma Peritonei}-- Accumulation of mucinous ascites and mucin-producing tumor nodules in the peritoneal cavity, usually from a ruptured low-grade appendiceal or ovarian mucinous neoplasm; treated with cytoreductive surgery and hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy.

\medterm{Cervical Cancer}-- A malignant tumour arising from the cervix, most commonly caused by persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus. It may be asymptomatic early or cause abnormal vaginal bleeding, discharge, or pelvic pain; prevention and early detection rely on HPV vaccination, screening, and assessment of abnormal results.
