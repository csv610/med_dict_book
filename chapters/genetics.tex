\chapter{Genetics}

\medterm{Achondroplasia}-- Autosomal dominant skeletal dysplasia (FGFR3 gene). Most common form of dwarfism. Features: short limbs, macrocephaly, frontal bossing, trident hand, spinal stenosis. Homozygous form lethal. Normal intelligence.

\medterm{Allele}-- Alternative form of a gene at a particular locus. Individuals inherit two alleles for each gene (one from each parent). Alleles can be dominant, recessive, or codominant.

\medterm{Angelman Syndrome}-- Maternal 15q11-q13 deletion or paternal uniparental disomy. Features: severe intellectual disability, ataxia, seizures, inappropriate laughter, microcephaly. UBE3A gene implicated.

\medterm{Anticodon}-- Three-nucleotide sequence on tRNA complementary to mRNA codon, ensuring correct amino acid placement during translation.

\medterm{Association Study}-- Comparison of allele frequencies between cases and controls. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) identify common variants with small effects on complex diseases.

\medterm{Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba Syndrome}-- Autosomal dominant PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS) caused by loss-of-function mutations in PTEN (10q23), allelic to Cowden syndrome. Features: macrocephaly, intestinal hamartomatous polyps, lipomas, hemangiomas, developmental delay, intellectual disability, characteristic pigmented macules on the glans penis, thyroid disease (Hashimoto, goiter, thyroid adenomas), and increased risk of breast, thyroid, and endometrial cancers (lower penetrance than Cowden). Diagnosis: clinical + PTEN testing. Management: cancer surveillance per PHTS guidelines, polypectomy for symptomatic hamartomas, supportive care for developmental issues.

\medterm{Banti Syndrome}-- Historical eponym for non-cirrhotic portal hypertension with splenomegaly and hypersplenism, originally described by Guido Banti as "splenic anemia with portal hypertension but without hepatic cirrhosis." Causes include extrahepatic portal vein thrombosis, congenital hepatic fibrosis, schistosomiasis, nodular regenerative hyperplasia, and idiopathic myelofibrosis (some cases). Presents with splenomegaly, pancytopenia (hypersplenism), ascites, and variceal bleeding. Diagnosis: patent hepatic veins on imaging, normal liver synthetic function, liver biopsy excluding cirrhosis. Treatment: endoscopic band ligation of varices, beta-blockade, splenectomy or spleno-renal shunt, addressing underlying cause.

\medterm{Bloom Syndrome}-- Rare autosomal recessive chromosomal instability syndrome caused by biallelic mutations in BLM (15q26.1) encoding a RecQ helicase. Clinical features include short stature, sun-sensitive telangiectatic facial erythema ("butterfly rash"), distinctive narrow facies, high-pitched voice, immunodeficiency with recurrent sinopulmonary infections, reduced fertility, and greatly increased cancer risk (leukemia, lymphoma, gastrointestinal and skin cancers; lifetime risk $\sim$ 80 percent; often multiple primary tumors at young age). Cytogenetic hallmark: elevated sister chromatid exchanges (SCEs; $\sim 10\times$ normal) on karyotype. Diagnosis: clinical + BLM genetic testing + SCE testing. Management: surveillance for malignancies, sun protection, treatment of infections; hematopoietic stem cell transplant if leukemia develops.

\medterm{Breast Cancer Genetics}-- Hereditary breast cancer: BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations (lifetime risk 45-85%). Other genes: TP53 (Li-Fraumeni), PTEN (Cowden), PALB2, CHEK2. Genetic counseling and testing indicated for early onset, bilateral, male breast cancer, Ashkenazi Jewish heritage.

\medterm{Cancer Genetics}-- Inherited cancer syndromes. See oncology.

\medterm{Carrier}-- Individual heterozygous for a recessive genetic disorder. Typically asymptomatic but can pass mutant allele to offspring. Carrier screening recommended for certain populations (e.g., Ashkenazi Jewish for Tay-Sachs).

\medterm{Chromosomal Abnormality}-- Change in chromosome number or structure. Numerical: aneuploidy (abnormal number, e.g., trisomy 21), polyploidy (extra sets). Structural: deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation.

\medterm{Chromosome}-- Thread-like structure of nucleic acids and protein found in nucleus, carrying genetic information. Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs): 22 pairs of autosomes, 1 pair of sex chromosomes (XX female, XY male). Chromosomes condense during cell division for visualization.

\medterm{Codon}-- Sequence of three nucleotides coding for one amino acid or stop signal. 64 possible codons: 61 code for amino acids, 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). Degeneracy: multiple codons code for same amino acid.

\medterm{Colorectal Cancer Genetics}-- Lynch syndrome (HNPCC): MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2 mutations. Autosomal dominant, early-onset CRC, endometrial cancer. Screening: colonoscopy every 1-2 years starting age 20-25. Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP): APC gene, hundreds-thousands of polyps, near 100% CRC risk if prophylactic colectomy not performed.

\medterm{Concordance}-- Probability both twins have same trait. Monozygotic (identical) twins > dizygotic (fraternal) for genetic traits. High concordance suggests genetic influence.

\medterm{Congenital Disorder}-- A medical condition or structural abnormality present in an individual from birth, which may be caused by genetic factors or environmental influences in the womb.

\medterm{copy number variation (CNV)}-- Deletions or duplications of DNA segments >1 kb. Contribute to genetic diversity and disease. Examples: 22q11.2 deletion, Williams syndrome (7q11.23 deletion).
\medterm{Cowden Syndrome}-- Autosomal dominant PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS), caused by loss-of-function mutations in PTEN (10q23) — a negative regulator of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. Multiple hamartomas and increased risk of benign and malignant tumors. Major manifestations: multiple trichilemmomas (facial), papillomatous papules of oral mucosa/gingiva, acral keratoses, plantar punctate keratoses; macrocephaly ($> 97$th percentile), Lhermitte-Duclos disease (cerebellar dysplastic gangliocytoma), gastrointestinal hamartomatous polyps; increased risk: breast cancer (women $\sim 67\%$ lifetime, men also increased), thyroid cancer (follicular, $\sim 6\%$), endometrial cancer ($\sim 28\%$), renal cell carcinoma, melanoma. Diagnosis: clinical criteria (International Cowden Consortium) confirmed by PTEN genetic testing. Management: intensive cancer surveillance starting early — annual breast MRI/mammography from age 30-35, annual thyroid ultrasound from adulthood, endometrial screening, colonoscopy if polyps or symptoms. Differentiate from other PHTS: Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba.

\medterm{Cri-du-Chat Syndrome}-- 5p deletion. Features: high-pitched cry (mimics cat), intellectual disability, microcephaly, cardiac defects, facial dysmorphism. Diagnosis: chromosomal analysis, FISH.

\medterm{CRISPR-Cas9}-- Genome editing tool derived from bacterial immune system. Cas9 enzyme guided by RNA to specific DNA sequence, creating double-strand break. Repaired by non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) or homology-directed repair (HDR). Applications: research, therapy.

\medterm{Cystic Fibrosis}-- Autosomal recessive disorder caused by CFTR gene mutations (most common: $\Delta$F508). Defective chloride channel causes thick mucus in lungs, pancreas, liver, reproductive tract. Symptoms: chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, pancreatic insufficiency, male infertility (absent vas deferens), meconium ileus. Diagnosis: sweat chloride test, genetic testing. Treatment: CFTR modulators (ivacaftor, lumacaftor/ivacaftor, elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor), airway clearance, enzymes, antibiotics.

\medterm{Deletion}-- Loss of chromosomal segment. Terminal deletion: loss from end. Interstitial deletion: loss from middle. Microdeletion syndromes: 22q11.2 deletion (DiGeorge), 5p deletion (Cri-du-chat).

\medterm{DiGeorge Syndrome}-- 22q11.2 deletion syndrome. Features: conotruncal cardiac defects, abnormal facies, thymic hypoplasia (immune deficiency), cleft palate, hypocalcemia. Variable expressivity. Detection: FISH, microarray.

\medterm{DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)}-- Double-stranded helical molecule carrying genetic information. Composed of nucleotides (sugar-phosphate backbone with bases adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine). Replication is semi-conservative. Genes are specific sequences encoding proteins or functional RNAs. Chromosomes are highly condensed DNA-protein complexes. Human genome: ~3 billion base pairs, ~20,000-25,000 genes.

\medterm{DNA Sequencing}-- Determination of nucleotide order in DNA. Sanger sequencing: chain termination method, gold standard for small targets. Next-generation sequencing (NGS): massively parallel sequencing, whole genome, whole exome, targeted panels.

\medterm{Dominant}-- Allele whose phenotype is expressed in heterozygotes. Autosomal dominant disorders: Huntington disease, marfan syndrome, achondroplasia. Requires only one copy for expression.

\medterm{Down Syndrome}-- Trisomy 21, most common viable trisomy. Features: intellectual disability, characteristic facies (flat face, upslanting palpebral fissures), hypotonia, heart defects, increased risk of leukemia, Alzheimer disease. Maternal age is major risk factor.

\medterm{Duplication}-- Presence of extra copy of chromosomal segment. Can cause gene dosage effects. Examples: Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1A (PMP22 duplication).

\medterm{Edward Syndrome}-- Trisomy 18. Features: severe intellectual disability, clenched hands, rocker-bottom feet, cardiac defects, feeding difficulties. Most die within first year.

\medterm{Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome}-- Group of connective tissue disorders (collagen defects). Types: classical (skin hyperextensibility, atrophic scarring), vascular (arterial/intestinal/uterine rupture risk), hypermobile (joint hypermobility, pain). Diagnosis: clinical, genetic testing.

\medterm{Enhancer}-- DNA sequence increasing transcription of target gene, can be distant from gene, orientation-independent. Binds activator proteins. Examples: LCR (locus control region) for beta-globin.

\medterm{Epigenetics}-- Heritable changes in gene expression without DNA sequence alteration. Mechanisms: DNA methylation, histone modification, non-coding RNA. Influenced by environment, diet, stress. Reversible.

\medterm{Epistasis}-- Interaction between genes where one gene masks or modifies expression of another. Examples: coat color in mice, ABO blood group modifications.

\medterm{Exon}-- A segment of a DNA or RNA molecule containing information coding for a protein or peptide sequence.

\medterm{Expressivity}-- Degree to which a genotype is expressed in phenotype. Variable expressivity: same genotype produces different phenotypic severity in different individuals. Examples: neurofibromatosis type 1, Marfan syndrome.

\medterm{Fragile X Syndrome}-- X-linked intellectual disability (FMR1 gene, CGG trinucleotide expansion). Features: intellectual disability, long face, large ears, macroorchidism, autism spectrum features. Most common inherited cause of intellectual disability. Premutation carriers at risk for FXTAS (tremor/ataxia syndrome).

\medterm{Frameshift Mutation}-- Insertion or deletion of nucleotides not in multiples of three, shifting reading frame. Usually produces nonfunctional protein. Examples: some cystic fibrosis mutations, Tay-Sachs disease.

\medterm{Gene}-- Basic unit of heredity, segment of DNA encoding a functional product (protein or RNA). Structure: promoter, coding sequence (exons), introns, terminator. Genes are located at specific positions (loci) on chromosomes.

\medterm{Gene Expression}-- Process by which information from a gene is used to synthesize functional product (protein or RNA). Steps: transcription, RNA processing, translation, post-translational modification. Regulated at multiple levels.

\medterm{Gene Therapy}-- Treatment by introducing, removing, or changing genetic material. Approaches: gene addition (functional copy), gene silencing (siRNA, antisense), gene editing (CRISPR-Cas9). Examples: Luxturna (RPE65 mutation), Zolgensma (SMN1 in spinal muscular atrophy).

\medterm{Genetic Counseling}-- Process of helping individuals understand and adapt to genetic aspects of disease. Includes risk assessment, education, psychosocial support, informed decision-making.

\medterm{Genetic Screening}-- Testing of asymptomatic individuals for genetic disorders. Newborn screening: metabolic disorders, hemoglobinopathies. Carrier screening: before pregnancy or during pregnancy. Prenatal screening: non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT), maternal serum screening.

\medterm{Genomic Imprinting}-- Parent-of-origin-specific gene expression. Only one allele (maternal or paternal) is expressed. Examples: Prader-Willi (paternal deletion 15q11-q13), Angelman (maternal deletion 15q11-q13).

\medterm{Genotype}-- Genetic makeup of an individual, the specific alleles present at one or more loci. Determines phenotype along with environmental factors.

\medterm{Haplotype}-- Set of alleles inherited together on same chromosome. Used in linkage analysis, population genetics, disease association studies.

\medterm{Hemochromatosis}-- Autosomal recessive iron overload disorder (HFE gene, C282Y mutation). Iron deposition in liver, pancreas, heart, joints, skin. Bronze diabetes, cirrhosis, cardiomyopathy, arthropathy. Treatment: phlebotomy, deferexamine.

\medterm{heteroplasmy}-- Inheritance through mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), passed only from mother. See \hyperlink{Mitochondrial Inheritance}{mitochondrial inheritance}.

\medterm{Heterozygous}-- Having two different alleles of a particular gene (Aa). The dominant allele is typically expressed in the phenotype.

\medterm{Homozygous}-- Having two identical alleles of a particular gene (AA or aa). Can be homozygous dominant or homozygous recessive.

\medterm{Huntington Disease}-- Autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion in HTT gene. Onset: 30-50 years. Symptoms: chorea, dementia, psychiatric disease. Anticipation: earlier onset in successive generations (paternal transmission). No cure; symptomatic treatment.

\medterm{imprinting}-- Parent-of-origin-specific gene expression. See \hyperlink{Genomic Imprinting}{genomic imprinting}.

\medterm{Intron}-- A non-coding segment of a gene that is transcribed into RNA but spliced out before the messenger RNA is translated into a protein.

\medterm{Inversion}-- Chromosomal segment reversed end-to-end. Paracentric: does not include centromere. Pericentric: includes centromere. Usually benign unless breakpoint disrupts gene.

\medterm{Karyotype}-- Display of chromosomes arranged in pairs, ordered by size and banding pattern. Used to detect chromosomal abnormalities. Resolution: ~5-10 Mb.

\medterm{Klinefelter Syndrome}-- Male with extra X chromosome (47,XXY). Features: tall stature, small testes, gynecomastia, infertility, learning difficulties. Incidence ~1 in 600 male births.

\medterm{Leukemia Genetics}-- Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL): Philadelphia chromosome (t9;22), Hyperdiploidy, ETV6-RUNX1 fusion. Acute myeloid leukemia (AML): t8;21, t15;17 (APL), FLT3 mutations. Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML): Philadelphia chromosome (BCR-ABL).

\medterm{Linkage}-- Co-inheritance of nearby genetic markers and disease gene. Measured by recombination fraction (theta). LOD score >3 indicates linkage.

\medterm{Locus}-- Specific position of gene on chromosome. Designated by chromosome number, arm (p/q), band, subband. Example: 7q31.2 (CFTR gene).

\medterm{Marfan Syndrome}-- Autosomal dominant connective tissue disorder (FBN1 gene, fibrillin-1). Features: tall stature, long limbs, arachnodactyly, pectus excavatum/carinatum, lens subluxation, aortic root dilation, mitral valve prolapse. Risk: aortic dissection, rupture. Management: beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, elective aortic root replacement if >5 cm.

\medterm{Methylation}-- Addition of methyl group to DNA (cytosine) or histones. DNA methylation at CpG islands typically silences genes. Hypomethylation associated with cancer.

\medterm{Microarray}-- Technology measuring expression of thousands of genes simultaneously or detecting copy number variations. Gene expression microarrays: compare expression patterns. SNP arrays: detect genetic variations. Comparative genomic hybridization (CGH): detect deletions/duplications.

\medterm{Mitochondrial Inheritance}-- Inheritance through mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), passed only from mother. Examples: Leber hereditary optic neuropathy, MELAS syndrome. Heteroplasmy: mixture of mutant and normal mtDNA.

\medterm{Monosomy}-- Presence of only one copy of a chromosome instead of two. Only compatible with life is monosomy X (Turner syndrome): short stature, webbed neck, ovarian dysgenesis, normal intelligence.

\medterm{Mosaicism}-- Presence of two or more cell lines with different genotypes in one individual. Can be somatic (affecting body cells) or germline (affecting gametes). Origin: post-zygotic mutation.

\medterm{Mutation}-- Permanent change in DNA sequence. Types: point mutation (single base change), insertion, deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation. Effects: silent, missense, nonsense, frameshift.

\medterm{Mutation Rate}-- Frequency of new mutations per generation or per base pair per replication. Varies by gene, genomic region, organism. High mutation rate in repetitive sequences.

\medterm{Neuroblastoma}-- Childhood sympathetic nervous system tumor. MYCN amplification poor prognosis. Presents as abdominal mass, opsoclonus-myoclonus syndrome. Spontaneous regression possible in infants.

\medterm{Neurofibromatosis Type 1}-- Autosomal dominant disorder (NF1 gene, neurofibromin). Features: café-au-lait spots, axillary/inguinal freckling, neurofibromas, Lisch nodules (iris hamartomas), optic glioma, skeletal dysplasia. Diagnosis: clinical criteria. Risk: malignant transformation of plexiform neurofibroma.

\medterm{Patau Syndrome}-- Trisomy 13. Features: severe intellectual disability, holoprosencephaly, cleft lip/palate, polydactyly, cardiac defects. Most die within first year.

\medterm{PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)}-- Technique amplifying specific DNA sequences exponentially. Components: template DNA, primers, DNA polymerase (Taq), nucleotides, buffer. Cycles: denaturation, annealing, extension. Applications: diagnostics, forensics, research.

\medterm{Pedigree}-- Diagram showing inheritance of traits through generations. Symbols: squares (males), circles (females), shaded (affected), half-shaded (carriers). Used to determine inheritance pattern and recurrence risk.

\medterm{Penetrance}-- Proportion of individuals with a particular genotype who express the expected phenotype. Complete penetrance: all with genotype show phenotype. Incomplete penetrance: some with genotype do not show phenotype.

\medterm{Pharmacogenetics}-- Study of genetic variation affecting drug response. Examples: TPMT (mercaptopurine toxicity), CYP2C9/VKORC1 (warfarin dosing), HLA-B*1502 (carbamazepine SJS in Asians), HLA-B*5701 (abacavir hypersensitivity), UGT1A1 (irinotecan toxicity).

\medterm{Phenotype}-- Observable characteristics of an individual, resulting from genotype and environmental interactions. Includes morphology, development, biochemical properties, behavior.

\medterm{Phenylketonuria (PKU)}-- Autosomal recessive disorder (PAH gene, phenylalanine hydroxylase). Elevated phenylalanine causes intellectual disability, seizures, eczema, musty odor. Newborn screening essential. Treatment: phenylalanine-restricted diet, sapropterin (BH4 cofactor).

\medterm{Pleiotropy}-- Single gene influencing multiple phenotypic traits. Examples: phenylketonuria (PKU) affects multiple systems; sickle cell anemia causes anemia, pain crises, organ damage.

\medterm{Point Mutation}-- Change in single nucleotide base pair. Substitutions: transition (purine to purine, pyrimidine to pyrimidine) or transversion (purine to pyrimidine). Effects: silent (same amino acid), missense (different amino acid), nonsense (stop codon).

\medterm{Polygenic Inheritance}-- Trait influenced by multiple genes, each with small effect. Examples: height, skin color, common diseases (diabetes, heart disease). Produces continuous variation.

\medterm{Prader-Willi Syndrome}-- Paternal 15q11-q13 deletion or maternal uniparental disomy. Features: neonatal hypotonia, feeding difficulties, hyperphagia/obesity, hypogonadism, intellectual disability, behavioral problems. Growth hormone treatment.

\medterm{Prenatal Diagnosis}-- Detection of genetic or structural disorders in fetus before birth. Methods: chorionic villus sampling (CVS, 10-13 weeks), amniocentesis (15-20 weeks), percutaneous umbilical blood sampling (PUBS, after 18 weeks). Non-invasive: NIPT (cell-free fetal DNA).

\medterm{Promoter}-- DNA sequence upstream of gene where RNA polymerase and transcription factors bind to initiate transcription. Core promoter: TATA box, transcription start site. Regulatory elements: enhancers, silencers.

\medterm{Recessive}-- Allele whose phenotype is masked in heterozygotes. Autosomal recessive disorders: cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia, Tay-Sachs disease. Requires two copies for expression.

\medterm{Recombinant DNA}-- DNA molecule formed by combining genetic material from different sources. Basis of biotechnology. Used to produce insulin, growth hormone, vaccines, gene therapy vectors.

\medterm{Restriction Enzyme}-- Bacterial enzyme cutting DNA at specific recognition sequences. Used in molecular cloning, DNA fingerprinting, restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) analysis.

\medterm{Retinoblastoma}-- Childhood eye cancer. RB1 gene mutation (autosomal dominant inheritance, but sporadic cases common). Hereditary form: bilateral, multifocal, earlier onset. Sporadic: unilateral, single focus. Treatment: chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, enucleation.

\medterm{Sickle Cell Disease}-- Autosomal recessive hemoglobinopathy (HbS). Glutamic acid → valine at position 6 of beta-globin. Homozygous (HbSS) most severe. Sickling under hypoxia causes vaso-occlusion, hemolysis. Symptoms: pain crises, acute chest syndrome, stroke, splenic sequestration, aplastic crisis (parvovirus B19). Diagnosis: hemoglobin electrophoresis, newborn screening. Treatment: hydroxyurea, transfusions, penicillin prophylaxis, stem cell transplant.

\medterm{single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)}-- Single base pair variation occurring in >1% of population. Most common genetic variation. Used in GWAS, forensics, pharmacogenetics.

\medterm{Tay-Sachs Disease}-- Autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder (HEXA gene, hexosaminidase A). Accumulation of GM2 ganglioside in neurons. Ashkenazi Jewish prevalence higher. Symptoms: developmental regression, cherry-red spot on macula, seizures, paralysis, death by age 4. No cure. Carrier screening recommended.

\medterm{Transcription Factor}-- Protein binding to specific DNA sequences, regulating transcription. Activators enhance transcription; repressors inhibit. Examples: p53, NF-kB, steroid hormone receptors.

\medterm{Translocation}-- Movement of chromosomal segment to non-homologous chromosome. Reciprocal: exchange between chromosomes. Robertsonian: fusion of two acrocentric chromosomes. Balanced: no genetic material lost; unbalanced: gain or loss.

\medterm{trinucleotide repeat}-- Expansion of three-nucleotide sequences causes diseases: Huntington (CAG), Fragile X (CGG), myotonic dystrophy (CTG), Friedreich ataxia (GAA). Anticipation common.

\medterm{Trisomy}-- Presence of three copies of a chromosome instead of two. Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome): intellectual disability, characteristic facies, heart defects. Trisomy 18 (Edwards): severe intellectual disability, multiple anomalies. Trisomy 13 (Patau): severe intellectual disability, holoprosencephaly, cleft lip/palate.

\medterm{Turner Syndrome}-- Female with missing or abnormal X chromosome (45,X). Features: short stature, webbed neck, shield chest, ovarian dysgenesis, normal intelligence. Incidence ~1 in 2500 female births.

\medterm{Whole Exome Sequencing}-- Sequencing only protein-coding regions (~1-2% of genome). More cost-effective than whole genome. Detects ~85% of disease-causing variants.

\medterm{Whole Genome Sequencing}-- Determining complete DNA sequence of an individual's genome. Cost decreasing, clinical applications expanding. Identifies coding and non-coding variants.

\medterm{Wilms Tumor}-- Childhood renal malignancy. WT1 gene mutations in some cases. Associated with WAGR syndrome, Denys-Drash, Beckwith-Wiedemann. Treatment: nephrectomy, chemotherapy, radiation.

\medterm{Wilson Disease}-- Autosomal recessive copper metabolism disorder (ATP7B gene). Copper accumulation in liver, brain, cornea. Kayser-Fleischer rings, hepatopathy, movement disorder, psychiatric symptoms. Treatment: chelation (penicillamine, trientine), zinc, liver transplant.

\medterm{X-Inactivation}-- Process inactivating one X chromosome in female mammals, forming Barr body. Occurs early in development, random in most tissues. Ensures dosage compensation.

\medterm{Amniocentesis}-- A prenatal diagnostic procedure in which amniotic fluid is sampled with a needle under imaging guidance. Fetal cells and cell-free DNA can be analyzed for chromosomal, molecular, or biochemical abnormalities; procedure-related pregnancy loss and other complications must be discussed.

\medterm{Autosomal Inheritance}-- Transmission of a genetic trait through one of the 22 non-sex chromosomes. Autosomal dominant traits may appear in successive generations, whereas autosomal recessive disease generally requires pathogenic variants on both homologous chromosomes.

\medterm{BRCA1 and BRCA2}-- Tumor-suppressor genes involved in homologous-recombination DNA repair. Germline pathogenic variants increase susceptibility to breast, ovarian, fallopian-tube, primary peritoneal, prostate, and pancreatic cancers and may guide surveillance and treatment.

\medterm{Chorionic Villus Sampling}-- Prenatal sampling of placental chorionic-villus tissue, usually performed in the first trimester, for chromosomal or molecular genetic testing. It provides earlier diagnosis than amniocentesis but has procedure-related risks and does not directly assess open neural-tube defects.

\medterm{De Novo Variant}-- A genetic variant present in an affected individual that was not detected in either biological parent, usually arising in a parental gamete or early embryogenesis. It may cause a dominant disorder and can have implications for recurrence risk in future pregnancies.

\medterm{Fluorescence In-Situ Hybridization (FISH)}-- A targeted cytogenetic technique using fluorescent DNA probes to detect or localize specific chromosome sequences. It can identify selected aneuploidies, microdeletions, gene rearrangements, or amplifications but does not survey the entire genome.

\medterm{Germline Variant}-- A DNA variant present in the cells that give rise to eggs or sperm and therefore potentially transmissible to offspring. It may also be present in most cells of the individual, unlike a variant restricted to a somatic tissue.

\medterm{Genetic Heterogeneity}-- The occurrence of similar or related phenotypes caused by different genetic mechanisms. Locus heterogeneity refers to variants in different genes causing the same phenotype; allelic heterogeneity refers to different variants in the same gene.

\medterm{Gene Panel}-- A laboratory test that sequences a selected group of genes associated with a clinical phenotype. Panels can provide efficient, focused analysis but may miss variants outside the selected genes or changes poorly detected by the platform.

\medterm{Genetic Variant of Uncertain Significance}-- A DNA variant for which available evidence is insufficient to classify it as benign or pathogenic. It should not generally be used alone to make predictive or treatment decisions and may be reclassified as evidence evolves.

\medterm{Germline Testing}-- Genetic testing performed to identify inherited or potentially heritable variants, usually from blood or saliva. Results may affect the patient, biological relatives, cancer surveillance, reproductive planning, and genetic counseling.

\medterm{Lynch Syndrome}-- An autosomal dominant hereditary cancer syndrome caused by pathogenic variants in DNA mismatch-repair genes or EPCAM. It increases the risk of colorectal, endometrial, ovarian, gastric, urinary-tract, and other cancers and requires intensified surveillance.

\medterm{Loss-of-Function Variant}-- A genetic variant that reduces or abolishes the normal activity of the encoded gene product. Disease risk depends on the gene, inheritance pattern, residual function, and whether one or both copies are affected.

\medterm{Missense Variant}-- A single-nucleotide substitution that changes one amino acid in a protein. Its clinical effect ranges from benign to pathogenic and must be assessed using molecular, functional, population, and clinical evidence.

\medterm{Nonsense Variant}-- A sequence variant that creates a premature stop codon, potentially producing a truncated protein or triggering nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. The effect depends on the gene, location, transcript, and residual protein production.

\medterm{Next-Generation Sequencing}-- Massively parallel sequencing technology that analyzes many DNA molecules simultaneously. It supports gene panels, whole-exome sequencing, whole-genome sequencing, and tumor sequencing, with limitations involving coverage, structural variants, mosaicism, and interpretation.

\medterm{Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing}-- Screening of cell-free placental DNA in maternal blood for selected fetal chromosomal abnormalities, especially trisomies 21, 18, and 13. It is highly sensitive screening, not a definitive diagnosis; positive results require confirmatory invasive testing.

\medterm{Oncogene}-- A gene that, when activated, overexpressed, or altered, can promote uncontrolled cell proliferation or survival. Oncogenes commonly arise from normal proto-oncogenes through mutation, amplification, translocation, or abnormal regulation.

\medterm{Pathogenic Variant}-- A genetic variant with sufficient validated evidence to be interpreted as causing or contributing to disease. Classification uses standardized evidence from population data, segregation, functional studies, computational analysis, and phenotype correlation.

\medterm{Polygenic Risk Score}-- A statistical estimate of disease susceptibility calculated from the combined effects of many genetic variants, each usually contributing a small amount of risk. It modifies but does not replace clinical risk assessment or environmental factors.

\medterm{Proto-Oncogene}-- A normal gene that regulates cell growth, division, differentiation, or survival and can become an oncogene after activating mutation, amplification, translocation, or overexpression.

\medterm{Recurrence Risk}-- The probability that a genetic condition will occur again in a future pregnancy or relative, estimated from the inheritance pattern, parental testing, penetrance, and observed or presumed pathogenic variant.

\medterm{Somatic Variant}-- A DNA variant acquired after conception in a non-germline cell lineage. It is not usually transmitted to offspring and may produce mosaic disease or contribute to cancer when it affects growth-control pathways.

\medterm{Splice-Site Variant}-- A variant affecting the sequences required for accurate removal of introns and joining of exons during RNA processing. It may cause abnormal transcripts, exon skipping, intron retention, or reduced functional protein.

\medterm{Tumor-Suppressor Gene}-- A gene whose normal product restrains cell proliferation, promotes DNA repair, induces apoptosis, or maintains genomic stability. Loss or inactivation of both functional copies is common in the development of many cancers, although mechanisms vary by gene.

\medterm{Whole-Genome Sequencing}-- Sequencing of nearly the complete nuclear genome, including coding and noncoding regions, together with mitochondrial DNA when covered by the assay. It can detect a broad range of variants but generates substantial interpretation and incidental-finding challenges.

\medterm{X-Linked Inheritance}-- Transmission of a pathogenic variant on the X chromosome. Phenotypes differ by sex, X-inactivation, and variant mechanism; fathers do not transmit an X-linked variant to their sons.

\medterm{Hartnup Disease}-- An autosomal-recessive disorder of neutral-amino-acid transport, especially in the intestine and renal tubules. Defective tryptophan handling can cause pellagra-like photosensitive dermatitis, cerebellar ataxia, and neuropsychiatric symptoms, often intermittently.

\medterm{Kearns-Sayre Syndrome}-- A mitochondrial cytopathy caused by large-scale mitochondrial DNA deletions or rearrangements. It classically begins before age 20 with progressive external ophthalmoplegia, pigmentary retinopathy, and cardiac-conduction disease, and may involve endocrine or neurological systems.

\medterm{Laron Syndrome}-- An inherited disorder of growth-hormone signaling, usually caused by growth-hormone-receptor defects. It produces severe postnatal growth failure, characteristic facial features, and very low insulin-like growth factor 1 despite elevated circulating growth hormone.

\medterm{Li-Fraumeni Syndrome}-- An inherited cancer-predisposition syndrome most often caused by a pathogenic TP53 variant. It confers high lifetime risks of sarcomas, breast cancer, brain tumors, adrenocortical carcinoma, leukemia, and other early-onset malignancies.

\medterm{Maffucci Syndrome}-- A rare congenital disorder characterized by multiple enchondromas together with soft-tissue hemangiomas or lymphangiomas. Skeletal deformity, limb shortening, pathological fractures, and increased risk of cartilage or other malignancy may occur.

\medterm{Menkes Kinky Hair Syndrome}-- An X-linked disorder of copper transport caused by ATP7A dysfunction. It produces progressive neurodegeneration, seizures, developmental delay, hypopigmented or brittle kinky hair, connective-tissue abnormalities, and failure to thrive.

\medterm{MERRF Syndrome}-- Myoclonic epilepsy with ragged-red fibres, a mitochondrial disorder usually caused by a pathogenic mitochondrial DNA variant. It features myoclonus, generalized epilepsy, ataxia, and skeletal-muscle mitochondrial abnormalities, with variable multisystem involvement.

\medterm{Muenke Syndrome}-- A craniosynostosis syndrome caused by a pathogenic FGFR3 variant. Premature fusion of one or more skull sutures may produce an abnormal head shape and can be accompanied by developmental, hearing, limb, or neurological findings.

\medterm{Norrie Disease}-- An X-linked retinal disorder, usually caused by pathogenic variants in NDP, characterized by congenital or early-onset blindness from abnormal retinal development. Hearing loss, developmental differences, and peripheral vascular or vitreoretinal complications may occur.

\medterm{Oculocerebrorenal Syndrome}-- An inherited peroxisomal disorder, also called Zellweger spectrum disorder, affecting the brain, eyes, kidneys, liver, and other organs. Severe forms present neonatally with hypotonia, seizures, feeding difficulty, distinctive facial features, and profound developmental impairment.

\medterm{Okihiro Syndrome}-- A congenital disorder, usually caused by pathogenic variants in SALL4, combining radial-ray limb abnormalities with Duane retraction anomaly or other eye-movement defects. Hearing, cardiac, renal, and vertebral anomalies may also occur.

\medterm{Overgrowth Syndrome}-- A group of congenital disorders characterized by excessive growth of the body, a region, or specific tissues, often with developmental or tumor-predisposition features. Causes include genomic imprinting abnormalities, mosaic variants, and alterations in growth-signaling pathways.

\medterm{Pallister-Hall Syndrome}-- A congenital disorder usually caused by pathogenic variants in GLI3. It is characterized by hypothalamic hamartoma and mesoaxial polydactyly and may include imperforate anus, renal or airway anomalies, and pituitary hormone abnormalities.

\medterm{Pierson Syndrome}-- A rare autosomal-recessive disorder caused by LAMB2 variants, classically combining congenital nephrotic syndrome with microcoria and structural abnormalities of the eyes. Kidney failure, developmental impairment, and severe visual disability may occur.

\medterm{Progeria}-- A rare genetic premature-aging syndrome, most commonly caused by a pathogenic LMNA variant. Children develop severe growth failure, characteristic skin and facial changes, loss of subcutaneous fat, joint stiffness, and early atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

\medterm{Refsum Disease}-- An autosomal-recessive peroxisomal disorder of phytanic-acid metabolism. It causes retinitis pigmentosa, anosmia, peripheral neuropathy, ataxia, hearing loss, ichthyosis, and shortened metacarpals or metatarsals with variable onset.

\medterm{Rothmund-Thomson Syndrome}-- A genetically heterogeneous disorder characterized by poikiloderma beginning in infancy, short stature, sparse hair, skeletal abnormalities, juvenile cataracts in some forms, and increased risk of osteosarcoma or other cancers.

\medterm{Russell-Silver Syndrome}-- A congenital growth disorder involving prenatal and postnatal growth restriction, relative macrocephaly, triangular face, body asymmetry, and feeding difficulty. It is often associated with abnormalities of genomic imprinting on chromosomes 7 or 11.

\medterm{Sandhoff Disease}-- An autosomal-recessive lysosomal storage disorder caused by deficiency of beta-hexosaminidase A and B, leading to accumulation of GM2 ganglioside. It causes progressive neurodegeneration, developmental regression, seizures, hypotonia, and sometimes organ enlargement.

\medterm{Sclerosteosis}-- A rare inherited sclerosing bone dysplasia characterized by progressive thickening of the skull and long bones, tall stature or syndactyly in some forms, and possible cranial-nerve compression. Hearing loss, facial palsy, headache, and raised intracranial pressure may occur.

\medterm{Seckel Syndrome}-- A rare recessive primordial dwarfism syndrome featuring severe proportionate growth restriction, microcephaly, characteristic facial features, and developmental or intellectual impairment. Skeletal, hematological, and other congenital abnormalities may occur.

\medterm{Shwachman Syndrome}-- An inherited multisystem disorder usually involving exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, bone-marrow dysfunction, and skeletal abnormalities. Children may have steatorrhea, poor growth, recurrent infections, neutropenia, anemia, or increased risk of myeloid malignancy.

\medterm{Smith-Magenis Syndrome}-- A genetic neurodevelopmental disorder, usually involving deletion of 17p11.2 or an RAI1 variant. It causes developmental delay, intellectual disability, distinctive facial features, sleep disturbance, behavioral dysregulation, and variable congenital abnormalities.

\medterm{Tangier Disease}-- An autosomal-recessive disorder of HDL metabolism caused by ABCA1 dysfunction. It produces very low HDL cholesterol, enlarged yellow-orange tonsils, hepatosplenomegaly, peripheral neuropathy, thrombocytopenia, and premature atherosclerosis in some patients.

\medterm{Temple Syndrome}-- A genomic-imprinting disorder involving chromosome 14q32 that causes prenatal and postnatal growth restriction, hypotonia, developmental delay, feeding difficulty, short stature, and abnormal pubertal development.

\medterm{Thanatophoric Dysplasia}-- A severe skeletal dysplasia, usually caused by an FGFR3 variant, with markedly shortened limbs, narrow thorax, macrocephaly, and characteristic skull and vertebral abnormalities. The severe thoracic restriction commonly causes neonatal respiratory failure.

\medterm{Timothy Syndrome}-- A rare multisystem disorder caused by pathogenic variants in CACNA1C, characterized by marked QT prolongation and congenital heart disease with syndactyly. Developmental, neurological, immune, and facial features may also occur.

\medterm{Townes Syndrome}-- A congenital disorder caused by pathogenic variants in SALL1, classically involving anal atresia and characteristic thumb abnormalities. Renal, cardiac, hearing, foot, and genital anomalies are common and variable.

\medterm{Trichothiodystrophy Syndrome}-- A group of inherited disorders characterized by brittle sulfur-deficient hair with a distinctive banded appearance, often accompanied by photosensitivity, ichthyosis, developmental delay, short stature, or recurrent infection.
