\chapter{Public Health}

\medterm{Administration for Community Living (ACL)}-- US agency for aging and disability. Examples: aging network, disability programs.

\medterm{Advocacy}-- Support for policy or program change. Examples: smoking cessation advocacy, vaccination advocacy, health equity advocacy.

\medterm{Air Quality}-- Measurement of air pollutants. Examples: particulate matter, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide.

\medterm{Airborne Precautions}-- For diseases spread by small particles. Examples: tuberculosis, measles, varicella. N95 respirator required, negative pressure room.

\medterm{AMR (Antimicrobial Resistance)}-- Bacteria resisting antibiotic effects. See \hyperlink{Antibiotic Resistance}{antibiotic resistance}.

\medterm{Antibiotic Resistance}-- Bacteria resisting antibiotic effects. Mechanisms: enzymatic degradation, target modification, efflux pumps, membrane permeability. Driven by antibiotic overuse.

\medterm{Antibiotic Stewardship}-- Program to optimize antibiotic use. Goals: improve outcomes, reduce resistance, decrease costs.

\medterm{Attributable Risk}-- Difference in risk between exposed and unexposed. AR = incidence exposed - incidence unexposed.

\medterm{Bias}-- Systematic error in study. Types: selection bias, information bias, confounding. Threatens validity.

\medterm{Bioethics}-- Ethical issues in medicine and biology. Examples: research ethics, end-of-life care, resource allocation.

\medterm{Blinding}-- Concealing group assignment. Single-blind: participants. Double-blind: participants and investigators. Triple-blind: participants, investigators, analysts.

\medterm{Carrier}-- Person harboring pathogen without symptoms. Can transmit disease. Examples: Typhoid Mary, pneumococcal carriers.

\medterm{Case-Control Study}-- Compares cases with disease to controls without. Retrospective. Measures odds ratio.

\medterm{Case-Fatality Rate}-- Proportion of cases dying from disease. Formula: deaths / cases. Measures severity.

\medterm{Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)}-- US public health agency. Examples: surveillance, outbreak response, guidelines, research.

\medterm{Climate Change and Health}-- Health effects of climate change. Examples: heat-related illness, vector-borne diseases, malnutrition, mental health.

\medterm{Clinical Guideline}-- Systematically developed statements to assist practice. Examples: CDC guidelines, WHO guidelines, specialty society guidelines.

\medterm{Clinical Practice Guideline}-- Systematically developed statements to assist practice. See \hyperlink{Clinical Guideline}{clinical guideline}.

\medterm{Cohort Study}-- Follows exposed and unexposed groups over time. Prospective or retrospective. Measures incidence, relative risk.

\medterm{Community Engagement}-- Involving community in health initiatives. Examples: participatory research, community advisory boards, coalitions.

\medterm{Community Health Worker}-- Lay health worker from community. Bridges community and healthcare. Examples: promotoras, CHWs.

\medterm{Confounding}-- Distortion of association by third variable. Examples: age confounding relationship between exposure and disease. Controlled by matching, stratification, regression.

\medterm{Contact Precautions}-- For highly contagious or multidrug-resistant organisms. Examples: C. difficile, MRSA, VRE. Gloves and gown required.

\medterm{Contact Tracing}-- Identifying and monitoring persons exposed to infectious disease. Critical for outbreak control. Examples: COVID-19, Ebola, TB.


\medterm{Cross-Sectional Study}-- Measures exposure and outcome at same time. Prevalence study. Cannot establish temporality.

\medterm{Cultural Competence}-- Understanding and respecting cultural differences in healthcare. Examples: language services, cultural humility, bias awareness.

\medterm{Disaster Preparedness}-- Planning for disasters. Examples: hurricane, earthquake, pandemic, terrorism. Components: response, recovery, mitigation.

\medterm{Droplet Precautions}-- For diseases spread by large droplets. Examples: influenza, pertussis, bacterial meningitis. Surgical mask required.

\medterm{Emerging Infectious Disease}-- New or reemerging infectious diseases. Examples: COVID-19, Zika, Ebola, MERS.

\medterm{Endemic}-- Disease constantly present in population. Examples: malaria in tropics, influenza in winter.

\medterm{Environmental Health}-- Study of environmental factors affecting health. Examples: air pollution, water quality, occupational hazards, climate change.

\medterm{Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)}-- US agency for environmental protection. Examples: air and water quality standards, pesticide regulation.

\medterm{Epidemic}-- Sudden increase in disease cases above expected level. Examples: cholera outbreak, measles outbreak.

\medterm{Epidemiology}-- Study of distribution and determinants of health-related states in populations. Branches: descriptive (who, when, where), analytic (why, how), experimental (intervention trials). Measures: incidence, prevalence, mortality rates, case-fatality rate.

\medterm{Ethics in Public Health}-- Ethical considerations in public health. Examples: autonomy vs. community, equity, transparency, accountability.

\medterm{Evidence-Based Medicine}-- Integration of best research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values. Foundation for clinical guidelines.

\medterm{Food and Drug Administration (FDA)}-- US agency for food and drug safety. Examples: vaccine approval, drug approval, food safety.

\medterm{Food Safety}-- Prevention of foodborne illness. Examples: HACCP, proper handling, temperature control, inspection.

\medterm{Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA)}-- US law prohibiting genetic discrimination. Examples: employment, health insurance.

\medterm{Global Health}-- Health issues transcending national boundaries. Examples: pandemics, health disparities, climate change.

\medterm{Hand Hygiene}-- Most important infection control measure. Methods: handwashing with soap and water, alcohol-based hand rub.

\medterm{Health Behavior}-- Actions affecting health. Examples: diet, exercise, smoking, alcohol, sexual behavior, medication adherence.

\medterm{Health Belief Model}-- Model predicting health behavior. Constructs: perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, cues to action, self-efficacy.

\medterm{Health Communication}-- Exchange of health information. Examples: public education, risk communication, provider-patient communication.

\medterm{Health Disparity}-- Difference in health outcomes between populations. Examples: racial disparities, socioeconomic disparities, geographic disparities.

\medterm{Health Economics}-- Study of resource allocation in health. Examples: cost-effectiveness analysis, budget impact, funding models.


\medterm{Health Education}-- Process of teaching health concepts. Examples: smoking cessation classes, diabetes education, prenatal classes.

\medterm{Health Equity}-- Attainment of highest possible health for all people. Requires addressing disparities.

\medterm{Health Impact Assessment}-- Systematic evaluation of health effects of policies, programs, projects. Examples: transportation, housing, education.

\medterm{Health Information Technology}-- Use of technology for health information. Examples: electronic health records, telemedicine, mobile health, decision support.

\medterm{Health Insurance}-- Mechanism for financing healthcare. Types: public (Medicare, Medicaid), private, mandatory, voluntary.

\medterm{Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)}-- US law protecting health information privacy. Examples: privacy rule, security rule, breach notification.

\medterm{Health Literacy}-- Ability to obtain, process, understand health information. Examples: reading labels, following instructions, communicating with providers.

\medterm{Health Policy}-- Decisions, plans, actions to achieve health goals. Examples: vaccination policy, smoking policy, tobacco control.

\medterm{Health Promotion}-- Process enabling people to control and improve health. Components: healthy public policy, supportive environments, community action, personal skills, reorient health services.

\medterm{Health Promotion Model}-- Framework for understanding health behavior. Factors: individual characteristics, interpersonal influences, situational influences.

\medterm{Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA)}-- US agency for health access. Examples: community health centers, Ryan White program, organ procurement.

\medterm{Health Surveillance}-- Ongoing collection, analysis, interpretation of health data. See \hyperlink{Surveillance}{surveillance}.

\medterm{Healthcare Financing}-- Payment for healthcare. Examples: insurance, out-of-pocket, government programs.

\medterm{Herd Immunity}-- Resistance of population to invasion by infectious agent when sufficient proportion immune. Threshold varies by disease (measles ~95%, polio ~80%).


\medterm{Incidence}-- Number of new cases of disease in a population during specified time period. Measures risk. Formula: new cases / population at risk × time unit.

\medterm{Infection Control}-- Prevention of healthcare-associated infections. Examples: hand hygiene, isolation precautions, environmental cleaning, antibiotic stewardship.

\medterm{International Health Regulations}-- WHO framework for public health emergencies. Countries report events, strengthen capacity.

\medterm{Isolation}-- Separation of sick persons with contagious disease. Examples: tuberculosis isolation, airborne infection isolation rooms.


\medterm{Isolation Precautions}-- Measures for disease transmission. Types: contact, droplet, airborne. PLUS standard precautions.

\medterm{Law and Public Health}-- Legal aspects of public health. Examples: quarantine authority, vaccination mandates, reporting requirements.

\medterm{Meta-Analysis}-- Statistical combination of results from multiple studies. Increases power, precision. Forest plot visualization.

\medterm{Mobile Health (mHealth)}-- Use of mobile devices for health. Examples: health apps, text message reminders, wearable devices.

\medterm{Mortality Rate}-- Deaths from disease in population. Crude: total deaths / total population. Specific: disease-specific deaths / population at risk.

\medterm{National Institutes of Health (NIH)}-- US medical research agency. Examples: clinical trials, research funding, institutes.

\medterm{Negative Predictive Value}-- Probability that negative test indicates no disease. NPV = true negatives / all negatives.

\medterm{Notifiable Disease}-- Disease required by law to be reported to health authorities. Examples: tuberculosis, measles, HIV, COVID-19.


\medterm{Occupational Health}-- Health of workers. Examples: workplace safety, occupational diseases, ergonomics.

\medterm{Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA)}-- US agency for workplace safety. Examples: standards, inspections, enforcement.

\medterm{Odds Ratio}-- Ratio of odds of exposure in cases vs. controls. Used in case-control studies. OR approximates RR for rare diseases.

\medterm{One Health}-- Approach recognizing health connections between humans, animals, environment. Examples: zoonotic diseases, antibiotic resistance, food safety.

\medterm{Outbreak}-- Similar to epidemic but more localized. Examples: foodborne outbreak, school outbreak.

\medterm{Pandemic}-- Epidemic spread across multiple countries/continents. Examples: COVID-19, Spanish flu, HIV/AIDS.

\medterm{Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (PPACA)}-- US healthcare reform law. Examples: insurance marketplace, Medicaid expansion, preventive services without cost sharing.

\medterm{Patient Safety}-- Prevention of harm from healthcare. Examples: medication errors, healthcare-associated infections, surgical errors. Strategies: checklists, hand hygiene, barcoding.

\medterm{Patient-Centered Care}-- Care respecting patient preferences, needs, values. Examples: shared decision-making, communication, coordination.

\medterm{Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)}-- Equipment for protection. Examples: gloves, gown, mask, goggles, respirator.

\medterm{Policy Change}-- Modification of policy. Examples: tobacco policy, sugar tax, clean indoor air.

\medterm{Population Attributable Risk}-- Proportion of disease in population attributable to exposure. PAR = (incidence total - incidence unexposed) / incidence total.

\medterm{Positive Predictive Value}-- Probability that positive test indicates disease. Depends on prevalence. PPV = true positives / all positives.

\medterm{Prevalence}-- Total number of cases (new and existing) in population at given time. Formula: all cases / total population. Prevalence = incidence × duration.

\medterm{Primary Prevention}-- Preventing disease before onset. Examples: vaccination, smoking cessation, safe sex, seat belts.

\medterm{Primordial Prevention}-- Preventing the emergence of disease risk factors by addressing social, economic, behavioural, and environmental conditions early. Examples: promoting healthy diets and physical activity in children, reducing tobacco exposure, and creating safe environments for exercise.

\medterm{Protective Factor}-- Attribute decreasing probability of disease. Examples: exercise (cardiovascular disease), vaccination (infectious disease).

\medterm{Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC)}-- Canadian public health agency. Examples: infectious disease control, health promotion, surveillance.

\medterm{Public Health Emergency}-- Event threatening public health. Examples: pandemic, bioterrorism, natural disaster. Declaration enables special powers.

\medterm{Public Health Law}-- Legal authority for public health actions. Examples: disease control, health promotion, emergency powers.
\medterm{Quality Improvement}-- Systematic efforts to improve healthcare. Methods: PDSA cycles, Lean, Six Sigma. Outcomes: safety, effectiveness, patient-centeredness.

\medterm{quarantine}-- Separation of exposed persons from public to prevent disease spread. Duration: incubation period. Examples: COVID-19, Ebola, cholera.

\medterm{Quarantine}-- Separation of exposed individuals to prevent disease spread.

\medterm{Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT)}-- Gold standard for intervention studies. Participants randomly assigned to intervention or control. Minimizes bias.

\medterm{Reemerging Disease}-- Previously controlled disease increasing. Examples: tuberculosis, measles, drug-resistant infections.

\medterm{Relative Risk}-- Ratio of disease risk in exposed vs. unexposed. RR = (a/(a+b)) / (c/(c+d)). RR > 1: increased risk. RR < 1: protective.

\medterm{Reservoir}-- Host where pathogen lives and multiplies. Examples: animals (zoonoses), humans (human reservoirs), environment (soil, water).

\medterm{Risk Communication}-- Communication about risk. Examples: outbreak communication, vaccine safety, environmental risk.

\medterm{Risk Factor}-- Attribute increasing probability of disease. Examples: smoking (lung cancer), hypertension (stroke), obesity (diabetes).

\medterm{Secondary Prevention}-- Early detection and treatment. Examples: screening (mammography, colonoscopy, blood pressure), self-examination.

\medterm{Sensitivity}-- Proportion of true positives correctly identified. High sensitivity: few false negatives. Screening tests should be sensitive.

\medterm{Social Cognitive Theory}-- Theory of learning in social context. Constructs: reciprocal determinism, observational learning, self-efficacy.

\medterm{Social Determinants of Health}-- Conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work. Include: income, education, employment, housing, social support, physical environment. Major influence on health equity.

\medterm{Specificity}-- Proportion of true negatives correctly identified. High specificity: few false positives. Confirmatory tests should be specific.

\medterm{Standard Precautions}-- Precautions for all patient care. Examples: hand hygiene, PPE, safe injection, respiratory hygiene.

\medterm{Study Design}-- Plan for research study. Types: observational (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional), experimental (RCT), ecological.

\medterm{Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA)}-- US agency for substance abuse and mental health. Examples: treatment, prevention, recovery.

\medterm{Surveillance}-- Systematic collection, analysis, interpretation of health data. Types: passive (reporting), active (proactive case finding), sentinel (selected sites).

\medterm{Systematic Review}-- Comprehensive summary of research on specific question. Uses explicit methods to minimize bias. Cochrane reviews example.

\medterm{Telemedicine}-- Remote delivery of healthcare using technology. Examples: video visits, remote monitoring, store-and-forward. Increases access.

\medterm{Tertiary Prevention}-- Reducing complications from established disease. Examples: rehabilitation, chronic disease management, palliative care.

\medterm{Transtheoretical Model}-- Model of behavior change stages. Stages: precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance.

\medterm{Universal Health Coverage}-- All people having access to needed health services without financial hardship. WHO goal.


\medterm{Vaccination}-- Administration of vaccine to stimulate immunity. Types: live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, mRNA, viral vector. Schedule varies by country.

\medterm{Vector}-- Organism transmitting pathogen. Examples: mosquitoes (malaria, dengue), ticks (Lyme disease), fleas (plague).

\medterm{Vector Control}-- Reduction of vector populations. Examples: insecticides, larvicides, environmental modification, personal protection.

\medterm{Vector-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted by vectors. Examples: malaria, dengue, Lyme disease, Zika virus.

\medterm{Water Safety}-- Prevention of waterborne disease. Examples: chlorination, filtration, monitoring, sanitation.

\medterm{World Health Organization (WHO)}-- UN agency for global health. Examples: International Health Regulations, health metrics, technical guidance.

\medterm{Zoonosis}-- Disease transmitted from animals to humans. Examples: rabies, Lyme disease, avian influenza, COVID-19.

\medterm{Attack Rate}-- The proportion of an at-risk population that develops a specified illness during a defined outbreak or exposure period. It is a cumulative incidence measure commonly used for foodborne and other localized outbreaks.

\medterm{Secondary Attack Rate}-- The proportion of susceptible contacts who develop infection after exposure to a primary case within a defined period. It helps estimate transmissibility in households, schools, healthcare settings, and other close-contact groups.

\medterm{Basic Reproduction Number (R0)}-- The expected number of secondary infections generated by one typical infectious person in a completely susceptible population, under specified conditions. It is a population and context parameter, not a fixed biological constant.

\medterm{Effective Reproduction Number (Rt)}-- The average number of secondary infections caused by an infectious person at a particular time, accounting for existing immunity, behavior, interventions, and population structure. Sustained transmission generally requires Rt above 1.

\medterm{Case Definition}-- Standardized clinical, laboratory, and epidemiologic criteria used to classify a person as a suspected, probable, or confirmed case for surveillance or outbreak investigation. It may differ from the criteria used for individual clinical diagnosis.

\medterm{Sentinel Surveillance}-- Ongoing collection of health data from selected reporting sites, providers, or populations chosen to represent trends rather than enumerate every event. It can provide early warning and monitor changes in disease activity.

\medterm{Syndromic Surveillance}-- Monitoring of symptoms, clinical encounters, laboratory requests, or other near-real-time signals before definitive diagnoses are available. It supports early detection of outbreaks and public-health threats but may have lower specificity.

\medterm{Screening Program}-- An organized public-health service that systematically invites a defined population to undergo testing for an asymptomatic condition, with quality-assured follow-up, diagnosis, treatment, and evaluation of benefits and harms.

\medterm{Lead-Time Bias}-- The appearance of improved survival after screening because diagnosis occurs earlier, even when the time of death is unchanged. It can make screening appear beneficial without reducing mortality.

\medterm{Length-Time Bias}-- The tendency of screening to preferentially detect slower-growing or less aggressive disease because it remains detectable for a longer preclinical period. It can exaggerate the apparent benefit of screening.

\medterm{Number Needed to Treat (NNT)}-- The number of people who must receive an intervention for one additional person to experience the specified beneficial outcome compared with the control over a defined time. It is the reciprocal of absolute risk reduction.

\medterm{Number Needed to Harm (NNH)}-- The number of people exposed to an intervention for one additional person to experience a specified adverse outcome compared with the control over a defined time. It is the reciprocal of absolute risk increase.

\medterm{Disability-Adjusted Life Year (DALY)}-- A population-health measure combining years of life lost because of premature mortality with years lived with disability. One DALY represents one lost year of healthy life under the chosen methods and disability weights.

\medterm{Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY)}-- A health-economic measure combining length of life with health-related quality of life on a scale where 1 represents full health and 0 represents death. It is used to compare cost-effectiveness across interventions.

\medterm{Age-Standardized Rate}-- A rate adjusted to a specified standard population age distribution so that populations with different age structures can be compared more fairly. It is commonly used for mortality, cancer, and disease-incidence comparisons.

\medterm{Health Inequity}-- A systematic, avoidable, and unjust difference in health or access to health resources between social groups. It differs from health variation that is not preventable or not linked to unfair social conditions.

\medterm{Population Attributable Fraction}-- The proportion of cases or disease burden in the total population that would theoretically be prevented if the exposure were eliminated, assuming the exposure is causal and other conditions remain unchanged.

\medterm{Positive Predictive Value in Surveillance}-- The proportion of reported signals or alerts that represent true events meeting the surveillance case definition. It is influenced by the specificity of the signal and the underlying prevalence of the condition.
