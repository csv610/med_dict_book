\chapter{General Medical Terms}

\medterm{Alternative Medicine}-- Any of various systems of healing or treating disease (such as homeopathy, acupuncture, or chiropractic) that are not included in the traditional curricula taught in Western medical schools. When used alongside conventional medicine, it is referred to as complementary medicine.

\medterm{Eponym}-- A medical term, disease, sign, symptom, or anatomical structure named after a person (usually the physician, surgeon, or scientist who first described it, or occasionally a patient). Examples include Parkinson's disease, Fallopian tube, and Babinski sign. Modern medical terminology increasingly favors descriptive clinical terms over eponyms to improve clarity and international standardization.

\medterm{Placebo Effect}-- A beneficial physical or psychological health outcome resulting from an inert treatment (such as a sugar pill, saline injection, or sham procedure) that cannot be attributed to the active properties of the treatment itself. Driven by patient expectations, classical conditioning, and the therapeutic doctor-patient relationship. In clinical trials, new therapies must demonstrate efficacy superior to placebo controls.

\medterm{Quarantine}-- The restriction of movement or isolation of individuals, animals, or goods who have been exposed to a contagious disease (or suspected of exposure) to prevent the potential spread of infection to others. Differentiated from isolation, which separates individuals who are already known to be ill and infectious.
