\chapter{General Medical Terms}

\medterm{Abdomen}-- The body region between the thorax and pelvis, containing the digestive organs, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, spleen, kidneys, and major blood vessels. Divided into nine regions (right/left hypochondriac, epigastric, umbilical, right/left lumbar, right/left inguinal) or four quadrants (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) for clinical description.

\medterm{Abscess}-- See emergency\_medicine.

\medterm{Acute}-- Sudden onset, short duration. Examples: appendicitis, pneumonia.

\medterm{Adherence}-- Extent to which patient follows treatment recommendations. Affected by factors: regimen complexity, side effects, beliefs, socioeconomic.

\medterm{Adverse Event}-- Unintended harm from medical care. Includes medical errors and complications.

\medterm{Advocacy}-- Acting on behalf of patient. Ensuring patient needs and preferences are addressed.

\medterm{Airborne Disease}-- Disease transmitted by airborne particles. Examples: tuberculosis, measles, COVID-19.

\medterm{Anatomy}-- Study of body structure. Regions: head, neck, thorax, abdomen, pelvis, extremities. Systems: skeletal, muscular, nervous, circulatory, respiratory, digestive, urinary, reproductive, endocrine, immune.

\medterm{Antibiotic Stewardship}-- Program to optimize antibiotic use. Improves outcomes, reduces resistance.

\medterm{Antimicrobial Resistance}-- Microorganisms becoming resistant to antibiotics. Major global health threat. Prevention: appropriate antibiotic use, infection control.

\medterm{Auscultation}-- The act of listening to sounds produced within the body, typically using a stethoscope. Essential for assessing heart sounds, lung sounds, bowel sounds, and vascular bruits. Normal findings: S1 and S2 heart sounds, vesicular breath sounds, normal bowel sounds. Abnormal: murmurs, crackles, wheezes, rubs, bruits.

\medterm{Auscultatory Gap}-- A transient silence between the first and second sounds of blood pressure measurement, sometimes heard in hypertensive patients. Can lead to underestimation of systolic pressure if not recognized.

\medterm{Barium Swallow}-- Radiographic examination of the esophagus using barium sulfate contrast. Evaluates swallowing function, motility, strictures, masses. Modified barium swallow assesses aspiration risk.

\medterm{Behavior Change}-- Modification of health-related behaviors. Theories: health belief model, transtheoretical model.

\medterm{Bias}-- Systematic error in study design or analysis. Types: selection, information, confounding.

\medterm{Biomarker}-- Measurable indicator of disease.

\medterm{Biomonitoring}-- Measurement of substances in body. Examples: blood glucose, drug levels, toxicants.

\medterm{Biopsy}-- Removal of tissue for histological examination to diagnose disease. Types: needle biopsy (fine needle aspiration, core needle), excisional, incisional, punch, endoscopic. Sites: breast, lung, liver, lymph node, bone marrow. Complications: bleeding, infection, seeding (rare).


\medterm{Blinding}-- Concealing treatment assignment in trials. Single-blind (patient), double-blind (patient and investigator).

\medterm{Blood Pressure}-- Force of blood against vessel wall. Measured in mmHg. Systolic/diastolic. Normal: <120/80.

\medterm{Blood-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted via blood. Examples: HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C.

\medterm{Capillary Refill Time}-- Time for color to return to blanched nail bed or skin after pressure. Normal: <2 seconds. Prolonged in shock, dehydration, peripheral vasoconstriction.

\medterm{Case Fatality Rate}-- Proportion of cases dying from disease. Measures severity.

\medterm{Case-Control Study}-- Observational study comparing cases with disease to controls without. Looks back at exposures.

\medterm{Chronic}-- Long-lasting, persistent. Examples: diabetes, hypertension.

\medterm{Clinical Presentation}-- Signs and symptoms of disease. Varies by disease, severity, patient factors.

\medterm{Clinical Trial}-- Research study testing new cancer treatments. See oncology.

\medterm{Cohort Study}-- Observational study following group over time. Can be prospective or retrospective.

\medterm{Communicable Disease}-- Disease transmissible between persons. Examples: influenza, TB, HIV.

\medterm{Communication}-- Exchange of information between provider and patient. Essential for diagnosis, treatment, relationship.

\medterm{Community-Acquired}-- Obtained outside hospital. Examples: community-acquired pneumonia.

\medterm{Comorbidity}-- Presence of one or more additional conditions co-occurring with a primary disease. Common in chronic illnesses (diabetes with hypertension, COPD with heart failure). Affects treatment decisions, prognosis, and healthcare utilization.


\medterm{Compliance}-- Following medical advice. Preferred term: adherence.

\medterm{Complication}-- Unexpected problem during disease or treatment. Examples: surgical complication, drug complication.

\medterm{Concomitant}-- Occurring simultaneously. Concomitant medication: additional drug taken with primary treatment.

\medterm{Condition}-- General term for health status. Can be acute or chronic.

\medterm{Confirmatory Test}-- Test following positive screening to confirm diagnosis.

\medterm{Confounding}-- Distortion of association by third variable. Controlled by randomization, matching, statistical adjustment.

\medterm{Contact Tracing}-- Identifying and monitoring contacts of infected person. Important for outbreak control.

\medterm{Contraindication}-- A condition or factor that renders a particular procedure, treatment, or medication inadvisable. Absolute contraindication: condition where intervention should never be performed. Relative contraindication: risks outweigh benefits but may be acceptable in specific circumstances. Examples: pregnancy (absolute for isotretinoin), severe renal impairment (relative for contrast CT).

\medterm{Control Group}-- Group not receiving experimental intervention. Used for comparison.

\medterm{Cost-Effectiveness}-- Comparison of costs and outcomes of interventions. Used for resource allocation.

\medterm{Costochondritis}-- Inflammation of the costochondral or costosternal junctions, the most common cause of chest wall pain. Presents with sharp, reproducible tenderness on palpation of the anterior chest, usually unilateral involving the 2nd to 5th ribs. May follow respiratory infection, trauma, or vigorous coughing, but often idiopathic. Affects 13-16 percent of chest pain presentations to emergency departments. Important differential diagnosis for cardiac chest pain. Diagnosis: clinical (reproducible tenderness, normal cardiac workup). Self-limited (weeks to months). Treatment: NSAIDs, local heat, reassurance, and activity modification. Recurrent cases may require intercostal nerve block or topical lidocaine.

\medterm{Counseling}-- Guidance to help patients make health decisions. Examples: smoking cessation, weight loss, medication adherence.

\medterm{Cross-Sectional Study}-- Observational study measuring prevalence at single time point.

\medterm{DALY}-- Disability-adjusted life year. Measure of disease burden.

\medterm{Diagnosis}-- Identification of disease. Based on history, examination, tests. Differential diagnosis considers multiple possibilities.

\medterm{Diagnostic Test}-- Procedure to identify disease. Examples: laboratory tests, imaging, biopsies.

\medterm{Differential Diagnosis}-- The process of distinguishing a particular disease or condition from others that present with similar symptoms. Systematic approach: consider life-threatening conditions first, use pretest probability, apply diagnostic criteria, and order targeted tests.

\medterm{Disease}-- Abnormal condition affecting body. Can be infectious, degenerative, neoplastic, etc.

\medterm{Disorder}-- Functional abnormality. Less severe than disease. Examples: sleep disorder, eating disorder.

\medterm{Diuresis}-- Increased urine production. Can be physiological (fluid overload) or pharmacological (diuretics). Osmotic diuresis (hyperglycemia), water diuresis (DI), saline diuresis (hypertonic saline).


\medterm{Documentation}-- Recording of patient encounters. Medical records, progress notes, discharge summaries. Legal and clinical importance.

\medterm{Echo}-- Ultrasound imaging of the heart, the primary modality for assessing cardiac structure and function. See \hyperlink{Echocardiography}{echocardiography}.

\medterm{Edema}-- Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues. Types: pitting (press leaves indentation), non-pitting (myxedema, lymphedema). Causes: heart failure, kidney disease, liver cirrhosis, venous insufficiency, lymphatic obstruction, medications (calcium channel blockers, NSAIDs). Assessment: degree, distribution, symmetry, associated symptoms.


\medterm{Endoscopy}-- Visual examination of internal organs using scope. Examples: colonoscopy, gastroscopy, bronchoscopy.

\medterm{Epidemiology}-- Study of disease distribution and determinants in populations. Measures: incidence, prevalence, mortality, morbidity.

\medterm{Ethics}-- Moral principles guiding practice. Principles: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice.

\medterm{Etiology}-- Cause or origin of disease. Examples: genetic, infectious, environmental, idiopathic.

\medterm{Evidence-Based Medicine}-- Practice informed by best available evidence. Combines clinical expertise, patient values, research evidence.

\medterm{Examination}-- Systematic assessment of a patient's health status. Components: inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation (IPPA). General survey: appearance, posture, gait, vital signs. Focused examination based on chief complaint.

\medterm{Fever}-- Elevated body temperature (>38°C or 100.4°F). Workup depends on duration, height, and patient factors. Common causes: infections (bacterial, viral), autoimmune disorders, malignancies, drug reactions. Management: antipyretics (acetaminophen, ibuprofen), treat underlying cause. Febrile seizures: generally benign, prevent recurrence with fever control.

\medterm{Fistula}-- See \hyperlink{Fistula (Pathology)}{Fistula (Pathology)}.

\medterm{Food-Borne Disease}-- Disease from contaminated food. Examples: salmonella, E. coli, listeria.
\medterm{Gait}-- Pattern of walking. Normal gait involves coordinated movement of legs, arms, trunk. Abnormal gait patterns: antalgic (pain), hemiplegic (stroke), parkinsonian (shuffling), ataxic (cerebellar), steppage (foot drop).


\medterm{Genetic Testing}-- Analysis of DNA for disease risk or diagnosis. Examples: BRCA testing, carrier screening.

\medterm{Guideline}-- Recommended practice based on evidence. Examples: NICE, ACC/AHA, WHO guidelines.

\medterm{Hand Hygiene}-- Cleaning hands to prevent infection transmission. Most important precaution.

\medterm{Health Disparity}-- Difference in health outcomes between populations. Due to social, economic, environmental factors.

\medterm{Health Economics}-- Study of resource allocation in healthcare. Scarcity requires prioritization.

\medterm{Health Literacy}-- Ability to obtain, process, understand health information. Affects health outcomes.

\medterm{Health Policy}-- Decisions, plans, and actions to achieve healthcare goals. Includes legislation, regulation, funding.

\medterm{Healthcare System}-- Organization providing healthcare services. Structures vary by country.

\medterm{Healthy Years Lost}-- Years of life lost due to premature death or disability. DALYs combine YLL and YLD.

\medterm{Hemostasis}-- The process of stopping bleeding. Primary: platelet plug formation. Secondary: coagulation cascade. Tertiary: vasoconstriction and fibrinolysis regulation.


\medterm{Herd Immunity}-- Population-level protection when sufficient individuals immune. Reduces disease spread.

\medterm{Homeostasis}-- The maintenance of stable internal conditions despite external changes. Key regulated variables: temperature, pH, glucose, electrolytes, blood pressure, fluid balance. Mechanisms: negative feedback loops (most common), positive feedback (rare, e.g., labor, blood clotting). Organs involved: hypothalamus, kidneys, lungs, liver, endocrine glands.

\medterm{Hospital-Acquired Infection}-- Infection acquired during hospitalization. Also: healthcare-associated infection (HAI). Examples: CLABSI, CAUTI, surgical site infection.

\medterm{Iatrogenic}-- An illness or condition caused by medical examination or treatment. Examples: hospital-acquired infections, drug side effects, surgical complications, radiation-induced damage. Prevention: proper technique, informed consent, monitoring, reporting systems.


\medterm{Idiopathic}-- Of unknown cause. Examples: idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, idiopathic thrombocytopenia.

\medterm{Illness}-- Subjective experience of being unwell. May not correspond to disease.

\medterm{Imaging}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Immunization}-- Administration of a vaccine to stimulate immune response and provide protection against infectious disease. See \hyperlink{Vaccination}{vaccination}.

\medterm{Incidence}-- Number of new cases of a disease in a population during a specified time period. Measures risk of developing disease. Incidence rate = new cases / population at risk x time unit.


\medterm{Infection}-- See infectious\_disease.

\medterm{Infection Control}-- Measures to prevent healthcare-associated infections: hand hygiene, personal protective equipment (PPE), isolation precautions (contact, droplet, airborne), sterilization, disinfection.

\medterm{Infectious Disease}-- Disease caused by pathogen. Examples: bacterial, viral, fungal, parasitic.

\medterm{Inflammation}-- The body's protective response to injury or infection. Cardinal signs: redness (rubor), heat (calor), swelling (tumor), pain (dolor), loss of function (functio laesa). Mediators: histamine, prostaglandins, cytokines, complement. Acute: neutrophil-predominant. Chronic: lymphocyte/macrophage-predominant.

\medterm{Informed Consent}-- The process by which a patient voluntarily confirms understanding of a proposed treatment or procedure, including risks, benefits, alternatives, and consequences of non-treatment. Elements: disclosure, comprehension, voluntariness, competence, consent. Documented on consent form. Exceptions: emergencies, waived consent (minimal risk research), therapeutic privilege.


\medterm{Inspection}-- Visual examination of the patient. Part of physical assessment. Observes symmetry, color, lesions, movement, posture.

\medterm{Isolation}-- Separation of sick individuals with contagious disease.

\medterm{Isolation Precautions}-- Measures to prevent spread of infection. Contact, droplet, airborne precautions.

\medterm{Jargon}-- Specialized language used by a particular group or profession. Medical jargon can hinder communication with patients. Examples: "stat" (immediately), "NPO" (nothing by mouth), "DX" (diagnosis). Modern trend toward plain language in patient communication.

\medterm{Joint Effusion}-- Excess fluid within a joint space. Common sites: knee (most accessible), ankle, wrist, shoulder. Causes: trauma, infection, inflammatory arthritis, crystal arthropathy. Detected by ballottement or bulge sign.


\medterm{Kernig Sign}-- Sign of meningeal irritation. Patient supine, hip and knee flexed 90 degrees; inability to extend knee indicates positive sign. Seen in meningitis, subarachnoid hemorrhage.

\medterm{Kussmaul Respirations}-- Deep, labored breathing pattern associated with diabetic ketoacidosis. Compensatory hyperventilation to blow off CO2 and correct metabolic acidosis. Name: Adolf Kussmaul, German physician.


\medterm{Laboratory Test}-- Analysis of body fluids or tissues. Includes hematology, chemistry, microbiology, immunology.

\medterm{Lateral}-- Positioned away from the midline of the body. Opposite of medial. Example: the thumb is lateral to the little finger in anatomical position.

\medterm{Leukocytosis}-- Elevated white blood cell count (>11,000/$\mu$L). Causes: infection, inflammation, stress, medications (corticosteroids), malignancy (leukemia). Neutrophilia, lymphocytosis, eosinophilia, monocytosis have different implications.


\medterm{Life Expectancy}-- Average years remaining for person at given age. Varies by country, sex, socioeconomic status.

\medterm{Likelihood Ratio}-- Probability of test result in disease vs. non-disease. LR+ >1 increases probability; LR- <1 decreases.

\medterm{Malpractice}-- Professional negligence by healthcare provider. Requires duty, breach, causation, damages.

\medterm{Mediate}-- To transmit or convey between two points. In medicine, mediation often refers to the mechanism by which a cause produces an effect. Mediated immunity involves immune cells; direct contact does not.

\medterm{Medication Error}-- See \hyperlink{Toxicology}{toxicology}.

\medterm{Meta-Analysis}-- Statistical combination of multiple studies. Increases power.

\medterm{Monitoring Test}-- Test to track disease progression or treatment response.

\medterm{Morbidity}-- Disease or illness in population. Morbidity rate: new cases per population per time.

\medterm{Mortality}-- Death from disease. Mortality rate: deaths per population per time.

\medterm{Murmur}-- Heart sound caused by turbulent blood flow. Grades I-VI. Systolic murmurs more common; diastolic murmurs always pathological. Evaluation: timing, location, radiation, intensity, quality, position changes.


\medterm{Nadir}-- The lowest point, particularly referring to the lowest white blood cell count after chemotherapy. Bone marrow suppression typically reaches nadir 7-14 days post-treatment. Important for infection risk assessment.

\medterm{Negative Predictive Value}-- Probability disease absent given negative test. Affected by prevalence.

\medterm{Nocebo}-- Negative placebo effect. Adverse events from negative expectations.

\medterm{Nodule}-- Small, palpable mass <2 cm. Can be solid or cystic. Examples: thyroid nodule, rheumatoid nodule, pulmonary nodule. Evaluation depends on location.


\medterm{Non-Adherence}-- Failure to follow treatment. Major cause of treatment failure.

\medterm{Non-Communicable Disease}-- Disease not transmitted between persons. Examples: cancer, heart disease, diabetes.

\medterm{Nosocomial}-- Hospital-acquired. Nosocomial infection: infection acquired in hospital.

\medterm{Oliguria}-- Decreased urine output (<400 mL/day in adults). Causes: prerenal (dehydration, heart failure), renal (acute kidney injury), postrenal (obstruction). Medical emergency if anuric.


\medterm{Opportunistic Infection}-- Infection in immunocompromised host. Examples: PCP, toxoplasmosis, candidiasis.

\medterm{Oropharynx}-- The posterior portion of the mouth leading to the esophagus, containing the tonsils and base of tongue. Area for swallowing and speech. Examination with tongue depressor and light source. Pathology: pharyngitis, tonsillitis, malignancy.

\medterm{Outbreak}-- Sudden increase in disease cases. Requires investigation and control measures.

\medterm{Oxygen Saturation}-- Percentage of hemoglobin bound with oxygen. Normal: 95-100%. Measured by pulse oximetry.

\medterm{Pain}-- Unpleasant sensory and emotional experience. Fifth vital sign. Assessment: location, intensity, character, timing.

\medterm{Palpation}-- Examination by touch to detect abnormalities in size, shape, consistency, and tenderness of organs or tissues. Uses fingers, palm, or heel of hand. Light vs. deep palpation. Bimanual palpation uses two hands.

\medterm{Pandemic}-- Worldwide epidemic. Examples: COVID-19, influenza pandemics.

\medterm{Pathogenesis}-- Mechanism of disease development. Steps: initiation, promotion, progression.

\medterm{Pathology}-- Study of disease. Includes general pathology (cell injury, inflammation, neoplasia) and systemic pathology (organ-specific diseases).

\medterm{Patient}-- Person receiving medical care. Also: patient zero (initial case), patient safety.

\medterm{Patient Education}-- Teaching patients about health conditions and self-management. Improves adherence, outcomes.

\medterm{Patient Safety}-- Prevention of harm during care. Goals: reduce errors, improve systems, promote culture of safety.

\medterm{Percussion}-- Tapping body surface to assess underlying structures. Sounds: dull (solid organ, fluid), resonant (lung), tympanic (gas-filled). Used to assess organ size, presence of fluid, air.


\medterm{Personal Protective Equipment}-- PPE: gloves, gowns, masks, eye protection. Used based on transmission-based precautions.

\medterm{Pharmacogenomics}-- Study of genetic variation affecting drug response. Personalizes medication selection.

\medterm{Physiology}-- Study of body function. Systems physiology: cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, gastrointestinal, endocrine, nervous, muscular, reproductive.

\medterm{Placebo}-- Inactive substance used as control in trials. Placebo effect: improvement from expectation.

\medterm{Positive Predictive Value}-- Probability disease present given positive test. Affected by prevalence.

\medterm{Predictive Test}-- Test predicting response to treatment.

\medterm{Prevalence}-- Proportion of population with disease at specific time. Measures disease burden.

\medterm{Prevention}-- Measures to prevent disease. Primary (before disease), secondary (early detection), tertiary (complication prevention).

\medterm{Privacy}-- Protection of patient information. HIPAA regulations in US. Confidentiality essential.

\medterm{Prognosis}-- Predicted disease outcome. Depends on disease, treatment, patient factors.

\medterm{Prognostic Test}-- Test predicting disease outcome.

\medterm{Protective Factor}-- Attribute decreasing disease probability. Examples: vaccination, exercise, healthy diet.

\medterm{Provider}-- Healthcare professional providing care. Physicians, nurses, therapists, pharmacists.

\medterm{Public Health}-- The science and practice of protecting and improving the health of communities through education, policy-making, and research for disease and injury prevention.

\medterm{Pulse}-- Rhythmic expansion of artery with each heartbeat. Normal: 60-100 bpm. Rate, rhythm, amplitude assessed.

\medterm{QALY}-- Quality-adjusted life year. Measure of health outcome incorporating quality of life.

\medterm{Quadrant}-- One of four sections of the abdomen (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) used for clinical description. RUQ: liver, gallbladder, duodenum, right kidney. LUQ: stomach, spleen, left kidney, pancreas tail. RLQ: appendix, cecum, right ovary. LLQ: sigmoid colon, left ovary.

\medterm{Quality Improvement}-- Systematic efforts to enhance care. Methods: PDSA cycles, root cause analysis, peer review.

\medterm{Quarantine}-- Separation of exposed individuals to prevent disease spread. For symptomatic incubation period.

\medterm{Quiescent}-- Inactive or dormant state of disease. Example: quiescent ulcerative colitis (no symptoms but histologic inflammation may persist).

\medterm{Randomization}-- Random assignment to treatment groups. Reduces bias.

\medterm{Randomized Controlled Trial}-- RCT: experimental study randomly assigning intervention. Gold standard for evidence.

\medterm{Receiver Operating Characteristic}-- ROC curve: plot of sensitivity vs. 1-specificity. Area under curve measures test accuracy.

\medterm{Recurrent}-- Disease returning after remission. Examples: recurrent UTI, herpes.

\medterm{Referred Pain}-- Pain perceived at location other than site of pathology. Examples: myocardial infarction (left arm/jaw), gallbladder (right shoulder), diaphragmatic irritation (shoulder).


\medterm{Relapse}-- Return of cancer after treatment. See \hyperlink{Recurrence}{recurrence}.

\medterm{Reliability}-- Consistency of measurement. Test-retest, inter-rater reliability.

\medterm{Remission}-- Decrease or disappearance of cancer signs. See oncology.

\medterm{Reporting}-- Mandatory notification of certain diseases to public health authorities.

\medterm{Resolution}-- Complete healing or disappearance of disease.

\medterm{Respiratory Rate}-- Number of breaths per minute. Normal: 12-20. Assessment of rate, rhythm, depth.

\medterm{Risk Assessment}-- Evaluation of likelihood and impact of health risks. Used for screening, prevention planning.

\medterm{Risk Factor}-- Attribute increasing disease probability. Examples: smoking, hypertension, obesity.

\medterm{Rx}-- Prescription or treatment. Symbol derives from Latin "requirere" (to take). Also used as abbreviation for remedy. Rx symbol: Rx or \&x;.

\medterm{Saturate}-- To absorb or dissolve as much as possible. Oxygen saturation (SpO2) measures percentage of hemoglobin binding sites occupied by oxygen.


\medterm{Screening}-- Testing asymptomatic individuals for disease. Criteria: disease should be important, detectable, treatable. Examples: mammography, colonoscopy, blood pressure measurement.

\medterm{Screening Test}-- Test for asymptomatic disease. Should be safe, acceptable, accurate.

\medterm{Sensitivity}-- Proportion of true positives correctly identified by a test. High sensitivity: few false negatives. Useful for screening. Formula: true positives / (true positives + false negatives).


\medterm{Sentinel Event}-- Unexpected occurrence involving death or serious physical/psychological injury. Requires root cause analysis.

\medterm{Sequelae}-- Conditions resulting from previous disease. Examples: post-polio syndrome, stroke sequelae.

\medterm{Sexually Transmitted Infection}-- STI: infection transmitted via sexual contact. Examples: syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, HIV.

\medterm{Sign}-- Objective evidence of disease. Examples: rash, murmur, edema. Observed by examiner.

\medterm{Social Determinants of Health}-- Conditions in which people live affecting health. Include income, education, employment, housing, environment.

\medterm{Specificity}-- Proportion of true negatives correctly identified. High specificity: few false positives. Useful for confirmation.

\medterm{Standard of Care}-- Accepted medical practice for particular circumstances. Legal and professional benchmark.

\medterm{Subacute}-- Between acute and chronic. Examples: subacute thyroiditis.

\medterm{Surveillance}-- Ongoing collection, analysis, interpretation of health data. Used for public health action.

\medterm{Survival Rate}-- Percentage of patients surviving for specified time after diagnosis. See oncology.

\medterm{Symptom}-- Subjective experience of disease. Examples: pain, fatigue, nausea. Reported by patient.

\medterm{Syndrome}-- Group of symptoms and signs occurring together. Examples: metabolic syndrome, irritable bowel syndrome.

\medterm{Systematic Review}-- Comprehensive literature review following protocol. GRADE system for evidence quality.

\medterm{Tachypnea}-- Rapid breathing (>20 breaths/min in adults). Causes: fever, pain, anxiety, pulmonary disease, metabolic acidosis. Assessment of rate, depth, pattern essential.


\medterm{Temperature}-- Measure of body heat. Normal: 36.5-37.5°C. Sites: oral, rectal, axillary, temporal.

\medterm{Tincture}-- Alcohol extract of plant or chemical substance. Common concentration: 10-40% alcohol. Examples: tincture of iodine (antiseptic), tincture of benzoin (skin protector). Also used for liquid medications.

\medterm{Treatment}-- Medical intervention for disease. Includes pharmacological, surgical, lifestyle, supportive.

\medterm{Ulnar}-- Relating to the ulna bone of the forearm or the ulnar side (medial side) of the hand. Ulnar nerve compression causes paresthesia in medial one and a half fingers.

\medterm{Universal Health Coverage}-- All people receiving needed health services without financial hardship. WHO goal.


\medterm{Urination}-- The biological process of releasing urine from the bladder through the urethra out of the body; also known as micturition.

\medterm{Vaccination}-- Administration of a vaccine to stimulate immune response and provide protection against infectious disease. Types: live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, mRNA, viral vector. Schedule varies by country. Herd immunity achieved when sufficient proportion immune.


\medterm{Validity}-- Accuracy of measurement. Internal validity: study correctness. External validity: generalizability.

\medterm{Vector-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted by vector (insect, tick). Examples: malaria, dengue, Lyme disease.

\medterm{Vital Signs}-- Key physiological measurements: temperature, pulse, respiration rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, pain. Normal ranges: T 36.5-37.5°C, HR 60-100, RR 12-20, BP <120/80, SpO2 >95%.


\medterm{Water-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted via contaminated water. Examples: cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A.

\medterm{Wedge resection}-- Surgical removal of a wedge-shaped piece of tissue, typically lung or breast. Used for diagnosis or treatment of localized lesions. Less extensive than lobectomy.

\medterm{Wheal}-- Raised, itchy swelling of skin (urticaria/hives). Caused by mast cell degranulation and histamine release. Examples: insect bite, allergic reaction, dermatographism.


\medterm{X-ray}-- See radiography.

\medterm{Xerosis}-- Abnormal dryness of skin or mucous membranes. Causes: aging, environmental factors, medications, systemic disease (hypothyroidism, diabetes), nutritional deficiencies. Treatment: emollients, humidification, treat underlying cause.


\medterm{Yield}-- The proportion of positive test results among those tested. In clinical trials, yield refers to the proportion of patients benefiting from intervention. Diagnostic yield: percentage of tests providing diagnostic information.


\medterm{Yield Stress}-- The stress at which a material begins to deform plastically (permanently), relevant to implant design and bone mechanics. Relevant to implant design and bone mechanics.

\medterm{Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome}-- Gastrin-secreting tumor called gastrinoma causing gastric acid hypersecretion, severe peptic ulcers, and diarrhea. See \hyperlink{Gastroenterology}{gastroenterology}.

\medterm{Zoonosis}-- Disease transmitted from animals to humans. Examples: rabies, Lyme disease, avian influenza.

\medterm{Red Flag}-- A symptom, sign, or test result suggesting a potentially serious condition that requires urgent assessment or escalation of care. Red flags are context-specific and should not be used as a substitute for clinical judgment.

\medterm{Clinical Reasoning}-- The cognitive process of collecting, weighting, and integrating clinical information to formulate diagnostic and management decisions. It includes problem representation, differential diagnosis, probability assessment, and recognition of cognitive bias.

\medterm{Triage}-- The rapid sorting of patients according to urgency, clinical risk, and expected resource needs so that those requiring immediate intervention are prioritized. Triage is dynamic and must be repeated when a patient’s condition changes.

\medterm{Multimorbidity}-- The coexistence of two or more chronic health conditions in the same person, without requiring one to be considered the primary disease. It complicates treatment, increases polypharmacy risk, and requires coordinated patient-centered care.

\medterm{Frailty}-- A state of increased vulnerability to physiologic stress caused by reduced multisystem reserve. It is associated with weakness, slowed mobility, fatigue, weight loss, falls, disability, hospitalization, and mortality but is distinct from chronological age alone.

\medterm{Shared Decision-Making}-- A collaborative process in which clinicians and patients combine best available evidence with patient values, preferences, goals, and circumstances to choose among reasonable options.

\medterm{Care Coordination}-- Deliberate organization and communication of healthcare activities among professionals, services, patients, and caregivers to support safe, continuous, and appropriate care across settings.

\medterm{Medication Reconciliation}-- A structured comparison of the patient’s complete medication history with current orders at transitions of care to identify omissions, duplications, interactions, incorrect doses, and unintended changes.

\medterm{Polypharmacy}-- Use of multiple medications by one patient, particularly when the regimen is unnecessary, inappropriate, or difficult to manage. It increases risks of adverse effects, interactions, nonadherence, and prescribing cascades.

\medterm{Clinical Deterioration}-- Worsening of physiologic function or symptoms that increases risk of organ failure, disability, or death. Changes in mental status, respiratory effort, perfusion, urine output, and vital signs may precede overt collapse.

\medterm{Discharge Planning}-- A multidisciplinary process that prepares a patient for safe transition from hospital or facility care to the next setting. It includes medication review, follow-up, education, equipment, self-management support, and communication of pending results.

\medterm{Continuity of Care}-- Consistent and coordinated care over time and across clinicians or settings, integrating the patient’s history, current needs, treatment plan, and preferences. It supports safety, adherence, prevention, and chronic-disease outcomes.

\medterm{Clinical Handover}-- Transfer of responsibility and relevant patient information from one healthcare professional or team to another. Effective handover communicates diagnosis, current status, risks, pending tasks, contingency plans, and accountability.

\medterm{Patient-Centered Outcome}-- An outcome that reflects what matters to the patient, including symptoms, function, quality of life, treatment burden, and ability to achieve personal goals, rather than relying only on laboratory or imaging measures.

\medterm{Diagnostic Uncertainty}-- The state in which available information does not establish a single diagnosis with sufficient confidence. It should be communicated transparently and managed with safety-netting, planned reassessment, and targeted additional evaluation.

\medterm{Zoster}-- A painful, vesicular rash caused by reactivation of latent varicella-zoster virus (VZV, human herpesvirus 3) in the dorsal root ganglia. See \hyperlink{Herpes Zoster (Shingles)}{herpes zoster (shingles)}.

\medterm{Qigong}-- A traditional mind-body practice combining regulated breathing, focused attention, and gentle movement or posture. It is used as a complementary wellness practice and should not replace evaluation or treatment of medical disease.

\medterm{A1c}-- A glycated form of hemoglobin that reflects average blood glucose over approximately the preceding two to three months. It is used to diagnose and monitor diabetes, although anemia, hemoglobin variants, altered red-cell survival, and assay limitations can affect interpretation.

\medterm{Abducens Nerve}-- The sixth cranial nerve, a motor nerve that supplies the lateral rectus muscle and abducts the eye. Lesion of the nerve causes failure of lateral gaze and horizontal diplopia.

\medterm{Accessory Nerve}-- The eleventh cranial nerve, formed by cranial and spinal roots. Its spinal component supplies the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles; injury can cause shoulder droop and weak head rotation.

\medterm{Abdominal Reflex}-- A superficial cutaneous reflex elicited by stroking the abdominal skin toward the umbilicus, producing contraction of the underlying abdominal muscles. Asymmetry may indicate corticospinal tract dysfunction.

\medterm{Abasia}-- Inability to walk despite preserved strength or the ability to move the legs in other circumstances, usually reflecting a disorder of gait coordination or another neurologic condition rather than paralysis alone.

\medterm{Acalculia}-- An acquired inability to perform previously learned numerical operations or understand numerical relationships, commonly associated with dominant parietal or other cortical dysfunction.

\medterm{Acapnia}-- An abnormally low partial pressure of carbon dioxide in arterial blood, usually caused by excessive alveolar ventilation. It may accompany respiratory alkalosis, light-headedness, or paresthesias.

\medterm{Acariasis}-- Infestation or disease caused by mites or ticks. Clinical manifestations vary with the organism and may include dermatitis, pruritus, tissue invasion, or transmission of an associated pathogen.

\medterm{Acathisia}-- A syndrome of inner restlessness accompanied by a compelling need to move, often with pacing or shifting of the legs. It is a clinically recognized movement disorder characterized by observable motor activity and subjective distress.

\medterm{Abetalipoproteinemia}-- A rare inherited disorder of lipoprotein assembly that causes very low circulating apoB-containing lipoproteins, fat malabsorption, deficiency of fat-soluble vitamins, acanthocytosis, and progressive neurologic or retinal disease.

\medterm{ABO Blood Group}-- The classification of blood according to the presence or absence of A and B antigens on red blood cells and the corresponding antibodies in plasma. The major groups are A, B, AB, and O.

\medterm{Acardia}-- Absence or severe malformation of the fetal heart, usually occurring in the setting of a rare twin perfusion sequence. The affected fetus cannot sustain independent circulation.

\medterm{Acetabular Labrum}-- A fibrocartilaginous rim attached to the margin of the acetabulum that deepens the hip socket, helps maintain a fluid seal, and contributes to hip-joint stability.

\medterm{Acetabular Notch}-- The indentation in the inferior margin of the acetabulum, bridged by the transverse ligament of the acetabulum and forming a passage for structures entering the hip joint.

\medterm{Acetabuloplasty}-- A surgical procedure that reshapes or augments the acetabular rim or socket to improve coverage of the femoral head in selected structural hip disorders.

\medterm{Bainbridge Reflex}-- An increase in heart rate produced by stretch of the atrial walls, especially during increased venous return. The reflex helps limit venous congestion by adjusting cardiac output.

\medterm{Ballism}-- A hyperkinetic movement disorder characterized by sudden, involuntary, large-amplitude, flinging movements of a limb or limbs. It is often related to dysfunction of the subthalamic region or connected basal-ganglia circuits.

\medterm{Ballistocardiography}-- The graphic recording of repetitive body movements produced by the ejection of blood with each heartbeat. It has been used to study cardiac mechanical activity and circulation.

\medterm{Band Keratopathy}-- A horizontal band of calcium deposition in the superficial cornea, typically across the interpalpebral area. It may occur with chronic ocular inflammation, hypercalcemia, renal disease, or longstanding eye disease and can impair vision.

\medterm{Banti Disease}-- A historical term for congestive splenomegaly associated with portal hypertension and hypersplenism. Modern evaluation usually identifies the underlying hepatic, portal-venous, or hematologic cause.

\medterm{Barognosis}-- The ability to recognize and distinguish the weight of objects by handling them, with intact primary sensation. Impaired barognosis indicates a cortical sensory deficit.

\medterm{Balanoposthitis}-- Inflammation of both the glans penis and the prepuce. It may cause pain, erythema, swelling, discharge, or difficulty retracting the foreskin and can result from infection, irritation, or inflammatory skin disease.

\medterm{Bacilluria}-- The presence of bacilli in the urine, usually referring to rod-shaped bacteria detected by microscopy or culture. Its clinical significance depends on symptoms, quantitative culture, and the organism identified.

\medterm{Bauhin Valve}-- The ileocecal valve, a mucosal sphincter at the junction of the terminal ileum and cecum that helps regulate passage of intestinal contents and limits reflux from the colon.

\medterm{B Chromosome}-- A supernumerary chromosome that is not part of the standard chromosome complement and is not required for normal development. It may occur in certain plants, animals, or tumors and varies in number between cells.

\medterm{Beals Syndrome}-- A congenital connective-tissue disorder characterized by congenital contractures, arachnodactyly, kyphoscoliosis, and crumpled ears. It is usually associated with pathogenic variants affecting fibrillin-2.

\medterm{Beau's Lines}-- Transverse grooves or depressions across a nail caused by temporary interruption of nail-matrix growth. They may follow severe illness, trauma, nutritional stress, or systemic disease and grow distally with the nail.

\medterm{Bence-Jones Protein}-- A monoclonal immunoglobulin light chain found in urine or blood, classically associated with plasma-cell disorders such as multiple myeloma. Detection contributes to evaluation of monoclonal gammopathy and kidney injury.

\medterm{Bends}-- A manifestation of decompression sickness caused by gas bubbles forming in tissues or blood after a rapid reduction in ambient pressure. It commonly produces deep joint or muscle pain but may also involve the nervous system or lungs.

\medterm{Benign Intracranial Hypertension}-- An older term for idiopathic intracranial hypertension, a disorder of raised intracranial pressure without an intracranial mass or hydrocephalus. Headache and papilledema are typical, and untreated disease may threaten vision.

\medterm{Bent Spine Syndrome}-- A marked, usually reducible forward flexion of the trunk caused by weakness or abnormal contraction of axial extensor muscles. It is also called camptocormia and may accompany neuromuscular or movement disorders.

\medterm{Berry Aneurysm}-- A small, rounded saccular aneurysm arising at an arterial branch point in the circle of Willis. Rupture is a major cause of nontraumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage.

\medterm{Biceps Reflex}-- A tendon reflex produced by tapping the biceps tendon, causing elbow flexion through the musculocutaneous nerve and predominantly C5--C6 spinal segments. It helps assess the corresponding peripheral and central pathways.

\medterm{Bicipital Groove}-- The intertubercular groove of the humerus that transmits the tendon of the long head of the biceps brachii, with the transverse humeral ligament and adjacent rotator-cuff structures contributing to tendon stability.

\medterm{Bicipital Tuberosity}-- The radial tuberosity, a roughened prominence on the proximal radius where the biceps brachii tendon inserts and produces flexion and supination of the forearm.

\medterm{Bicuspid Valve}-- A heart valve with two leaflets rather than the usual three. The term most often refers to congenital bicuspid aortic valve, which can cause aortic stenosis, regurgitation, infective endocarditis, or ascending-aortic disease.

\medterm{Biliary Fever}-- Fever associated with infection or inflammation of the biliary tract, classically acute ascending cholangitis. It may occur with right-upper-quadrant pain and jaundice and can progress rapidly to sepsis.

\medterm{Bilirubinuria}-- The presence of conjugated bilirubin in urine. It indicates that water-soluble bilirubin has entered the circulation, often because of hepatocellular disease or biliary obstruction.

\medterm{Binet-Simon Scale}-- An early developmental assessment used to estimate intellectual functioning in children by comparing performance with age-based tasks. Modern evaluation uses standardized, validated developmental and cognitive instruments.

\medterm{Biosensor}-- A device that combines a biological recognition element with a physical or chemical transducer to detect and measure an analyte. Medical examples include sensors for glucose, pathogens, hormones, and cardiac biomarkers.

\medterm{Bird Flu}-- Influenza caused by avian-adapted influenza A viruses. Human infection is uncommon but may cause severe respiratory disease, and public-health assessment depends on exposure history, testing, and infection-control measures.

\medterm{Biotelemetry}-- The remote measurement and transmission of physiologic data from a person or animal. Clinical applications include ambulatory cardiac rhythm monitoring, temperature monitoring, and intensive-care observation.

\medterm{Bacterial Toxin}-- A poisonous molecule produced by bacteria that damages host cells or disrupts normal physiology. Exotoxins are secreted or released from viable bacteria, whereas endotoxin refers principally to lipopolysaccharide from the outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria.

\medterm{Bitot's Spots}-- Foamy, whitish, triangular patches on the conjunctiva, usually near the temporal limbus, associated classically with vitamin A deficiency. They may accompany night blindness and xerosis of the conjunctiva or cornea.

\medterm{Bitewing}-- A dental radiograph that records the crowns of the upper and lower teeth and the adjacent alveolar bone in one image. It is particularly useful for detecting interproximal caries and assessing crestal bone levels.

\medterm{Black Hairy Tongue}-- A benign elongation and dark discoloration of the filiform papillae on the dorsal tongue caused by impaired desquamation and trapping of debris or pigment. Predisposing factors include smoking, poor oral hygiene, dry mouth, and some exposures.

\medterm{Black Lung}-- Coal workers' pneumoconiosis caused by prolonged inhalation of coal-mine dust. It ranges from simple anthracosis to progressive massive fibrosis and may cause cough, breathlessness, and permanent lung impairment.

\medterm{Blalock-Taussig Operation}-- A surgical systemic-to-pulmonary shunt that increases pulmonary blood flow in selected cyanotic congenital heart disease. The modern modified procedure commonly uses a prosthetic conduit rather than direct sacrifice of a subclavian artery.

\medterm{Blennorrhea}-- A mucopurulent discharge from a mucous membrane, especially the urethra or conjunctiva. The finding requires clinical assessment for infectious and noninfectious causes rather than treatment based on the symptom alone.

\medterm{Blepharoconjunctivitis}-- Inflammation involving the eyelid margins and conjunctiva. It may cause crusting, itching, redness, tearing, irritation, and recurrent symptoms associated with infection, allergy, or eyelid-gland dysfunction.

\medterm{Blepharoplasty}-- Surgical reconstruction or reshaping of the eyelids, often involving removal or repositioning of excess skin, muscle, or orbital fat. It may be functional, cosmetic, or reconstructive.

\medterm{Body Ringworm}-- Dermatophyte infection of the glabrous skin, also called tinea corporis. It typically produces an expanding annular scaly lesion with a more active border and central clearing.

\medterm{Boeck's Sarcoid}-- An older eponym for sarcoidosis, a multisystem granulomatous disease characterized by noncaseating granulomas and commonly involving the lungs, intrathoracic lymph nodes, skin, or eyes.

\medterm{Bornholm Disease}-- An acute epidemic pleurodynia caused most often by coxsackieviruses. It produces sudden attacks of severe chest or upper-abdominal muscle pain, fever, and sometimes headache or vomiting.

\medterm{Borreliosis}-- Infection caused by bacteria of the genus Borrelia. Human disease includes Lyme borreliosis and relapsing fever, with manifestations varying according to species, stage, and organ involvement.

\medterm{Botryomycosis}-- A chronic bacterial infection characterized by granule-forming lesions that can resemble a fungal infection. It most often affects skin and subcutaneous tissue but may involve viscera in susceptible patients.

\medterm{Bouchard's Node}-- A hard, palpable enlargement of the proximal interphalangeal joint caused by osteoarthritis. It reflects osteophyte formation and may be associated with pain, stiffness, and reduced finger motion.

\medterm{Bougienage}-- The use of a bougie to dilate or explore a narrowed tubular structure, such as the esophagus or urethra. It is a procedure that requires assessment of the cause and risks of the stricture.

\medterm{Boutonneuse Fever}-- A tick-borne rickettsial infection, usually caused by Rickettsia conorii, characterized by fever, headache, rash, and often a dark inoculation eschar called a tache noire.

\medterm{Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy}-- A fatal transmissible prion disease of cattle causing progressive neurologic degeneration. It is related to variant Creutzfeldt--Jakob disease in humans through exposure to contaminated tissue.

\medterm{Bowman's Gland}-- A serous gland in the olfactory mucosa whose secretion helps dissolve odorant molecules and cleanse the olfactory surface. It is located in the lamina propria beneath the olfactory epithelium.

\medterm{Branchial Arch}-- One of the paired embryonic arches that contribute to development of facial, pharyngeal, laryngeal, muscular, skeletal, vascular, and neural structures of the head and neck.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft}-- An embryonic groove between adjacent branchial arches. Persistence of cleft remnants can produce congenital branchial cysts, sinuses, or fistulas, most commonly along the lateral neck.

\medterm{Breakbone Fever}-- A traditional name for dengue fever, reflecting the severe myalgia and arthralgia that may accompany the acute viral illness.

\medterm{Broca's Aphasia}-- A nonfluent language disorder caused by dysfunction of the dominant inferior frontal language region. Speech is effortful and reduced, while comprehension is relatively preserved compared with repetition and fluent language production.

\medterm{Broca's Area}-- A language-related region of the dominant inferior frontal gyrus involved in speech production, language formulation, and aspects of syntax. Lesions may cause expressive aphasia.

\medterm{Bright's Disease}-- A historical term encompassing several forms of kidney disease, particularly glomerular disorders associated with proteinuria, edema, and altered urinary findings. Modern diagnosis specifies the underlying renal lesion.

\medterm{Brill's Disease}-- A recrudescent form of epidemic typhus caused by reactivation of latent Rickettsia prowazekii infection years after the original illness. It is usually milder than the initial infection but can transmit to body lice.

\medterm{Bromhidrosis}-- Excessive or unpleasant body odor produced when microbial metabolism acts on sweat or skin secretions. It is often prominent in the axillae or feet and may be associated with hyperhidrosis or local skin disease.

\medterm{Bromoderma}-- A skin eruption or dermatitis attributed to exposure to bromine or bromide compounds. It may present with acneiform, pustular, or other inflammatory lesions.

\medterm{Bronchial Artery}-- An artery arising from the systemic circulation that supplies the walls of the bronchi, connective tissue of the lungs, and visceral pleura. It differs from the pulmonary arteries, which carry blood for gas exchange.

\medterm{Bronchial Tree}-- The branching airway system formed by the trachea, main bronchi, lobar and segmental bronchi, bronchioles, and terminal airways leading toward the alveoli.

\medterm{Bronchial Vein}-- A vein draining portions of the bronchial walls and larger airways. It empties partly into the azygos system and partly into pulmonary veins, creating a small physiologic venous admixture.

\medterm{Bronchiolectasis}-- Permanent abnormal dilation of bronchioles, usually caused by chronic inflammation, infection, or obstruction. It may accompany bronchiectasis and contribute to impaired mucus clearance and recurrent infection.

\medterm{Bronchostenosis}-- Narrowing of a bronchus that restricts airflow. Causes include scarring, tumors, foreign bodies, inflammation, external compression, or postinfectious and postprocedural changes.

\medterm{Brown Lung Disease}-- An occupational airway disease associated with inhalation of cotton, flax, or hemp dust, classically causing byssinosis. Symptoms may include chest tightness, cough, wheezing, and work-related airflow limitation.

\medterm{Brudzinski Sign}-- Flexion of the hips and knees that occurs involuntarily when the neck is passively flexed. It is a meningeal irritation sign that may occur in meningitis but lacks sufficient sensitivity or specificity to establish the diagnosis alone.

\medterm{Brunner's Gland}-- A mucus-secreting gland in the submucosa of the duodenum. Its alkaline secretion helps protect the mucosa from gastric acid and provides conditions favorable for intestinal enzymes.

\medterm{Buccal Gland}-- A minor salivary gland located in the cheek. It secretes mucus into the oral cavity to lubricate the buccal mucosa and assist formation and handling of the food bolus.

\medterm{Bulbar Paralysis}-- Weakness or paralysis of muscles supplied by the lower cranial nerves, causing dysarthria, dysphagia, impaired gag or cough, and sometimes respiratory compromise. Causes include brainstem disease, motor-neuron disease, and neuromuscular disorders.

\medterm{Buerger's Disease}-- Thromboangiitis obliterans, a nonatherosclerotic inflammatory disease of small and medium arteries and veins, strongly associated with tobacco exposure. It causes distal ischemia, pain, ulcers, and sometimes gangrene.

\medterm{Buphthalmos}-- Enlargement of the eye caused by elevated intraocular pressure during early childhood, when the ocular coats are still distensible. It is classically associated with congenital or infantile glaucoma.

\medterm{Byssinosis}-- An occupational obstructive airway disease caused by inhalation of textile fibers, especially cotton, flax, or hemp dust. It may cause chest tightness and cough that worsen during or after work exposure.

\medterm{Bursectomy}-- Surgical excision of a bursa, usually performed for persistent painful bursitis, recurrent infection, or another structural problem that has not responded to appropriate conservative management.

\medterm{Cabot Ring}-- A threadlike or ring-shaped inclusion in a red blood cell, sometimes seen in severe anemia or disorders of erythropoiesis. It represents residual nuclear material or mitotic spindle remnants.

\medterm{Cacosmia}-- A distorted or unpleasant perception of smell, in which odors may seem foul or disagreeable. It can occur with sinonasal disease, infection, seizures, trauma, or other neurologic disorders.

\medterm{Caecitis}-- Inflammation of the cecum. Causes include infection, inflammatory bowel disease, ischemia, neutropenic enterocolitis, and extension of adjacent inflammatory disease.

\medterm{Caecopexy}-- Surgical fixation of the cecum to the abdominal wall or retroperitoneum, performed to prevent or treat abnormal cecal mobility or recurrent cecal volvulus.

\medterm{Caecostomy}-- Surgical creation of an opening from the cecum to the abdominal wall for decompression or diversion of intestinal contents. It may be temporary or part of treatment for selected distal obstruction.

\medterm{Caecotomy}-- Surgical incision into the cecum, usually to remove a foreign body, access the bowel, or facilitate another abdominal procedure.

\medterm{Caisson Disease}-- Decompression sickness caused by formation of inert-gas bubbles after a rapid reduction in ambient pressure. It can cause joint pain, neurologic injury, respiratory symptoms, or circulatory collapse.

\medterm{Café au Lait Spot}-- A flat, sharply demarcated, light-brown skin macule caused by increased melanin. Multiple lesions may be a feature of neurofibromatosis type 1 or other genetic disorders.

\medterm{Caldwell-Luc Operation}-- A surgical approach to the maxillary sinus through the canine fossa of the anterior maxilla. It is used selectively for access to maxillary sinus disease, lesions, or foreign bodies.

\medterm{Campimetry}-- Measurement and mapping of the visual field, usually by presenting stimuli at different locations while the patient maintains fixation. It helps detect and characterize defects from retinal, optic-nerve, or retrochiasmal disease.

\medterm{Camptodactyly}-- A congenital or acquired flexion deformity of one or more fingers, usually caused by shortening or abnormality of tendons, muscles, fascia, or joints. It often affects the proximal interphalangeal joints.

\medterm{Campylobacteriosis}-- Infection caused by Campylobacter species, commonly producing fever, abdominal pain, and inflammatory diarrhea that may be bloody. Complications can include dehydration, bacteremia, reactive arthritis, or Guillain--Barré syndrome.

\medterm{Canal of Schlemm}-- A circular endothelial-lined channel at the corneoscleral junction that drains aqueous humor from the anterior chamber into the episcleral venous circulation. Impaired outflow contributes to glaucoma.

\medterm{Canavan Disease}-- An autosomal-recessive leukodystrophy caused by aspartoacylase deficiency, leading to accumulation of N-acetylaspartate and progressive spongy degeneration of cerebral white matter. It typically presents in infancy with hypotonia followed by developmental impairment and macrocephaly.

\medterm{Cancrum Oris}-- Noma, a rapidly destructive gangrenous infection of the mouth and face that occurs mainly in severely malnourished or immunocompromised children. It begins with gingival or mucosal necrosis and can extend through facial tissues.

\medterm{Candidemia}-- The presence of Candida species in the bloodstream. It is an invasive fungal infection associated with intravascular devices, surgery, intensive care, immunosuppression, and substantial risk of organ dissemination.

\medterm{Canicola Fever}-- A form of leptospirosis associated particularly with Leptospira interrogans serovar canicola. It may cause fever, headache, myalgia, conjunctival suffusion, and renal or hepatic involvement.

\medterm{Canker Sore}-- A common aphthous ulcer of the oral mucosa, appearing as a painful, shallow, round or oval ulcer with an erythematous border. Most heal spontaneously, but recurrent, severe, or atypical ulcers require assessment.

\medterm{Cannulation}-- The insertion of a cannula into a blood vessel, body cavity, duct, or other anatomical passage to permit drainage, infusion, monitoring, sampling, or access for a procedure.

\medterm{Capgras Syndrome}-- A delusional misidentification syndrome in which a person believes that someone familiar has been replaced by an identical impostor. It can occur in neurodegenerative disease, psychosis, brain injury, or other neurologic conditions.

\medterm{Capillaroscopy}-- Examination of the small vessels in the nail-fold or another accessible surface using magnification. It can reveal capillary dilation, hemorrhage, avascular areas, and abnormal architecture in connective-tissue and microvascular disease.

\medterm{Capsular Contracture}-- Abnormal tightening and hardening of the fibrous capsule around an implanted device, especially a breast implant. It may cause pain, distortion, firmness, or displacement.

\medterm{Capsulectomy}-- Surgical removal of part or all of a fibrous capsule surrounding an organ, implant, or other structure. The indication depends on contracture, infection, pathology, or the need to revise a reconstruction.

\medterm{Capsule of Glisson}-- The connective-tissue capsule surrounding the liver, which sends fibrous septa inward along portal tracts and supports the organ's vessels, ducts, and nerves.

\medterm{Capsule of Tenon}-- A fascial sheath surrounding the globe of the eye and separating it from orbital fat. It contributes to eye movement and forms a potential space used in some ophthalmic procedures.

\medterm{Cardioplegia}-- Deliberate temporary arrest and protection of the heart during cardiac surgery, usually achieved with a cold or blood-based solution that alters myocardial electrical and metabolic activity.

\medterm{Cardiospasm}-- Spasm or impaired relaxation of the lower esophageal sphincter, historically used as a term for achalasia. It causes dysphagia, regurgitation, and sometimes chest discomfort.

\medterm{Cardiotoxicity}-- Injury or functional impairment of the heart caused by a toxic exposure, systemic disease, or treatment. Manifestations include arrhythmia, myocardial dysfunction, ischemia, or heart failure.

\medterm{Cardioversion}-- Restoration of a normal cardiac rhythm from an arrhythmia by synchronized electrical shock or, in some contexts, a planned pharmacologic intervention. Synchronized shock avoids delivery during vulnerable ventricular repolarization.

\medterm{Carditis}-- Inflammation of the heart or one of its layers, including the myocardium, endocardium, or pericardium. It may result from infection, immune-mediated disease, or systemic inflammation.

\medterm{Caries}-- Progressive destruction of calcified tooth tissue caused by acid-producing microbial biofilm acting on fermentable carbohydrates. Untreated disease can extend from enamel into dentin and pulp.

\medterm{Cariogenic}-- Capable of promoting dental caries, especially by producing acid from fermentable carbohydrates or supporting a plaque environment that demineralizes tooth structure.

\medterm{Camptocormia}-- A marked forward flexion of the trunk that increases while standing or walking and improves when lying down. It can occur with axial myopathy, Parkinsonian disorders, dystonia, or other neuromuscular disease.
