\chapter{General Medical Terms}

\medterm{Abdomen}-- The body region between the thorax and pelvis, containing the digestive organs, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, spleen, kidneys, and major blood vessels. Divided into nine regions (right/left hypochondriac, epigastric, umbilical, right/left lumbar, right/left inguinal) or four quadrants (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) for clinical description.

\medterm{Auscultation}-- The act of listening to sounds produced within the body, typically using a stethoscope. Essential for assessing heart sounds, lung sounds, bowel sounds, and vascular bruits. Normal findings: S1 and S2 heart sounds, vesicular breath sounds, normal bowel sounds. Abnormal: murmurs, crackles, wheezes, rubs, bruits.

\medterm{Biopsy}-- Removal of tissue for histological examination to diagnose disease. Types: needle biopsy (fine needle aspiration, core needle), excisional, incisional, punch, endoscopic. Sites: breast, lung, liver, lymph node, bone marrow. Complications: bleeding, infection, seeding (rare).

\medterm{Comorbidity}-- Presence of one or more additional conditions co-occurring with a primary disease. Common in chronic illnesses (diabetes with hypertension, COPD with heart failure). Affects treatment decisions, prognosis, and healthcare utilization.

\medterm{Contraindication}-- A condition or factor that renders a particular procedure, treatment, or medication inadvisable. Absolute contraindication: condition where intervention should never be performed. Relative contraindication: risks outweigh benefits but may be acceptable in specific circumstances. Examples: pregnancy (absolute for isotretinoin), severe renal impairment (relative for contrast CT).

\medterm{Diff diagnosis}-- The process of distinguishing a particular disease or condition from others that present with similar symptoms. Systematic approach: consider life-threatening conditions first, use pre-test probability, apply diagnostic criteria, order targeted tests.

\medterm{Edema}-- Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues. Types: pitting (press leaves indentation), non-pitting (myxedema, lymphedema). Causes: heart failure, kidney disease, liver cirrhosis, venous insufficiency, lymphatic obstruction, medications (calcium channel blockers, NSAIDs). Assessment: degree, distribution, symmetry, associated symptoms.

\medterm{Examination}-- Systematic assessment of a patient's health status. Components: inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation (IPPA). General survey: appearance, posture, gait, vital signs. Focused examination based on chief complaint.

\medterm{Homeostasis}-- The maintenance of stable internal conditions despite external changes. Key regulated variables: temperature, pH, glucose, electrolytes, blood pressure, fluid balance. Mechanisms: negative feedback loops (most common), positive feedback (rare, e.g., labor, blood clotting). Organs involved: hypothalamus, kidneys, lungs, liver, endocrine glands.

\medterm{Incidence}-- Number of new cases of a disease in a population during a specified time period. Measures risk of developing disease. Incidence rate = new cases / population at risk x time unit.

\medterm{Inflammation}-- The body's protective response to injury or infection. Cardinal signs: redness (rubor), heat (calor), swelling (tumor), pain (dolor), loss of function (functio laesa). Mediators: histamine, prostaglandins, cytokines, complement. Acute: neutrophil-predominant. Chronic: lymphocyte/macrophage-predominant.

\medterm{Informed Consent}-- The process by which a patient voluntarily confirms understanding of a proposed treatment or procedure, including risks, benefits, alternatives, and consequences of non-treatment. Elements: disclosure, comprehension, voluntariness, competence, consent. Documented on consent form. Exceptions: emergencies, waived consent (minimal risk research), therapeutic privilege.

\medterm{Iatrogenic}-- An illness or condition caused by medical examination or treatment. Examples: hospital-acquired infections, drug side effects, surgical complications, radiation-induced damage. Prevention: proper technique, informed consent, monitoring, reporting systems.

\medterm{Jargon}-- Specialized language used by a particular group or profession. Medical jargon can hinder communication with patients. Examples: "stat" (immediately), "NPO" (nothing by mouth), "DX" (diagnosis). Modern trend toward plain language in patient communication.

\medterm{Kussmaul Respirations}-- Deep, labored breathing pattern associated with diabetic ketoacidosis. Compensatory hyperventilation to blow off CO2 and correct metabolic acidosis. Name: Adolf Kussmaul, German physician.

\medterm{Lateral}-- Positioned away from the midline of the body. Opposite of medial. Example: the thumb is lateral to the little finger in anatomical position.

\medterm{Mediate}-- To transmit or convey between two points. In medicine, mediation often refers to the mechanism by which a cause produces an effect. Mediated immunity involves immune cells; direct contact does not.

\medterm{Nadir}-- The lowest point, particularly referring to the lowest white blood cell count after chemotherapy. Bone marrow suppression typically reaches nadir 7-14 days post-treatment. Important for infection risk assessment.

\medterm{Oropharynx}-- The posterior portion of the mouth leading to the esophagus, containing the tonsils and base of tongue. Area for swallowing and speech. Examination with tongue depressor and light source. Pathology: pharyngitis, tonsillitis, malignancy.

\medterm{Palpation}-- Examination by touch to detect abnormalities in size, shape, consistency, and tenderness of organs or tissues. Uses fingers, palm, or heel of hand. Light vs. deep palpation. Bimanual palpation uses two hands.

\medterm{Quadrant}-- One of four sections of the abdomen (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) used for clinical description. RUQ: liver, gallbladder, duodenum, right kidney. LUQ: stomach, spleen, left kidney, pancreas tail. RLQ: appendix, cecum, right ovary. LLQ: sigmoid colon, left ovary.

\medterm{Rx}-- Prescription or treatment. Symbol derives from Latin "requirere" (to take). Also used as abbreviation for remedy. Rx symbol: Rx or \&x;.

\medterm{Sensitivity}-- Proportion of true positives correctly identified by a test. High sensitivity: few false negatives. Useful for screening. Formula: true positives / (true positives + false negatives).

\medterm{Tincture}-- Alcohol extract of plant or chemical substance. Common concentration: 10-40% alcohol. Examples: tincture of iodine (antiseptic), tincture of benzoin (skin protector). Also used for liquid medications.

\medterm{Ulnar}-- Relating to the ulna bone of the forearm or the ulnar side (medial side) of the hand. Ulnar nerve compression causes paresthesia in medial one and a half fingers.

\medterm{Vaccination}-- Administration of a vaccine to stimulate immune response and provide protection against infectious disease. Types: live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, mRNA, viral vector. Schedule varies by country. Herd immunity achieved when sufficient proportion immune.

\medterm{Wedge resection}-- Surgical removal of a wedge-shaped piece of tissue, typically lung or breast. Used for diagnosis or treatment of localized lesions. Less extensive than lobectomy.

\medterm{Xerosis}-- Abnormal dryness of skin or mucous membranes. Causes: aging, environmental factors, medications, systemic disease (hypothyroidism, diabetes), nutritional deficiencies. Treatment: emollients, humidification, treat underlying cause.

\medterm{Yield}-- The proportion of positive test results among those tested. In clinical trials, yield refers to the proportion of patients benefiting from intervention. Diagnostic yield: percentage of tests providing diagnostic information.

\medterm{Zoster}-- See herpes zoster (shingles).

\medterm{Auscultatory Gap}-- A transient silence between the first and second sounds of blood pressure measurement, sometimes heard in hypertensive patients. Can lead to underestimation of systolic pressure if not recognized.

\medterm{Barium Swallow}-- Radiographic examination of the esophagus using barium sulfate contrast. Evaluates swallowing function, motility, strictures, masses. Modified barium swallow assesses aspiration risk.

\medterm{Capillary Refill Time}-- Time for color to return to blanched nail bed or skin after pressure. Normal: <2 seconds. Prolonged in shock, dehydration, peripheral vasoconstriction.

\medterm{Diuresis}-- Increased urine production. Can be physiological (fluid overload) or pharmacological (diuretics). Osmotic diuresis (hyperglycemia), water diuresis (DI), saline diuresis (hypertonic saline).

\medterm{Echo}-- See echocardiography.

\medterm{Fistula}-- Abnormal connection between two epithelial surfaces. Examples: arteriovenous fistula, enterocutaneous fistula, rectovaginal fistula. Can be congenital or acquired (surgery, infection, trauma, malignancy).

\medterm{Gait}-- Pattern of walking. Normal gait involves coordinated movement of legs, arms, trunk. Abnormal gait patterns: antalgic (pain), hemiplegic (stroke), parkinsonian (shuffling), ataxic (cerebellar), steppage (foot drop).

\medterm{Hemostasis}-- The process of stopping bleeding. Primary: platelet plug formation. Secondary: coagulation cascade. Tertiary: vasoconstriction and fibrinolysis regulation.

\medterm{Inspection}-- Visual examination of the patient. Part of physical assessment. Observes symmetry, color, lesions, movement, posture.

\medterm{Joint Effusion}-- Excess fluid within a joint space. Common sites: knee (most accessible), ankle, wrist, shoulder. Causes: trauma, infection, inflammatory arthritis, crystal arthropathy. Detected by ballottement or bulge sign.

\medterm{Kernig Sign}-- Sign of meningeal irritation. Patient supine, hip and knee flexed 90 degrees; inability to extend knee indicates positive sign. Seen in meningitis, subarachnoid hemorrhage.

\medterm{Leukocytosis}-- Elevated white blood cell count (>11,000/$\mu$L). Causes: infection, inflammation, stress, medications (corticosteroids), malignancy (leukemia). Neutrophilia, lymphocytosis, eosinophilia, monocytosis have different implications.

\medterm{Murmur}-- Heart sound caused by turbulent blood flow. Grades I-VI. Systolic murmurs more common; diastolic murmurs always pathological. Evaluation: timing, location, radiation, intensity, quality, position changes.

\medterm{Nodule}-- Small, palpable mass <2 cm. Can be solid or cystic. Examples: thyroid nodule, rheumatoid nodule, pulmonary nodule. Evaluation depends on location.

\medterm{Oliguria}-- Decreased urine output (<400 mL/day in adults). Causes: prerenal (dehydration, heart failure), renal (acute kidney injury), postrenal (obstruction). Medical emergency if anuric.

\medterm{Percussion}-- Tapping body surface to assess underlying structures. Sounds: dull (solid organ, fluid), resonant (lung), tympanic (gas-filled). Used to assess organ size, presence of fluid, air.

\medterm{Quiescent}-- Inactive or dormant state of disease. Example: quiescent ulcerative colitis (no symptoms but histologic inflammation may persist).

\medterm{Referred Pain}-- Pain perceived at location other than site of pathology. Examples: myocardial infarction (left arm/jaw), gallbladder (right shoulder), diaphragmatic irritation (shoulder).

\medterm{Saturate}-- To absorb or dissolve as much as possible. Oxygen saturation (SpO2) measures percentage of hemoglobin binding sites occupied by oxygen.

\medterm{Tachypnea}-- Rapid breathing (>20 breaths/min in adults). Causes: fever, pain, anxiety, pulmonary disease, metabolic acidosis. Assessment of rate, depth, pattern essential.

\medterm{Urination}-- See micturition.

\medterm{Vital Signs}-- Key physiological measurements: temperature, pulse, respiration rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, pain. Normal ranges: T 36.5-37.5°C, HR 60-100, RR 12-20, BP <120/80, SpO2 >95%.

\medterm{Wheal}-- Raised, itchy swelling of skin (urticaria/hives). Caused by mast cell degranulation and histamine release. Examples: insect bite, allergic reaction, dermatographism.

\medterm{X-ray}-- See radiography.

\medterm{Yield Stress}-- In biomechanics, the stress at which a material begins to deform plastically. Relevant to implant design and bone mechanics.

\medterm{Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome}-- See gastroenterology.

\medterm{Abscess}-- See emergency\_medicine.

\medterm{Biopsy}-- See general.

\medterm{Comorbidity}-- See general.

\medterm{Diuresis}-- See general.

\medterm{Edema}-- See general.

\medterm{Fever}-- Elevated body temperature (>38°C or 100.4°F). Workup depends on duration, height, and patient factors. Common causes: infections (bacterial, viral), autoimmune disorders, malignancies, drug reactions. Management: antipyretics (acetaminophen, ibuprofen), treat underlying cause. Febrile seizures: generally benign, prevent recurrence with fever control.

\medterm{Anatomy}-- Study of body structure. Regions: head, neck, thorax, abdomen, pelvis, extremities. Systems: skeletal, muscular, nervous, circulatory, respiratory, digestive, urinary, reproductive, endocrine, immune.

\medterm{Physiology}-- Study of body function. Systems physiology: cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, gastrointestinal, endocrine, nervous, muscular, reproductive.

\medterm{Pathology}-- Study of disease. Includes general pathology (cell injury, inflammation, neoplasia) and systemic pathology (organ-specific diseases).

\medterm{Etiology}-- Cause or origin of disease. Examples: genetic, infectious, environmental, idiopathic.

\medterm{Pathogenesis}-- Mechanism of disease development. Steps: initiation, promotion, progression.

\medterm{Clinical Presentation}-- Signs and symptoms of disease. Varies by disease, severity, patient factors.

\medterm{Diagnosis}-- Identification of disease. Based on history, examination, tests. Differential diagnosis considers multiple possibilities.

\medterm{Prognosis}-- Predicted disease outcome. Depends on disease, treatment, patient factors.

\medterm{Treatment}-- Medical intervention for disease. Includes pharmacological, surgical, lifestyle, supportive.

\medterm{Prevention}-- Measures to prevent disease. Primary (before disease), secondary (early detection), tertiary (complication prevention).

\medterm{Screening}-- Testing asymptomatic individuals for disease. Criteria: disease should be important, detectable, treatable. Examples: mammography, colonoscopy, blood pressure measurement.

\medterm{Epidemiology}-- Study of disease distribution and determinants in populations. Measures: incidence, prevalence, mortality, morbidity.

\medterm{Public Health}-- See public health.

\medterm{Patient}-- Person receiving medical care. Also: patient zero (initial case), patient safety.

\medterm{Provider}-- Healthcare professional providing care. Physicians, nurses, therapists, pharmacists.

\medterm{Clinical Trial}-- See oncology.

\medterm{Evidence-Based Medicine}-- Practice informed by best available evidence. Combines clinical expertise, patient values, research evidence.

\medterm{Guideline}-- Recommended practice based on evidence. Examples: NICE, ACC/AHA, WHO guidelines.

\medterm{Standard of Care}-- Accepted medical practice for particular circumstances. Legal and professional benchmark.

\medterm{Malpractice}-- Professional negligence by healthcare provider. Requires duty, breach, causation, damages.

\medterm{Informed Consent}-- See informed consent.

\medterm{Privacy}-- Protection of patient information. HIPAA regulations in US. Confidentiality essential.

\medterm{Ethics}-- Moral principles guiding practice. Principles: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice.

\medterm{Advocacy}-- Acting on behalf of patient. Ensuring patient needs and preferences are addressed.

\medterm{Communication}-- Exchange of information between provider and patient. Essential for diagnosis, treatment, relationship.

\medterm{Documentation}-- Recording of patient encounters. Medical records, progress notes, discharge summaries. Legal and clinical importance.

\medterm{Quality Improvement}-- Systematic efforts to enhance care. Methods: PDSA cycles, root cause analysis, peer review.

\medterm{Patient Safety}-- Prevention of harm during care. Goals: reduce errors, improve systems, promote culture of safety.

\medterm{Medication Error}-- See toxicology.

\medterm{Adverse Event}-- Unintended harm from medical care. Includes medical errors and complications.

\medterm{Sentinel Event}-- Unexpected occurrence involving death or serious physical/psychological injury. Requires root cause analysis.

\medterm{Hospital-Acquired Infection}-- Infection acquired during hospitalization. Also: healthcare-associated infection (HAI). Examples: CLABSI, CAUTI, surgical site infection.

\medterm{Antimicrobial Resistance}-- Microorganisms becoming resistant to antibiotics. Major global health threat. Prevention: appropriate antibiotic use, infection control.

\medterm{Antibiotic Stewardship}-- Program to optimize antibiotic use. Improves outcomes, reduces resistance.

\medterm{Infection Control}-- See infection control.

\medterm{Hand Hygiene}-- Cleaning hands to prevent infection transmission. Most important precaution.

\medterm{Personal Protective Equipment}-- PPE: gloves, gowns, masks, eye protection. Used based on transmission-based precautions.

\medterm{Isolation Precautions}-- Measures to prevent spread of infection. Contact, droplet, airborne precautions.

\medterm{Vaccination}-- See vaccination.

\medterm{Immunization}-- See vaccination.

\medterm{Herd Immunity}-- Population-level protection when sufficient individuals immune. Reduces disease spread.

\medterm{Outbreak}-- Sudden increase in disease cases. Requires investigation and control measures.

\medterm{Pandemic}-- Worldwide epidemic. Examples: COVID-19, influenza pandemics.

\medterm{Surveillance}-- Ongoing collection, analysis, interpretation of health data. Used for public health action.

\medterm{Reporting}-- Mandatory notification of certain diseases to public health authorities.

\medterm{Quarantine}-- Separation of exposed individuals to prevent disease spread. For symptomatic incubation period.

\medterm{Isolation}-- Separation of sick individuals with contagious disease.

\medterm{Contact Tracing}-- Identifying and monitoring contacts of infected person. Important for outbreak control.

\medterm{Risk Assessment}-- Evaluation of likelihood and impact of health risks. Used for screening, prevention planning.

\medterm{Risk Factor}-- Attribute increasing disease probability. Examples: smoking, hypertension, obesity.

\medterm{Protective Factor}-- Attribute decreasing disease probability. Examples: vaccination, exercise, healthy diet.

\medterm{Prevalence}-- Proportion of population with disease at specific time. Measures disease burden.

\medterm{Incidence}-- See incidence.

\medterm{Mortality}-- Death from disease. Mortality rate: deaths per population per time.

\medterm{Morbidity}-- Disease or illness in population. Morbidity rate: new cases per population per time.

\medterm{Case Fatality Rate}-- Proportion of cases dying from disease. Measures severity.

\medterm{Survival Rate}-- See oncology.

\medterm{Life Expectancy}-- Average years remaining for person at given age. Varies by country, sex, socioeconomic status.

\medterm{Healthy Years Lost}-- Years of life lost due to premature death or disability. DALYs combine YLL and YLD.

\medterm{DALY}-- Disability-adjusted life year. Measure of disease burden.

\medterm{QALY}-- Quality-adjusted life year. Measure of health outcome incorporating quality of life.

\medterm{Cost-Effectiveness}-- Comparison of costs and outcomes of interventions. Used for resource allocation.

\medterm{Health Economics}-- Study of resource allocation in healthcare. Scarcity requires prioritization.

\medterm{Health Policy}-- Decisions, plans, and actions to achieve healthcare goals. Includes legislation, regulation, funding.

\medterm{Healthcare System}-- Organization providing healthcare services. Structures vary by country.

\medterm{Universal Health Coverage}-- All people receiving needed health services without financial hardship. WHO goal.

\medterm{Health Disparity}-- Difference in health outcomes between populations. Due to social, economic, environmental factors.

\medterm{Social Determinants of Health}-- Conditions in which people live affecting health. Include income, education, employment, housing, environment.

\medterm{Health Literacy}-- Ability to obtain, process, understand health information. Affects health outcomes.

\medterm{Patient Education}-- Teaching patients about health conditions and self-management. Improves adherence, outcomes.

\medterm{Counseling}-- Guidance to help patients make health decisions. Examples: smoking cessation, weight loss, medication adherence.

\medterm{Behavior Change}-- Modification of health-related behaviors. Theories: health belief model, transtheoretical model.

\medterm{Compliance}-- Following medical advice. Preferred term: adherence.

\medterm{Adherence}-- Extent to which patient follows treatment recommendations. Affected by factors: regimen complexity, side effects, beliefs, socioeconomic.

\medterm{Non-Adherence}-- Failure to follow treatment. Major cause of treatment failure.

\medterm{Placebo}-- Inactive substance used as control in trials. Placebo effect: improvement from expectation.

\medterm{Nocebo}-- Negative placebo effect. Adverse events from negative expectations.

\medterm{Blinding}-- Concealing treatment assignment in trials. Single-blind (patient), double-blind (patient and investigator).

\medterm{Randomization}-- Random assignment to treatment groups. Reduces bias.

\medterm{Control Group}-- Group not receiving experimental intervention. Used for comparison.

\medterm{Cohort Study}-- Observational study following group over time. Can be prospective or retrospective.

\medterm{Case-Control Study}-- Observational study comparing cases with disease to controls without. Looks back at exposures.

\medterm{Cross-Sectional Study}-- Observational study measuring prevalence at single time point.

\medterm{Randomized Controlled Trial}-- RCT: experimental study randomly assigning intervention. Gold standard for evidence.

\medterm{Meta-Analysis}-- Statistical combination of multiple studies. Increases power.

\medterm{Systematic Review}-- Comprehensive literature review following protocol. GRADE system for evidence quality.

\medterm{Bias}-- Systematic error in study design or analysis. Types: selection, information, confounding.

\medterm{Confounding}-- Distortion of association by third variable. Controlled by randomization, matching, statistical adjustment.

\medterm{Validity}-- Accuracy of measurement. Internal validity: study correctness. External validity: generalizability.

\medterm{Reliability}-- Consistency of measurement. Test-retest, inter-rater reliability.

\medterm{Sensitivity}-- See sensitivity.

\medterm{Specificity}-- Proportion of true negatives correctly identified. High specificity: few false positives. Useful for confirmation.

\medterm{Positive Predictive Value}-- Probability disease present given positive test. Affected by prevalence.

\medterm{Negative Predictive Value}-- Probability disease absent given negative test. Affected by prevalence.

\medterm{Likelihood Ratio}-- Probability of test result in disease vs. non-disease. LR+ >1 increases probability; LR- <1 decreases.

\medterm{Receiver Operating Characteristic}-- ROC curve: plot of sensitivity vs. 1-specificity. Area under curve measures test accuracy.

\medterm{Diagnostic Test}-- Procedure to identify disease. Examples: laboratory tests, imaging, biopsies.

\medterm{Screening Test}-- Test for asymptomatic disease. Should be safe, acceptable, accurate.

\medterm{Confirmatory Test}-- Test following positive screening to confirm diagnosis.

\medterm{Monitoring Test}-- Test to track disease progression or treatment response.

\medterm{Prognostic Test}-- Test predicting disease outcome.

\medterm{Predictive Test}-- Test predicting response to treatment.

\medterm{Genetic Testing}-- Analysis of DNA for disease risk or diagnosis. Examples: BRCA testing, carrier screening.

\medterm{Pharmacogenomics}-- Study of genetic variation affecting drug response. Personalizes medication selection.

\medterm{Biomarker}-- See biomarker.

\medterm{Biomonitoring}-- Measurement of substances in body. Examples: blood glucose, drug levels, toxicants.

\medterm{Laboratory Test}-- Analysis of body fluids or tissues. Includes hematology, chemistry, microbiology, immunology.

\medterm{Imaging}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Endoscopy}-- Visual examination of internal organs using scope. Examples: colonoscopy, gastroscopy, bronchoscopy.

\medterm{Pulse}-- Rhythmic expansion of artery with each heartbeat. Normal: 60-100 bpm. Rate, rhythm, amplitude assessed.

\medterm{Blood Pressure}-- Force of blood against vessel wall. Measured in mmHg. Systolic/diastolic. Normal: <120/80.

\medterm{Respiratory Rate}-- Number of breaths per minute. Normal: 12-20. Assessment of rate, rhythm, depth.

\medterm{Temperature}-- Measure of body heat. Normal: 36.5-37.5°C. Sites: oral, rectal, axillary, temporal.

\medterm{Oxygen Saturation}-- Percentage of hemoglobin bound with oxygen. Normal: 95-100%. Measured by pulse oximetry.

\medterm{Pain}-- Unpleasant sensory and emotional experience. Fifth vital sign. Assessment: location, intensity, character, timing.

\medterm{Symptom}-- Subjective experience of disease. Examples: pain, fatigue, nausea. Reported by patient.

\medterm{Sign}-- Objective evidence of disease. Examples: rash, murmur, edema. Observed by examiner.

\medterm{Syndrome}-- Group of symptoms and signs occurring together. Examples: metabolic syndrome, irritable bowel syndrome.

\medterm{Disease}-- Abnormal condition affecting body. Can be infectious, degenerative, neoplastic, etc.

\medterm{Illness}-- Subjective experience of being unwell. May not correspond to disease.

\medterm{Disorder}-- Functional abnormality. Less severe than disease. Examples: sleep disorder, eating disorder.

\medterm{Condition}-- General term for health status. Can be acute or chronic.

\medterm{Acute}-- Sudden onset, short duration. Examples: appendicitis, pneumonia.

\medterm{Chronic}-- Long-lasting, persistent. Examples: diabetes, hypertension.

\medterm{Subacute}-- Between acute and chronic. Examples: subacute thyroiditis.

\medterm{Recurrent}-- Disease returning after remission. Examples: recurrent UTI, herpes.

\medterm{Relapse}-- See recurrence.

\medterm{Remission}-- See oncology.

\medterm{Resolution}-- Complete healing or disappearance of disease.

\medterm{Sequelae}-- Conditions resulting from previous disease. Examples: post-polio syndrome, stroke sequelae.

\medterm{Complication}-- Unexpected problem during disease or treatment. Examples: surgical complication, drug complication.

\medterm{Comorbidity}-- See comorbidity.

\medterm{Concomitant}-- Occurring simultaneously. Concomitant medication: additional drug taken with primary treatment.

\medterm{Idiopathic}-- Of unknown cause. Examples: idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, idiopathic thrombocytopenia.

\medterm{Iatrogenic}-- See iatrogenic.

\medterm{Nosocomial}-- Hospital-acquired. Nosocomial infection: infection acquired in hospital.

\medterm{Community-Acquired}-- Obtained outside hospital. Examples: community-acquired pneumonia.

\medterm{Opportunistic Infection}-- Infection in immunocompromised host. Examples: PCP, toxoplasmosis, candidiasis.

\medterm{Infectious Disease}-- Disease caused by pathogen. Examples: bacterial, viral, fungal, parasitic.

\medterm{Non-Communicable Disease}-- Disease not transmitted between persons. Examples: cancer, heart disease, diabetes.

\medterm{Communicable Disease}-- Disease transmissible between persons. Examples: influenza, TB, HIV.

\medterm{Zoonosis}-- Disease transmitted from animals to humans. Examples: rabies, Lyme disease, avian influenza.

\medterm{Vector-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted by vector (insect, tick). Examples: malaria, dengue, Lyme disease.

\medterm{Airborne Disease}-- Disease transmitted by airborne particles. Examples: tuberculosis, measles, COVID-19.

\medterm{Blood-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted via blood. Examples: HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C.

\medterm{Sexually Transmitted Infection}-- STI: infection transmitted via sexual contact. Examples: syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, HIV.

\medterm{Water-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted via contaminated water. Examples: cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A.

\medterm{Food-Borne Disease}-- Disease from contaminated food. Examples: salmonella, E. coli, listeria.
