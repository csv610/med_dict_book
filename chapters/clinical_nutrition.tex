\chapter{Clinical Nutrition}

\medterm{Anorexia Nervosa}-- Restriction of energy intake leading to low body weight, fear of weight gain, body image disturbance. Medical complications: bradycardia, hypotension, osteoporosis, amenorrhea, refeeding syndrome.

\medterm{Anthropometry}-- Body measurements: weight, height, BMI, waist, hip, skinfold thickness. Assessment of nutritional status and body composition.

\medterm{Antioxidant}-- Molecule preventing oxidation damage. Vitamins C and E, beta-carotene, selenium, polyphenols. Diets rich in antioxidants associated with reduced chronic disease risk.

\medterm{Appetite Regulation}-- Complex control by hormones (leptin, ghrelin, insulin, CCK), neural signals, psychological factors. Leptin signals satiety, ghrelin signals hunger.

\medterm{Bariatric Surgery}-- Surgical intervention for obesity. Indications: BMI >= 40, or BMI >= 35 with comorbidities. Procedures: gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy, adjustable band, biliopancreatic diversion. Requires lifelong nutritional monitoring.

\medterm{Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)}-- Energy expenditure at rest for basic physiological functions. Accounts for 60-75% of total energy expenditure. Affected by age, sex, body composition, thyroid status, genetics. Measured by indirect calorimetry.

\medterm{Binge Eating Disorder}-- Recurrent binge eating without compensatory behaviors. Associated with obesity, metabolic syndrome.

\medterm{Biochemical Assessment}-- Blood tests: albumin, prealbumin, transferrin, electrolytes, vitamins, minerals, glucose, lipids. Reflect nutritional status and metabolic function.

\medterm{BMI (Body Mass Index)}-- Weight in kg divided by height in meters squared. Screening tool for weight categories: underweight (<18.5), normal (18.5-24.9), overweight (25-29.9), obese (>= 30). Limitations: does not distinguish muscle from fat, may misclassify athletes, elderly, certain ethnic groups.

\medterm{Bulimia Nervosa}-- Binge eating followed by compensatory behaviors (vomiting, laxatives, exercise). Medical complications: electrolyte imbalance, dental erosion, esophageal rupture.

\medterm{Calcium}-- Most abundant mineral. Bone health, muscle contraction, nerve transmission, blood clotting. Sources: dairy, leafy greens, fortified foods. Regulation: PTH, vitamin D, calcitonin. Deficiency: osteoporosis, osteomalacia, tetany.

\medterm{Caloric Restriction}-- Reduced calorie intake without malnutrition. Associated with longevity, reduced chronic disease risk. Requires careful planning.

\medterm{Calorie}-- Unit of energy. 1 calorie = energy to raise 1 gram water 1 degree C. Food calories actually kilocalories (kcal). Carbohydrate: 4 kcal/g, protein: 4 kcal/g, fat: 9 kcal/g, alcohol: 7 kcal/g.

\medterm{Cancer Nutrition}-- Malnutrition common in cancer. Hypermetabolism, cachexia, treatment side effects. Nutritional support improves outcomes. High protein, high calorie.

\medterm{Carbohydrate Counting}-- Method for diabetes management. Matching insulin to carbohydrate intake. Education essential.

\medterm{Celiac Disease}-- Gluten-sensitive enteropathy. See \hyperlink{Gastroenterology}{gastroenterology}.

\medterm{Chylomicron}-- Transports dietary lipids. See biochemistry.

\medterm{Cultural Food Practices}-- Food preferences, restrictions, traditions vary by culture. Cultural competence in nutrition counseling important.

\medterm{Daily Value}-- Nutrition labeling reference values. Based on 2000 calorie diet. Useful for comparing products.

\medterm{DASH Diet}-- Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. Emphasizes: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy, reduced saturated fat, sodium <2300 mg/day. Lowers blood pressure effectively.

\medterm{Dehydration}-- Deficit of total body water. Hypertonic (water loss > sodium), isotonic (loss proportional), hypotonic (sodium loss > water). Causes: vomiting, diarrhea, fever, inadequate intake, diabetes insipidus. Treatment: oral rehydration or IV fluids.

\medterm{Diabetes Mellitus}-- A metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from insufficient insulin production, insulin resistance, or both. See \hyperlink{Endocrinology}{endocrinology}.

\medterm{Diabetic Diet}-- Carbohydrate counting, glycemic index consideration, balanced meals. Medical nutrition therapy improves glycemic control.

\medterm{Dietary Assessment}-- Methods: 24-hour recall, food frequency questionnaire, food diary. Evaluation of intake adequacy, patterns, preferences.

\medterm{Dietary Reference Intakes}-- Reference values for nutrient intake: RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance), AI (Adequate Intake), UL (Tolerable Upper Intake Level), EAR (Estimated Average Requirement).

\medterm{Eating Disorder}-- A group of conditions characterized by abnormal eating habits and distorted body image, including anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder, and avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID). See psychiatry.

\medterm{Edema}-- Fluid accumulation in tissues. Causes: heart failure, renal disease, liver cirrhosis, venous insufficiency, hypoalbuminemia, lymphatic obstruction. Management: treat underlying cause, diuretics, compression, sodium restriction.

\medterm{Electrolyte Balance}-- Homeostasis of sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, phosphate. Regulated by kidneys, hormones (aldosterone, ADH, PTH). Imbalance causes neuromuscular, cardiac, metabolic dysfunction.

\medterm{Emotional Eating}-- Eating in response to emotions rather than hunger. Stress, boredom, sadness triggers. Cognitive behavioral therapy may help.

\medterm{Empty Calories}-- Foods providing energy but few nutrients. Sugary drinks, candies, alcohol. Contribute to obesity, nutrient deficiency.

\medterm{Enrichment}-- Restoration of nutrients lost during processing. Examples: enriched flour, restored B vitamins.

\medterm{Enteral Nutrition}-- Delivery of nutrients via gastrointestinal tract. Routes: nasogastric, nasoduodenal, gastrostomy (PEG), jejunostomy. Indications: dysphagia, neurological disease, head/neck cancer, prolonged inability to eat. Complications: aspiration, diarrhea, tube displacement, sinusitis.

\medterm{Eosinophilic Esophagitis}-- Chronic immune-mediated condition characterized by dense eosinophilic infiltration of the esophageal mucosa defined as fifteen or more eosinophils per high-power field, in the absence of other causes of esophageal eosinophilia. See \hyperlink{Gastroenterology}{gastroenterology}.

\medterm{Essential Nutrient}-- Nutrient that cannot be synthesized by body, must be obtained from diet. Essential amino acids, fatty acids, vitamins, minerals.

\medterm{Fatty Acid}-- See biochemistry.

\medterm{Fiber}-- Indigestible carbohydrate from plant cell walls. Soluble (oats, beans, apples) lowers cholesterol, blood glucose. Insoluble (whole grains, vegetables) adds bulk, prevents constipation. Recommended: 25-30 g/day.

\medterm{FODMAP Diet}-- Restriction of Fermentable Oligo-, Di-, Mono-saccharides And Polyols. Used for IBS management. Low FODMAP diet followed by gradual reintroduction.

\medterm{Food Allergy}-- Immune-mediated reaction to food protein. IgE-mediated: anaphylaxis. Non-IgE: EoE, food protein-induced enterocolitis. Common allergens: milk, egg, peanut, tree nut, fish, shellfish, soy, wheat.

\medterm{Food Deserts}-- Areas with limited access to affordable, healthy food. Urban and rural disparities.

\medterm{Food Intolerance}-- Adverse reaction not immune-mediated. Lactose intolerance, gluten sensitivity (non-celiac), histamine intolerance, FODMAP sensitivity. Diagnosis: elimination diet, challenge.

\medterm{Food Security}-- Access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food. Food insecurity associated with poor nutrition, health disparities.

\medterm{Fortification}-- Addition of nutrients to food. Examples: iodized salt, vitamin D milk, folic acid grains. Public health strategy for deficiency prevention.

\medterm{Free Radical}-- Molecule with unpaired electron. See biochemistry.

\medterm{Fructose Malabsorption}-- Poor absorption of fructose leading to GI symptoms. Fermentation by bacteria produces gas, osmotic diarrhea. Low-fructose diet beneficial.

\medterm{Functional Food}-- Food with potentially health benefits beyond basic nutrition. Examples: probiotics, omega-3 fortified foods, plant sterols.

\medterm{Gastric Bypass}-- Surgical weight loss procedure. Roux-en-Y gastric bypass creates small stomach pouch and bypasses part of small intestine. Causes restriction and malabsorption. Risks: nutritional deficiencies, dumping syndrome, ulcers.

\medterm{Gluten-Free Diet}-- Eliminates gluten (wheat, barley, rye). Essential for celiac disease, non-celiac gluten sensitivity. Risk of nutrient deficiency if not supplemented. May be unnecessarily restrictive for others.

\medterm{Glycemic Index}-- Ranking of carbohydrates by effect on blood glucose. High GI foods spike glucose. Low GI foods provide sustained energy. Useful for diabetes management.

\medterm{Glycemic Load}-- Combines glycemic index with carbohydrate amount. More practical measure of blood glucose impact.

\medterm{GMO Food}-- Genetically modified organisms. Safety established by major scientific organizations. Labeling controversy.

\medterm{Gut Microbiome}-- Trillion microorganisms in digestive tract. Influences immunity, metabolism, mental health. Affected by diet, antibiotics, stress, birth mode. Diversity associated with health.

\medterm{HIV/AIDS Nutrition}-- Malnutrition due to increasedneedrequirement, malabsorption, side effects. Supplementation, opportunistic infection management.

\medterm{Hunger}-- Physiological drive to eat. Regulated by ghrelin, blood glucose, stomach distension. Psychological factors also influence.

\medterm{Hyperkalemia}-- Serum potassium >5.0 mEq/L. Causes: renal failure, ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics, tissue breakdown. Symptoms: muscle weakness, arrhythmias, ECG changes (peaked T waves). Treatment: calcium gluconate (cardioprotection), insulin + glucose, albuterol, kayexalate, dialysis.

\medterm{Hypernatremia}-- Serum sodium >145 mEq/L. Causes: water loss, diabetes insipidus, inadequate intake. Symptoms: thirst, confusion, seizures. Treatment: gradual correction with hypotonic fluids.

\medterm{Hypokalemia}-- Serum potassium <3.5 mEq/L. Causes: diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea, hyperaldosteronism. Symptoms: weakness, cramps, arrhythmias, ECG changes (U waves). Treatment: oral or IV potassium replacement.

\medterm{Hyponatremia}-- Serum sodium <135 mEq/L. Causes: SIADH, heart failure, cirrhosis, renal disease, diuretics, excessive water intake. Symptoms: nausea, headache, confusion, seizures. Treatment: fluid restriction, hypertonic saline (severe).

\medterm{Inflammation (Dietary)}-- Chronic low-grade inflammation linked to diet. Western diet pro-inflammatory (saturated fat, sugar, refined grains). Mediterranean diet anti-inflammatory.

\medterm{Inflammatory Bowel Disease Diet}-- Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis. Nutritional support during flares. Exclusive enteral nutrition for Crohn induction. Individual triggers vary.

\medterm{Intermittent Fasting}-- Cycling between fasting and eating periods. Methods: alternate-day fasting, 5:2 diet, time-restricted eating. Potential benefits: weight loss, metabolic health. Evidence emerging.

\medterm{Iodine}-- Required for thyroid hormones. Deficiency: goiter, hypothyroidism, cretinism. Prevention: iodized salt, seafood. Excess can cause thyroid dysfunction.

\medterm{Iron}-- Essential for hemoglobin, myoglobin, electron transport. Heme iron (animal) better absorbed than non-heme (plant). Deficiency: most common nutritional deficiency worldwide. Causes: blood loss, increasedneedrequirement, poor intake. Diagnosis: low ferritin, low iron, high TIBC. Treatment: oral iron, IV iron, treat underlying cause.

\medterm{Irritable Bowel Syndrome Diet}-- See gastroenterology. Low FODMAP diet effective for symptom control.

\medterm{Ketogenic Diet}-- High fat, adequate protein, very low carbohydrate diet (<50g/day). Induces ketosis. Medical uses: epilepsy, obesity, metabolic syndrome. Risks: nutrient deficiency, kidney stones, dyslipidemia.

\medterm{Ketosis}-- Metabolic state with elevated ketone bodies. Physiological: fasting, ketogenic diet. Pathological: diabetic ketoacidosis. Distinguish from ketonemia vs ketonuria.

\medterm{Lactose Intolerance}-- Inability to digest lactose due to lactase deficiency. Symptoms: bloating, diarrhea, gas after dairy. Common in adults, especially Asian, African, Hispanic descent. Treatment: lactose restriction, lactase enzyme, calcium supplementation.

\medterm{Liver Disease Diet}-- Protein restriction in hepatic encephalopathy. Sodium restriction in cirrhosis with ascites. Vitamin supplementation common.

\medterm{Macrobiotics}-- Diet emphasizing whole grains, vegetables, beans. May be nutritionally inadequate if restrictive. Long-term followers generally healthy but may have deficiencies.

\medterm{Macronutrients}-- Nutrients needed in large amounts: carbohydrates, protein, fat. Provide energy and structural components.

\medterm{Magnesium}-- Cofactor for 300+ enzymes, muscle and nerve function, blood glucose control, blood pressure regulation. Deficiency: muscle cramps, arrhythmias, seizures. Risk: alcoholism, diabetes, GI losses.

\medterm{Malabsorption}-- Impaired nutrient absorption from GI tract. Causes: celiac disease, Crohn disease, pancreatic insufficiency, short bowel syndrome, bacterial overgrowth. Symptoms: diarrhea, weight loss, nutritional deficiencies. Diagnosis: stool fat, blood tests, endoscopy, breath tests.

\medterm{Malnutrition}-- Deficiency, excess, or imbalance of nutrients leading to adverse effects on tissue, body form, and function. Undernutrition: protein-energy malnutrition, micronutrient deficiencies. Overnutrition: overweight, obesity, related comorbidities. Screen with MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment), MUST (Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool).

\medterm{Medical Nutrition Therapy}-- Individualized nutrition intervention by registered dietitian. Conditions: diabetes, renal disease, GI disorders, cancer, obesity.

\medterm{Mediterranean Diet}-- Traditional eating pattern of Greece, Southern Italy. Emphasizes: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, olive oil, moderate fish/poultry, limited red meat. Associated with reduced cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cognitive decline.

\medterm{Metabolic Rate}-- See basal metabolic rate.

\medterm{Metabolic Syndrome}-- Cluster of conditions: abdominal obesity, hypertension, hyperglycemia, high triglycerides, low HDL. Increases cardiovascular disease, diabetes risk.

\medterm{Micronutrients}-- Vitamins and minerals required in small amounts. Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) vs. water-soluble (B complex, C). Deficiency causes specific syndromes. Toxicity possible with supplements.

\medterm{Micronutrients}-- Nutrients needed in small amounts: vitamins, minerals. Essential for enzymatic, hormonal, structural functions.

\medterm{Mindful Eating}-- Paying attention to hunger cues, eating slowly, savoring food. May reduce overeating, improve digestion.

\medterm{Monounsaturated Fat}-- Liquid at room temperature. Sources: olive oil, avocado, nuts. Cardiovascular benefits.

\medterm{Night Eating Syndrome}-- Delayed circadian pattern of eating. Evening hyperphagia, morning anorexia, insomnia. Associated with obesity, depression.

\medterm{Non-Celiac Gluten Sensitivity}-- Symptom improvement with gluten-free diet without celiac disease or wheat allergy. Diagnosis of exclusion.

\medterm{Nutrient Density}-- Amount of nutrients per calorie. Whole foods more nutrient-dense than processed foods. Importance for weight management, health.

\medterm{Nutrition Education}-- Teaching patients about healthy eating. Cultural sensitivity, health literacy appropriate. Motivational interviewing effective.

\medterm{Nutritional Assessment}-- Comprehensive evaluation: dietary intake, anthropometrics, biochemical tests, clinical examination. Dietitian involvement essential.

\medterm{Nutritional Screening}-- Quick assessment of malnutrition risk. Tools: MNA, MUST, NRS-2002. Identified at-risk patients receive comprehensive assessment.

\medterm{Obesity}-- Excess body fat defined as BMI >= 30 kg/m2. Classes: I (30-34.9), II (35-39.9), III (>= 40). Complications: diabetes, cardiovascular disease, sleep apnea, osteoarthritis, certain cancers. Treatment: diet, exercise, behavioral therapy, pharmacotherapy, bariatric surgery.

\medterm{Omega-3}-- Polyunsaturated fatty acids (EPA, DHA, ALA). Anti-inflammatory, cardiovascular benefits. Sources: fatty fish, flaxseed, walnuts. Supplementation common.

\medterm{Omega-6}-- Polyunsaturated fatty acids (linoleic acid). Pro-inflammatory in excess. Balance with omega-3 important. Sources: vegetable oils, nuts, seeds.

\medterm{Organic Food}-- Produced without synthetic pesticides, fertilizers, GMOs, antibiotics, growth hormones. Nutritional equivalence debated. Price premium.

\medterm{Orthorexia}-- Obsession with healthy eating. May lead to nutritional deficiency, social isolation.

\medterm{Pancreatic Insufficiency}-- See gastroenterology. Pancreatic enzyme replacement with meals. Fat-soluble vitamin supplementation.

\medterm{Parenteral Nutrition}-- Intravenous nutrition bypassing GI tract. Total parenteral nutrition (TPN) provides complete nutrition. Indications: bowel failure, short bowel syndrome, prolonged NPO, malabsorption. Risks: infection, liver dysfunction, metabolic complications, line complications.

\medterm{Phospholipid}-- Lipid containing phosphate group, major component of cell membranes. See biochemistry.

\medterm{Pica}-- Compulsive eating of non-nutritive substances (ice, dirt, clay). Associated with iron deficiency, pregnancy, developmental disorders.

\medterm{Plate Method}-- Simple portion control: half plate vegetables, quarter plate protein, quarter plate grains. Promotes balanced eating.

\medterm{Polyunsaturated Fat}-- Liquid at room temperature. Sources: fish, vegetable oils, nuts. Essential fatty acids.

\medterm{Portion Control}-- Managing serving sizes. Larger portions available today contribute to obesity. Using smaller plates, reading labels helpful.

\medterm{Potassium}-- Major intracellular cation. Essential for cardiac function, muscle contraction, blood pressure regulation. Deficiency: hypokalemia (weakness, arrhythmias). Sources: fruits, vegetables, potatoes, beans.

\medterm{Prebiotics}-- Non-digestible fibers feeding beneficial gut bacteria. Sources: garlic, onions, leeks, asparagus, bananas, whole grains.

\medterm{Probiotics}-- Live microorganisms conferring health benefit. Strains: Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium. Used for antibiotic-associated diarrhea, IBS, IBD. Evidence mixed.

\medterm{Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)}-- Severe deficiency of protein and calories. Types: marasmus (chronic, wasting), kwashiorkor (acute, edema, fatty liver), marasmic-kwashiorkor. Risk: famine, poverty, alcoholism, malignancy, malabsorption. Treatment: gradual refeeding, monitor refeeding syndrome.

\medterm{Refeeding Syndrome}-- Life-threatening fluid and electrolyte shifts when nutrition restarted in malnourished patient. Hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, fluid overload, cardiac failure. Prevention: start low (25-30 kcal/kg), advance slowly, supplement electrolytes, monitor closely.

\medterm{Renal Diet}-- Restricted protein, sodium, potassium, phosphorus for kidney disease. Individualized based on stage. Dialysis increases proteinneedrequirement.

\medterm{Sarcopenia}-- Age-related loss of muscle mass and function. Contributing factors: decreased protein intake, reduced physical activity, hormonal changes. Screening: grip strength, gait speed, calf circumference. Treatment: protein supplementation, resistance exercise.

\medterm{Satiety}-- Feeling of fullness after eating. Affected by meal composition, volume, fiber, protein. Satiety hormones: CCK, PYY, GLP-1.

\medterm{Saturated Fat}-- Solid at room temperature. Sources: meat, dairy, coconut oil, palm oil. Controversial cardiovascular effects. Current guidelines limit intake.

\medterm{Selenium}-- Trace mineral, component of antioxidant enzymes. Deficiency: Keshan disease (cardiomyopathy), Kashin-Beck disease (osteoarthropathy). Sources: Brazil nuts, seafood, meat.

\medterm{Short Bowel Syndrome}-- Malabsorption after extensive small bowel resection. Nutritional support: enteral, parenteral. Adaptation over time. Transplantation considered.

\medterm{Sleeve Gastrectomy}-- Removal ofmajor stomach, leaving sleeve. Restrictive procedure. Lower malabsorption risk than bypass. Effective weight loss.

\medterm{Sodium}-- Major extracellular cation. Fluid balance, nerve function, muscle contraction. Recommended intake <2300 mg/day. Excess associated with hypertension. Sources: salt, processed foods.

\medterm{Supplement Safety}-- Supplements not strictly regulated. Contamination, mislabeling risks. Consult healthcare provider before use.
\medterm{Supplementation}-- Dietary supplements for nutrient deficiency or increasedneedrequirement. Vitamins, minerals, protein powders, fiber. Risk of toxicity with fat-soluble vitamins, heavy metal contamination.

\medterm{Thermogenesis}-- Heat production from food digestion, muscle activity, brown fat. Diet-induced thermogenesis ~10% of TDEE.

\medterm{Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)}-- Total calories burned in 24 hours: BMR + thermic effect of food + physical activity. Estimation guides dietary recommendations. Activity factor multiplies BMR.

\medterm{Trans Fat}-- Artificially hydrogenated fat. Increases cardiovascular risk. Banned in many countries. Read labels.

\medterm{Triglyceride}-- See biochemistry.

\medterm{Underweight}-- BMI < 18.5 kg/m2. Associated with osteoporosis, infertility, anemia, weakened immunity, surgical complications. Causes: hypermetabolism, malabsorption, eating disorders, malignancy, chronic infection.

\medterm{Vegan Diet}-- Excludes all animal products. Risk of deficiencies: vitamin B12, iron, calcium, vitamin D, omega-3, zinc, iodine. Requires planning or supplementation. Associated with lower BMI, reduced cardiovascular risk.

\medterm{Vegetarian Diet}-- Excludes meat, fish, poultry. Lacto-ovo includes dairy and eggs. Vegan excludes all animal products. Nutrient adequacy requires attention.

\medterm{Vitamin A}-- Fat-soluble vitamin. Functions: vision (rhodopsin), immunity, reproduction, cellular differentiation. Sources: liver, dairy, orange vegetables (beta-carotene). Deficiency: night blindness, xerophthalmia. Toxicity: teratogenic, liver damage.

\medterm{Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)}-- Water-soluble vitamin, coenzyme in carbohydrate metabolism. Deficiency: beriberi (wet, dry), Wernicke-Korsakoff. Risk: alcoholism, bariatric surgery, dialysis. Treatment: supplementation.

\medterm{Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)}-- Required for nerve function, red blood cell formation, DNA synthesis. Deficiency: megaloblastic anemia, neurological damage. Risk: vegans, elderly, pernicious anemia, metformin use, bariatric surgery. Requires intrinsic factor for absorption.

\medterm{Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)}-- Precursor for FAD and FMN. Deficiency: cheilosis, glossitis, seborrheic dermatitis. Common with B-complex deficiency.

\medterm{Vitamin B3 (Niacin)}-- Precursor for NAD+ and NADP+. Deficiency: pellagra (diarrhea, dermatitis, dementia). Sources: meat, fish, poultry, grains.

\medterm{Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)}-- Component of coenzyme A. Deficiency rare. Involved in fatty acid metabolism.

\medterm{Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)}-- Coenzyme in amino acid metabolism. Deficiency: seizures, anemia, dermatitis. Risk: isoniazid therapy, alcoholism.

\medterm{Vitamin B7 (Biotin)}-- Coenzyme for carboxylation reactions. Deficiency rare, may occur with raw egg consumption (avidin binds biotin).

\medterm{Vitamin B9 (Folate)}-- Essential for DNA synthesis, red blood cell formation. Deficiency: megaloblastic anemia, neural tube defects in pregnancy. Sources: leafy greens, legumes, fortified grains. Supplementation recommended before and during pregnancy.

\medterm{Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)}-- Water-soluble antioxidant, collagen synthesis, immune function. Deficiency: scurvy (bleeding gums, petechiae, poor wound healing). Sources: citrus, berries, peppers, broccoli. Smokers require additional intake.

\medterm{Vitamin D}-- Fat-soluble vitamin, calcium-phosphate homeostasis, immune modulation. Sources: sunlight, fatty fish, fortified dairy. Deficiency: rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults), osteoporosis. Risk: limited sun exposure, dark skin, elderly, malabsorption. Supplementation recommended.

\medterm{Vitamin E}-- Fat-soluble antioxidant. Deficiency rare. Hemolytic anemia in prematurity. Sources: nuts, seeds, vegetable oils.

\medterm{Vitamin K}-- Fat-soluble vitamin for clotting factors II, VII, IX, X. Gut bacteria produce K2. Deficiency: bleeding, especially in newborns (prophylaxis). Sources: leafy greens (K1), fermented foods (K2). Antibiotics can cause deficiency.

\medterm{Waist Circumference}-- Measure of central obesity. Thresholds: men >102 cm, women >88 cm (increased risk). Better predictor of metabolic risk than BMI. Waist-to-hip ratio also used.

\medterm{Water}-- Essential for life. Adult requirements: ~2-3 L/day. Dehydration: lethargy, tachycardia, hypotension, confusion. Elderly at risk due to decreased thirst sensation.

\medterm{Zinc}-- Trace mineral for enzymatic function, immunity, wound healing, taste. Deficiency: growth retardation, hypogonadism, impaired immunity, dysgeusia. Sources: meat, shellfish, legumes, nuts.
