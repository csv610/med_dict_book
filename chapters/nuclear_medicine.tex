\chapter{Nuclear Medicine & Molecular Imaging}

\medterm{Biological Half-Life}-- Time required for the human body to eliminate half the administered quantity of a drug or radiopharmaceutical through metabolic and excretory processes. Different from physical half-life (radioactive decay) and effective half-life (combined biological and physical elimination). Formula: 1/T_effective = 1/T_physical + 1/T_biological.

\medterm{Fludeoxyglucose F-18 (FDG)}-- Radiotracer used in PET imaging, analog of glucose labeled with fluorine-18. Taken up by cells via glucose transporters and phosphorylated by hexokinase, trapped intracellularly. Uptake reflects metabolic activity. Used for oncology (tumor detection, staging, response assessment), neurology (epilepsy, dementia), cardiology (viability). Standardized uptake value (SUV) quantifies uptake.

\medterm{Positron Emission Tomography (PET)}-- Functional imaging modality using positron-emitting radionuclides. Common isotope: fluorine-18 (110 min half-life). Detection: annihilation photons (511 keV) detected in coincidence. Tracers: FDG (metabolism), florbetapir (amyloid), flortaucipir (tau), PSMA (prostate cancer). Combined with CT (PET/CT) or MRI (PET/MRI) for anatomical correlation.

\medterm{Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography (SPECT)}-- Nuclear medicine imaging using gamma-emitting isotopes (technetium-99m most common). Rotating gamma camera acquires images. Applications: myocardial perfusion, bone scans, thyroid imaging, renal scans, brain perfusion. Lower resolution than PET but more widely available and cost-effective.

\medterm{Technetium-99m}-- Most widely used radionuclide in nuclear medicine. Half-life: 6 hours. Emits 140 keV gamma rays. Produced from molybdenum-99/technetium-99m generator. Used with various ligands: MDP (bone), sestamibi (myocardium), DTPA (renal), nanocolloid (lymphatic), HMPAO (brain perfusion).

\medterm{Radioimmunoassay (RIA)}-- Sensitive immunoassay using radiolabeled antigens or antibodies. Competitive binding principle. Used for hormone measurement (thyroid, reproductive), tumor markers, drug levels. Largely replaced by non-radioactive methods (ELISA) but still used for some assays.

\medterm{Theranostics}-- Combined therapy and diagnostics using matched radiopharmaceutical pairs. Diagnostic isotope (e.g., Ga-68) identifies target, therapeutic isotope (e.g., Lu-177) delivers treatment. Examples: Ga-68/Lu-177 DOTATATE (neuroendocrine tumors), PSMA theranostics (prostate cancer).

\medterm{Radioactive Iodine (I-131)}-- Used for thyroid imaging and treatment. I-131 emits beta particles (therapy) and gamma rays (imaging). Half-life: 8 days. Used for hyperthyroidism (Graves, toxic nodules) and thyroid cancer ablation. I-123 for diagnostics (shorter half-life, pure gamma).

\medterm{Bone Scan}-- Skeletal imaging using Tc-99m MDP. Detects osteoblastic activity. Sensitivity: high; specificity: low. Used for metastasis detection, fracture, infection, metabolic bone disease. "Hot spots" indicate increased uptake; "cold spots" indicate decreased uptake.

\medterm{Myocardial Perfusion Imaging}-- Assessment of blood flow to heart muscle. Tracers: Tc-99m sestamibi, Tc-99m tetrofosmin, thallium-201. Stress test (exercise or pharmacological) followed by rest imaging. Defects indicate ischemia (reversible) or infarction (fixed).

\medterm{Renal Scintigraphy}-- Renal imaging using Tc-99m compounds. MAG3 (tubular secretion) for functional assessment, DTPA (glomerular filtration) for GFR measurement. Used for obstruction, function, transplant monitoring, renin-mediated hypertension.

\medterm{Ventilation/Perfusion (V/Q) Scan}-- Pulmonary embolism diagnosis. Ventilation: inhaled Tc-99m DTPA aerosol. Perfusion: injected Tc-99m MAA. Mismatch (perfusion defect with preserved ventilation) suggests PE. High single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) sensitivity.

\medterm{Thyroid Scintigraphy}-- Thyroid imaging using I-123, I-131, or Tc-99m pertechnetate. Assesses function, nodules, ectopic tissue. Hot nodules (functioning) rarely malignant; cold nodules require ultrasound/FNA.

\medterm{Hepatobiliary Scintigraphy}-- Liver/gallbladder imaging using Tc-99m mebrofenin or disofenin. Assess biliary patency, gallbladder function, bile leak, acute cholecystitis (non-visualization of gallbladder).

\medterm{Gastrointestinal Bleeding Scan}-- Detection of GI bleeding using Tc-99m labeled RBCs. Sensitive for active bleeding (>0.1 mL/min). Localizes bleeding site. Alternative: tagged RBC scan, CT angiography.

\medterm{Salivary Gland Scintigraphy}-- Salivary gland function assessment using Tc-99m pertechnetate. Used for Sjogren syndrome, obstruction, radiation sialadenitis.

\medterm{Lymphoscintigraphy}-- Lymphatic system imaging using Tc-99m nanocolloid injected near lesion. Used for sentinel lymph node biopsy (melanoma, breast cancer), lymphedema evaluation.

\medterm{Cardiac SPECT}-- Myocardial perfusion imaging using SPECT. Quantifies ischemia, infarction, viability. Stress-rest protocol. gated SPECT assesses ventricular function.

\medterm{Brain SPECT}-- Cerebral perfusion imaging using Tc-99m HMPAO or ECD. Used for epilepsy localization, dementia differentiation, cerebrovascular disease.

\medterm{Theranostic Pair}-- See theranostics.

\medterm{Radionuclide Therapy}-- Internal radiation therapy using radiopharmaceuticals. Examples: I-131 (thyroid), Lu-177 DOTATATE (neuroendocrine tumors), Lu-177 PSMA (prostate cancer), Ra-223 (bone metastases), Y-90 microspheres (liver cancer).

\medterm{Radiation Safety}-- Principles: time, distance, shielding. ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable). Radioactive materials handled in designated areas. Waste disposal per regulations. Pregnancy screening for women of childbearing age.

\medterm{PET/CT}-- Combined PET and CT imaging. CT provides anatomical localization and attenuation correction. Essential for accurate interpretation. Low-dose CT for attenuation correction; diagnostic CT for detailed anatomy.

\medterm{PET/MRI}-- Combined PET and MRI. Superior soft tissue contrast, no ionizing radiation from CT. Useful for neurology, oncology, cardiology. Longer scan time, higher cost.

\medterm{Standardized Uptake Value (SUV)}-- Quantitative PET measure: tissue activity divided by injected dose per body weight. Normalizes for body size, injection dose. SUVmax: maximum pixel value in lesion. SUVmean: average lesion uptake. Background: blood pool, liver.

\medterm{Half-life (Physical)}-- Time for radioactive isotope to decay to half its original activity. F-18: 110 min; Tc-99m: 6 hours; I-131: 8 days; Ga-68: 68 min. Affects imaging timing, radiation dose.

\medterm{Radiopharmaceutical}-- Radioactive compound used for diagnostic or therapeutic purposes. Components: radionuclide + targeting molecule. Selectivity determines biodistribution and image quality.

\medterm{Auger Electrons}-- Low-energy electrons emitted during electron capture decay. Short range (nanometers), high linear energy transfer. Potential for targeted radiotherapy if localized near DNA.

\medterm{Bremsstrahlung}-- Braking radiation produced when beta particles decelerate in matter. Continuous spectrum. Detected in I-131 imaging (poor quality). Used for Y-90 bremsstrahlung SPECT.

\medterm{Coincidence Detection}-- Principle of PET imaging. Two 511 keV photons detected simultaneously (within nanoseconds) by opposing detectors. Line of response defined. Time-of-flight PET improves localization.

\medterm{Decay Correction}-- Mathematical adjustment for radioactive decay during imaging. Essential for quantitative comparison between studies.

\medterm{Energy Window}-- Range of photon energies accepted by gamma camera. Typically 20% centered on photopeak. Reduces scatter, improves image quality.

\medterm{Image Reconstruction}-- Mathematical process converting projection data to cross-sectional images. Iterative methods (OSEM) superior to filtered back projection. Corrects for attenuation, scatter, resolution.

\medterm{Isotope}-- See radionuclide.

\medterm{Mammography}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Nanocolloid}-- Tc-99m labeled colloidal particles for lymphatic imaging. Particle size 10-100 nm. Used for sentinel node detection.

\medterm{Positron}-- Anti-particle of electron. Emitted by PET radionuclides. Annihilation with electron produces two 511 keV photons.

\medterm{Quantification}-- Measurement of radiotracer uptake or clearance. SUV (PET), clearance rates (renal), ejection fraction (cardiac). Essential for treatment monitoring.

\medterm{Resolution}-- Ability to distinguish small details. PET: 4-6 mm; SPECT: 8-12 mm; CT: sub-mm. Limited by physics (positron range, non-collinearity).

\medterm{Sensitivity}-- Ability to detect small amounts of tracer. PET more sensitive than SPECT. Affected by detector efficiency, acquisition time.

\medterm{Specific Activity}-- Radioactivity per unit mass of compound. High specific activity essential for receptor targeting (low receptor density).

\medterm{Tracer Principle}-- Administered in amounts too small to produce pharmacological effect. Dose optimized for imaging, not therapeutic.

\medterm{Uptake}-- Cellular accumulation of radiotracer. Depends on blood flow, membrane transport, metabolism, binding.

\medterm{Whole-Body Scan}-- Acquisition of images covering entire body. Used for oncology staging, infection localization. PET/CT typically neck-to-mid-thigh or full body.

\medterm{X-ray Attenuation}-- See CT.

\medterm{Fluorine-18}-- PET radionuclide, half-life 110 minutes. Produced in cyclotron via O-18(p,n)F-18 reaction. Used in FDG and other tracers. Requires on-site or nearby cyclotron due to short half-life.

\medterm{Gallium-68}-- PET radionuclide, half-life 68 minutes. Produced from Ge-68/Ga-68 generator. Used in DOTATATE, PSMA, and other theranostic applications.

\medterm{Indium-111}-- SPECT radionuclide, half-life 2.8 days. Used in WBC labeling, octreotide scanning. Emits gamma rays at 171 and 245 keV.

\medterm{Iodine-123}-- Pure gamma emitter, half-life 13 hours. Used for thyroid imaging. No beta emission, lower radiation dose than I-131.

\medterm{Iodine-131}-- See radioactive iodine.

\medterm{Molybdenum-99}-- Parent isotope of Tc-99m, half-life 66 hours. Produced in nuclear reactors. Generator systems provide Tc-99m for 1 week.

\medterm{Thallium-201}-- SPECT radionuclide, half-life 73 hours. Used for myocardial perfusion imaging. Mechanism: K+ analog, taken up by Na+/K+ ATPase.

\medterm{Yttrium-90}-- Beta emitter, half-life 64 hours. Used in radioembolization (Y-90 microspheres) for liver cancer. Bremsstrahlung imaging possible.

\medterm{Lutetium-177}-- Beta and gamma emitter, half-life 6.7 days. Used in peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) and PSMA therapy. Both therapeutic and imaging capabilities.

\medterm{Radium-223}-- Alpha emitter, half-life 11.4 days. Used for bone metastases from prostate cancer. Mimics calcium, targets bone turnover sites.

\medterm{Cobalt-57}-- Calibration source for gamma cameras. Half-life 271 days. Used for quality control.

\medterm{Barium-133}-- Calibration source. Half-life 10.5 years. Used for energy calibration.

\medterm{Selenium-75}-- Calibration source. Half-life 119 days. Used for uniformity correction.

\medterm{Phantom}-- Device simulating human anatomy for quality control and calibration. Types: line source, uniform, Jaszczak, NEMA IU body phantom.

\medterm{Gamma Camera}-- Imaging device using NaI(Tl) scintillation crystal, photomultiplier tubes, collimator. Detects gamma rays from radiopharmaceuticals. Anger camera principle.

\medterm{Collimator}-- Lead device with holes to define gamma ray direction. Types: parallel-hole, pinhole, converging, diverging. Resolution vs. sensitivity trade-off.

\medterm{Scintillation Crystal}-- Sodium iodide doped with thallium (NaI:Tl) crystal converts gamma rays to light photons. Coupled to photomultiplier tubes.

\medterm{Photomultiplier Tube}-- Converts light photons to electrical signals. Array of PMTs positioned behind crystal. Positioning circuit determines interaction location.

\medterm{PET Scanner}-- Ring of detectors capturing coincidence events. BGO, LSO, or LYSO crystals. Time-of-flight PET improves signal-to-noise.

\medterm{Cycle Time}-- Time for complete image acquisition. PET: minutes. SPECT: 20-30 minutes.

\medterm{Field of View}-- Area imaged simultaneously. PET: larger FOV than SPECT.

\medterm{Spectral Analysis}-- Energy discrimination to reject scatter. Energy window typically 20% centered on photopeak.

\medterm{Dead Time}-- Period after each event during which system cannot process another event. Limits count rate. Corrected electronically.

\medterm{Random Events}-- Coincidence events from unrelated photons. Reduced by timing window and energy discrimination.

\medterm{Scatter Events}-- Photons deflected from original path. Corrected by energy windowing and scatter correction algorithms.

\medterm{Attenuation Correction}-- Compensation for photon absorption in tissue. CT-based (PET/CT), transmission source (SPECT), or MR-based (PET/MRI).

\medterm{Normalization}-- Correction for detector efficiency variations. Performed daily with uniform source.

\medterm{Quality Control}-- Daily, weekly, monthly tests ensure system performance. Uniformity, energy, sensitivity, spatial resolution, count rate performance.

\medterm{American College of Nuclear Medicine}-- Professional organization setting standards for nuclear medicine practice.

\medterm{SNMMI}-- Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging. Professional society for nuclear medicine physicians and technologists.

\medterm{IAEA}-- International Atomic Energy Agency. Sets safety standards for radiation use in medicine.

\medterm{NCRP}-- National Council on Radiation Protection and Measurements. US organization providing radiation protection guidance.

\medterm{IRMM}-- International Radiation Protection Association. Global organization for radiation protection.

\medterm{Occupational Exposure}-- Radiation exposure to nuclear medicine staff. Monitored with dosimeters. Limits: 50 mSv/year whole body, 150 mSv/year lens, 500 mSv/year extremities.

\medterm{Public Exposure}-- Radiation exposure to public from nuclear medicine patients. Guidelines for discharge after therapy. I-131 therapy: radiation safety instructions for 3-7 days.

\medterm{Radiation Dose}-- Effective dose from diagnostic procedures. PET/CT: 15-25 mSv. Bone scan: 3-5 mSv. Myocardial perfusion: 8-15 mSv. Justification: benefit should exceed risk.

\medterm{Pediatric Nuclear Medicine}-- Special considerations for children: lower doses, sedation often needed, distraction techniques. Dose reduction: weight-based activity, optimized protocols.

\medterm{Pregnancy}-- Nuclear medicine generally contraindicated during pregnancy. Breastfeeding: interruption period depends on radiopharmaceutical. I-131: permanent cessation. Tc-99m: 24 hours.

\medterm{Radiopharmacy}-- Preparation and dispensing of radiopharmaceuticals. Good manufacturing practice (GMP) required. Sterility, pyrogen testing, quality control.

\medterm{Generator}-- System for producing short-lived radionuclide from longer-lived parent. Tc-99m from Mo-99. Elution with saline. Quality control: Mo-99 breakthrough, Al-3+ breakthrough, sterility.

\medterm{Kit}-- Lyophilized kit for Tc-99m labeling. Add Tc-99m pertechnetate, wait for labeling. Examples: MDP, sestamibi, HMPAO.

\medterm{Synthesis Module}-- Automated system for radiopharmaceutical preparation. PET tracer synthesis: FDG, Florbetapir. Quality control: radiochemical purity, pH, sterility.

\medterm{HPLC}-- High-performance liquid chromatography for radiochemical purity analysis.

\medterm{TLC}-- Thin-layer chromatography for radiochemical purity.

\medterm{Radiochemistry}-- Chemistry of radiopharmaceuticals. Labeling, purification, quality control.

\medterm{Radiobiology}-- Study of radiation effects on biological systems. DNA damage, cell death, repair mechanisms.

\medterm{Radiation Biology}-- See radiobiology.

\medterm{Stochastic Effects}-- Random radiation effects with no threshold. Cancer, genetic effects. Probability increases with dose.

\medterm{Deterministic Effects}-- Radiation effects with threshold dose. Cataracts, skin erythema, bone marrow suppression. Severity increases with dose.

\medterm{LD50}-- Lethal dose for 50% of population. For humans: ~4 Gy whole body without treatment.

\medterm{Relative Biological Effectiveness (RBE)}-- Ratio of dose of reference radiation to dose of test radiation for same effect. Alpha particles: RBE 10-20.

\medterm{Linear Energy Transfer (LET)}-- Energy deposited per unit path length. High LET (alpha, neutrons) more biologically effective.

\medterm{Oxygen Enhancement Ratio}-- Ratio of dose under hypoxic vs. aerobic conditions. Higher for low LET radiation.

\medterm{Therapeutic Index}-- Ratio of toxic dose to therapeutic dose. Wider index = safer drug.

\medterm{Specific Absorbed Fraction}-- Fraction of emitted energy absorbed in target organ.

\medterm{MIRD Schema}-- Medical Internal Radiation Dose. Method for calculating radiation absorbed doses.

\medterm{OLINDA}-- Organ Level Internal Dose Assessment. Software for MIRD calculations.

\medterm{Dosimetry}-- Measurement and calculation of radiation dose. Patient dosimetry for therapy.

\medterm{Compton Scattering}-- Photon interaction with loosely bound electron. Major interaction in diagnostic energy range.

\medterm{Photoelectric Effect}-- Photon absorption by inner shell electron. Major interaction at low energies, high Z materials.

\medterm{Pair Production}-- Photon interaction producing electron-positron pair. Requires energy >1.022 MeV.

\medterm{Annihilation}-- Positron-electron annihilation producing two 511 keV photons in opposite directions.

\medterm{Scintillation}-- Light emission from crystal when irradiated. NaI(Tl) scintillation decay time: 230 ns.

\medterm{Photopeak}-- Full-energy peak in gamma spectrum. Used for energy window setting.

\medterm{Energy Resolution}-- Ability to distinguish different gamma energies. FWHM of photopeak. NaI(Tl): ~10% at 140 keV.

\medterm{Spatial Resolution}-- Ability to distinguish small objects. PET: 4-6 mm. SPECT: 8-12 mm.

\medterm{Sensitivity}-- See sensitivity above.

\medterm{Count Rate}-- Number of detected events per second. Dead time losses at high count rates.

\medterm{Signal-to-Noise Ratio}-- Ratio of true counts to statistical noise. Improved by longer acquisition, higher activity.

\medterm{Image Processing}-- Post-acquisition manipulation: filtering, reconstruction, correction.

\medterm{Filtered Back Projection}-- Traditional image reconstruction method. Fast but noisy.

\medterm{Iterative Reconstruction}-- Modern reconstruction method. OSEM (Ordered Subsets Expectation Maximization). Better quality, longer computation.

\medterm{Attenuation Correction}-- See attenuation correction.

\medterm{Scatter Correction}-- Correction for scattered photons. Dual energy window method.

\medterm{Resolution Recovery}-- Correction for detector resolution degradation with distance.

\medterm{Motion Correction}-- Correction for patient motion during acquisition. Respiratory gating, navigation.

\medterm{Quantitative PET}-- Measurement of absolute tracer concentration. SUV, kinetic modeling.

\medterm{Kinetic Modeling}-- Mathematical description of tracer behavior. Compartmental models, graphical analysis.

\medterm{Patlak Plot}-- Linear graphical method for quantitative PET.

\medterm{SUV}-- See standardized uptake value.

\medterm{Metabolic Tumor Volume}-- Total volume of metabolically active tumor on PET. Prognostic in lymphoma.

\medterm{Total Lesion Glycolysis}-- Product of metabolic tumor volume and mean SUV. Prognostic marker.

\medterm{Response Evaluation Criteria}-- RECIST criteria for tumor response. PERCIST for PET.

\medterm{Deauville Criteria}-- 5-point scale for PET response in lymphoma.

\medterm{Lugano Classification}-- Staging and response assessment for lymphoma.

\medterm{PSMA}-- Prostate-specific membrane antigen. Target for PET imaging and therapy in prostate cancer.

\medterm{DOTATATE}-- Somatostatin analog labeled with Ga-68 for PET imaging of neuroendocrine tumors.

\medterm{Amyloid PET}-- Tracers: Florbetapir, Flutemetamol, Florbetaben. Detect amyloid plaques in Alzheimer disease.

\medterm{Tau PET}-- Tracers: Flortaucipir, MK-6240. Detect tau tangles in neurodegenerative diseases.

\medterm{Perfusion PET}-- Tracers: Rb-82, N-13 ammonia, O-15 water. Measure blood flow.

\medterm{Myocardial Viability}-- PET assessment of hibernating myocardium. FDG uptake indicates viable myocardium.

\medterm{Infection Imaging}-- WBC scanning, gallium scanning, FDG PET. Detection of occult infection.

\medterm{Inflammation Imaging}-- FDG PET sensitive for inflammatory processes. Differentiate infection from malignancy challenging.

\medterm{Oncology PET}-- FDG PET/CT standard for many cancers. Staging, response assessment, restaging.

\medterm{Neurology PET}-- Dementia evaluation, epilepsy localization, Parkinson plus syndromes.

\medterm{Cardiac PET}-- Viability, perfusion, amyloidosis. Quantitative perfusion measurement.

\medterm{Therapy Monitoring}-- PET for early response assessment. Chang criteria in lymphoma.

\medterm{Research Applications}-- Novel tracers, pharmacokinetics, receptor imaging, enzyme imaging.

\medterm{Clinical Trials}-- Phase I-IV trials for new radiopharmaceuticals. Safety, efficacy, dosimetry.

\medterm{Regulatory Approval}-- FDA approval process for radiopharmaceuticals. IND, NDA applications.

\medterm{Compounding}-- Preparation of radiopharmaceuticals for individual patients. USP <797> standards.

\medterm{Waste Management}-- Decay-in-storage for short-lived isotopes. Disposal per regulations.

\medterm{Shielding}-- Lead, tungsten, acrylic shielding for radiation protection.

\medterm{Transport}-- Regulations for shipping radioactive materials. Package types: excepted, A, B.

\medterm{Emergency Procedures}-- Spill response, contamination control, radiation exposure.

\medterm{Documentation}-- Record keeping for radioactive materials. Inventory, disposal, patient release.

\medterm{Accreditation}-- ACR, SNMMI accreditation for nuclear medicine facilities.

\medterm{Board Certification}-- American Board of Nuclear Medicine certification.

\medterm{Continuing Education}-- Mandatory CME for board certification maintenance.

\medterm{Multidisciplinary Team}-- Nuclear medicine involves physicians, technologists, physicists, pharmacists, nurses.

\medterm{Patient Preparation}-- Fasting for FDG PET, hydration, bladder emptying. Medication review.

\medterm{Patient Positioning}-- Supine, arms raised for PET. Comfort and reproducibility important.

\medterm{Acquisition Time}-- Typical PET acquisition: 2-3 minutes per bed position. Total: 20-30 minutes.

\medterm{Image Display}-- Transaxial, coronal, sagittal, MIP, fused images. Windowing for optimal visualization.

\medterm{Reporting}-- Structured reporting with standardized terminology. BI-RADS, LI-RADS, PI-RADS equivalents for PET.

\medterm{Critical Findings}-- Unexpected significant findings require immediate notification. Incidentalomas.

\medterm{Follow-up Recommendations}-- Based on guidelines. Fleischner Society for lung nodules, RECIST for response.
