\chapter{Vascular Surgery \& Angiology}

\medterm{Acute Limb Ischemia (ALI)}-- Sudden, rapid decrease in lower extremity arterial perfusion threatening limb viability, typically caused by acute arterial thrombosis or embolism. Presentation: the 6 Ps—pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis, poikilothermia. Diagnosis: clinical, Doppler ultrasound, CT angiography. Treatment: emergency revascularization (thrombolysis, thrombectomy, bypass) within 6 hours for salvageable limb.

\medterm{Aneurysm}-- Abnormal focal dilation of blood vessel. Types: arterial (aortic, peripheral), venous (rare). Risk factors: atherosclerosis, hypertension, smoking, genetic disorders (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos), infection. Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA): screening by ultrasound in men 65-75 who smoked. Repair indicated if >5.5 cm, symptomatic, or expanding.

\medterm{Arterial Ulcer}-- Wound caused by inadequate arterial blood supply. Location: toes, heels, pressure points. Appearance: painful, punched-out, pale base, minimal granulation. Assessment: ABI, doppler pressures, angiography. Treatment: revascularization, wound care, offloading.

\medterm{Bypass Graft}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein (saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). Indications: peripheral arterial disease, coronary artery disease, aortic aneurysm. Long-term patency depends on graft type, inflow/outflow, patient factors.

\medterm{Carotid Stenosis}-- Narrowing of carotid artery, usually from atherosclerosis. Risk: ischemic stroke. Asymptomatic: screening by auscultation (bruit), ultrasound if risk factors. Symptomatic: TIA or stroke. Treatment: carotid endarterectomy (CEA) or carotid artery stenting (CAS) if >70% stenosis, performed within 2 weeks of symptoms.

\medterm{Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)}-- Blood clot in deep veins, usually lower extremity. Risk factors: Virchow triad (stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury). Symptoms: leg swelling, pain, warmth, redness. Diagnosis: D-dimer, compression ultrasound. Treatment: anticoagulation (heparin, DOACs), compression stockings, IVC filter if contraindication to anticoagulation.

\medterm{Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD)}-- Atherosclerotic narrowing of arteries supplying limbs. Symptoms: intermittent claudication (pain with walking, relieved by rest), critical limb ischemia (rest pain, ulcers, gangrene). Diagnosis: ABI <0.9, Doppler, angiography. Treatment: risk factor modification, exercise, cilostazol, revascularization for severe disease.

\medterm{Varicose Veins}-- Dilated, tortuous superficial veins, usually lower extremity. Caused by venous valve incompetence. Symptoms: aching, heaviness, swelling, cosmetic concern. Complications: thrombophlebitis, bleeding, ulceration. Treatment: compression stockings, sclerotherapy, endovenous ablation, surgical stripping.

\medterm{Aortic Dissection}-- A spectrum of life-threatening conditions involving the thoracic aorta, including aortic dissection, intramural hematoma (IMH), and penetrating aortic ulcer (PAU). See acute aortic syndromes.

\medterm{Aortic Aneurysm}-- Abnormal focal dilation of blood vessel. See aneurysm.

\medterm{Buerger Disease}-- See thromboangiitis obliterans.

\medterm{Calciphylaxis}-- Calcification of small blood vessels in kidney disease. See calcific uremic arteriolopathy.

\medterm{Chronic Venous Insufficiency (CVI)}-- Inadequate venous return causing venous hypertension. Symptoms: edema, skin changes (hyperpigmentation, lipodermatosclerosis), venous ulcer. Diagnosis: venous duplex ultrasound. Treatment: compression therapy, wound care, surgical intervention if indicated.

\medterm{Claudication}-- Atherosclerotic occlusive disease of arteries supplying the lower limbs, causing reduced blood flow. See peripheral arterial disease.

\medterm{Dialysis Access}-- Vascular access for hemodialysis. Types: arteriovenous fistula (AVF, gold standard), arteriovenous graft (AVG), central venous catheter. AVF: autogenous, durable, lower complication rate. AVG: prosthetic, used when veins unsuitable. Complications: stenosis, thrombosis, infection, steal syndrome.

\medterm{Embolectomy}-- Surgical removal of embolus from blood vessel. Indications: acute arterial occlusion, pulmonary embolism. Techniques: Fogarty catheter, open surgical extraction. Often combined with thrombolysis.

\medterm{Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR)}-- Minimally invasive technique for abdominal aortic aneurysm repair using stent graft. Accessed via femoral arteries. Advantages: lower perioperative mortality, faster recovery. Disadvantages: need for surveillance, endoleak risk, may require reintervention.

\medterm{Femoral Artery}-- Major artery supplying lower extremity. Common site for peripheral arterial disease, catheterization access, aneurysm. Pulse palpable in femoral triangle. Bruit indicates stenosis.

\medterm{Hemodialysis}-- Vascular access for hemodialysis. See dialysis access.

\medterm{Hypercoagulable State}-- Condition increasing thrombosis risk. Inherited: Factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, protein C/S deficiency, antithrombin deficiency. Acquired: cancer, pregnancy, oral contraceptives, antiphospholipid syndrome, myeloproliferative disorders.

\medterm{Inguinal Hernia}-- Protrusion of abdominal contents through the inguinal canal. See geriatrics.

\medterm{Lymphedema}-- See geriatrics.

\medterm{May-Thurner Syndrome}-- Compression of left common iliac vein by right common iliac artery. Causes left lower extremity DVT. Diagnosis: CT/MR venography, phlebography. Treatment: angioplasty and stenting.

\medterm{Neuropathic Ulcer}-- A chronic wound of the foot in a patient with diabetes, resulting from peripheral neuropathy, peripheral artery disease, and impaired wound healing. See diabetic foot ulcer.

\medterm{Phlebitis}-- The formation of a blood clot (thrombus) within a vein, most commonly in the deep veins of the lower extremities (deep vein thrombosis, DVT). See venous thrombosis.

\medterm{Raynaud Phenomenon}-- Vasospastic disorder causing digital ischemia. Primary: benign, symmetric. Secondary: associated with connective tissue disease. Triggered by cold, stress. Treatment: warmth, calcium channel blockers, smoking cessation.

\medterm{Saphenous Vein}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein (saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). See bypass graft.

\medterm{Steal Syndrome}-- Ischemia of distal extremity due to blood flow diversion to bypass graft or AV fistula. Most common: upper extremity after AV fistula creation. Symptoms: hand pain, pallor, numbness, weakness. Treatment: banding, ligation, revision.

\medterm{Thrombectomy}-- Surgical removal of thrombus. Techniques: open thrombectomy, catheter-directed thrombolysis, mechanical thrombectomy. Indications: acute arterial occlusion, DVT with phlegmasia.

\medterm{Thromboangiitis Obliterans (Buerger Disease)}-- Segmental inflammatory disease of small/medium arteries and veins, strongly associated with smoking. Affects upper and lower extremities. Symptoms: ischemic rest pain, Raynaud, superficial thrombophlebitis, ulceration, gangrene. Treatment: complete smoking cessation (only effective intervention), sympathectomy, amputation for gangrene.

\medterm{Varicose Ulcer}-- Impaired venous return from the lower extremities due to venous valve incompetence or obstruction. See chronic venous insufficiency.

\medterm{Aortic Aneurysm Screening}-- Abnormal focal dilation of blood vessel. See aneurysm.

\medterm{Arterial Duplex}-- Ultrasound technique combining B-mode imaging and Doppler flow assessment. Used for evaluating arterial stenosis, aneurysm, graft patency. Non-invasive, widely available.

\medterm{Bypass Surgery}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein (saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). See bypass graft.

\medterm{Carotid Endarterectomy}-- Narrowing of carotid artery, usually from atherosclerosis. See carotid stenosis.

\medterm{DVT Prevention}-- Formation of a blood clot in a deep vein, most commonly in the lower extremities. See deep vein thrombosis.

\medterm{Endovascular Repair}-- See EVAR.

\medterm{Fasciotomy}-- Surgical incision through the fascia to relieve compartment syndrome. See emergency medicine.

\medterm{Hemangioma}-- See vascular anomalies.

\medterm{IVC Filter}-- Formation of a blood clot in a deep vein, most commonly in the lower extremities. See deep vein thrombosis.

\medterm{Ligation}-- The surgical process of tying off a blood vessel or duct using a suture or clip to prevent bleeding or block fluid flow. See vascular surgery.

\medterm{Marfan Syndrome}-- An autosomal dominant connective tissue disorder caused by mutations in the FBN1 gene (fibrillin-1). See genetics.

\medterm{PAD Management}-- Atherosclerotic occlusive disease of arteries supplying the lower limbs, causing reduced blood flow. See peripheral arterial disease.

\medterm{Phlebography}-- Venography using contrast dye. Used for diagnosing venous thrombosis, malformations. Largely replaced by duplex ultrasound.

\medterm{Popliteal Aneurysm}-- Aneurysm of popliteal artery behind knee. Associated with AAA. Risk: thrombosis, embolism, compression. Treatment: surgical repair or endovascular stenting.

\medterm{Radiocontrast}-- Substance enhancing image visibility. See contrast agent.

\medterm{Stenosis}-- Narrowing of vessel lumen. Causes: atherosclerosis, thrombosis, dissection, external compression. Symptoms depend on location and severity. Treatment: medical, angioplasty, stenting, bypass.

\medterm{Takayasu Arteritis}-- Large-vessel vasculitis affecting aorta and major branches. Predominantly young Asian women. Symptoms: pulselessness, blood pressure discrepancy, claudication, visual symptoms. Treatment: corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, surgical/endovascular intervention for stenosis.

\medterm{Trauma (Vascular)}-- See vascular trauma.

\medterm{Ulcer (Vascular)}-- See arterial ulcer, venous ulcer, diabetic foot ulcer.

\medterm{Vasculitis}-- Inflammation of blood vessels causing tissue ischemia and necrosis. See rheumatology.

\medterm{Wound Care}-- Management of traumatic wounds: irrigation, debridement, closure (sutures, staples, glue), dressing. See emergency medicine.

\medterm{Acute Aortic Syndrome}-- A spectrum of life-threatening conditions involving the thoracic aorta, including aortic dissection, intramural hematoma (IMH), and penetrating aortic ulcer (PAU). See acute aortic syndromes.

\medterm{Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm}-- Abnormal focal dilation of blood vessel. See aneurysm.

\medterm{Arteriovenous Fistula}-- Vascular access for hemodialysis. See dialysis access.

\medterm{Atherectomy}-- Mechanical removal of atherosclerotic plaque. Types: directional, laser, rotational, orbital. Used for peripheral arterial disease.

\medterm{Barthel Index}-- Standardized measure of functional independence in activities of daily living (ADL). See geriatrics.

\medterm{Calcific Uremic Arteriolopathy}-- Calcification of small blood vessels in kidney disease. Painful skin necrosis. Treatment: sodium thiosulfate, wound care.

\medterm{Color Doppler}-- Ultrasound technique combining B-mode imaging and Doppler flow assessment. See arterial duplex.

\medterm{Compartment Syndrome}-- Increased pressure within fascial compartment compromising circulation. See emergency medicine.

\medterm{Duplex Ultrasound}-- Ultrasound technique combining B-mode imaging and Doppler flow assessment. See arterial duplex.

\medterm{Embolization}-- Intentional blocking of blood vessel. Used for hemorrhage control, tumor devascularization, aneurysm treatment.

\medterm{Fluoroscopy}-- Real-time X-ray imaging used to visualize dynamic processes. See radiology.

\medterm{Graft}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein (saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). See bypass graft.

\medterm{Hemodialysis}-- Vascular access for hemodialysis. See dialysis access.

\medterm{Interventional Radiology}-- Image-guided minimally invasive procedures. See radiology.

\medterm{Kinetics}-- See pharmacology.

\medterm{Laser Ablation}-- Laser energy for vessel closure. Endovenous laser ablation for varicose veins.

\medterm{Magnetic Resonance Angiography}-- See MR angiography.

\medterm{Nephrostomy}-- An artificial opening created between the kidney and the skin to drain urine directly when the normal pathway is blocked. See urology.

\medterm{Occlusion}-- Complete blockage of vessel. May be natural (thrombosis) or iatrogenic (embolization).

\medterm{Percutaneous Transluminal Angioplasty}-- Balloon dilation of arterial stenosis. Often combined with stenting.

\medterm{Quadrant}-- One of four sections of the abdomen (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) used for clinical description. See general.

\medterm{Radial Artery}-- Common access site for coronary angiography. Alternative to femoral artery.

\medterm{Stent}-- Tubular scaffold maintaining vessel/duct patency. See medical implants.

\medterm{Thrombolysis}-- Pharmacological dissolution of thrombus. tPA, streptokinase, alteplase. Used for acute arterial occlusion, PE, stroke.

\medterm{Ultrasound}-- Imaging using high-frequency sound waves (2-18 MHz). See radiology.

\medterm{Venous Insufficiency}-- Impaired venous return from the lower extremities due to venous valve incompetence or obstruction. See chronic venous insufficiency.

\medterm{X-ray}-- See radiology.

\medterm{Yield}-- The proportion of positive test results among those tested. See geriatrics.

\medterm{Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome}-- Gastrin-secreting tumor called gastrinoma causing gastric acid hypersecretion, severe peptic ulcers, and diarrhea. See gastroenterology.

\medterm{Aorta}-- Main arterial trunk carrying blood from heart to body. Thoracic and abdominal segments. AAA most common aneurysm.

\medterm{Artery}-- Blood vessel carrying blood away from heart. Thick muscular walls. Pulse palpable.

\medterm{Vein}-- Blood vessel carrying blood to heart. Thinner walls, valves prevent backflow. Varicose veins common.

\medterm{Capillary}-- Smallest blood vessel. Site of exchange between blood and tissues.

\medterm{Arteriole}-- Small artery leading to capillaries. Regulates blood flow and blood pressure.

\medterm{Venule}-- Small vein draining capillaries. Collects blood from tissues.

\medterm{Collateral Circulation}-- Alternative blood flow pathways. Develop with chronic obstruction. Important for limb salvage.

\medterm{Perfusion}-- Blood flow through tissue. Adequate perfusion essential for organ function.

\medterm{Ischemia}-- Reduced blood flow causing oxygen deprivation. Can lead to tissue damage or death.

\medterm{Infarction}-- Tissue death from prolonged ischemia. Examples: myocardial infarction, cerebral infarction.

\medterm{Thrombosis}-- Formation of blood clot within vessel. Can obstruct flow. Arterial or venous.

\medterm{Embolism}-- Traveling clot or debris blocking vessel. Source: DVT (PE), heart (stroke), artery (limb ischemia).

\medterm{Atherosclerosis}-- Plaque buildup in arteries. Major cause of PAD, CAD, stroke. Risk factors: smoking, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia.

\medterm{Plaque}-- Atherosclerotic buildup of lipid, cholesterol, calcium, cells. Can rupture causing thrombosis.

\medterm{Stenosis}-- Narrowing of vessel. Graded as mild (<50%), moderate (50-70%), severe (>70%).

\medterm{Occlusion}-- Complete blockage of vessel. Can be acute (embolism, thrombosis) or chronic (atherosclerosis).

\medterm{Angioplasty}-- Balloon dilation of stenotic vessel. Often combined with stenting.

\medterm{Stenting}-- Placement of mesh tube to keep vessel open. Drug-eluting stents reduce restenosis.

\medterm{Bypass}-- Surgical creation of alternative route around blocked vessel. Uses vein or prosthetic graft.

\medterm{Graft}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein (saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). See bypass graft.

\medterm{Endovascular}-- Minimally invasive vascular procedure via catheter. Examples: angioplasty, stenting, aneurysm repair.

\medterm{Open Surgery}-- Traditional surgical approach with incision. Used for bypass, aneurysm repair, thrombectomy.

\medterm{Vascular Imaging}-- Techniques for visualizing blood vessels: duplex ultrasound, CT angiography, MR angiography, conventional angiography.

\medterm{ABI}-- Ankle-brachial index. Ratio of ankle to arm systolic pressure. Normal: 1.0-1.4. <0.9 indicates PAD.

\medterm{Toe Pressure}-- Pressure in toe digits. Useful when ankle pressures non-compressible (diabetes, renal disease).

\medterm{Segmental Pressures}-- Blood pressure measurements at different leg levels. Localizes level of obstruction.

\medterm{Pulse Examination}-- Assessment of arterial pulses. Absent or weak pulses indicate disease.

\medterm{Bruits}-- Turbulent blood flow sounds heard with stethoscope. Indicates stenosis.

\medterm{Aneurysm Repair}-- Surgical or endovascular treatment of aneurysm. Indications: size, symptoms, expansion rate.

\medterm{AAA Repair}-- See abdominal aortic aneurysm.

\medterm{Thoracic Aortic Aneurysm}-- Aneurysm of thoracic aorta. Associated with hypertension, Marfan syndrome, bicuspid aortic valve.

\medterm{Dissection}-- A tear in the intima of the aorta allowing blood to dissect through the media, creating a false lumen. See aortic dissection.

\medterm{Vascular Malformation}-- Abnormal blood vessel development. Arteriovenous malformation, venous malformation, lymphatic malformation.

\medterm{Hemangioma}-- Benign vascular tumor. Common in infants. Most resolve spontaneously.

\medterm{Arteriovenous Malformation}-- Abnormal connection between artery and vein without capillary bed. Can bleed or cause symptoms.

\medterm{Dialysis Access}-- Vascular access for hemodialysis. See dialysis access.

\medterm{Steal Syndrome}-- Ischemia of distal extremity due to blood flow diversion to bypass graft or AV fistula. See steal syndrome.

\medterm{Complications (Vascular)}-- Post-procedural issues: bleeding, infection, thrombosis, embolism, limb ischemia.

\medterm{Postoperative Care}-- Monitoring after vascular surgery. Watch for bleeding, graft patency, compartment syndrome.

\medterm{Anticoagulation}-- Blood thinning medication. Used for DVT, PE, arterial thrombosis, after stenting.

\medterm{Antiplatelet Therapy}-- Medication preventing platelet aggregation. Aspirin, clopidogrel. Used after stenting, bypass.

\medterm{Compression Therapy}-- External pressure on veins. Used for DVT prevention, venous insufficiency, varicose veins.

\medterm{Lymphedema Management}-- See geriatrics.

\medterm{Wound Care}-- Management of traumatic wounds: irrigation, debridement, closure (sutures, staples, glue), dressing. See emergency medicine.

\medterm{Diabetic Foot}-- See endocrinology.

\medterm{Risk Factor Modification}-- Smoking cessation, blood pressure control, lipid management, diabetes control, exercise.

\medterm{Exercise Program}-- Supervised exercise improves claudication. Walking program 30-45 minutes, 3x/week.

\medterm{Smoking Cessation}-- Critical for PAD patients. Increases amputation risk if continued.

\medterm{Hyperlipidemia}-- See lipid disorders. Statin therapy reduces cardiovascular events in PAD.

\medterm{Hypertension}-- See cardiology. Target BP <130/80 in PAD patients.

\medterm{Diabetes Management}-- Tight glucose control reduces complications. Foot examination essential.

\medterm{Pain (Vascular)}-- Claudication pain with walking. Rest pain indicates critical ischemia.

\medterm{Claudication Distance}-- Distance walked before pain onset. Improves with exercise, medication, revascularization.

\medterm{Critical Limb Ischemia}-- See acute limb ischemia.

\medterm{Amputation}-- Last resort for unsalvageable limb. Below-knee, above-knee, transmetatarsal.

\medterm{Rehabilitation}-- See rehabilitation medicine. Post-amputation or post-revascularization.

\medterm{Prognosis}-- PAD prognosis depends on disease severity, comorbidities. Cardiovascular risk elevated.

\medterm{Follow-up}-- Regular surveillance after vascular procedures. Monitor for restenosis, graft patency.

\medterm{Patient Education}-- Teach about disease, medications, lifestyle modification, warning signs.
