# `cli.pdf` Missing-Term Review

## Scope and method

`cli.pdf` is *Clinical Cases in Internal Medicine* (2022), a 474-page textbook containing 50 cases. The review focused on clinically important diseases, syndromes, signs, symptoms, investigations, and procedures appearing in the case discussions and differential diagnoses. Medicine and drug names were excluded.

Dictionary coverage was checked against the `\\medterm{...}` headwords in `chapters/*.tex`. British/American spelling, abbreviations, synonyms, and cross-references were treated as equivalent where the existing entry was adequate.

## Terms that should be added

These are clinically important concepts from the cases that are absent as standalone dictionary headwords.

| Recommended headword | Source location | Why it should be added |
|---|---|---|
| **Cullen's Sign** | Case 1.7, acute pancreatitis, pp. 59, 66 | Classic periumbilical ecchymosis indicating severe haemorrhagic pancreatitis or intra-abdominal bleeding. |
| **Grey Turner's Sign** | Case 1.7, acute pancreatitis, pp. 59, 66 | Classic flank ecchymosis associated with retroperitoneal haemorrhage and severe pancreatitis. |
| **Hyperventilation Syndrome** | Case 2.1, asthma differential, pp. 87–96 | Common functional respiratory differential that is clinically distinct from asthma and pulmonary disease. |
| **Bronchiolitis Obliterans** | Case 2.4, COPD differential, p. 114 | Important cause of fixed, often severe airflow obstruction and a distinct pulmonary diagnosis. |
| **Sacroiliitis** | Case 3.3, back-pain differential, pp. 186–196 | Key inflammatory and infectious cause of back or buttock pain and an important spondyloarthritis finding. |
| **Spinal Epidural Abscess** | Case 3.3, listed as spinal abscess, pp. 186–196 | Time-critical red-flag diagnosis in back pain; the more precise clinical headword should be used. |

| **Post-Transfusion Purpura** | Case 6.3, thrombocytopenia differential, pp. 361–366 | Distinct severe immune-mediated thrombocytopenic disorder that is not represented by the existing ITP entry. |

## Recommended but not mandatory

These terms are valid additions but are less essential because they are rare, organism-specific, or already substantially covered by a broader entry.

| Term | Current coverage or reason for lower priority |
|---|---|
| **Aeromonas Gastroenteritis** | Appears as an organism-level cause of acute bloody diarrhoea; a bacterial-colitis entry can cover it. |
| **Non-cholera Vibrio Gastroenteritis** | Appears in the same differential; a separate headword is optional unless water- and seafood-borne infections are being expanded. |
| **Chronic Progressive External Ophthalmoplegia** | Relevant mitochondrial disorder, but rare and peripheral to the myasthenia case. |
| **Tolosa–Hunt Syndrome** | Rare painful ophthalmoplegia syndrome; useful for a specialist expansion but not essential for the core dictionary. |
| **Unstable Haemoglobin Disorders** | The PDF lists this as a category in haemolytic anaemia; it can be covered under a broader haemoglobinopathy entry. |

## Covered adequately; do not add duplicates

The following PDF terms were initially possible candidates but already have adequate coverage through exact entries, synonyms, or existing definitions:

- Tropical Sprue
- Collagen Colitis → Collagenous Colitis
- Microscopic Colitis
- Autoimmune Hepatitis
- Gastric Volvulus
- Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis
- Aspergilloma → covered in Aspergillosis
- CT Pulmonary Angiography → covered by CT Pulmonary Angiography / Pulmonary Angiography entries
- Eaton–Lambert Syndrome → Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome
- Goodpasture Disease → Goodpasture Syndrome / Anti-GBM Disease
- Anti-GBM Glomerulonephritis → Anti-GBM Disease
- Chronic Eosinophilic Pneumonia
- Collagenous Colitis
- Atypical Parkinsonism → Parkinsonism with specific subtypes already represented

The recently added terms from the earlier review—such as Peptic Ulcer Bleeding, Duodenitis, Oesophagitis, Oesophageal Ulcer, Autoimmune Enteropathy, Bacterial Overgrowth, Intestinal Lymphoma, Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding, and Soy Protein Intolerance—are now present and were not counted as missing.

## Recommendation

Add the 11 terms in the first table. They are clinically meaningful, non-drug concepts that fill genuine standalone gaps. Add the five lower-priority terms only if the dictionary is being expanded toward a more exhaustive internal-medicine reference.

No dictionary chapter was modified as part of this review.
