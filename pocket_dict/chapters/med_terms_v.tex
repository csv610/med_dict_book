\medterm{Voice} Sound produced by vibration of the vocal folds as air passes through the larynx, then shaped by the throat, mouth, and nose for speech.

\medterm{Voice And Speech} The products of complex coordinated activity of the respiratory, laryngeal, and
articulatory systems.

\medterm{Voice Box} The larynx, an organ in the neck containing the vocal folds and forming part of the airway. It produces voice and helps protect the lower respiratory tract during swallowing.

\medterm{Voice Disorders} Conditions affecting voice quality, pitch, loudness, or ease of speaking because of problems involving the vocal folds, airway, nerves, or voice use.

\synonyms
Difficulty Speaking

\medterm{Void} To empty the urinary bladder by urinating; in other medical contexts, void may mean an empty space or absence of a substance.

\medterm{Voiding Cystourethrogram} Fluoroscopic imaging of the bladder and urethra during voiding, after filling the
bladder with contrast via a catheter. Indications: evaluation of vesicoureteral reflux
(VUR) in children with recurrent UTIs, posterior urethral valves in males, and
post-surgical assessment of the bladder/urethra.

\medterm{Volar} Relating to the palm of the hand or sole of the foot, especially the surface opposite the dorsum.

\medterm{Volatile Anesthetics} Halogenated inhaled anesthetic agents, including sevoflurane, desflurane, and
isoflurane, that reversibly depress central nervous system function. They provide
dose-dependent hypnosis and immobility but may cause hypotension, respiratory
depression, postoperative nausea, and malignant hyperthermia in susceptible individuals.

\medterm{Volatile Oils} Plant oils containing compounds that evaporate readily at room temperature.

\synonyms
Essential oils

\medterm{Volkmann Contracture} A permanent flexion deformity of the hand, wrist, and fingers caused by ischemic injury and fibrosis of forearm muscles.
It most often follows untreated or inadequately treated compartment syndrome after fracture, crush injury, vascular compromise, or severe swelling.
The degree of functional loss depends on the muscles and nerves affected and may include pain, weakness, sensory impairment, and restricted finger extension.
Prevention requires urgent recognition of limb ischemia and compartment pressure; established contractures require reconstructive assessment and may need surgical release or tendon procedures.
\medterm{Volume Of Distribution} A theoretical pharmacokinetic parameter that relates the total amount of drug in the
body to the plasma concentration, calculated as Vd equals amount of drug in the body
divided by plasma concentration. It represents the apparent volume into which a drug
distributes and is not a true anatomical volume.

\medterm{Volume Overload} Excess total body sodium and water, causing peripheral or pulmonary oedema; common causes include heart failure, kidney failure, liver disease, and excessive intravenous fluid, and treatment depends on the cause and severity.

\medterm{Voluntary} Under conscious control or performed by choice, such as voluntary movement. Voluntary action is distinguished from reflex, autonomic, or involuntary activity.

\medterm{Volutrauma} Lung injury caused by excessive mechanical stretch, usually from an inappropriately large tidal volume
during ventilation. Risk is reduced by lung-protective ventilation tailored to the patient’s
predicted body weight, lung mechanics, and clinical condition.

\medterm{Volvulus} Twisting of a loop of intestine around its mesenteric attachment point causing luminal
obstruction and compromise of the mesenteric blood supply which can rapidly progress to
bowel ischemia.

\medterm{Volvulus - Childhood} Twisting of a loop of intestine around its mesentery, causing obstruction and potentially loss of blood supply. In children it is often related to intestinal malrotation and is a surgical emergency when acute.

\medterm{Vomit} To eject stomach or upper gastrointestinal contents through the mouth; the expelled material may also be called vomitus. Persistent vomiting can cause dehydration and electrolyte abnormalities.

\medterm{Vomiting} Reflex expulsion of gastric contents. Causes: GI, neurological, metabolic, toxic,
psychological. Treatment: antiemetics, identify cause, prevent aspiration.

\medterm{Vomiting Blood} The passage of blood or coffee-ground material in vomit, usually indicating upper gastrointestinal bleeding. It is a potentially serious symptom requiring prompt medical evaluation.

\medterm{Vomitus} Material expelled from the stomach or upper gastrointestinal tract during vomiting. Its color and contents can provide clues but do not by themselves establish a diagnosis.

\medterm{Von Gierke Disease} Glycogen storage disease type I, an inherited deficiency of glucose-6-phosphatase or its transport system that causes fasting hypoglycemia, lactic acidosis, enlarged liver, and excess uric acid or lipids.

\medterm{Von Hippel-Lindau Disease} Autosomal dominant syndrome caused by mutations in the VHL tumor suppressor gene on
chromosome three predisposing to hemangioblastomas of the central nervous system and
retina, clear cell renal cell carcinoma, pheochromocytoma, pancreatic cysts and
neuroendocrine tumors, and endolymphatic sac tumors.

\medterm{Von Recklinghausen’s Disease} Alternate index label; see \textit{Neurofibromatosis type 1}.

\medterm{Von Recklinghausen’s Neurofibromatosis} Alternate index label; see \textit{Neurofibromatosis type 1}.

\medterm{Von Recklinghausen’s Phakomatosis} Alternate index label; see \textit{Neurofibromatosis type 1}.

\medterm{Von Willebrand Disease} An inherited or acquired bleeding disorder caused by deficient or abnormal von Willebrand factor, which helps platelets adhere
and carries factor VIII. It commonly causes nosebleeds, easy bruising, heavy menstrual
bleeding, or prolonged bleeding after procedures.

\medterm{VOR Reflex} The vestibulo-ocular reflex stabilizes gaze by moving the eyes opposite to head movement through inner-ear, brainstem, and eye-muscle pathways. Damage can cause oscillopsia, vertigo, imbalance, and abnormal head-impulse testing.

\medterm{Voriconazole} A triazole antifungal agent used in the treatment of invasive aspergillosis, candidemia,
and other serious fungal infections. Voriconazole inhibits the fungal cytochrome P450
enzyme lanosterol 14-alpha demethylase, preventing ergosterol synthesis and disrupting
fungal cell membrane integrity.

\medterm{Voyeurism} A paraphilic disorder involving recurrent intense sexual arousal from observing an unsuspecting person who is naked, disrobing, or engaging in sexual activity, when it causes distress, impairment, or involves nonconsenting behavior.

\medterm{Voyeuristic Disorder} A paraphilic disorder involving recurrent, intense sexual arousal from observing an
unsuspecting person who is naked, disrobing, or engaging in sexual activity. The
individual has acted on these urges or they cause marked distress or interpersonal
difficulty. Duration \(\geq 6\) months. Most common in males with onset in adolescence.

\medterm{VPCs} Alternate index label; see \textit{Premature ventricular contractions (PVCs)}.

\medterm{VRE} Enterococcus bacteria that are resistant to vancomycin, often because of acquired changes in cell-wall precursors.
VRE can cause urinary, bloodstream, wound, or other healthcare-associated infections.
Treatment requires susceptibility-guided selection of an effective antimicrobial and
source control when needed.

\medterm{VSD} Ventricular septal defect, a hole in the wall separating the heart’s ventricles.

\synonyms
Ventricular septal defect

\medterm{Vulnerary} A substance traditionally believed to promote wound healing.

\medterm{Vulva} The external female genital structures, including the mons pubis, labia, clitoris, and vestibule. It protects the openings of the urinary and reproductive tracts and contains specialized sensory and glandular tissue.

\synonyms
female external genitalia

\medterm{Vulvar} Relating to the vulva, the external female genital structures including the labia, clitoris, vestibule, and associated glands.

\medterm{Vulvar Intraepithelial Neoplasia} A premalignant or potentially premalignant squamous lesion of the vulva. Most usual-type lesions are related to high-risk HPV, whereas differentiated lesions are commonly associated with lichen sclerosus and have a higher risk of invasive carcinoma.

\medterm{Vulvar Melanoma} A malignant melanocytic neoplasm arising in vulvar skin or mucosa, most often affecting the labia in older adults.
It may present as a pigmented or amelanotic mass, plaque, ulcer, bleeding lesion, or persistent area of altered pigmentation.
Diagnosis requires biopsy with assessment of tumor thickness, ulceration, mitotic activity, and regional or distant spread.
Management is based on stage and resectability and may involve local excision, evaluation of regional lymph nodes, and systemic treatment for advanced disease.

\medterm{Vulvar Squamous Cell Carcinoma} The most common invasive vulvar malignancy, arising through HPV-related or HPV-independent pathways. It usually presents as a persistent mass, ulcer, plaque, or wart-like lesion and requires biopsy for diagnosis.

\medterm{Vulvectomy} Surgical removal of part or all of the vulva, performed for selected cancers, precancerous lesions, or severe persistent disease.

\medterm{Vulvitis} Inflammation of the vulva (external female genitalia), often presenting with pruritus,
burning, irritation, erythema, edema, and discharge. Vulvitis is rarely isolated and is
most commonly part of vulvovaginitis (inflammation of both vulva and vagina).

\medterm{Vulvitis, Yeast} Inflammation of the vulva associated with vulvovaginal candidiasis, usually caused by Candida species. It may cause itching, burning, redness, swelling, and soreness and should be distinguished from other causes of vulvar symptoms.

\medterm{Vulvodynia} Vulvodynia is chronic vulvar pain without an identifiable specific cause, often described as burning or rawness and triggered by touch, pressure, or occurring spontaneously.

\synonyms
Chronic Vulvar Pain

\medterm{Vulvovaginal Candidiasis} Symptomatic infection of the vulva and vagina, usually caused by Candida albicans.
Pruritus, soreness, erythema, and thick discharge are common; diagnosis is clinical with
microscopy or culture when recurrent, severe, atypical, or treatment-resistant.
