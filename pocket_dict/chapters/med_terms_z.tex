\medterm{Z-Plasty} A reconstructive operation that rearranges nearby skin in a Z-shaped pattern. It can lengthen a tight scar, change the direction of a scar, and improve movement or appearance.

\medterm{Z-Score (DEXA)} A measure from dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry that shows how many standard deviations a person's bone mineral density is above or below the expected mean for an age-, sex-, and population-matched reference group. \medterm{Z-Score (Echocardiography)} The number of standard deviations a child’s heart or blood-vessel measurement is above or below the expected mean for body size, usually body surface area. It helps describe whether a structure is smaller or larger than expected.

\synonyms

\medterm{Z-Thumb Deformity} A thumb deformity in which the metacarpophalangeal joint is flexed and the interphalangeal joint is extended, creating a Z shape. It can occur in rheumatoid arthritis.

\medterm{Zahn Lines} Alternating light and dark layers seen in a blood clot that formed while blood was flowing. The light layers contain platelets and fibrin, while the dark layers contain more red blood cells.

\medterm{Zanamivir} An inhaled neuraminidase inhibitor used to treat or prevent influenza A and B when started appropriately. It may cause bronchospasm and is used cautiously in people with underlying airway disease.

\medterm{Zidovudine} A nucleoside reverse-transcriptase inhibitor used with other antiretroviral medicines to treat HIV infection. It can also be used in some settings to reduce transmission during pregnancy and birth; adverse effects include anemia and low white-cell counts.

ZIKV

\medterm{Zika Virus Infection} Infection caused by Zika virus, usually spread by infected \textit{Aedes} mosquitoes and sometimes through sexual contact, pregnancy, or infected blood. It is often mild or symptom-free but can harm fetal development and is rarely linked to Guillain--Barré syndrome.

\medterm{Zinc} An essential mineral needed for many body reactions, normal growth, immunity, reproduction, and wound healing. Too little can cause deficiency; excessive supplements can cause stomach symptoms and copper deficiency.
