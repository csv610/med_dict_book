
\medterm{Jaccoud's arthropathy}-- A non-erosive, reducible deformity of the metacarpophalangeal joints caused by chronic
periarticular fibrosis and tendon laxity, most commonly associated with systemic lupus
erythematosus.

\medterm{Jacksonian Epilepsy (Focal Seizures)}-- A type of focal (partial) seizure originating in the primary motor cortex, characterized
by clonic (rhythmic jerking) or tonic (sustained contraction) movements that spread
sequentially to adjacent body parts along the motor homunculus (Jacksonian march). Named
after John Hughlings Jackson (1835-1911), who first described the phenomenon.

\medterm{JAK-STAT Pathway}-- A rapid signaling pathway for cytokines and growth factors (interferons, interleukins,
erythropoietin, growth hormone) that lack intrinsic enzymatic activity. Ligand binding
to the cytokine receptor causes receptor dimerization and activation of associated Janus
kinases (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, TYK2) by trans-phosphorylation.

\medterm{JAK2}-- Janus kinase 2, an intracellular tyrosine kinase that transmits signals from several cytokine and growth-factor receptors. Activating JAK2 variants occur in some myeloproliferative neoplasms.

\medterm{Janeway Lesions}-- Painless, erythematous or hemorrhagic macules on the palms and soles, caused by septic
microemboli in infective endocarditis. They are a minor criterion in the Duke criteria.
Unlike Osler nodes, Janeway lesions are painless and represent microabscesses in the
dermis. They are transient and may disappear quickly.

\medterm{Janus Kinase Inhibitors}-- JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib, abrocitinib) block
JAK-STAT signaling downstream of cytokine receptors (IL-6, IFN-gamma, IL-2, IL-7, IL-15,
IL-21, GM-CSF, erythropoietin, thrombopoietin). Selectivity: JAK1/JAK3 (tofacitinib),
JAK1/JAK2 (baricitinib), JAK1 (upadacitinib, filgotinib, abrocitinib).

\medterm{Japanese Encephalitis}-- A mosquito-borne viral infection of the central nervous system caused by Japanese
encephalitis virus. Most infections are asymptomatic, but severe disease can cause
fever, altered consciousness, seizures, movement disorders, and lasting neurological
disability.

\medterm{Japanese Encephalitis Vaccine}-- A vaccine that protects against Japanese encephalitis, a mosquito-borne viral infection of
the central nervous system. Vaccine type, schedule, and eligibility depend on age,
destination or exposure risk, and local recommendations.

\medterm{Jaundice}-- Yellowish discoloration of the skin, sclerae, and mucous membranes caused by
hyperbilirubinemia (serum bilirubin greater than 2-3 mg/dL), resulting from an imbalance
between bilirubin production and clearance.

\medterm{Jaundice (Icterus)}-- Yellow discoloration of the skin, sclerae, or mucous membranes caused by an increased bilirubin concentration in the blood.

\medterm{Jaundice in newborns}-- Yellow discoloration of a newborn's skin or eyes caused by elevated bilirubin.

\medterm{Jaw Pain}-- Pain in the jaw, jaw joint, or chewing muscles caused by temporomandibular disorders, dental disease, infection, injury,
or less commonly heart or nerve disease. Pain with chest discomfort, sweating, breathlessness, or exertion
requires urgent assessment; persistent pain or restricted jaw movement requires dental or medical review.

\medterm{Jejunal Atresia}-- Congenital complete interruption or absence of a segment of the jejunal lumen. It
presents in a newborn with intestinal obstruction, commonly causing bilious vomiting,
abdominal distension, and failure to pass meconium, and generally requires surgery.

\medterm{Jejunostomy}-- A surgically created opening into the jejunum, often used for placement of a feeding tube when nutrition cannot safely be given through the stomach.

\medterm{Jejunum}-- Middle segment (2.5 m) with prominent circular folds and tall villi for a velvety
appearance. Richer blood supply and thicker walls than the ileum. Primary nutrient
absorption site for carbohydrates, amino acids, fatty acids, vitamins, minerals. Fewer
Peyer's patches. More commonly affected by Crohn's disease.

\medterm{Job Modification}-- Changes to work duties, equipment, schedule, or environment that accommodate a health-related limitation and support safe, effective work.

\medterm{Job Syndrome}-- A primary immunodeficiency, usually caused by pathogenic variants affecting STAT3,
characterized by eczema, recurrent staphylococcal skin and lung infections, and markedly
elevated immunoglobulin E. Skeletal, dental, and characteristic facial findings may also
occur.

\medterm{Jock Itch}-- A fungal infection of the groin and inner thighs, also called tinea cruris. It causes an itchy, scaly, ring-shaped or spreading
rash, often worsened by warmth and moisture. Pain, fever, extensive involvement, or failure to improve with
appropriate care requires clinical evaluation.

\medterm{Joint}-- Articulation between two or more bones permitting movement. Structural classification:
fibrous (sutures, syndesmoses, gomphoses -- immovable/slightly movable), cartilaginous
(synchondroses, symphyses -- slightly movable), synovial (freely movable -- plane,
hinge, pivot, condyloid, saddle, ball-and-socket).

\medterm{Joint Contracture}-- Persistent limitation of passive joint movement caused by shortening or fibrosis of
muscle, tendon, capsule, ligament, fascia, skin, or other periarticular tissue. It may
follow immobilization, neurological disease, trauma, inflammation, burns, or congenital
disorders.

\medterm{Joint Diseases}-- A broad category of pathological conditions affecting the structure and function of
joints. Classification by etiology: (1) Degenerative: osteoarthritis (most common,
non-inflammatory, affects load-bearing joints--knee, hip, spine).

\medterm{Joint Effusion}-- Excess fluid within a joint space. Common sites: knee (most accessible), ankle, wrist,
shoulder. Causes: trauma, infection, inflammatory arthritis, crystal arthropathy.
Detected by ballottement or bulge sign.

\medterm{Joint Pain}-- Discomfort arising from articular structures including cartilage, synovium, ligaments,
and periarticular soft tissues due to inflammation, degeneration, injury, or infection.

\medterm{Joint Prosthesis}-- An artificial joint or implant designed to replace a damaged or diseased biological
joint, restoring mobility and relieving chronic pain.

\medterm{Joint Replacement}-- Surgical replacement of damaged joint surfaces with prosthetic components to reduce pain and improve
function.

\medterm{Joint Replacement (Arthroplasty)}-- A surgical procedure in which a damaged or arthritic joint surface is removed and
replaced with a prosthetic implant, most commonly performed for the hip (total hip
arthroplasty) and knee (total knee arthroplasty).

\medterm{Joint Replacement Rehabilitation}-- Exercise, education, and functional training after joint replacement to restore safe
movement and independence.

\medterm{Joint Stiffness}-- Reduced ease or range of joint movement, often worse after rest. Causes include arthritis, injury,
inflammation, prolonged immobility, or muscle and tendon disorders. Sudden swelling, redness,
fever, severe pain, or inability to use the joint requires assessment.

\medterm{Joint Swelling}-- Enlargement of a joint caused by excess synovial fluid, blood, inflammation, infection,
trauma, or structural disease. It may be accompanied by pain, warmth, stiffness, or
restricted movement and can indicate inflammatory or septic arthritis.

\medterm{Jugular Vein}-- One of the major veins draining blood from the head, face, and neck. There are two
pairs: internal jugular vein (IJV) and external jugular vein (EJV).

\medterm{Juvenile angiofibroma}-- A rare, benign but locally aggressive vascular tumor of the nasopharynx that occurs
predominantly in adolescent boys. It may cause recurrent nosebleeds, nasal obstruction, or facial swelling.

\medterm{Juvenile Arthritis}-- A general term for inflammatory arthritis beginning in childhood. Persistent joint swelling, pain, stiffness, or
limited movement may occur, and causes and subtypes vary.

\medterm{Juvenile Astrocytoma}-- An astrocytic glioma arising in a child or adolescent. Many are low-grade and may cause
seizures, headache, visual symptoms, endocrine disturbance, or focal neurological
deficits depending on location; diagnosis requires neuroimaging and tumour evaluation.

\medterm{Juvenile Dermatomyositis}-- An inflammatory muscle disease of childhood characterized by proximal weakness and
distinctive skin findings such as heliotrope rash and Gottron papules. It may also affect blood vessels, muscles, or
other organs.

\medterm{Juvenile Hypermobility Syndrome}-- Excessive joint movement in a child or adolescent associated with pain, instability, fatigue, or recurrent
sprains, without another defined connective-tissue disorder. Assessment considers the pattern of hypermobility,
skin and systemic features, and functional impact; management may include physiotherapy and joint protection.


\medterm{Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis}-- A group of inflammatory arthritis disorders that begin before age 16 and persist for at least six weeks
without another identified cause. Subtypes differ in the number of joints affected,
systemic symptoms, associated eye disease, and laboratory findings.


\medterm{Juvenile Osteoporosis}-- Reduced bone mass or impaired bone strength occurring during childhood or adolescence,
increasing fracture risk. It may be primary or secondary to chronic disease, nutritional
deficiency, endocrine disorder, immobility, or medication exposure.

\medterm{Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis}-- An older term for juvenile idiopathic arthritis, chronic inflammatory arthritis beginning
before adulthood.

\medterm{Juvenile Xanthogranuloma}-- A benign non-Langerhans-cell histiocytic disorder that most often presents in infancy or
early childhood as a yellow-red papule or nodule. Skin lesions are usually self-limited,
but ocular or visceral involvement can occur and requires appropriate assessment.

\medterm{Juxta-articular}-- Located next to or close to a joint.

\medterm{Juxtaglomerular Apparatus}-- Specialized structure located at the vascular pole of the glomerulus where the distal
tubule comes into contact with the afferent arteriole. Composed of macula densa cells in
the tubular wall, juxtaglomerular cells in the afferent arteriolar wall that produce
renin, and extraglomerular mesangial cells.

\medterm{Juxtavesical}-- Located next to or near the urinary bladder.

\medterm{Jet Lag}-- Temporary sleep and circadian disruption after rapid travel across time zones.

\medterm{Jaw Fracture}-- A break in the upper or lower jawbone.

\medterm{Jackknife Position}-- A prone position in which the operating table is flexed at the hips, raising the buttocks and lowering the head and legs.

\medterm{Jaw Tumors and Cysts}-- Abnormal growths or fluid-filled sacs that develop in the jawbone or nearby tissues.

\medterm{Jellyfish Stings}-- Skin injury caused by contact with jellyfish tentacles, which release venom and may cause pain or systemic symptoms.

\medterm{Jet Lag Disorder}-- Temporary sleep and circadian disruption after rapid travel across multiple time zones.

\medterm{Jaccoud Arthropathy}-- A deforming but usually nonerosive arthropathy, classically associated with previous rheumatic fever or systemic lupus erythematosus, causing reducible ulnar deviation and other hand deformities.

\medterm{Junctional Rhythm}-- A cardiac rhythm arising from the atrioventricular junction rather than the sinoatrial node, usually with a narrow QRS complex and a slow or absent preceding atrial impulse.

\medterm{Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy}-- A generalized epilepsy syndrome that typically begins in adolescence and causes myoclonic jerks, often after awakening, with possible generalized tonic-clonic or absence seizures.

\medterm{Juvenile Polyposis Syndrome}-- An inherited disorder characterized by multiple hamartomatous polyps in the gastrointestinal tract and an increased risk of colorectal and other gastrointestinal cancers.

\medterm{Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction}--A short-lived systemic reaction, with fever, chills, headache, muscle aches, and worsening of existing symptoms, that may occur soon after antibiotic treatment of certain bacterial infections, especially spirochaetal infections.

\medterm{Pre-hepatic Jaundice (Haemolytic Jaundice)}--Jaundice caused by excessive breakdown of red blood cells before bilirubin reaches the liver, producing increased unconjugated bilirubin.

\medterm{Hepatic Jaundice (Hepatocellular Jaundice)}--Jaundice caused by impaired processing or excretion of bilirubin by damaged liver cells.

\medterm{Post-hepatic Jaundice (Obstructive Jaundice)}--Jaundice caused by obstruction of bile flow after bilirubin has been processed by the liver, commonly from gallstones, strictures, or tumours.

\medterm{Kernicterus (Nuclear Jaundice)}--Brain injury caused by deposition of excessive unconjugated bilirubin in the basal ganglia and other brain regions, usually in a severely jaundiced newborn.

\medterm{Mandible (Lower Jaw)}--The large movable bone forming the lower jaw and carrying the lower teeth.

\medterm{Maxilla (Upper Jaw)}--The paired bones forming the upper jaw, the front part of the hard palate, and parts of the walls of the nose and eye sockets.

\medterm{Jaw Jerk Reflex}--A stretch reflex of the jaw muscles elicited by tapping the chin while the mouth is slightly open; an exaggerated response may indicate an upper motor-neuron lesion above the pons.

\medterm{Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)}--The height of the jugular venous column above the right atrium, used clinically as an indirect estimate of right atrial pressure and fluid status.

\medterm{Neuromuscular Junction (Myoneural Junction)}--The specialized synapse where a motor nerve ending communicates with a skeletal-muscle fibre to initiate contraction.
