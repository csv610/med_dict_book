\medterm{Juvenile Xanthogranuloma} A rare, usually harmless growth of immune cells in the skin of an infant or young child, appearing as a firm red, yellow, or brown bump. It often fades without treatment, but rarely affects the eye or internal organs.

\medterm{Juxta-Articular} Located next to a joint. The term may describe bone, soft tissue, swelling, a cyst, or a deposit beside a joint rather than within the joint itself.

\medterm{Juxtaglomerular Apparatus} A group of specialized kidney cells that senses blood flow and salt levels and releases renin. It helps regulate blood pressure, blood volume, and the body’s salt balance.

\medterm{Juxtaglomerular Cells} Specialized cells in the kidney that release renin when blood flow or salt delivery to the kidney is low. Renin helps regulate blood pressure and the body's salt and fluid balance.

\synonyms
JG Cells; Granular Cells

\medterm{Juxtaglomerular Cell Tumor} A rare kidney tumor that releases renin, a hormone that helps control blood pressure. It can cause high blood pressure and low blood potassium.
\synonyms
Reninoma; Juxtaglomerular Renin-Secreting Tumor

\medterm{Juxtavesical} Next to the urinary bladder. The term often describes the lower part of a ureter as it approaches the bladder.
\medterm{J Wave} A notch or slur at the end of the QRS complex, or a positive deflection near the junction of the QRS complex and ST segment on an electrocardiogram. An Osborn wave is a prominent J wave classically associated with hypothermia. Similar terminal QRS changes may occur in early-repolarization patterns and other conditions.
