\chapter{Pocket Dictionary: Q Terms}

\medterm{Q Fever}-- A zoonotic disease caused by Coxiella burnetii, an obligate intracellular Gram-negative
bacterium. Q fever is transmitted to humans primarily by inhalation of contaminated
aerosols from infected livestock (cattle, sheep, goats) and their products (placenta,
birth fluids, urine, feces, milk). Incubation: 2--3 weeks.

\medterm{Q Wave}-- The negative deflection that follows the P wave in some electrocardiographic leads,
representing initial ventricular depolarization.

\medterm{QALY}-- Quality-adjusted life year. Measure of health outcome incorporating quality of life.

\medterm{QRS Complex}-- The deflection representing ventricular depolarization, consisting of a Q wave (initial
downward deflection), R wave (upward deflection), and S wave (downward deflection after
R). Normal duration is 0.06-0.10 seconds (1.5-2.5 small boxes). Widened QRS (>0.12 s)
indicates bundle branch block, ventricular rhythm, or hyperkalemia.

\medterm{QT Interval}-- The time from the onset of the QRS complex to the end of the T wave, representing total
ventricular depolarization and repolarization. Normal QT is 0.35-0.44 seconds. The
corrected QT (QTc) adjusts for heart rate using Bazett's formula: QTc = QT / square root
of RR interval.

\medterm{QT Prolongation}-- An electrocardiographic finding characterized by prolongation of the QT interval,
representing the time from ventricular depolarization to repolarization, which
predisposes to the potentially fatal arrhythmia torsades de pointes. The corrected QT
interval should be less than 440 milliseconds in men and 460 milliseconds in women.

\medterm{Quad Screen (Maternal Serum Screening)}-- Second-trimester prenatal screening (15-22 weeks) for fetal aneuploidy and neural tube
defects. Measures 4 markers: AFP (alpha-fetoprotein: elevated in NTD, low in Down
syndrome), hCG (elevated in Down syndrome), uE3 (unconjugated estriol: low in Down
syndrome), and inhibin A (elevated in Down syndrome).

\medterm{Quadrant}-- One of four sections of the abdomen (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) used for clinical description.
RUQ: liver, gallbladder, duodenum, right kidney. LUQ: stomach, spleen, left kidney,
pancreas tail. RLQ: appendix, cecum, right ovary. LLQ: sigmoid colon, left ovary.

\medterm{Quadrantectomy}-- Surgical removal of one quadrant of an organ, most commonly a breast-conserving
operation removing a breast tumour with a surrounding margin of tissue. The exact
procedure and need for additional treatment depend on pathology and clinical staging.

\medterm{Quadriceps Femoris}-- The four-headed muscle group on the anterior thigh consisting of rectus femoris and the
three vasti. It extends the knee; rectus femoris also flexes the hip, and the group
contributes to standing, walking, and shock absorption.

\medterm{Quadriceps Femoris Muscle}-- Four-muscle anterior thigh group: rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis,
vastus intermedius, all inserting on the tibial tuberosity via the patellar ligament.
Innervated by femoral nerve (L2-L4). Primary knee extensor for walking, running, rising
from seated. VMO is important for patellar tracking.

\medterm{Quadriceps tendon ruptures}-- A tear of the tendon connecting the quadriceps muscle to the patula, causing inability
to extend the knee and requiring surgical repair, typically in middle-aged or older
patients.

\medterm{Quadriplegia}-- Paralysis of all four limbs and trunk, resulting from a spinal cord lesion at the
cervical level or a bilateral brainstem lesion. Common causes include traumatic spinal
cord injury, stroke, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord tumors, and demyelinating disease.

\medterm{Quadruplet Pregnancy}-- A pregnancy involving four fetuses. It carries increased risks of preterm birth, fetal
growth restriction, hypertensive and metabolic complications, and maternal morbidity and
therefore requires specialized multidisciplinary antenatal care.

\medterm{Quality Assurance}-- Systematic efforts to enhance care. See quality improvement.

\medterm{Quality Control}-- Daily, weekly, monthly tests ensure system performance. Uniformity, energy, sensitivity,
spatial resolution, count rate performance.

\medterm{Quality Control (Laboratory)}-- Procedures using control materials, calibration, equipment checks, and documented review
to ensure that a laboratory method remains accurate and precise during routine testing.

\medterm{Quality Improvement}-- Systematic efforts to improve healthcare. Methods: PDSA cycles, Lean, Six Sigma.
Outcomes: safety, effectiveness, patient-centeredness.

\medterm{Quality of Life}-- Perception of well-being including physical, psychological, social, spiritual domains.
See geriatrics.

\medterm{Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY)}-- A health-economic measure combining length of life with health-related quality of life
on a scale where 1 represents full health and 0 represents death. It is used to compare
cost-effectiveness across interventions.

\medterm{Quantification}-- Measurement of radiotracer uptake or clearance. SUV (PET), clearance rates (renal),
ejection fraction (cardiac). Essential for treatment monitoring.

\medterm{Quantitative PET}-- Measurement of absolute tracer concentration. SUV, kinetic modeling.

\medterm{Quarantine}-- Separation of exposed individuals to prevent disease spread. For symptomatic incubation
period.

\medterm{Quickening}-- The first perception of fetal movement by the mother, typically felt between 16-20 weeks
in multiparous women and 18-22 weeks in nulliparous women. Described initially as a
subtle fluttering sensation that becomes stronger and more regular as pregnancy
progresses.

\medterm{Quiescent}-- Inactive or dormant state of disease. Example: quiescent ulcerative colitis (no symptoms
but histologic inflammation may persist).

\medterm{Quinine}-- An alkaloid derived from cinchona bark, with antimalarial, antipyretic, and skeletal
muscle relaxant properties. Quinine is a blood schizonticide with activity against all
Plasmodium species, but resistance has emerged in P. falciparum.

\medterm{Quinsy}-- Peritonsillar abscess, also known as quinsy, is a collection of pus in the peritonsillar
space between the tonsillar capsule and the superior pharyngeal constrictor muscle,
typically developing as a complication of acute tonsillitis, and it is the most common
deep neck space infection. See Peritonsillar Abscess.

\medterm{Quorum Sensing}-- Cell-density-dependent bacterial communication system in which bacteria produce and
detect small signaling molecules (autoinducers—acyl-homoserine lactones in
gram-negative, autoinducing peptides in gram-positive, AI-2 inter-species) to coordinate
population-wide behaviors.
