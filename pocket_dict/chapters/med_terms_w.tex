
\medterm{Waardenburg Syndrome}-- A group of inherited disorders characterized by sensorineural hearing loss and
pigmentary abnormalities of the eyes, hair, or skin. Dystopia canthorum, limb
abnormalities, or intestinal aganglionosis may occur in particular genetic forms.

\medterm{WAGR Syndrome}-- A contiguous-gene deletion syndrome involving Wilms tumor predisposition, aniridia,
genitourinary anomalies, and variable developmental delay. It usually involves
chromosome 11p13 and requires tumor surveillance and multidisciplinary care.

\medterm{Waist Circumference}-- A measure of abdominal adiposity taken at a standardized site. Larger measurements are associated
with increased cardiometabolic risk, but clinically used thresholds vary by sex, ancestry,
and guideline; it complements, rather than replaces, body-mass index and other assessment.

\medterm{Waldenstrom Macroglobulinemia}-- A rare B-cell lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma characterized by bone marrow infiltration by
lymphoplasmacytic cells and production of monoclonal IgM paraprotein. It accounts for
less than 2 percent of hematologic malignancies and typically affects older adults
(median age 63-65 years).

\medterm{Waldenstrom's macroglobulinemia}-- A lymphoplasmacytic cancer of the bone marrow that produces excess IgM and may cause
anemia, enlarged lymph nodes, or hyperviscosity symptoms.

\medterm{Waldenström's macroglobulinemia}-- An indolent B-cell lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma characterized by the production of a
monoclonal IgM paraprotein, causing hyperviscosity syndrome with visual disturbances,
bleeding, and neurological symptoms. It typically affects elderly patients and involves
the bone marrow, lymph nodes, spleen, and liver.

\medterm{Walker}-- Walking aid for stability. Types: standard, rollator (wheels), knee walkers.

\medterm{Wallerian Degeneration}-- The breakdown of the portion of a nerve fiber separated from its cell body after nerve injury.

\medterm{Walking Speed}-- The rate at which a person walks, often measured over a defined distance. It can provide information about mobility and function, but no single speed determines disability in every person.

\medterm{Wandering Spleen}-- Abnormal mobility of the spleen caused by laxity or absence of its supporting ligaments.
It may be asymptomatic or cause abdominal pain, splenic enlargement, torsion,
infarction, or acute abdomen.

\medterm{Warfarin Management}-- Management of treatment with warfarin, a vitamin-K antagonist that reduces production of several
clotting factors. Dosing is adjusted using the INR and depends on the indication, bleeding
risk, medicines, diet, liver function, and genetic factors. Regular monitoring and attention
to interactions are essential.

\medterm{Warthin Tumor}-- A benign salivary-gland neoplasm composed of oncocytic epithelial and lymphoid tissue,
usually arising in the parotid gland. It is strongly associated with tobacco exposure,
may be multifocal or bilateral, and is generally managed according to symptoms,
diagnosis, and growth.

\medterm{Warts}-- Small, usually benign growths of the skin or mucous membranes caused by human papillomavirus infection.

\medterm{Warty Dyskeratoma}-- A benign follicular keratinizing tumor presenting as a solitary umbilicated papule or
nodule, often on the head or neck. Histology shows a cup-shaped invagination filled with
keratin and acantholytic dyskeratotic cells.

\medterm{Watchful Waiting}-- Planned observation without immediate active treatment, with clinical review and treatment
if symptoms develop or the condition progresses. It may be used for selected cancers and other slowly progressive
conditions.

\medterm{Water Birth (Underwater Delivery)}-- Delivery of a baby while the birthing person remains in a tub or pool of warm water.
It may reduce perceived labor pain for some people, but evidence about delivery-related
benefits and risks is limited. Eligibility, monitoring, and facility policies determine whether
it is offered.

\medterm{Water Flosser}-- Device using water stream to clean between teeth. Adjunct to brushing and flossing.

\medterm{Water Intoxication}-- A potentially life-threatening dilutional hyponatremia caused by excessive water intake or
retention relative to the body's ability to excrete free water. Severe cases can cause headache,
confusion, seizures, or coma.

\medterm{Water Soluble Toxins}-- Toxic substances that dissolve readily in water. Their elimination depends on the
substance, kidney function, metabolism, protein binding, and other properties; some may be removed by dialysis.

\medterm{Water-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted via contaminated water. Examples: cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A.

\medterm{Water-Deprivation Test}-- A supervised dynamic test assessing the kidney's ability to concentrate urine when
free-water intake is withheld, followed when appropriate by desmopressin. It helps
distinguish central diabetes insipidus, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, and primary
polydipsia but requires careful monitoring for hypernatremia and dehydration.

\medterm{Watery Eyes}-- Excessive tearing caused by irritation, allergy, infection, dry-eye reflex tearing, or
blockage of the tear-drainage system. Persistent unilateral tearing or pain requires
ocular assessment.

\medterm{Waxy Casts}-- Broad refractile cylindrical structures with smooth appearance indicating severe chronic
kidney disease. Form from degeneration of granular casts in dilated tubules. Broad
diameter reflects formation in dilated collecting ducts. Relatively specific for
advanced CKD with significant tubular atrophy.

\medterm{Weakness}-- Reduced muscle strength or ability to generate force, which may be generalized or
limited to one body region. Causes include nerve or muscle disease, stroke, spinal
disorders, metabolic abnormalities, infection, and deconditioning; sudden one-sided
weakness is an emergency.

\medterm{Wear Debris}-- Particles generated by mechanical abrasion or degradation of implant materials.
Macrophage-mediated inflammation around debris can produce osteolysis, implant
loosening, and failure of an arthroplasty.

\medterm{Weaver Syndrome}-- A congenital overgrowth and neurodevelopmental disorder associated with pathogenic
variants in EZH2. It may cause prenatal overgrowth, advanced bone age, characteristic
facial features, hypotonia, developmental delay, and variable intellectual disability.

\medterm{Weber Test}-- The Weber test is a screening test for lateralization of hearing that uses a vibrating
tuning fork (typically 512 Hz) placed on the midline of the skull (vertex or forehead)
to determine whether the sound is heard equally in both ears or louder in one ear,
providing information about the type and side of hearing loss.

\medterm{Wedge resection}-- Surgical removal of a wedge-shaped piece of tissue, typically lung or breast. Used for
diagnosis or treatment of localized lesions. Less extensive than lobectomy.

\medterm{Wegener's Granulomatosis}-- Wegener's granulomatosis, now called granulomatosis with polyangiitis, is a systemic
vasculitis characterized by necrotizing granulomatous inflammation of the upper and
lower respiratory tracts and pauci-immune necrotizing glomerulonephritis.

\medterm{Weight Gain in Pregnancy}-- Recommended gestational weight gain according to the Institute of Medicine (IOM)
guidelines, based on prepregnancy BMI. Underweight (BMI <18.5): 28-40 lbs (12.5-18 kg).
Normal weight (BMI 18.5-24.9): 25-35 lbs (11.5-16 kg). Overweight (BMI 25-29.9): 15-25
lbs (7--11.5 kg). Obese (BMI \(\geq 30\)): 11--20 lbs (5--9 kg).

\medterm{Weight Loss}-- The intentional reduction of body weight, typically through a combination of dietary
modification, increased physical activity, and behavioral changes. Achieving sustainable
weight loss requires creating a consistent calorie deficit while preserving lean muscle
mass through adequate protein intake and strength training.

\medterm{Weight Loss and Management}-- Weight loss and management encompasses the ongoing process of achieving and maintaining
a healthy body weight through sustainable lifestyle modifications.

\medterm{Weight Loss Medications}-- Pharmacologic agents approved for the treatment of obesity when used in conjunction with
lifestyle modifications.

\medterm{Weil's Disease}-- A zoonotic bacterial infection caused by spirochaetes of the genus Leptospira,
transmitted through contact with water or soil contaminated with the urine of infected
animals (rodents, cattle, dogs). See Leptospirosis.

\medterm{Wen (Epidermoid Cyst)}-- A common benign cyst beneath the skin that is lined by squamous epithelium and contains keratin debris.

\medterm{Werner Syndrome}-- An autosomal-recessive progeroid disorder caused most often by pathogenic variants in
the WRN gene. It typically presents after puberty with premature ageing, short stature,
bilateral cataracts, scleroderma-like skin changes, thin limbs, diabetes, osteoporosis,
vascular disease, and increased risk of malignancy.

\medterm{Wernicke's Encephalopathy}-- An acute neurologic disorder caused by thiamine deficiency, classically involving confusion,
eye-movement abnormalities, and gait or coordination impairment. It requires prompt
thiamine treatment.

\medterm{Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome}-- A neurologic disorder caused by thiamine deficiency, combining acute or previous
Wernicke encephalopathy with persistent memory impairment and confabulation in some patients. Causes include alcohol-use
disorder, malnutrition, and prolonged vomiting.

\medterm{West Nile Encephalitis}-- Inflammation of the brain caused by neuroinvasive West Nile virus, transmitted mainly by mosquitoes. It may cause
fever, headache, confusion, tremor, weakness, seizures, or coma. There is no specific routine antiviral treatment;
severe illness requires hospital-based supportive care.


\medterm{West Nile Virus}-- A mosquito-borne flavivirus that can cause asymptomatic infection, a febrile illness, or neuroinvasive
disease such as meningitis, encephalitis, or acute flaccid paralysis. Risk of severe disease
is higher in older adults and people with certain medical conditions.

\medterm{West Syndrome}-- An infantile epileptic encephalopathy characterized by epileptic spasms, hypsarrhythmia
on electroencephalography, and developmental regression or arrest. Causes include
structural brain disease, genetic disorders, metabolic disease, or acquired brain
injury.

\medterm{Westermark Sign}-- A focal area of decreased pulmonary vascularity distal to an occluded pulmonary artery segment on a chest
radiograph. It may occur with pulmonary embolism but is neither sensitive nor specific.

\medterm{Wharton Jelly}-- A gelatinous substance (mucopolysaccharide matrix) surrounding the umbilical cord
vessels, composed primarily of hyaluronic acid and chondroitin sulfate. It protects the
umbilical vessels from compression and kinking during fetal movement and uterine
contractions.

\medterm{Wheal}-- Raised, itchy swelling of skin (urticaria/hives). Caused by mast cell degranulation and
histamine release. Examples: insect bite, allergic reaction, dermatographism.

\medterm{Wheelchair}-- Mobility device for patients with walking difficulties. Types: manual, power.
Components: seat, backrest, wheels, footrests, armrests. Assessment: seating evaluation,
pressure relief, posture. Training: mobility, transfers, safety.

\medterm{Wheelchair Seating and Positioning}-- Assessment and configuration of a wheelchair, cushion, supports, and posture to optimize
stability, propulsion, comfort, function, pressure distribution, and prevention of
musculoskeletal deformity.

\medterm{Wheelchair Skills}-- Training for wheelchair users: propulsion, maneuvering, obstacles, slopes, stairs,
transfers.

\medterm{Wheezes}-- High-pitched, continuous, musical sounds heard primarily during expiration, caused by
airway narrowing. They are a hallmark of asthma and COPD. Expiratory wheezing indicates
intrathoracic airway obstruction; inspiratory wheezing indicates extrathoracic
obstruction.

\medterm{Wheezing}-- A continuous, high-pitched, musical adventitious lung sound produced by turbulent
airflow through narrowed or compressed lower airways during expiration (and occasionally
inspiration). Pathophysiology: bronchospasm, mucosal edema, airway inflammation, or
intraluminal secretions.

\medterm{Whiplash Injury}-- An acceleration-deceleration injury to the cervical spine, most commonly resulting from
rear-end motor vehicle collisions. Mechanism: sudden hyperextension followed by
hyperflexion of the neck causes stretching or tearing of cervical muscles, ligaments,
facet joint capsules, and annulus fibrosus of intervertebral discs.

\medterm{Whipple Disease}-- A rare systemic bacterial infection caused by Tropheryma whipplei, affecting the small
intestine and multiple organ systems. Presents with arthralgias (prodromal, years before
GI symptoms), weight loss, chronic diarrhea, steatorrhea, and abdominal pain.

\medterm{Whipple Procedure}-- Pancreaticoduodenectomy for cancers of the pancreatic head, distal bile duct, or
ampulla. Involves removal of the pancreatic head, duodenum, distal bile duct, and
gallbladder with three anastomoses: hepaticojejunostomy, pancreaticojejunostomy, and
gastrojejunostomy. The pylorus-preserving variant maintains the entire stomach.

\medterm{Whipple Triad}-- A clinical framework supporting the diagnosis of symptomatic hypoglycemia: symptoms consistent with
hypoglycemia, a documented low blood-glucose concentration, and improvement after the
glucose level is raised. It is used when evaluating possible hypoglycemia, including
suspected insulinoma.

\medterm{Whipworm (Trichuriasis)}-- Intestinal infection caused by the soil-transmitted helminth Trichuris trichiura
(whipworm), endemic in tropical and subtropical regions with poor sanitation. Humans
acquire infection by ingesting embryonated eggs from contaminated soil, food, or water.

\medterm{Whispered Pectoriloquy}-- The ability to hear whispered words clearly through the stethoscope over consolidated
lung tissue. It is a sign of pulmonary consolidation. Normally, whispered words are
barely audible over the lung fields. It is one of the classic signs of pneumonia on
physical examination.

\medterm{White Blood Cell Count}-- WBC count: the total number of leukocytes per unit volume (normal $4.0$--$11.0\times10^9/\mathrm{L}$),
part of the CBC with differential (neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils,
basophils). Leukocytosis: infection (neutrophilia), inflammation, stress, steroids,
leukemia (marked), corticosteroids.

\medterm{White Blood Cell Labeling}-- Nuclear medicine technique labeling WBCs with Tc-99m. Used for infection localization.

\medterm{White Cell}-- The cells of the immune system that defend the body against infection and foreign
substances. Produced in the bone marrow from hematopoietic stem cells.

\medterm{White Cell Count}-- A laboratory measurement of the number of white blood cells (leukocytes) in a given
volume of blood, part of the complete blood count (CBC). Normal range: 4,000-11,000
cells/\(\mu\mathrm{L}\) (4-11 × 10\(^9\)/L). Differential white cell count measures the proportion
of each leukocyte type: neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, basophils.

\medterm{White Infarction}-- White infarction, also known as anemic infarction, occurs in solid organs with
end-arterial circulation where collateral blood supply is limited, resulting in pale,
well-demarcated areas of coagulative necrosis.

\medterm{White Matter}-- Regions of the brain and spinal cord composed primarily of myelinated nerve fibers,
responsible for transmitting signals between brain regions.

\medterm{Whites (Leucorrhoea)}-- A non-bloody, physiological or pathological vaginal discharge. Physiological
leucorrhoea: normal, clear to whitish, odourless discharge that varies with the
menstrual cycle (increased at ovulation, pre-menstrually, during pregnancy, sexual
arousal, and with oral contraceptive use).

\medterm{Whole Exome Sequencing}-- Sequencing the protein-coding regions of the genome, which make up a small fraction of total DNA.
It can identify variants associated with disease but does not detect all genetic causes;
its yield depends on the disorder, test quality, and interpretation.

\medterm{Whole Genome Sequencing}-- Determining complete DNA sequence of an individual's genome. Cost decreasing, clinical
applications expanding. Identifies coding and non-coding variants.

\medterm{Whole-Body Scan}-- Acquisition of images covering entire body. Used for oncology staging, infection
localization. PET/CT typically neck-to-mid-thigh or full body.

\medterm{Whole-Brain Radiation Therapy}-- Radiation directed at the entire brain, used for multiple brain metastases,
leptomeningeal disease, and prophylactic cranial irradiation in small cell lung cancer
and acute lymphoblastic leukemia.

\medterm{Whole-Genome Sequencing}-- Sequencing of nearly the complete nuclear genome, including coding and noncoding
regions, together with mitochondrial DNA when covered by the assay. It can detect a
broad range of variants but generates substantial interpretation and incidental-finding
challenges.

\medterm{Whooping Cough}-- A highly contagious bacterial infection caused by Bordetella pertussis, producing
characteristic paroxysmal cough with whooping inspiratory sound. See Pertussis.

\medterm{Williams Syndrome}-- A microdeletion syndrome involving the elastin gene on chromosome 7q11.23. Features:
elfin facies, supravalvular aortic stenosis, hypercalcemia, intellectual disability with
strong verbal skills, and overly friendly personality. Often associated with connective
tissue abnormalities.

\medterm{Wilms Tumor}-- A malignant embryonal tumor of the kidney in children. It may present as an abdominal mass, pain,
hematuria, or an incidental finding and may be associated with syndromes such as WAGR or
Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome. Treatment and prognosis depend on stage and tumor biology.

\medterm{Wilms' tumor (Nephroblastoma)}-- An embryonal malignant tumor of the kidney that usually occurs in young children. It may be
associated with congenital syndromes, and treatment and prognosis depend on tumor stage,
histology, and molecular features.

\medterm{Wilson Disease}-- Autosomal recessive copper metabolism disorder (ATP7B gene). Copper accumulation in
liver, brain, cornea. Kayser-Fleischer rings, hepatopathy, movement disorder,
psychiatric symptoms. Treatment: chelation (penicillamine, trientine), zinc, liver
transplant.

\medterm{Wilson's disease}-- An autosomal recessive disorder of copper metabolism caused by mutations in the ATP7B
gene, leading to impaired biliary copper excretion and toxic copper accumulation in the
liver, brain, cornea, and other organs.

\medterm{Windowing}-- Adjusting CT image brightness and contrast to optimize visualization of specific
tissues. Bone window: wide window width, high level. Soft tissue window: narrow width,
mid level. Lung window: wide width, low level.

\medterm{Windpipe}-- The windpipe, a 10-12 cm fibrocartilaginous tube from the larynx (C6) to the carina
(T4/T5) where it bifurcates into main bronchi. See Trachea.

\medterm{Wireless Motility Capsule}-- Ingestible capsule that measures pH, pressure, and temperature throughout the
gastrointestinal tract. Provides regional transit times through the stomach, small
bowel, and colon in a single test. The pH sensor identifies transition points between
segments based on characteristic pH changes.

\medterm{Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome}-- X-linked recessive disorder (WAS gene mutation, encodes WASP protein, regulates actin
polymerization in hematopoietic cells). Triad: microthrombocytopenia (small platelets,
<70 fl), eczema, recurrent infections (encapsulated bacteria, viruses). Combined
immunodeficiency with progressive T-cell lymphopenia.

\medterm{Withdrawal Reflex}-- A protective polysynaptic spinal reflex in which a painful stimulus causes flexion
(withdrawal) of the stimulated limb, removing it from the source of injury, with
reciprocal extension of the contralateral limb (crossed extensor reflex) to maintain
posture.

\medterm{Withdrawal Symptoms}-- Physical or psychological symptoms that occur when a person stops or reduces a substance or medicine
after the body has adapted to it. The symptoms depend on the substance, duration of use,
and the person’s health.

\medterm{Withdrawal Syndrome}-- A predictable set of signs and symptoms that occur when a drug is discontinued or the
dose is reduced after chronic use, reflecting the physiological rebound from drug
adaptation.

\medterm{Wolff-Parkinson-White Syndrome}-- A pre-excitation syndrome caused by an accessory pathway (bundle of Kent) connecting
atria and ventricles, bypassing the AV node. ECG shows short PR interval (<0.12 s),
delta wave (slurred upstroke of QRS), and widened QRS.

\medterm{Wolfram Syndrome}-- Autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the WFS1 gene encoding wolframin.
Classic triad of diabetes mellitus, optic atrophy, and diabetes insipidus. Additional
features include sensorineural deafness, neurodegeneration, and psychiatric illness.
Onset of diabetes typically in the first decade.

\medterm{Wolman's Disease}-- A severe lysosomal acid-lipase deficiency presenting in infancy with hepatosplenomegaly,
adrenal calcification, malabsorption, vomiting, diarrhea, and failure to thrive. It is a
severe form of lysosomal acid-lipase deficiency and can be fatal early without
treatment.

\medterm{Wood Lamp Examination}-- Inspection of skin under long-wave ultraviolet A light in a darkened room. It can
accentuate pigmentary differences and produce characteristic fluorescence in selected
infections, but findings are not independently diagnostic.

\medterm{Woods Corkscrew Maneuver}-- An internal rotation maneuver for shoulder dystocia where the operator inserts a hand
and rotates the fetal shoulders 180 degrees by applying pressure to the posterior aspect
of the anterior shoulder. This disimpacts the shoulder from behind the symphysis pubis.
Can be repeated if the shoulder re-impacts in the new position.

\medterm{Woods Screw Maneuver}-- A rotational maneuver used during shoulder dystocia to rotate the fetal shoulders and assist
delivery.

\medterm{Word Recognition Scoring}-- An audiologic test measuring how accurately a person repeats or identifies spoken words at a
comfortable listening level.

\medterm{Word Salad}-- Severe disorganized speech consisting of a jumble of words and phrases that lack meaning
or logical connection. It is a hallmark of formal thought disorder in schizophrenia and
represents the most severe form of disorganized speech. It reflects profound disruption
of language processing.

\medterm{Wound}-- An injury in which tissue is damaged by trauma, surgery, pressure, heat, chemicals, or other causes. Wounds may be open or
closed and vary in depth, contamination, and effect on nearby structures.

\medterm{Wound Care}-- Management of traumatic wounds: irrigation, debridement, closure (sutures, staples,
glue), dressing. Tetanus prophylaxis based on vaccination history and wound type.
Antibiotics for contaminated wounds, animal/human bites, or signs of infection. Healing:
primary, secondary, tertiary intention.

\medterm{Wound Complication}-- A problem that develops during healing, such as infection, separation of wound edges, bleeding, fluid
collection, tissue death, or excessive scarring. Increasing pain, redness, swelling, drainage,
fever, or wound opening should be assessed promptly.

\medterm{Wound Dehiscence}-- Separation of the layers of a surgical wound after closure, most commonly occurring
within 3-14 days postoperatively.

\medterm{Wound Healing}-- Complex process of tissue repair. Phases: hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation,
remodeling. Implant integration depends on healing.

\medterm{Wound Healing Phases}-- Wound healing involves overlapping stages of hemostasis, inflammation, tissue formation, epithelial coverage, and remodeling. The
duration and result depend on the wound, blood supply, infection, nutrition, and overall
health.

\medterm{Wound Infection}-- Infection of a wound by microorganisms, causing findings such as increasing pain, redness, warmth,
swelling, pus, fever, or delayed healing.

\medterm{Wound VAC}-- Vacuum-assisted closure for wound healing. Foam dressing connected to vacuum pump.
Removes exudate, reduces edema, promotes granulation. Used for complex wounds, surgical
sites, pressure ulcers.

\medterm{Wrisberg Ganglion}-- A sensory ganglion associated with the intermediate nerve of the facial nerve.

\medterm{Wrist Drop}-- Inability to extend the wrist and fingers, usually caused by radial nerve dysfunction.

\medterm{Wrist Joint}-- Condyloid synovial joint between distal radius and articular disc proximally and
scaphoid/lunate/triquetrum distally. Allows flexion, extension, radial/ulnar deviation,
circumduction. The triangular fibrocartilage complex absorbs ulnar side load.

\medterm{Wrist Pain}-- Pain in or around the wrist, commonly caused by sprain, fracture, tendinitis, arthritis,
or nerve compression such as carpal tunnel syndrome. Persistent pain, deformity,
numbness, or weakness requires assessment.

\medterm{Wuchereria}-- A genus of filarial nematodes that includes Wuchereria bancrofti, a major cause of
lymphatic filariasis. Infection is transmitted by mosquitoes and may cause lymphangitis,
chronic lymphedema, hydrocele, and elephantiasis.
\medterm{Wheat Allergy}-- An immune reaction to proteins in wheat that may cause skin, digestive, or breathing symptoms.
\medterm{Whiplash}-- A neck injury caused by a rapid back-and-forth movement of the head.
\medterm{Whipple's Disease}-- A rare bacterial infection that can impair intestinal absorption and affect multiple organs.
\medterm{Wheeze}-- A high-pitched whistling sound produced by narrowed airways during breathing.
\medterm{Wright Stain}-- A blood-cell stain used to identify and classify cells in a blood smear.
