
\medterm{D and C}-- Dilation and curettage, a procedure in which the cervix is dilated and tissue is removed
from the uterine cavity for diagnosis, treatment, or management of pregnancy-related
conditions.

\medterm{D and E}-- Dilation and evacuation, a surgical procedure in which the cervix is dilated and uterine
contents are removed by suction and instruments.

\medterm{D-Dimer}-- A blood marker produced when cross-linked fibrin is broken down. It may be elevated in thrombosis and in
many other conditions. A negative result can help exclude venous thromboembolism in
appropriately selected patients with a low or intermediate pretest probability; the cutoff
depends on the assay and clinical setting.

\medterm{D\&C Instruments}-- Instruments used to dilate the cervix and remove tissue from the uterus during dilation and curettage.

\medterm{Dabigatran}-- A direct oral anticoagulant that reversibly inhibits thrombin (factor IIa). Used for
stroke prevention in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation and treatment/prophylaxis of venous
thromboembolism. Fixed dosing, rapid onset, predictable pharmacokinetics, no routine
monitoring. Prodrug requiring acid environment; capsules must not be opened.

\medterm{Dacrocytes (Teardrop Cells)}-- Teardrop-shaped red blood cells formed as erythrocytes are squeezed and deformed while
exiting a fibrotic or infiltrated bone marrow space. Characteristic feature of Primary
Myelofibrosis (JAK2 V617F mutation), bone marrow infiltration (myelophthisic anemia from
metastatic cancer, granulomas, or leukemia), and severe thalassemia.

\medterm{Dacryoadenitis}-- Inflammation of the lacrimal gland, presenting as tender swelling in the superolateral
aspect of the upper eyelid with characteristic S-shaped ptosis. It can be acute or
chronic in presentation.

\medterm{Dacryocystitis}-- Inflammation of the lacrimal sac, most commonly caused by obstruction of the
nasolacrimal duct leading to stagnation of tears and secondary bacterial infection
(Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae).

\medterm{Dacryocystography}-- An imaging study in which contrast material is introduced into the lacrimal drainage system to identify narrowing or obstruction.

\medterm{Dacryocystorhinostomy}-- A surgical procedure creating a passage between the lacrimal sac and the nasal cavity to
bypass an obstructed nasolacrimal duct.

\medterm{Dacryostenosis}-- Narrowing or obstruction of the lacrimal drainage passages, which can cause excessive
tearing and recurrent infection of the lacrimal sac.

\medterm{Dactylitis}-- Inflammation of an entire digit (finger or toe), producing a diffuse, fusiform swelling
often described as sausage-shaped.

\medterm{Dactylosymphysis}-- Abnormal fusion or webbing of adjacent digits, usually present from birth.

\medterm{Daily Value}-- A reference amount of a nutrient used on food labels to help compare a serving with usual daily nutrient needs. Values are based on a 2,000-calorie diet unless otherwise stated.

\medterm{Daltonism}-- Color-vision deficiency, especially difficulty distinguishing certain colors because of
abnormal or absent retinal cone function.

\medterm{DALY}-- Disability-adjusted life year, a measure combining years of healthy life lost because of premature death and disability.

\medterm{Damage-Control Resuscitation}-- A trauma strategy prioritizing rapid hemorrhage control, permissive approaches to blood
pressure when appropriate, prevention of hypothermia, correction of coagulopathy, and
balanced blood-product resuscitation rather than large-volume crystalloid
administration.

\medterm{Damage-Control Surgery}-- A staged operative strategy for critically ill or severely injured patients in whom
definitive repair is unsafe because of hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy, or
physiologic instability. Initial surgery controls hemorrhage and contamination, followed
by resuscitation and later definitive reconstruction.

\medterm{Dandruff}-- Scaling and shedding of the scalp skin, commonly associated with seborrheic dermatitis
or dry skin.

\medterm{Dandruff and Scalp Conditions}-- Dandruff is a common scalp condition presenting as flaking, itching, and mild
inflammation caused by overgrowth of the yeast Malassezia, dry skin, or seborrheic
dermatitis. Other scalp conditions include psoriasis, contact dermatitis, ringworm tinea
capitis, and head lice.

\medterm{Dandy Fever}-- Dengue fever, an acute mosquito-borne viral illness causing fever, headache, severe
muscle or joint pain, and sometimes hemorrhage or shock.

\medterm{Dandy-Walker Malformation}-- A congenital hindbrain malformation characterized by hypoplasia or aplasia of the
cerebellar vermis, cystic dilation of the fourth ventricle, and posterior fossa
enlargement with upward displacement of the tentorium, transverse sinuses, and torcular
herophili.

\medterm{Dandy-Walker Syndrome}-- A congenital abnormality involving the cerebellum and the fluid spaces around it, often causing
hydrocephalus or developmental problems.

\medterm{Dane Particle}-- The complete infectious particle of hepatitis B virus, consisting of the viral core and
envelope.

\medterm{Dantrolene}-- A skeletal-muscle relaxant used urgently to treat malignant hyperthermia and sometimes used for
severe muscle spasticity. It reduces calcium release inside muscle cells; liver injury and
muscle weakness are important adverse effects.

\medterm{Dapsone}-- A medicine used for leprosy, dermatitis herpetiformis, and selected bacterial or inflammatory
conditions. It can cause hemolytic anemia, methemoglobinemia, or severe skin reactions;
testing for glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency may be needed before treatment.

\medterm{Daptomycin resistance}-- Resistance to daptomycin can develop through mutations in genes encoding cell membrane
phospholipid synthesis. The mechanism involves changes in the bacterial cell membrane
that reduce daptomycin binding. Resistance is more common in VRE than MRSA. Resistance
can emerge during therapy, particularly in endocarditis and bacteremia.

\medterm{Darier's Disease}-- An inherited skin disorder characterized by abnormal keratinization, greasy crusted
papules, and nail changes, often worsened by heat and sweating.

\medterm{Dark Adaptation}-- The process by which the eyes become increasingly sensitive to dim light after moving
from bright illumination into darkness.

\medterm{Dartos}-- A layer of smooth muscle and connective tissue beneath the skin of the scrotum that
wrinkles the scrotal skin in response to temperature and other stimuli.

\medterm{Darwin's Tubercle}-- A variable small thickening or projection on the rim of the external ear, regarded as a
remnant of an ancestral mammalian ear form.

\medterm{DASH Diet}-- Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. Emphasizes: fruits, vegetables, whole grains,
low-fat dairy, reduced saturated fat, sodium <2300 mg/day. Lowers blood pressure
effectively.

\medterm{Daubenton's Plane}-- An anatomical reference plane extending from the lower margin of the orbit to the upper
margin of the external auditory opening, used in describing skull orientation.

\medterm{Dawn Phenomenon}-- Physiological increase in blood glucose occurring in the early morning hours between
approximately three and eight AM driven by counter-regulatory hormone surges including
growth hormone, cortisol, and catecholamines. More pronounced in patients with diabetes
who lack the compensatory insulin response.

\medterm{De Novo Variant}-- A genetic variant present in an affected individual that was not detected in either
biological parent, usually arising in a parental gamete or early embryogenesis. It may
cause a dominant disorder and can have implications for recurrence risk in future
pregnancies.

\medterm{De Quervain Tendinitis}-- Painful stenosing inflammation of the tendons of the abductor pollicis longus and
extensor pollicis brevis in the first dorsal wrist compartment. It causes radial wrist
pain worsened by thumb movement or grasping and is assessed clinically.

\medterm{De Quervain Tenosynovitis}-- De Quervain tenosynovitis: first dorsal compartment (APL, EPB) stenosis. Clinical:
radial wrist pain, Finkelstein test (thumb in palm, ulnar deviation), swelling over
radial styloid.

\medterm{De-Escalation}-- A set of verbal and nonverbal techniques used to reduce agitation and prevent violence
while preserving dignity and safety. It includes calm communication, appropriate
personal space, clear limits, environmental modification, and offering choices.

\medterm{Dead Space}-- The volume of ventilated air that does not participate in gas exchange. Anatomic dead
space: the conducting airways (nose, pharynx, trachea, bronchi, terminal bronchioles)
that warm, humidify, and filter inspired air but contain no alveoli for gas exchange.
Approximately 150 mL in a 70 kg adult.

\medterm{Dead Space Ventilation}-- Ventilation of alveoli without perfusion (V/Q = infinity). Anatomic dead space is the
conducting airways (~150 mL). Physiologic dead space includes alveoli without perfusion.
Increased in PE, hypotension, and emphysema. Measured by the Bohr equation.

\medterm{Dead Time}-- The brief period after an event during which a detector or recording system cannot register another event, limiting the measured count rate.

\medterm{Deafferentation}-- Loss or interruption of sensory nerve input from a body region to the central nervous
system, which may produce abnormal sensation or deafferentation pain.

\medterm{Deafness}-- Complete or severe loss of hearing. Hearing loss may be conductive, sensorineural, or mixed, depending
on whether the problem affects sound transmission, the inner ear or auditory nerve, or
both.

\medterm{Death}-- The permanent end of the functions that maintain a living organism.

\medterm{Deauville Criteria}-- A five-point visual scale that compares fluorodeoxyglucose uptake on PET with reference tissues to assess treatment response in lymphoma.

\medterm{Debridement}-- The surgical removal of dead, damaged, or infected tissue from a wound to promote
healing and prevent infection.

\medterm{Debulking}-- Surgical removal of as much tumor as possible when complete resection is not feasible,
aiming to reduce tumor burden (cytoreduction) and improve response to subsequent
therapy; central to ovarian cancer management.

\medterm{Decapitation}-- Separation of the head from the body, seen in severe trauma (vehicle accidents, train
injuries, explosions), industrial accidents, hanging with judicial execution, or
homicide. Complete decapitation is almost always rapidly fatal due to exsanguination and
brain ischemia.

\medterm{Decay Correction}-- Mathematical adjustment for radioactive decay during imaging. Essential for quantitative
comparison between studies.

\medterm{Decerebrate Posture}-- Abnormal extension of the arms and legs caused by severe dysfunction of the brainstem
below the red nucleus. It is a sign of serious neurologic injury and may occur with
traumatic brain injury, hemorrhage, or raised intracranial pressure.

\medterm{Decerebrate Posturing}-- Abnormal extension of the arms and legs, often with inward rotation of the arms, caused by severe dysfunction below the cerebral hemispheres. It indicates serious neurologic injury, but posture alone does not determine outcome.

\medterm{Decerebration}-- Severe loss of cerebral function, often with extension and rigid posturing of the limbs
from brainstem dysfunction; it may result from major injury or increased intracranial
pressure.

\medterm{Decibel}-- A logarithmic unit (dB) used to measure the intensity of sound. The decibel scale
compares a given sound pressure level to a reference level (0 dB = 20 micropascals, the
threshold of normal hearing at 1 kHz). The scale is logarithmic: an increase of 10 dB
represents a tenfold increase in sound intensity. Normal conversation: ~60 dB.

\medterm{Decidua}-- The transformed endometrium of pregnancy, shed after delivery. Three regions: decidua
basalis (under the placenta, site of trophoblast invasion), decidua capsularis (over the
gestational sac, covering the embryo), and decidua parietalis (the remainder of the
uterine lining).

\medterm{Deciduitis}-- Inflammation of the decidua, the specialized uterine lining of pregnancy.

\medterm{Deciduosis}-- The presence of extensive or ectopic decidual tissue, usually associated with pregnancy
and sometimes involving the cervix, peritoneum, or other sites.

\medterm{Decision-Making Capacity}-- The clinical ability to understand relevant information, appreciate its consequences,
reason about options, and communicate a stable choice regarding a specific decision.
Capacity is decision-specific and may fluctuate; it is distinct from legal competence.

\medterm{Decompensated Cirrhosis}-- Cirrhosis complicated by clinically significant liver dysfunction or portal
hypertension, commonly manifested by ascites, variceal hemorrhage, hepatic
encephalopathy, or jaundice. It carries substantially higher mortality than compensated
cirrhosis and warrants assessment for transplant and complication prevention.

\medterm{Decomposition}-- Postmortem breakdown of the body through autolysis and putrefaction. It begins within 24
to 72 hours at ambient temperature, producing green cecal discoloration, venous
marbling, gas distension, skin slippage, and purge fluid.

\medterm{Decompression sickness}-- A systemic illness caused by nitrogen bubble formation in tissues during rapid
decompression, presenting with joint pain, skin mottling, and neurological deficits,
treated with hyperbaric oxygen therapy.

\medterm{Deconditioning}-- Physiological decline from reduced activity or immobilization. Effects: muscle atrophy,
weakness, decreased cardiorespiratory fitness, balance impairment, increased fall risk.
Preventable with early mobilization, exercise. Reversible with rehabilitation.

\medterm{Decongestants}-- Medications that reduce nasal congestion by constricting blood vessels in the nasal
mucosa, decreasing swelling and mucus production. Systemic decongestants:
pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine (oral), which stimulate alpha-adrenergic receptors
causing vasoconstriction.

\medterm{Decontamination}-- Measures that reduce continued absorption of a toxic substance from the skin, eyes,
gastrointestinal tract, or clothing. Interventions are exposure-specific and may include
removal from the source, irrigation, washing, activated charcoal, or specialist
procedures.

\medterm{Decorticate Posture}-- Abnormal flexion of the arms with extension of the legs, indicating severe damage
affecting the cerebral hemispheres, internal capsule, or thalamus. It is a sign of major
brain injury and impaired consciousness.

\medterm{Decorticate Posturing}-- Abnormal flexion of the arms with extension of the legs, caused by severe dysfunction of the brain. It indicates serious neurologic injury, but posture alone does not determine outcome.

\medterm{Decreased Breath Sounds}-- Diminished air movement heard on auscultation, indicating decreased ventilation. Causes
include pneumothorax, pleural effusion, atelectasis, and mucus plugging. Unilateral
decreased breath sounds may indicate tension pneumothorax or large effusion.

\medterm{Decubitus Ulcer}-- Localized damage to the skin and/or underlying soft tissue caused by prolonged pressure,
shear, or friction, most commonly over bony prominences. See pressure injury.

\medterm{Deep}-- Located further inward from the surface of the body.

\medterm{Deep Tendon Reflex}-- The monosynaptic stretch reflex elicited by tapping a tendon with a reflex hammer,
testing the integrity of the reflex arc (sensory afferent, spinal cord, motor efferent).
Common reflexes: biceps (C5-6), triceps (C7), brachioradialis (C5-6), knee/patellar
(L3-4), ankle/Achilles (S1). Graded 0 (absent) to 4+ (hyperactive, clonus).

\medterm{Deep Vein Thrombosis}-- A blood clot in a deep vein, most often in a leg. It may cause swelling or pain but can be asymptomatic; diagnosis and treatment depend on clinical probability and testing.

\medterm{Deep Vein Thrombosis}-- A clot in a deep vein, usually of the lower limb, caused by a combination of reduced blood flow, increased clotting tendency, or vessel-wall injury. Anticoagulant treatment is used when indicated.

\medterm{Deep Vein Thrombosis Ultrasound}-- Venous duplex ultrasound used to evaluate suspected deep-vein thrombosis. The test
assesses vein compressibility and blood flow; an abnormal result is interpreted with the
clinical probability and, when needed, repeat or additional testing.

\medterm{Deep Venous Reflux}-- Retrograde flow through incompetent venous valves in the deep venous system. It
contributes to chronic venous hypertension, edema, skin changes, and venous ulcer
formation.

\medterm{Deep venous thrombosis}-- Formation of a blood clot in a deep vein, most often in the leg.

\medterm{Defecation}-- The process of eliminating feces from the body. Filling of the rectum triggers stretch
receptors that initiate the defecation reflex via the sacral spinal cord (S2-S4),
causing contraction of the rectal wall and relaxation of the internal anal sphincter.

\medterm{Defecography}-- Dynamic imaging of the rectum and pelvic floor during defecation using fluoroscopy or
MRI. The rectum is filled with contrast paste and the patient performs defecation while
images are captured. Evaluates rectal prolapse, intussusception, rectocele, enterocele,
and pelvic floor dyssynergia.

\medterm{Defense Mechanisms}-- Psychological processes, often outside conscious awareness, that help a person manage distress
or conflict. Examples include denial, projection, repression, humor, and sublimation.

\medterm{Defense Mechanisms (Forensic)}-- Physical evidence patterns on a body demonstrating active resistance against an
assailant prior to incapacitation.

\medterm{Defense Wounds}-- Injuries sustained by a victim attempting to ward off an attack, typically found on the
flexor surfaces of hands, palms, wrists, and forearms (incised/stab wounds from knives
or contusions/abrasions from blunt force).

\medterm{Defense Wounds (Inscribed)}-- Cuts or other injuries on the hands or forearms that may occur when a person attempts to protect against an attack.
The distribution and morphology of defensive injuries may help
reconstruct an assault but are not independently proof of a particular event.

\medterm{Defibrillation}-- Delivery of an unsynchronized electrical shock to terminate ventricular fibrillation or
pulseless ventricular tachycardia by depolarizing a critical mass of myocardium. It is
distinct from synchronized cardioversion, which times the shock to the QRS complex.

\medterm{Defibrillator}-- Device delivering electric shock to heart. Types: external (AED, manual), internal
(implantable). Used for ventricular fibrillation, pulseless ventricular tachycardia.
Energy: biphasic 120-200 J.

\medterm{Defibrillator Implant}-- An implanted cardioverter-defibrillator that detects and treats dangerous ventricular rhythms with
electrical pacing or shock.

\medterm{Degenerative Spine Disease}-- Lumbar spinal stenosis: central (neurogenic claudication: bilateral leg pain with
walking, relieved by sitting/flexion), lateral recess, foraminal. Imaging: MRI (central
canal AP diameter <10mm, cross-sectional area <70mm2).

\medterm{Dehydration}-- Deficit of total body water. Hypertonic (water loss > sodium), isotonic (loss
proportional), hypotonic (sodium loss > water). Causes: vomiting, diarrhea, fever,
inadequate intake, diabetes insipidus. Treatment: oral rehydration or IV fluids.

\medterm{Dehydration in Children}-- A deficit of total body water, usually caused by gastrointestinal losses, inadequate
intake, fever, or renal losses. Clinical assessment includes mental status, heart rate,
mucous membranes, tears, capillary refill, urine output, weight change, and perfusion
rather than a single sign.

\medterm{Delamanid}-- A nitroimidazole derivative used in the treatment of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis.
Delamanid inhibits mycolic acid synthesis in Mycobacterium tuberculosis, disrupting the
cell wall. It is indicated for pulmonary multidrug-resistant tuberculosis in combination
with other agents. The drug is administered orally twice daily.

\medterm{Delavirdine}-- A non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) that was previously used in
combination antiretroviral therapy for HIV-1 infection but is now rarely used due to the
availability of more effective and better-tolerated agents.

\medterm{Delayed Cord Clamping}-- The practice of delaying umbilical cord clamping for \(\geq 30\)--60 seconds after delivery,
allowing placental transfusion of blood to the newborn. Benefits: increases neonatal
blood volume by 30\%, improves iron stores (ferritin, hemoglobin) for the first 6
months, reduces anemia, and improves transitional circulation.

\medterm{Delayed Ejaculation}-- A sexual dysfunction characterized by a persistent or recurrent delay in, infrequency
of, or absence of ejaculation during partnered sexual activity, lasting \(\geq 6\) months and
causing marked distress. Prevalence: 1-5\% of men. Lifelong vs. acquired.

\medterm{Delayed Emergence}-- Prolonged time to regain consciousness after discontinuation of anesthetic agents.
Failure to regain consciousness within 30-60 minutes of completing surgery.

\medterm{Delayed Hypersensitivity}-- A delayed immune reaction, usually mediated by T cells rather than antibodies, that develops
hours to days after exposure. Examples include contact dermatitis, the tuberculin reaction,
and some drug eruptions.

\medterm{Delayed Puberty}-- Failure to develop secondary sexual characteristics by age thirteen in girls and age
fourteen in boys. Causes include constitutional delay which is the most common, chronic
illness, malnutrition, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism from Kallmann syndrome or pituitary
disease, and hypergonadotropic hypogonadism from gonadal failure.

\medterm{Deletion}-- Loss of chromosomal segment. Terminal deletion: loss from end. Interstitial deletion:
loss from middle. Microdeletion syndromes: 22q11.2 deletion (DiGeorge), 5p deletion
(Cri-du-chat).

\medterm{Delirium}-- An acute and fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness caused by an underlying medical, substance-related, or environmental factor. It differs from the usually more gradual course of dementia.

\medterm{Delirium in Anesthesia}-- Delirium is an acute confusional state characterized by fluctuating consciousness,
inattention, disorganized thinking, and altered level of awareness. Postoperative
delirium occurs in 15 to 50 percent of elderly surgical patients, with higher rates in
hip fracture and cardiac surgery.

\medterm{Delirium Tremens}-- A severe, potentially life-threatening form of alcohol withdrawal characterized by fluctuating
confusion, agitation, hallucinations, and marked autonomic overactivity. It usually occurs
after cessation or major reduction of prolonged heavy alcohol use and requires urgent
medical treatment.

\medterm{Delivery}-- The process of childbirth: vaginal delivery (spontaneous vertex most common;
assisted—vacuum, forceps) or cesarean delivery (abdominal). Stages of labor culminate in
delivery of the infant and placenta.

\medterm{Delivery of Posterior Arm}-- A shoulder dystocia maneuver: the operator's hand enters the posterior vagina along the
fetal chest, finds the fetal hand, grasps the forearm, sweeps it across the chest and
face, and delivers the posterior arm and hand over the perineum. This reduces the
bisacromial diameter by 20\% and is often highly effective.

\medterm{Deltoid Muscle}-- Large triangular muscle forming the shoulder contour, from lateral clavicle, acromion,
and scapular spine to the deltoid tuberosity. Innervated by the axillary nerve (C5-C6).
Anterior fibers flex/medially rotate, middle fibers abduct (primary abductor beyond 15
degrees), posterior fibers extend/laterally rotate.

\medterm{Delusion}-- A fixed belief that is not consistent with available evidence or the person’s
sociocultural context and is not readily changed by reasoned discussion. Delusions may
be persecutory, grandiose, referential, somatic, nihilistic, or erotomanic.

\medterm{Delusional Disorder}-- A disorder characterized by the presence of one or more delusions lasting one month or
more in the context of relatively preserved functioning. The delusions are non-bizarre
(involving real-life situations that could conceivably occur, such as being followed,
infected, loved from afar, or conspired against).

\medterm{Dementia}-- A group of symptoms involving progressive decline in memory, thinking, communication, or daily functioning. Causes include
Alzheimer disease, vascular disease, Lewy body disease, frontotemporal disorders, and other conditions.
Evaluation is important because some causes of cognitive decline may be treatable.


\medterm{Dementia (Major Neurocognitive Disorder)}-- Dementia is the common clinical term for an acquired syndrome of decline in one or more
cognitive domains that interferes with independence in daily activities. Major
neurocognitive disorder is the preferred diagnostic classification term.

\medterm{Demyelinating Disease}-- A disease in which the myelin sheath surrounding nerve fibres is damaged, slowing or
blocking neural conduction.

\medterm{Dendrite}-- The branched, tapering neuronal processes that receive synaptic input from other neurons
and conduct graded potentials toward the cell body. Dendrites contain receptors, ion
channels, and postsynaptic densities.

\medterm{Dendritic Cell}-- The most potent professional antigen-presenting cell, capturing antigens in tissues and
migrating to lymph nodes to present peptides on MHC molecules to T cells, initiating
adaptive immunity.

\medterm{Dendritic Ulcer}-- A branching epithelial ulcer of the cornea, classically caused by herpes simplex virus. It may cause a red eye, pain, light sensitivity, tearing, or reduced vision and requires ophthalmic assessment.

\medterm{Dengue}-- A mosquito-borne viral infection that commonly causes fever, headache, muscle pain, joint pain, and rash. Severe
disease can cause bleeding or shock.

\medterm{Dengue fever}-- An acute mosquito-borne viral illness caused by dengue virus (DENV-1, -2, -3, -4), a
flavivirus transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes.

\medterm{Dengue virus}-- A positive-sense RNA virus belonging to the Flaviviridae family that causes dengue
fever. The virus has four serotypes, and infection with one serotype provides lifelong
immunity to that serotype but only temporary cross-protection against others.

\medterm{Denial}-- A defense mechanism in which a person refuses to accept an unpleasant reality. It is a
common initial response to traumatic events and terminal illness diagnoses. While
adaptive in the short term, chronic denial can prevent appropriate medical treatment and
psychological processing.

\medterm{Denosumab for Osteoporosis}-- Fully human monoclonal antibody targeting RANK ligand preventing osteoclast formation
activation and survival. Reduces fracture risk at the spine hip and non-vertebral sites.
Administered as subcutaneous injection every six months. Advantages include no renal
dose adjustment and effectiveness even with oral bisphosphonate intolerance.

\medterm{Dens}-- The tooth-like projection of the second cervical vertebra that forms a pivot for rotation of the first vertebra.

\medterm{Dens Evaginatus}-- An extra cusp or tubercle on a tooth, most often a premolar. It may contain an extension of the dental pulp and may fracture or become affected by caries.

\medterm{Dens Invaginatus}-- An inward folding of enamel and dentin that may extend toward or into the dental pulp, allowing debris to collect and increasing the risk of caries and pulpal infection.

\medterm{Dental Abscess}-- A localized collection of pus arising from infection of a tooth or the surrounding
periodontal tissues. It may cause toothache, percussion tenderness, gingival swelling,
facial swelling, fever, trismus, or regional lymphadenopathy; spread into deep facial
spaces can threaten the airway and requires urgent assessment.

\medterm{Dental Age}-- An estimate of developmental age based on the formation, mineralization, and eruption of the teeth. It is useful in children but is not an exact measure of chronological age.

\medterm{Dental Anesthesia}-- The use of local anesthetic, sedation, or, when necessary, general anesthesia to control pain and anxiety during dental care.

\medterm{Dental Caries}-- A disease in which bacterial acids progressively demineralize and damage the teeth. See Dental Cavities.

\medterm{Dental Cavities}-- Permanent areas of tooth decay caused by acid-producing bacteria acting on dietary
carbohydrates. Cavities progress from enamel demineralization to dentin and pulp
involvement, causing sensitivity, pain, or infection.

\medterm{Dental Ceramic}-- A ceramic material used to make dental restorations such as crowns, veneers, and bridges. Examples include feldspathic ceramic, lithium disilicate, and zirconia.

\medterm{Dental Disease}-- Disorders affecting the teeth, gums, or supporting tissues, including tooth decay, gum disease,
oral infections, and tooth loss. Prevention includes fluoride, regular brushing and flossing,
limiting frequent sugar exposure, and routine dental examinations.

\medterm{Dental Extraction}-- Removal of a tooth from its alveolar socket. Indications include unrestorable disease,
advanced periodontal destruction, impaction, orthodontic treatment, or selected
prosthodontic needs; risks include bleeding, infection, nerve injury, fracture, and
alveolar osteitis.

\medterm{Dental Implant}-- A biocompatible fixture placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth. The fixture may become integrated with surrounding bone and is restored with a crown, bridge, or denture.

\medterm{Dental Plaque}-- Biofilm of bacteria on tooth surfaces. Initiated by acquired pellicle. Maturation leads
to disease if not removed. Disrupted by mechanical cleaning.

\medterm{Dental Pulp Necrosis}-- Death of the dental pulp, usually following untreated inflammation, trauma, or loss of
blood supply. The tooth generally fails sensibility testing and may develop apical
periodontitis or a periapical abscess.

\medterm{Dental Pulpitis}-- Inflammation of the dental pulp caused by caries, trauma, restorative procedures,
cracks, or other irritation. Reversible pulpitis produces brief provoked pain, whereas
irreversible pulpitis causes spontaneous or lingering pain and may require pulpal
treatment or extraction.

\medterm{Dental Radiographs}-- Imaging for dental diagnosis. Intraoral: periapical (tooth root and surrounding bone),
bitewing (interproximal caries detection, crestal bone level), and occlusal (larger
area, localization).

\medterm{Dental Radiography}-- Imaging techniques for dental diagnosis: bitewing (caries, bone levels), periapical
(root, periapical tissues), panoramic (full mouth overview), CBCT (3D imaging).
Radiation dose: dental radiography low exposure. ALARA principle applies.

\medterm{Dental Restorative Materials}-- Materials used to restore teeth: amalgam, composite resin, glass ionomer, ceramic, gold
alloy. Selection based on location, aesthetics, strength, cost, patient factors.
Composite: tooth-colored, adhesive, conservative preparation. Ceramic: excellent
aesthetics, brittle, expensive. Gold: durable, biocompatible, aesthetic concern.

\medterm{Dental Screening}-- Examination of the teeth, gums, and mouth to identify dental disease or risk before serious symptoms
develop.

\medterm{Dental Sealant}-- Thin protective coating applied to chewing surfaces of molars. Prevents caries by
sealing pits and fissures.

\medterm{Dental Traumatology}-- Management of dental injuries. Types: enamel fractures, enamel-dentin fractures, pulp
exposure, root fractures, avulsion. Immediate care critical for avulsed teeth.

\medterm{Dentin}-- Calcified tissue beneath enamel forming bulk of tooth. Contains dentinal tubules with
fluid and processes. Sensitive to stimuli when exposed. Reparative dentin formed in
response to irritation.

\medterm{Dentinogenesis Imperfecta}-- An inherited disorder of dentin formation that can cause translucent blue-gray or amber teeth that fracture, wear, or become discolored easily. Some forms occur with osteogenesis imperfecta.

\medterm{Dentistry (Geriatric)}-- Oral health challenges in elderly: xerostomia, root caries, periodontal disease, denture
problems, oral cancer, medication side effects. Assessment: complete oral exam, cancer
screening, denture fit, caries risk. Prevention: fluoride, oral hygiene, regular dental
visits. Adaptations: handholds, magnification, positioning.

\medterm{Dentition}-- The teeth present in the mouth, including their number, arrangement, development, and eruption.

\medterm{Denture}-- A removable dental prosthesis that replaces some or all missing teeth and associated
oral tissues. Partial dentures replace selected teeth while complete dentures replace an
entire dental arch; fit, oral hygiene, tissue health, and regular review are important
for function and prevention of mucosal injury.

\medterm{Dependence}-- A state in which repeated exposure to a substance produces physiologic adaptation, so that stopping or reducing it may cause withdrawal. In substance-use disorders, dependence may occur with or without addiction.

\medterm{Dependent Personality Disorder}-- A pattern of submissive and clinging behavior related to an excessive need to be taken
care of.

\medterm{Depersonalization-Derealization Disorder}-- A disorder involving persistent or recurrent feelings of detachment from one's thoughts,
feelings, body, or surroundings, while reality testing remains intact. Symptoms cause
clinically significant distress or impairment.

\medterm{Depolarization}-- The change in membrane potential toward a less negative (more positive) value, typically
caused by inward sodium or calcium current. In neurons, depolarization to threshold
triggers an action potential.

\medterm{Depot Implant}-- An implant designed to release a medicine gradually over an extended period after placement in the body.

\medterm{Deprescribing}-- A planned, supervised process of reducing the dose or stopping a medication for which
harms, burden, or lack of benefit outweigh expected benefit. It requires review of
indication, withdrawal risk, disease recurrence, and patient goals.

\medterm{Depression}-- Major depressive disorder is a common, disabling mood disorder characterized by
persistent sadness and loss of interest or pleasure in activities, affecting
approximately 7 percent of adults worldwide annually.

\medterm{Depression (Geriatric)}-- Mood disorder common in elderly, often underdiagnosed. Symptoms: sad mood, anhedonia,
sleep/appetite changes, fatigue, guilt, concentration difficulty, suicidal ideation. May
present as cognitive impairment ("pseudodementia"). Risk factors: chronic illness,
social isolation, bereavement, medications.

\medterm{Depression Screening}-- Major depressive disorder is a common, disabling mood disorder characterized by
persistent sadness and loss of interest or pleasure in activities, affecting
approximately 7 percent of adults worldwide annually. See depression.

\medterm{Dermal Structure}-- The dermis has two layers: papillary dermis (superficial, thin, containing capillaries,
lymphatics, and Meissner corpuscles) and reticular dermis (deep, thick, containing dense
irregular connective tissue, collagen, elastin, hair follicles, sweat glands, and
sebaceous glands).

\medterm{Dermatitis}-- Inflammation of the skin, often used interchangeably with eczema. Atopic dermatitis:
chronic pruritic inflammation in flexural areas, associated with asthma and allergies,
driven by skin barrier dysfunction and type 2 inflammation. Contact dermatitis: allergic
(type IV hypersensitivity) or irritant (direct toxicity).

\medterm{Dermatitis Herpetiformis}-- A chronic, intensely pruritic, autoimmune blistering skin condition characterized by
grouped, symmetric erythematous papules, urticarial plaques, and vesicles occurring
primarily on extensor surfaces (elbows, knees, buttocks, scalp). It is a cutaneous
manifestation of gluten-sensitive enteropathy (celiac disease).

\medterm{Dermatofibroma}-- A common benign dermal nodule, typically on the lower extremities of young adults. It
presents as a firm, hyperpigmented papule that dimples when pinched (Fitzpatrick sign).
It is thought to result from reactive fibrosis following minor trauma or insect bites.
Dermatofibromas are asymptomatic and require no treatment.

\medterm{Dermatofibrosarcoma}-- Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans is a locally aggressive fibrohistiocytic tumor of the
skin. It typically presents as a slowly enlarging, indurated plaque on the trunk, which
may develop nodules over time. It has a high local recurrence rate (up to 50 percent
with simple excision) but low metastatic potential.

\medterm{Dermatofibrosarcoma Protuberans}-- A rare locally aggressive cutaneous sarcoma arising from dermal fibroblasts. It presents
as a slow-growing, firm, reddish-purple plaque or nodule, typically on the trunk. It has
a high local recurrence rate but low metastatic potential. Treatment is wide local
excision with Mohs micrographic surgery or imatinib for unresectable disease.

\medterm{Dermatographism}-- Urticaria factitia: the development of linear wheals (hives) within minutes of stroking
or scratching the skin, the most common form of physical urticaria. Mast cell
hyper-reactivity releases histamine after minor mechanical pressure. Associated with
chronic spontaneous urticaria and atopy.

\medterm{Dermatomyositis}-- An inflammatory myopathy with characteristic skin manifestations: Gottron papules (over
knuckles), heliotrope rash (purple periorbital), shawl/V sign erythema, and proximal
muscle weakness; associated with interstitial lung disease, and in adults an increased
risk of malignancy (ovary, lung, GI).

\medterm{Dermatopathology}-- The study of skin disease through examination of tissue specimens, usually with microscopy and
related laboratory methods. It helps identify inflammatory, infectious, benign, and
malignant skin disorders.

\medterm{Dermatophytosis}-- A superficial infection of keratinized skin, hair, or nails caused by dermatophyte
fungi, producing conditions such as tinea corporis, tinea pedis, and onychomycosis.

\medterm{Dermis}-- Thick connective tissue layer beneath the epidermis providing structural support,
elasticity, and nourishment. Two layers: papillary (superficial, loose connective tissue
with dermal papillae interdigitating with epidermal ridges) and reticular (deep, dense
irregular connective tissue).

\medterm{Dermoid}-- A benign cystic teratoma containing elements derived from ectoderm, including skin, hair
follicles, sebaceous glands, and sometimes teeth. Ovarian dermoid cysts are the most
common germ cell tumor of the ovary, typically occurring in young women. They are
usually asymptomatic but may cause torsion, rupture, or malignant transformation.

\medterm{Dermoid Cyst}-- A usually benign cyst lined by tissue resembling skin and sometimes containing hair, glands, or other skin
structures.

\medterm{Dermoscopy}-- Noninvasive examination of skin lesions with magnification and polarized or nonpolarized
illumination that reveals structures not visible to the unaided eye. It improves
assessment of pigmented lesions and selected inflammatory or infectious conditions but
does not replace biopsy when malignancy is suspected.

\medterm{Descending Colon}-- From the splenic flexure to the left iliac fossa, approximately 25-30 cm, and mostly
retroperitoneal and fixed to the posterior abdominal wall. The phrenicocolic ligament
supports the splenic flexure. Anteriorly it is related to small intestine; posteriorly
to the left kidney. It absorbs water and electrolytes and compacts fecal matter.

\medterm{Descending Thoracic Aorta}-- The portion of the aorta extending from the aortic arch to the diaphragm. It lies in the
posterior mediastinum and gives branches to the thoracic wall and organs.

\medterm{Desmoid Tumor}-- A locally aggressive but non-metastasizing fibrous tumor (fibromatosis), associated with
familial adenomatous polyposis. Treatment includes surgery, radiation, and systemic
therapy; recurrence after resection is common.

\medterm{Desquamative Interstitial Pneumonia}-- A rare ILD characterized by uniform filling of alveoli with macrophages containing brown
pigment (hemosiderin-laden). It is strongly associated with smoking. HRCT shows
bilateral ground-glass opacities with lower lobe predominance. Smoking cessation and
corticosteroids are the main treatments.

\medterm{Detached Retina}-- Separation of the neurosensory retina from the underlying retinal pigment epithelium
(RPE), a medical emergency requiring prompt surgical repair to prevent permanent vision
loss.

\medterm{Deterministic Effects}-- Tissue reactions caused by radiation that generally occur only above a threshold dose, with severity increasing as the dose rises. Examples include skin injury, cataracts, and tissue damage after substantial exposure.

\medterm{Detoxification}-- Removal of toxic substance from body. Methods: activated charcoal, gastric lavage
(rarely used), whole bowel irrigation, hemodialysis, hemoperfusion, chelation,
supportive care.

\medterm{Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip}-- A spectrum of hip abnormalities in infants and children, ranging from mild acetabular
dysplasia to complete hip dislocation. The femoral head is partially or completely
displaced from the acetabulum due to capsular laxity and acetabular underdevelopment.

\medterm{Developmental Milestones}-- Skills and abilities that children typically acquire at predictable ages. Examples:
social smile at 2 months, rolling over at 4-6 months, sitting independently at 6-8
months, walking at 12 months, and speaking two-word phrases at 2 years. Delays in
reaching milestones may indicate developmental delay, autism, or neurologic impairment.

\medterm{Developmental Regression}-- Loss of previously acquired developmental skills. Regression is a neurologic red flag
requiring prompt evaluation for genetic, metabolic, neurodegenerative, epileptic,
infectious, or structural causes.

\medterm{Developmental Surveillance}-- The ongoing process of eliciting caregiver concerns, observing the child, documenting
developmental history, and monitoring milestones during routine care. It differs from
formal developmental screening, which uses standardized instruments at specified ages.

\medterm{Deviated Septum}-- Displacement of the nasal septum from the midline, congenital or acquired from trauma,
and present to some degree in most adults. Significant deviation narrows one nasal
cavity, impairing airflow and sinus drainage and producing obstruction, congestion,
recurrent sinusitis, epistaxis, and sleep disturbance.

\medterm{Devic Disease}-- Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder, an autoimmune disease that primarily affects the optic nerves and
spinal cord.

\medterm{DEXA (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry)}-- Measures bone mineral density (BMD) at spine and hip. T-score compares to young adult
reference. Osteoporosis: T-score <= -2.5. Osteopenia: -1.0 to -2.5. Low radiation dose.

\medterm{Dexamethasone}-- A potent corticosteroid used to reduce inflammation and suppress excessive immune activity. It is
used for selected allergic, respiratory, neurologic, cancer-related, and endocrine conditions.
Longer or higher-dose courses can increase infection risk, blood glucose, and bone loss.

\medterm{Dexamethasone Suppression Test}-- A dynamic test used to assess cortisol regulation. In normal physiology, dexamethasone
suppresses ACTH and cortisol; failure of suppression supports endogenous
hypercortisolism but requires interpretation with medication, absorption, and
comorbidity considerations.

\medterm{Dexmedetomidine}-- A relatively selective alpha-2 adrenergic agonist used for sedation and as an adjunct to
analgesia. It can produce cooperative sedation with limited respiratory depression but
may cause bradycardia and hypotension.

\medterm{Dextroamphetamine}-- A central nervous system stimulant (d-amphetamine isomer) increasing dopamine and
norepinephrine. Used for ADHD and narcolepsy. Available as immediate and
extended-release, and in mixed amphetamine salts (Adderall) and lisdexamfetamine
(prodrug).

\medterm{Dextrocardia}-- A congenital cardiac positional anomaly where the heart is located in the right
hemithorax with the apex pointing to the right.

\medterm{Diabetes}-- A group of disorders characterized by persistently elevated blood glucose caused by
inadequate insulin secretion, impaired insulin action, or both. Chronic hyperglycemia
can damage the eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart, and blood vessels. Major forms include type
1, type 2, and gestational diabetes.

\medterm{Diabetes and Metabolic Health}-- Closely linked through insulin resistance, obesity, dyslipidemia, and hypertension,
collectively known as the metabolic syndrome. Poor metabolic health increases the risk
of developing type 2 diabetes and worsens glycemic control in existing diabetes.

\medterm{Diabetes Complications}-- Diabetes complications arise from chronic hyperglycemia damaging blood vessels and
nerves over time. Microvascular complications include diabetic retinopathy leading to
vision loss, nephropathy progressing to kidney failure, and neuropathy causing pain and
loss of sensation.

\medterm{Diabetes insipidus}-- A disorder characterized by polyuria (excessive urine output exceeding 40 mL/kg/24
hours) and polydipsia due to deficient secretion of antidiuretic hormone (ADH,
vasopressin) from the posterior pituitary (central diabetes insipidus) or renal
resistance to ADH action (nephrogenic diabetes insipidus).

\medterm{Diabetes Management}-- The coordinated use of nutrition, physical activity, medicines when indicated, glucose monitoring, and screening for complications to maintain health and reduce diabetes-related risk.

\medterm{Diabetes Medications}-- Diabetes medications lower blood glucose through various mechanisms: metformin reduces
hepatic glucose production; sulfonylureas stimulate insulin secretion; SGLT2 inhibitors
increase urinary glucose excretion; GLP-1 receptor agonists enhance incretin effects;
and insulin replaces deficient hormone.

\medterm{Diabetes Mellitus}-- A metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from insufficient
insulin production, insulin resistance, or both. Type 1 diabetes results from autoimmune
destruction of pancreatic beta cells, while type 2 is driven by genetic and lifestyle
factors leading to insulin resistance.

\medterm{Diabetes Mellitus Type 1}-- An autoimmune disease characterized by destruction of pancreatic beta cells leading to
absolute insulin deficiency. Usually presents in childhood or young adulthood with acute
onset of polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, and diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) at diagnosis
in many.

\medterm{Diabetes Mellitus Type 2}-- A progressive metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and relative
insulin deficiency, strongly associated with obesity and physical inactivity. Typically
presents in adults but increasingly seen in younger populations. Diagnosis: fasting
glucose \(\geq 126\) mg/dL, HbA1c \(\geq 6.5\%\), or OGTT \(\geq 200\) mg/dL.

\medterm{Diabetes Prevention}-- Preventing diabetes focuses on reducing the risk of developing type 2 diabetes through
lifestyle interventions and risk factor management. Maintaining a healthy body weight,
engaging in regular physical activity, and following a balanced diet low in refined
sugars and saturated fats are cornerstone strategies.

\medterm{Diabetic Autonomic Neuropathy}-- Dysfunction of autonomic nerves caused by diabetes, affecting cardiovascular,
gastrointestinal, genitourinary, sudomotor, and pupillary systems. Manifestations
include orthostatic hypotension, resting tachycardia, gastroparesis, bladder
dysfunction, erectile dysfunction, and impaired hypoglycemia awareness.

\medterm{Diabetic Cardiomyopathy}-- Damage to the heart muscle associated with diabetes that occurs independently of coronary artery
disease or hypertension. It may cause impaired relaxation, reduced pumping function, heart
failure, and rhythm problems; glucose and cardiovascular risk control can reduce risk.

\medterm{Diabetic Complications}-- The chronic and acute consequences of diabetes mellitus. Microvascular: retinopathy
(leading cause of blindness), nephropathy (leading cause of ESRD—albuminuria, declining
GFR), neuropathy (peripheral—stocking-glove, autonomic—gastroparesis, erectile
dysfunction, postural hypotension, cardiac autonomic).

\medterm{Diabetic Diet}-- An individualized eating plan that considers carbohydrate amount and timing, overall nutrition, cultural preferences, and treatment goals to support blood-glucose control.

\medterm{Diabetic Foot}-- Foot problems associated with diabetes, including neuropathy, deformity, ulcers, infection, and impaired
healing.

\medterm{Diabetic Foot Disease}-- Complex multifactorial condition resulting from the combination of peripheral
neuropathy, peripheral arterial disease, and impaired immune function in diabetes.
Neuropathy leads to loss of protective sensation and foot deformities. Peripheral
arterial disease impairs healing. Minor trauma progresses to ulceration and infection.

\medterm{Diabetic Foot Ulcer}-- A chronic wound of the foot in a patient with diabetes, resulting from peripheral
neuropathy, peripheral artery disease, and impaired wound healing. Neuropathic ulcers:
painless, at pressure points (plantar metatarsal heads, heel), punched-out appearance,
surrounded by callus.

\medterm{Diabetic Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State}-- A severe complication of diabetes marked by extreme hyperglycemia, increased serum osmolality, and dehydration with little or no ketoacidosis. It can cause altered consciousness and requires urgent hospital treatment.

\medterm{Diabetic Ketoacidosis}-- An acute, life-threatening diabetes complication characterized by ketone production and
metabolic acidosis, usually with elevated blood glucose. It may be triggered by infection,
insufficient insulin, myocardial infarction, pancreatitis, or other physiologic stress.

\medterm{Diabetic Ketoacidosis}-- A life-threatening complication of diabetes involving hyperketonemia and metabolic
acidosis, with or without marked hyperglycemia. It is commonly precipitated by infection,
missed insulin, new-onset diabetes, or another acute illness.

\medterm{Diabetic Ketoacidosis Monitoring}-- Monitoring during DKA management includes hourly blood glucose measurement, serum
electrolytes every two to four hours, venous blood gas assessment, beta-hydroxybutyrate
levels, and fluid balance.

\medterm{Diabetic Macular Edema}-- The accumulation of fluid within the macula due to breakdown of the blood-retinal
barrier in diabetic retinopathy, resulting in thickening of the macula and central
vision loss, and it can occur at any stage of diabetic retinopathy, from mild
non-proliferative to proliferative disease.

\medterm{Diabetic Nephropathy}-- Kidney disease caused by diabetes mellitus, the leading cause of ESRD worldwide.
Pathophysiology: hyperglycemia-induced glomerular hyperfiltration, basement membrane
thickening, mesangial expansion, and glomerulosclerosis. Presents with progressive
albuminuria (microalbuminuria macroalbuminuria) and declining GFR.

\medterm{Diabetic Neuropathy}-- Nerve dysfunction caused by diabetes. It may cause distal sensory loss and pain or affect autonomic functions such as digestion, blood pressure, sweating, bladder function, or sexual function.

\medterm{Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy}-- The most common complication of diabetes mellitus, affecting approximately fifty percent
of patients with long-standing disease. It is a length-dependent, sensorimotor
polyneuropathy that affects the distal lower extremities first, in a glove-and-stocking
distribution, and progresses proximally over time.

\medterm{Diabetic Retinopathy}-- A microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus and a leading cause of blindness in
working-age adults. Non-proliferative DR (NPDR): microaneurysms, dot-blot hemorrhages,
exudates (hard and cotton-wool spots), and venous beading. Proliferative DR (PDR):
retinal neovascularization, vitreous hemorrhage, and tractional retinal detachment.

\medterm{Diagnosis}-- The identification of a disease or other health condition through clinical history, examination, and appropriate investigations. A differential diagnosis considers alternative explanations for the findings.

\medterm{Diagnostic microbiology}-- The laboratory identification of microorganisms causing disease. Methods include
microscopy, culture, biochemical testing, serology, and molecular methods. Rapid
diagnostic tests provide same-day results. MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry revolutionized
bacterial identification.

\medterm{Diagnostic Radiology}-- The medical specialty that uses imaging methods to diagnose and characterize disease, including
X-ray, ultrasound, computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and nuclear medicine.

\medterm{Diagnostic Test}-- A test used to evaluate a person with symptoms, signs, or an abnormal screening result
to establish, exclude, or characterize a disease. Its usefulness depends on analytic
validity, clinical performance, and the consequences of the result.

\medterm{Diagnostic Uncertainty}-- The state in which available information does not establish a single diagnosis with
sufficient confidence. It should be communicated transparently and managed with
safety-netting, planned reassessment, and targeted additional evaluation.

\medterm{Dialectical Behavior Therapy}-- A comprehensive cognitive-behavioral treatment originally developed for borderline
personality disorder. It combines cognitive-behavioral techniques with mindfulness and
acceptance strategies. Core modules include mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion
regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness.

\medterm{Dialysis}-- Removal of excess fluid and waste products from the blood when kidney function is inadequate, using hemodialysis
or peritoneal dialysis.

\medterm{Dialysis Access}-- A vascular route used for hemodialysis, such as an arteriovenous fistula, arteriovenous graft, or central venous catheter. Each option has different benefits and risks.

\medterm{Dialysis Adequacy}-- The degree to which a dialysis prescription removes uremic solutes and controls fluid,
electrolyte, and acid-base balance while meeting patient-centered treatment goals.
Measures such as Kt/V and urea reduction ratio are used for hemodialysis, but clinical
status remains essential.

\medterm{Dialysis Disequilibrium Syndrome}-- Neurological complications during hemodialysis in severely uremic patients. Headache,
nausea, confusion, seizures, and coma. Results from rapid solute removal causing
cerebral edema. Prevent with gradual dialysis initiation and shorter initial sessions.

\medterm{Diaper Rash}-- Irritant inflammation of the skin covered by a diaper, commonly caused by prolonged
contact with urine or stool, friction, moisture, or Candida infection. It causes redness
and soreness; persistent, severe, or spreading rash requires evaluation for infection or
another skin condition.

\medterm{Diaphragm}-- Dome-shaped musculotendinous partition separating thoracic/abdominal cavities, primary
inspiration muscle. Central tendon with muscular periphery from xiphoid, lower six
costal cartilages, and lumbar vertebrae via crura and arcuate ligaments. Innervated by
phrenic nerves (C3-C5). Contraction flattens the dome increasing thoracic volume.

\medterm{Diaphragmatic Hernia}-- Protrusion of abdominal contents through a defect or opening in the diaphragm into the
thoracic cavity. It may be congenital or acquired after trauma and can cause respiratory
compromise, bowel obstruction, or strangulation; diagnosis is based on imaging and
management depends on symptoms, contents, and the risk of organ compromise.

\medterm{Diaphragmatic rupture}-- A tear in the diaphragm most commonly caused by blunt or penetrating thoracoabdominal
trauma, with the left side affected in approximately 75 percent of cases due to the
protective effect of the liver on the right. Herniation of abdominal viscera into the
thorax may cause respiratory distress, bowel obstruction, or strangulation.

\medterm{Diaphysis}-- The shaft of a long bone, comprising thick cortical (compact) bone surrounding the
medullary cavity, which contains bone marrow in children and is largely yellow (fatty)
marrow in adults. Enclosed by periosteum; the nutrient artery enters here. Fractures of
the diaphysis heal by callus formation (enchondral and intramembranous).

\medterm{Diarrhea}-- Frequent passage of loose or watery stools. It may be acute or persistent and can result from infection,
medicines, dietary factors, inflammation, malabsorption, or other conditions. Associated
symptoms and the need for testing or treatment depend on severity, duration, and the
person’s clinical features.

\medterm{Diarrhea, Constipation, Gas, and Bloating}-- These common gastrointestinal symptoms arise from disruptions in normal digestive
function caused by diet, infections, medications, or underlying conditions like
irritable bowel syndrome. Diarrhea involves increased stool frequency and water content,
while constipation involves infrequent or difficult passage of stool.

\medterm{Diastasis Recti}-- Separation and thinning of the linea alba between the two rectus abdominis muscles,
commonly occurring during or after pregnancy. It produces a midline abdominal bulge that
becomes prominent with raised intra-abdominal pressure.

\medterm{Diastole}-- The phase of the cardiac cycle when the heart muscle relaxes, allowing the chambers to
fill with blood.

\medterm{Diastolic Dysfunction}-- Impaired relaxation, compliance, or filling of the left or right ventricle during
diastole. It can raise filling pressures and cause exertional dyspnoea, pulmonary
congestion, or heart failure with preserved ejection fraction; echocardiography assesses
relaxation, chamber structure, and estimated filling pressure.

\medterm{Diastolic Murmurs}-- Murmurs occurring between S2 and the next S1. Early diastolic decrescendo murmurs
indicate semilunar valve regurgitation (aortic regurgitation, pulmonary regurgitation).
Mid-diastolic low-pitched rumbles indicate AV valve stenosis (mitral stenosis, tricuspid
stenosis). Late diastolic murmurs may indicate mitral stenosis with sinus rhythm.

\medterm{Diathermy}-- The use of high-frequency electrical current to generate heat for surgical purposes,
including cutting tissue, coagulating blood vessels to stop bleeding (hemostasis), and
destroying abnormal tissue (electrocautery, electrocoagulation).

\medterm{Diatom Test}-- Forensic technique used to support the diagnosis of drowning; microscopic silica-shelled
diatoms present in drowning water enter lungs, penetrate alveolar capillaries, and
circulate to distant closed organs (femur bone marrow, brain) only if active circulation
was present at time of submersion.

\medterm{Dichorionic Twins}-- Twins that each have a separate chorion and amnion. All dizygotic twins are dichorionic, and some
monozygotic twins are as well. Each fetus usually has a separate placental circulation,
although the placentas may appear fused; complications include preterm birth and growth
disturbance.

\medterm{Diclofenac}-- A nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used for short-term treatment of pain and inflammation.
It may cause stomach bleeding, kidney injury, fluid retention, or cardiovascular events;
topical forms may have fewer systemic effects.

\medterm{DICOM}-- Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine. Standard for medical image exchange.

\medterm{Dicyclomine}-- An anticholinergic medicine used to reduce intestinal smooth-muscle spasms in irritable bowel syndrome.
It may cause dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention, or confusion and
is not suitable for everyone with bowel symptoms.

\medterm{Didanosine}-- A nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) used in the treatment of HIV-1
infection, typically as part of combination antiretroviral therapy. Didanosine is an
adenosine analogue that inhibits viral reverse transcriptase, leading to chain
termination of viral DNA synthesis.

\medterm{Dietary Assessment}-- The systematic evaluation of a person’s food and nutrient intake, using methods such as a food diary, 24-hour recall, or food-frequency questionnaire.

\medterm{Dietary Fat}-- A macronutrient composed mainly of triglycerides and fatty acids that provides
approximately 9 kcal per gram. Fat supports cell membranes, energy storage, absorption
of vitamins A, D, E, and K, and synthesis of signaling molecules; the type of fat
affects cardiometabolic risk.

\medterm{Dietary Reference Intakes}-- Reference values for nutrient intake: RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance), AI (Adequate
Intake), UL (Tolerable Upper Intake Level), EAR (Estimated Average Requirement).

\medterm{Dieulafoy Lesion}-- Large caliber persistent submucosal artery in the stomach wall that can cause massive
gastrointestinal bleeding. The artery is abnormally large and superficial, lacking the
normal tapering into smaller arterioles. Presents with hematemesis or melena without a
visible ulcer.

\medterm{Differential Diagnosis}-- The process of distinguishing a particular disease or condition from others that present
with similar symptoms. Systematic approach: consider life-threatening conditions first,
use pretest probability, apply diagnostic criteria, and order targeted tests.

\medterm{Differentiation}-- The process by which cells develop specialized structures and functions. In pathology, the term also describes how closely
tumor cells resemble the normal tissue from which they arose.

\medterm{Difficult Airway}-- A clinical situation in which a trained clinician has difficulty with oxygenation, ventilation,
supraglottic airway placement, laryngoscopy, or tracheal intubation. It may result from
anatomical features, illness, trauma, or limited equipment and requires an appropriate
airway plan.

\medterm{Difficult Intubation}-- Difficulty placing an endotracheal tube despite appropriate technique and equipment. Risk may be increased
by limited mouth opening or neck movement, abnormal airway anatomy, facial trauma,
obesity, or a previous difficult airway. Preparation includes assessment, preoxygenation,
and backup airway plans.

\medterm{Diffuse Axonal Injury}-- Widespread injury to nerve fibers caused by acceleration or deceleration of the brain. It may produce prolonged impaired consciousness, and MRI can show characteristic lesions.

\medterm{Diffuse Esophageal Spasm}-- A motility disorder characterised by premature, uncoordinated esophageal contractions,
causing intermittent chest pain and dysphagia for liquids and solids. Endoscopy excludes
structural disease; high-resolution manometry confirms premature contractions.

\medterm{Diffusing Capacity}-- Ability of lungs to transfer gas from alveoli to blood. Reduced in emphysema, ILD, and
anemia. Increased in pulmonary hemorrhage and left-to-right shunts.

\medterm{Diffusing Capacity of the Lung}-- The capacity of the respiratory membrane to transfer gas from alveolar air into
pulmonary capillary blood. It depends on surface area, membrane thickness, gas
properties, hemoglobin, and pulmonary capillary blood volume and is commonly measured
with carbon monoxide as DLCO.

\medterm{Diffusion}-- The net movement of molecules from an area of higher concentration to lower
concentration due to random thermal motion (Brownian motion).

\medterm{Diffusion Restriction}-- Reduced movement of water molecules detected by diffusion-sensitive MRI sequences,
usually appearing bright on DWI with corresponding low ADC. It may indicate acute
ischemia but can also occur with abscess, hypercellular tumor, seizure, and other
processes.

\medterm{Diffusion-Weighted Imaging}-- An MRI technique that detects differences in the movement of water molecules within tissue. Restricted diffusion may occur in acute stroke, abscess, hypercellular tumors, and other conditions.

\medterm{DiGeorge Syndrome}-- A disorder usually caused by a deletion of part of chromosome 22 that can affect development of the thymus, parathyroid glands, heart, palate, and immune system. It is also called 22q11.2 deletion syndrome.

\medterm{Digestion}-- The process by which the body breaks down ingested food into smaller molecules that can
be absorbed and utilized. Digestion begins in the mouth (mechanical--mastication;
enzymatic--salivary amylase for starch, lingual lipase).

\medterm{Digestive Health}-- The efficient functioning of the gastrointestinal tract in breaking down food, absorbing
nutrients, and eliminating waste. Digestive health depends on factors such as diet,
hydration, gut microbiome balance, and the integrity of the digestive mucosa.

\medterm{Digital Clubbing}-- Bulbous enlargement of distal phalanges with loss of nail-bed angle (> 180 degrees).
Associated with lung cancer, ILD, bronchiectasis, and cyanotic heart disease.

\medterm{Digital ischemia}-- Reduced arterial blood flow to the fingers causing pain, pallor, coolness, and potential
tissue necrosis. Causes include Raynaud phenomenon, thromboangiitis obliterans,
cholesterol emboli, and arterial thrombosis. Severe cases may require amputation of
affected digits.

\medterm{Digital Mucous Cyst}-- A benign ganglion cyst arising from the distal interphalangeal joint, presenting as a
translucent, dome-shaped nodule on the dorsal aspect of the finger near the nail fold.
It is associated with osteoarthritis of the DIP joint.

\medterm{Digital Subtraction Angiography}-- An imaging technique that subtracts precontrast images from contrast-enhanced images to show blood vessels more clearly. It can also guide selected vascular procedures.

\medterm{Digitalis (Digoxin) Toxicity}-- Nausea, vomiting, visual changes (yellow halos), arrhythmias. Risk: narrow therapeutic
index, renal impairment, hypokalemia. Treatment: digoxin immune Fab, cardiac monitoring,
correct electrolytes.

\medterm{Digitalis toxicity}-- A potentially life-threatening adverse effect of cardiac glycoside therapy (digoxin,
digitoxin) characterized by a narrow therapeutic index (0.5-2.0 ng/mL for digoxin).

\medterm{Digoxin}-- A cardiac glycoside used in selected patients with heart failure or atrial fibrillation to improve symptoms
or slow the heart rate. It has a narrow safety margin; nausea, visual changes, confusion,
or irregular heartbeat may indicate toxicity.

\medterm{Digoxin Immune Fab}-- Antibody fragments that bind free digoxin and related cardiac glycosides, reducing their
tissue activity. Indications include life-threatening dysrhythmia, severe hyperkalemia
from acute toxicity, or a potentially massive ingestion.

\medterm{Dilated Cardiomyopathy}-- A disease of the heart muscle in which one or more chambers enlarge and contract poorly,
usually causing reduced ejection fraction. Causes include inherited disease, infection,
toxins, medicines, pregnancy, and ischemic or inflammatory injury.

\medterm{Dilated Cardiomyopathy Prognosis}-- Prognosis varies widely and depends on ventricular function, symptoms, cause, arrhythmias,
comorbidities, and response to treatment. Guideline-directed therapy can improve cardiac
function in some people, while severe disease may lead to heart failure, arrhythmia, or the
need for advanced therapies.

\medterm{Dilation and Curettage}-- Surgical dilation of the cervix and curettage (scraping) of the uterine lining.
Indications: incomplete or missed abortion (miscarriage management), therapeutic
abortion, endometrial sampling (abnormal uterine bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding,
endometrial hyperplasia/cancer), and removal of retained products of conception (RPOC).

\medterm{Dilator}-- An instrument or device used to gradually widen a body opening or passage.

\medterm{Diltiazem}-- A calcium-channel blocker used to treat hypertension, angina, and some rapid heart rhythms. It slows
conduction through the atrioventricular node and may cause low blood pressure, slow heart
rate, constipation, or worsening heart failure.

\medterm{Dilute Russell Viper Venom Time}-- A phospholipid-dependent clotting assay used mainly to detect lupus anticoagulant. It is
commonly performed as part of laboratory evaluation for antiphospholipid syndrome, with
confirmatory testing required because anticoagulants and other factor abnormalities can
affect the result.

\medterm{Dimercaprol}-- Chelating agent for arsenic, mercury, gold. Given IM. Side effects: hypertension,
tachycardia, nausea, pain at injection site.

\medterm{Dinoprostone (Cervidil, Prepidil)}-- A prostaglandin E2 medicine used to soften the cervix and help initiate
labor. It is available in several formulations, and administration requires obstetric monitoring because it can cause
excessive uterine activity or fetal effects.

\medterm{Dipeptidyl Peptidase-4 Inhibitors}-- Oral glucose-lowering medications including sitagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin, and
alogliptin that inhibit the enzyme degrading incretin hormones GLP-1 and GIP. Resulting
increase in active incretin levels enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion and
suppresses glucagon. Neutral effect on weight and low hypoglycemia risk.

\medterm{Diphenhydramine}-- A first-generation H1 antihistamine with potent anticholinergic and sedative effects.
Used for allergic reactions, urticaria/pruritus, motion sickness, insomnia (OTC sleep
aid), and as an antitussive; IV form for anaphylaxis (adjunct) and acute dystonic
reactions.

\medterm{Diphtheria}-- An acute, toxin-mediated infectious disease caused by Corynebacterium diphtheriae, a
gram-positive aerobic bacillus that produces a potent exotoxin that inhibits protein
synthesis via ADP-ribosylation of elongation factor 2, causing local tissue necrosis and
systemic toxicity.

\medterm{Diphtheria Vaccine}-- Toxoid vaccine containing formalin-inactivated diphtheria toxin, providing immunity
against the exotoxin produced by Corynebacterium diphtheriae rather than the bacterium
itself.

\medterm{Diphyllobothrium latum}-- The fish tapeworm, a large cestode infecting humans through consumption of raw or
undercooked freshwater fish. It can grow up to 10 meters in length and absorbs vitamin
B12 in the intestine, potentially causing megaloblastic anemia. Diagnosis is by
identification of eggs or proglottids in stool. Treatment is with praziquantel.

\medterm{Diplopia}-- Double vision, in which one object is perceived as two. It may result from ocular misalignment, nerve disease,
muscle disease, or a brain disorder.

\medterm{Diplopia (Double Vision)}-- The perception of two images of a single object, classified by onset, duration, and
whether it resolves with covering one eye. Monocular diplopia (persists with one eye
covered): caused by ocular surface abnormalities (dry eye, irregular astigmatism,
cataract, corneal scar, lens dislocation)--usually benign.

\medterm{Direct Antiglobulin Test}-- An immunohematologic test that detects immunoglobulin and/or complement bound to the
surface of a patient’s erythrocytes. A positive result supports in-vivo red-cell
coating, as in autoimmune hemolytic anemia, hemolytic transfusion reactions, and
hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn.

\medterm{Direct Coombs Test}-- Direct antiglobulin test: detects antibodies or complement (C3) bound to the surface of
RBCs in vivo. Positive in: autoimmune hemolytic anemia (warm: IgG, cold: C3),
drug-induced hemolytic anemia, hemolytic transfusion reaction, and hemolytic disease of
the newborn (HDN). Grading: 1+ to 4+.

\medterm{Direct Oral Anticoagulants}-- DOACs directly inhibit specific coagulation factors. Direct thrombin inhibitors:
dabigatran. Factor Xa inhibitors: rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban, betrixaban. They have
predictable pharmacokinetics, fixed dosing, and routine monitoring is not required. They
have fewer drug interactions than warfarin.

\medterm{Direct Veneer}-- Composite restoration placed chairside for aesthetic improvement. Conservative
alternative to laboratory veneer.

\medterm{Directly Observed Therapy}-- Supervision of medication ingestion to ensure adherence. Standard of care for TB
treatment. Ensures completion of the full treatment course and reduces development of
drug resistance.

\medterm{Dirofilaria repens}-- A filarial nematode transmitted by mosquitoes that causes subcutaneous or ocular
dirofilariasis in humans, an accidental dead-end host. It presents as a solitary,
migratory subcutaneous nodule, often on the trunk or extremities, or as a conjunctival
or periorbital mass.

\medterm{Disability}-- A limitation in body function or structure, activity, or participation that results from a health condition and interaction with the person’s environment.

\medterm{Disability Evaluation}-- Assessment of functional limitations for legal, insurance, or benefits purposes.
Objective measures, standardized tests.

\medterm{Disability-Adjusted Life Year}-- A population-health measure combining years of life lost because of premature mortality
with years lived with disability. One DALY represents one lost year of healthy life
under the chosen methods and disability weights.

\medterm{Disaster Preparedness}-- Planning and organizing resources, training, and procedures to reduce harm and support effective response and recovery during disasters.

\medterm{Discectomy}-- Surgical removal of a herniated intervertebral disc fragment compressing a nerve root.
Microscopic discectomy (most common): small incision, microscope, minimal muscle
dissection.

\medterm{Discharge Planning}-- A multidisciplinary process that prepares a patient for safe transition from hospital or
facility care to the next setting. It includes medication review, follow-up, education,
equipment, self-management support, and communication of pending results.

\medterm{Discitis}-- Inflammation or infection of an intervertebral disc, often involving adjacent vertebral
endplates. It may cause severe localized back or neck pain, stiffness, fever, and
reduced mobility; causes include haematogenous infection, postoperative infection, and
inflammatory disease, with MRI being the preferred imaging study.

\medterm{Discography}-- An imaging procedure in which contrast material is injected into an intervertebral disc to assess disc structure and reproduce pain. Its diagnostic value is limited and its use is selective.

\medterm{Disease}-- Abnormal condition affecting body. Can be infectious, degenerative, neoplastic, etc.

\medterm{Disease Reporting}-- Mandatory notification of specified diseases or health events to public-health
authorities.

\medterm{Disease-Free Survival}-- The length of time after treatment during which a patient remains alive without
detectable evidence of the disease. The precise definition depends on the cancer and the
study protocol.

\medterm{Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drug}-- A medication that reduces inflammatory disease activity and helps prevent structural
damage or disability rather than providing only symptomatic relief. Conventional
synthetic, biologic, and targeted synthetic DMARDs are used according to disease and
patient factors.

\medterm{Disinfection}-- The elimination of most pathogenic microorganisms on inanimate surfaces. Disinfection
does not eliminate all forms of microbial life, particularly spores. High-level
disinfection eliminates all microorganisms except spores. Intermediate-level
disinfection eliminates mycobacteria, most viruses, and bacteria.

\medterm{Disk Diffusion Susceptibility Test}-- A standardized method in which antimicrobial-impregnated disks are placed on an
inoculated agar surface and zones of growth inhibition are measured. Zone diameters are
interpreted using validated organism- and drug-specific breakpoints.

\medterm{Diskectomy}-- The surgical removal of herniated intervertebral disk material that compresses nerve
roots or the spinal cord, causing radiculopathy or myelopathy. Indicated in severe disk
herniation causing unremitting pain, progressive motor weakness, or cauda equina
syndrome.

\medterm{Diskitis}-- Infection or inflammation of an intervertebral disc and adjacent vertebral endplates,
usually caused by bacterial infection. It causes localized back pain, stiffness, fever,
and reluctance to move, particularly in children.

\medterm{Dislocation}-- Complete displacement of the articular surfaces of a joint, resulting in loss of normal
bony alignment and often associated with ligamentous, capsular, and neurovascular
injury.

\medterm{Dislocation of the lens}-- Partial or complete displacement of the crystalline lens from its normal position within
the pupillary axis due to disruption or weakness of the zonular fibers. Causes include
Marfan syndrome, homocystinuria, trauma, and prior intraocular surgery; it may cause
acute angle-closure glaucoma or blurred vision.

\medterm{Disofenin Scan}-- Hepatobiliary imaging using Tc-99m disofenin. Similar to mebrofenin scan.

\medterm{Disorder}-- A disturbance of normal structure or function that causes symptoms, impairment, or clinically important risk. The term does not necessarily indicate a particular level of severity.

\medterm{Disorientation}-- Difficulty knowing one's time, place, situation, or identity. It may occur with delirium, infection,
metabolic disturbances, intoxication or withdrawal, brain injury, or dementia. Sudden
disorientation is an urgent medical symptom.

\medterm{Dissection}-- A tear in the inner layer of a blood vessel that allows blood to track between the vessel-wall layers and create a false channel. In the aorta, this is called aortic dissection.

\medterm{Disseminated coccidioidomycosis}-- Coccidioides infection that spreads beyond the lungs to skin, bone, meninges, or other
organs.

\medterm{Disseminated cutaneous blastomycosis}-- Blastomyces infection involving multiple areas of skin, usually after spread from a
pulmonary infection.

\medterm{Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation}-- A pathological state of simultaneous systemic activation of coagulation and
fibrinolysis, leading to widespread microvascular thrombosis that causes organ ischemia
and dysfunction, paradoxically accompanied by consumptive coagulopathy and increased
bleeding risk.

\medterm{Disseminated Mycobacterium avium–intracellulare infection}-- Widespread infection with Mycobacterium avium complex,
usually occurring in people with severe immune suppression.

\medterm{Disseminated varicella infection}-- Varicella-zoster virus infection that spreads beyond the skin and may involve the lungs,
brain, liver, or other organs.

\medterm{Dissociation}-- A disruption in the normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, emotion,
perception, behavior, and sense of self. It exists on a spectrum from normal
(daydreaming) to pathological (DID, depersonalization-derealization).

\medterm{Dissociative Amnesia}-- Inability to recall important autobiographical information, usually of a traumatic or
stressful nature, that is inconsistent with ordinary forgetting. The amnesia may be
localized, selective, generalized, or continuous.

\medterm{Dissociative Disorder}-- DSM-5 category comprising disruption of the normally integrated functions of
consciousness, memory, identity, perception, and behaviour, including dissociative
amnesia, dissociative identity disorder, and depersonalization-derealization disorder.
Strongly associated with severe early-life trauma.

\medterm{Dissociative Identity Disorder}-- The presence of two or more distinct personality states or an experience of possession
involving marked discontinuity in sense of self, agency, affect, behavior, memory,
perception, cognition, and/or sensory-motor functioning.

\medterm{Distal}-- Positioned further away from the trunk of the body or point of origin of a limb.

\medterm{Distal Radius Fractures}-- The most common upper extremity fracture. Classification: AO/OTA: A (extra-articular), B
(partial articular), C (complete articular); Frykman. Colles fracture: dorsal
angulation/displacement (fall on outstretched hand). Smith fracture: volar angulation
(fall on flexed wrist).

\medterm{Distal Tubule}-- Segment of the nephron extending from the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle to
the collecting duct. The early distal tubule reabsorbs sodium and chloride through the
NCC cotransporter, which is the target of thiazide diuretics.

\medterm{Distraction Osteogenesis}-- Surgical bone lengthening by gradual distraction. Used for jaw deformities, trauma.

\medterm{Distributive Shock}-- Shock caused by pathologic vasodilation and maldistribution of blood flow, producing
inadequate effective circulating volume despite variable cardiac output. Causes include
sepsis, anaphylaxis, neurogenic shock, and some toxic syndromes.

\medterm{Disulfiram}-- An aversive agent used to treat alcohol use disorder. Inhibits acetaldehyde
dehydrogenase, causing accumulation of acetaldehyde when alcohol is consumed—producing
flushing, tachycardia, nausea, vomiting, and hypotension ('acetaldehyde syndrome').
Provides a strong deterrent to drinking but requires motivation and abstention.

\medterm{Diuresis}-- Increased urine production. Can be physiological (fluid overload) or pharmacological
(diuretics). Osmotic diuresis (hyperglycemia), water diuresis (DI), saline diuresis
(hypertonic saline).

\medterm{Diuretic}-- A drug that increases urine production, used for edema (heart failure, cirrhosis, renal
disease), hypertension, and electrolyte disorders.

\medterm{Diverticular Disease}-- Condition characterized by the presence of diverticula, which are outpouchings of the
colonic mucosa and submucosa through defects in the muscularis propria at sites where
vasa recta penetrate the bowel wall.

\medterm{Diverticulitis}-- Inflammation and infection of colonic diverticula, a common complication of
diverticulosis affecting 10-25 percent of individuals with diverticula. The
pathophysiology involves microperforation of a diverticulum with fecal contents entering
the pericolic tissues, inciting inflammation and potential abscess formation.

\medterm{Diverticulosis}-- The presence of diverticula, small mucosal outpouchings through the colonic wall at
sites of vascular penetration, most commonly in the sigmoid colon (90 percent of cases).
Prevalence increases with age, affecting over 50 percent of individuals older than 60
years and nearly 70 percent by age 85.

\medterm{Diverticulosis and Diverticulitis}-- Diverticulosis is a condition in which small, bulging pouches (diverticula) form in the
lining of the digestive tract, most commonly in the colon. Diverticulitis occurs when
these pouches become inflamed or infected, causing abdominal pain, fever, and changes in
bowel habits.

\medterm{Dix-Hallpike Maneuver}-- The Dix-Hallpike maneuver is a diagnostic test used to identify benign paroxysmal
positional vertigo by detecting the characteristic nystagmus and vertigo that occur when
displaced otoconia within the posterior semicircular canal move in response to gravity
during specific head positioning.

\medterm{Dizziness}-- A nonspecific symptom that may mean vertigo, light-headedness, presyncope, or unsteadiness. Causes
include disorders of the vestibular system, cardiovascular system, nervous system,
vision, medications, or metabolism.

\medterm{DMFT Index}-- An epidemiologic index recording the number of permanent teeth that are decayed, missing
because of caries, or filled. It estimates cumulative caries experience but does not
measure current lesion activity or distinguish all causes of tooth loss.

\medterm{DMSA Scan}-- A nuclear-medicine study using technetium-labeled dimercaptosuccinic acid to show renal cortical function and areas of cortical injury or scarring.

\medterm{DNA}-- Deoxyribonucleic acid, the hereditary material in humans and most organisms. Double
helix of two antiparallel strands (sugar-phosphate backbone, nitrogenous bases: A-T,
G-C). Human genome: ~3 billion base pairs, ~20,000 protein-coding genes.

\medterm{DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)}-- Double-stranded helical molecule carrying genetic information. Composed of nucleotides
(sugar-phosphate backbone with bases adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine). Replication
is semi-conservative. Genes are specific sequences encoding proteins or functional RNAs.
Chromosomes are highly condensed DNA-protein complexes.

\medterm{DNA Double Helix}-- The molecular structure of DNA described by Watson and Crick in 1953, consisting of two
antiparallel polynucleotide chains wound around each other in a right-handed helix. The
sugar-phosphate backbone is on the exterior; nitrogenous bases project inward.

\medterm{DNA Profiling}-- Analysis of polymorphic DNA markers, commonly short tandem repeats, to compare
biological samples for human identification or kinship assessment. Results are
statistical associations and require validated laboratory procedures, appropriate
controls, and documented sample integrity.

\medterm{DNA Repair}-- The cellular processes that detect and correct damage or copying errors in DNA. Major pathways include
base-excision, nucleotide-excision, mismatch, and double-strand-break repair.

\medterm{DNA Repair Mechanisms}-- Cellular processes that correct DNA damage caused by replication errors, chemicals,
radiation, and reactive oxygen species. Mismatch repair (MMR): corrects base-base
mismatches and insertion-deletion loops that escape proofreading during replication.

\medterm{DNA Replication}-- The semi-conservative process by which DNA duplicates before cell division, producing
two identical daughter molecules each containing one original and one new strand.

\medterm{DNA Sequencing}-- Determination of the nucleotide order in DNA. Sanger sequencing uses chain termination and is useful for
selected small targets. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) uses massively parallel
sequencing, whole genome, whole exome, targeted panels.

\medterm{Do Not Resuscitate}-- DNR: order not to perform CPR. Can be combined with DNI (do not intubate).

\medterm{DOACs}-- Direct oral anticoagulants are a class of anticoagulant drugs that directly inhibit
specific coagulation factors, providing an alternative to warfarin with more predictable
pharmacokinetics and no routine monitoring.

\medterm{Dobutamine Stress Echocardiography}-- Pharmacological stress test using dobutamine (beta-1 agonist) to increase heart rate,
contractility, and myocardial oxygen demand. New or worsening wall motion abnormalities
indicate ischemia. Preferred when patients cannot exercise. Beta-blockers should be held
before testing.

\medterm{Docusate}-- A stool softener (surfactant laxative) that increases water and fat incorporation into
stool by lowering surface tension, facilitating defecation in patients who should avoid
straining (post-MI, post-surgery, hemorrhoids) and for opioid-related constipation.
Onset 12-72 hours.

\medterm{Domestic Violence}-- A pattern of coercive control and physical, sexual, psychological, or economic abuse by
one intimate partner or household member against another. Forensic evaluation documents
patterned injuries, defensive wounds, strangulation signs, burns, and injuries of
differing ages.

\medterm{Dominant}-- Allele whose phenotype is expressed in heterozygotes. Autosomal dominant disorders:
Huntington disease, marfan syndrome, achondroplasia. Requires only one copy for
expression.

\medterm{Donor Insemination}-- A fertility treatment in which sperm from a donor (anonymous or known) is placed into a
woman's vagina, cervix, or uterus to achieve pregnancy. Indications: male factor
infertility (azoospermia, severe oligospermia), genetic disorders in the male partner,
single women, and same-sex female couples.

\medterm{Donovanosis}-- A chronic, progressive, ulcerative genital infection caused by the intracellular
gram-negative bacterium Klebsiella granulomatis (formerly Calymmatobacterium
granulomatis). Also known as granuloma inguinale.

\medterm{Dopamine Agonist}-- A class of medications that stimulate dopamine receptors (D1-D5), mimicking the effects
of endogenous dopamine.

\medterm{Doppler Ultrasound}-- Ultrasound technique using the Doppler effect to evaluate blood flow. Color Doppler:
direction and velocity of flow (red = toward transducer, blue = away). Spectral Doppler:
waveform analysis (arterial: pulsatile; venous: continuous). Pulsed-wave: measures flow
at a specific depth.

\medterm{Doppler Velocimetry}-- Ultrasound measuring blood flow velocity in umbilical and fetal vessels. Assesses fetal
well-being.

\medterm{Doravirine}-- A non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) used in combination
antiretroviral therapy for HIV-1 infection. Doravirine binds to the HIV reverse
transcriptase enzyme and inhibits its activity through a unique mechanism that maintains
activity against strains resistant to first-generation NNRTIs.

\medterm{Dornase Alfa}-- A recombinant human DNase that cleaves extracellular DNA in airway secretions, reducing
sputum viscosity. It is used in CF to improve lung function and reduce exacerbations. It
is administered as a nebulized solution once or twice daily. It has become a standard
part of CF airway clearance regimens.

\medterm{Dorsal}-- Relating to the back side of the body; synonym for posterior in human anatomy.

\medterm{Dorsal Root Ganglion}-- A swelling on the dorsal root of a spinal nerve containing the cell bodies of primary
sensory neurons. Its pseudounipolar neurons convey peripheral sensory information to the
spinal cord.

\medterm{Dosage}-- The amount and frequency of a drug administered to achieve a therapeutic effect while
minimizing toxicity. Components: dose (amount, e.g., mg), dosing interval (frequency),
route, and duration.

\medterm{Dose}-- Amount of substance administered. Factors: weight, age, renal/hepatic function, drug
interactions. Toxic dose varies by substance and individual.

\medterm{Dose-Limiting Toxicity}-- An adverse effect of an anticancer treatment severe enough to prevent further dose
escalation or require dose reduction according to a predefined protocol. DLTs are used
in early-phase clinical trials to estimate a tolerable dose.

\medterm{Dose-Response Curve}-- A graphical representation of the relationship between the dose of a drug and the
magnitude of its biological effect. The x-axis represents dose (typically on a
logarithmic scale), and the y-axis represents the response (as percentage of maximum
effect).

\medterm{Dose-Response Relationship}-- The dose-response relationship describes the quantitative relationship between the dose
of a drug and the magnitude of the pharmacological response it produces. This
relationship is typically represented as a sigmoidal curve when response is plotted
against the log of the dose.

\medterm{Dosimetry}-- Measurement and calculation of radiation dose. Patient dosimetry for therapy.

\medterm{DOTATATE}-- Somatostatin analog labeled with Ga-68 for PET imaging of neuroendocrine tumors.

\medterm{Double Outlet Right Ventricle}-- A congenital heart defect where more than 50\% of both great arteries arise from the
right ventricle, with a VSD as the only outlet from the left ventricle.

\medterm{Double-Balloon Enteroscopy}-- Advanced endoscopic technique using an enteroscope equipped with balloons at the tip of
both the endoscope and an overtube allowing systematic advancement and visualization of
the entire small bowel. The balloons are alternately inflated and deflated to anchor the
bowel wall and advance the endoscope.

\medterm{Douche}-- A stream of water or solution directed into a body cavity, most commonly the vagina, for
cleansing, therapeutic, or contraceptive purposes.

\medterm{Down Syndrome}-- Trisomy 21, most common viable trisomy. Features: intellectual disability,
characteristic facies (flat face, upslanting palpebral fissures), hypotonia, heart
defects, increased risk of leukemia, Alzheimer disease. Maternal age is major risk
factor.

\medterm{Doxazosin}-- A selective alpha-1 adrenergic blocker causing vascular smooth muscle relaxation and
reduced peripheral resistance. Used for hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia
(relaxes prostatic smooth muscle). Extended-release formulation reduces first-dose
hypotension.

\medterm{Doxycycline}-- A tetracycline antibiotic used for many bacterial infections, acne, and some tick-borne diseases.
It can cause stomach upset, sun sensitivity, and esophageal irritation; use in pregnancy
and young children requires specific clinical consideration.

\medterm{DPP-4 Inhibitors}-- Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors that prevent degradation of endogenous GLP-1 and GIP,
enhancing incretin effects on insulin secretion. Examples include sitagliptin,
saxagliptin, linagliptin, and alogliptin. They are well-tolerated with low hypoglycemia
risk and weight neutrality. They have modest glucose-lowering effects.

\medterm{Drain (Surgical)}-- Tube for fluid evacuation. Types: closed suction (Jackson-Pratt, Hemovac), open
(Penrose), pigtail. Indications: abscess, hematoma, seroma, biliary, urinary.
Complications: infection, blockage, migration.

\medterm{Drainage Procedures}-- Image-guided drainage of fluid collections. Abscess, hematoma, biloma, urinoma. CT or
ultrasound guidance.

\medterm{Dreams (Geriatric)}-- Sleep disturbances common. Nightmares, insomnia. Evaluate for delirium, depression,
medications.

\medterm{DRESS Syndrome}-- Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms, a severe, potentially
life-threatening adverse drug reaction. Typically occurs 2-8 weeks after drug initiation
(latency longer than SJS/TEN). Causative drugs: anticonvulsants (phenytoin,
carbamazepine, lamotrigine), allopurinol, sulfonamides, dapsone, minocycline.

\medterm{Dressler Syndrome}-- A delayed pericarditis occurring 2-10 weeks after myocardial infarction or cardiac
surgery. It is an autoimmune-mediated inflammation caused by antibodies against
myocardial antigens released during tissue injury. Symptoms include fever, pleuritic
chest pain, and pericardial friction rub. ECG may show diffuse ST elevation.

\medterm{Drift (Sit-to-Stand)}-- A functional assessment of lower-limb strength, balance, and mobility. Timed sit-to-stand, chair-rise,
and timed-up-and-go tests may be used; results are interpreted according to the person's
age, baseline function, assistive devices, and clinical purpose.

\medterm{Driving Evaluation}-- Assess driving fitness after neurological, musculoskeletal conditions. On-road testing,
vehicle modifications.

\medterm{Driving Rehabilitation}-- Training for adaptive driving: hand controls, left-foot accelerator, other
modifications.

\medterm{Drooling}-- Excessive flow of saliva from the mouth, caused by increased production, impaired
swallowing, or inability to keep saliva in the mouth. It may occur with neurologic
disease, oral disorders, medication effects, or developmental conditions.

\medterm{Droplet precautions}-- Infection control measures used for pathogens transmitted through respiratory droplets.
Precautions include surgical masks, patient placement in single rooms, and spatial
separation. Droplet precautions are used for influenza, pertussis, and meningococcal
infections. The distance for spatial separation is typically 3-6 feet.

\medterm{Drowning}-- Respiratory impairment from submersion/immersion in liquid. Drowning can be fatal or
non-fatal. Pathophysiology: laryngospasm followed by aspiration of fluid, surfactant
washout, pulmonary edema, and hypoxemia. Fresh water: washes out surfactant, causes
alveolar collapse. Salt water: draws fluid into alveoli (pulmonary edema).

\medterm{Drowning (Dry/Wet Signs)}-- Respiratory impairment caused by submersion or immersion in liquid. So-called dry and wet signs are unreliable for determining the cause of death.

\medterm{Drowsiness}-- An increased tendency to fall asleep or reduced alertness, ranging from mild sleepiness
to difficulty staying awake. Causes include inadequate sleep, medical illness, sleep
disorders, substance use, and medication effects; sudden or severe drowsiness may
indicate an urgent neurologic or metabolic problem.

\medterm{Drug Allergy}-- An adverse immune-mediated reaction to a medication, representing a hypersensitivity
response that is distinct from side effects, toxicities, and idiosyncratic reactions.

\medterm{Drug Antagonism}-- An interaction where one drug blocks or decreases the effectiveness of another, such as
when an antidote blocks a poison.

\medterm{Drug Bioavailability}-- The fraction of an administered drug that reaches systemic circulation unchanged. Oral
bioavailability is affected by first-pass metabolism, absorption, and drug formulation.
IV administration has 100\% bioavailability. Bioavailability influences drug dosing and
route selection.

\medterm{Drug Classification Systems}-- Systems that group medicines according to properties such as their chemical structure, mechanism of action, therapeutic use, or effects on the body.

\medterm{Drug Clearance}-- The volume of plasma completely cleared of drug per unit time. Hepatic clearance depends
on hepatic blood flow and enzyme activity; renal clearance depends on glomerular
filtration, tubular secretion, and reabsorption. Total clearance determines maintenance
dose.

\medterm{Drug Dependence}-- A state of physiological or psychological adaptation to a drug, characterized by a
withdrawal syndrome upon discontinuation or dose reduction. Physical dependence: the
body has adapted to the presence of the drug, and abrupt cessation produces a
predictable withdrawal syndrome specific to the drug class.

\medterm{Drug Hypersensitivity Reactions}-- Immune-mediated adverse reactions to medicines. They may be immediate or delayed and can range from a mild rash to severe systemic illness.

\medterm{Drug Hypersensitivity Syndrome}-- A severe delayed drug reaction occurring 2 to 8 weeks after drug initiation. It presents
with fever, rash (morbilliform, facial edema, exfoliative dermatitis), lymphadenopathy,
eosinophilia, and internal organ involvement (hepatitis most common, also nephritis,
pneumonitis, myocarditis).

\medterm{Drug Interaction}-- A change in the effect or concentration of one medicine caused by another medicine, food, substance,
or disease.

\medterm{Drug Interactions}-- Drug interactions occur when one drug affects the activity of another drug when both are
administered together, potentially leading to therapeutic failure or toxicity.
Pharmacokinetic interactions alter the absorption, distribution, metabolism, or
excretion of a drug.

\medterm{Drug Metabolism Pharmacogenomics}-- Pharmacogenomics of drug metabolism examines how genetic variations affect individual
responses to drugs, enabling personalized medicine approaches to prescribing.

\medterm{Drug Overdose}-- Excessive ingestion of medication. Common: opioids, benzodiazepines, acetaminophen,
salicylates, tricyclic antidepressants. Management: ABCs, activated charcoal, specific
antidotes (naloxone, flumazenil, N-acetylcysteine), supportive care.

\medterm{Drug Overdose Management}-- Management of poisoning begins with assessment and support of airway, breathing,
circulation, and neurologic status. Decontamination, enhanced elimination, and specific antidotes are considered only
when appropriate for the substance, timing, and patient.

\medterm{Drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms}-- A severe hypersensitivity syndrome characterized by fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, and
multiorgan involvement triggered by medications, requiring prompt drug cessation and
systemic corticosteroids.

\medterm{Drug Resistance}-- The ability of microorganisms, cancer cells, or parasites to survive and proliferate
despite exposure to drugs that were previously effective.

\medterm{Drug Screening}-- Urine, blood, hair testing for substances of abuse. Panels: opiates, cocaine,
amphetamines, cannabinoids, PCP, benzodiazepines, barbiturates. False positives
possible. Confirmation by GC/MS or LC-MS/MS.

\medterm{Drug Testing}-- Laboratory analysis of biological specimens to detect drugs, metabolites, or poisons,
used in clinical, workplace, sports, and forensic contexts. Specimens include blood,
urine, oral fluid, hair, and postmortem tissues.

\medterm{Drug Therapeutics}-- The use of medicines to prevent, diagnose, or treat disease while balancing expected benefit and
potential harm.

\medterm{Drug Tolerance}-- A decreased pharmacological response to a drug after repeated administration, requiring
dose escalation to achieve the same effect.

\medterm{Drug Toxicity}-- Harmful effects caused by a medicine or its metabolites. Toxicity may result from excessive exposure, accumulation, interactions, or an unpredictable individual reaction.

\medterm{Drug Withdrawal}-- A clinically significant syndrome or return of symptoms caused by abrupt reduction or
cessation of a drug after physiologic adaptation or dependence has developed. Tapering
is required for some medications to reduce withdrawal and rebound effects.

\medterm{Drug-Drug Interactions}-- When one drug alters the pharmacokinetics or pharmacodynamics of another.
Pharmacokinetic interactions affect absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion
(e.g., CYP inhibitors increasing warfarin levels).

\medterm{Drug-Eluting Balloon (Vascular)}-- A vascular balloon catheter that delivers a medicine to the vessel wall during dilation,
without leaving a permanent implant.

\medterm{Drug-Eluting Stent}-- A vascular stent that slowly releases a medicine to reduce tissue growth and the risk of restenosis after angioplasty.

\medterm{Drug-Eluting Stent (Vascular)}-- A vascular stent coated with medicine that is released gradually to reduce tissue growth
and recurrent narrowing.

\medterm{Drug-Facilitated Sexual Assault}-- Sexual contact occurring when a person's capacity to consent or resist is impaired by
alcohol, medication, or another drug, whether administered covertly or knowingly
consumed. Medical care includes urgent safety assessment, treatment of injuries,
toxicology sampling when indicated, prophylaxis, and trauma-informed support.

\medterm{Drug-Induced Autoimmunity}-- Drug-induced lupus (DIL): procainamide, hydralazine, isoniazid, minocycline, TNF
inhibitors, anti-TNF; anti-histone antibodies, positive ANA, negative
anti-dsDNA/anti-Sm; symptoms resolve after drug cessation.

\medterm{Drug-Induced Liver Injury}-- Liver damage caused by a medicine, herbal product, or dietary supplement. It may produce
abnormal liver tests, hepatitis, cholestasis, or liver failure; evaluation requires considering
the exposure, timing, competing causes, and severity.

\medterm{Drusen}-- Yellowish-white deposits of extracellular material that accumulate beneath the retinal
pigment epithelium between the retinal pigment epithelium basement membrane and Bruch
membrane, and they are the hallmark clinical finding in dry age-related macular
degeneration.

\medterm{Dry Eye Disease}-- Dry eye disease, also known as keratoconjunctivitis sicca, is a multifactorial disorder
of the ocular surface characterized by loss of homeostasis of the tear film, resulting
in symptoms of ocular discomfort, visual disturbance, and tear film instability, with
potential damage to the ocular surface.

\medterm{Dry Eye Syndrome}-- A multifactorial disease of the ocular surface characterized by tear film instability,
hyperosmolarity, and inflammation. Aqueous-deficient dry eye: Sjogren syndrome
(autoimmune), non-Sjogren (lacrimal gland dysfunction, age). Evaporative dry eye:
meibomian gland dysfunction (most common), incomplete blink, environmental factors.

\medterm{Dry Powder Inhaler}-- A breath-actuated inhaler delivering powdered medication. Does not require hand-breath
coordination. Requires adequate inspiratory flow. Examples include Advair Diskus and
Spiriva HandiHaler.

\medterm{Dry Skin}-- Abnormally dry, rough, or scaly skin caused by reduced moisture and disruption of the
skin barrier. It is common with aging, low humidity, frequent washing, and eczema and
may cause itching or fissuring.

\medterm{Dry Socket (Alveolar Osteitis)}-- A painful postoperative complication of tooth extraction, most commonly occurring after
the removal of mandibular third molars (wisdom teeth). It is caused by the premature
dissolution or loss of the blood clot in the extraction socket, exposing the underlying
alveolar bone and nerve endings to the oral cavity.

\medterm{DTaP Vaccine}-- Diphtheria, Tetanus, and acellular Pertussis vaccine given at 2, 4, 6, and 15-18 months,
and 4-6 years. Protects against three serious bacterial diseases. Diphtheria causes
respiratory and cardiac complications. Tetanus causes lockjaw and muscle rigidity.
Pertussis causes whooping cough, most severe in young infants.

\medterm{Dual Antiplatelet Therapy}-- The combination of aspirin and a P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel, prasugrel, or ticagrelor)
for prevention of thrombotic events after acute coronary syndrome and percutaneous
coronary intervention. Duration varies: 1 month after bare-metal stent, 6-12 months
after drug-eluting stent, and 12 months after ACS.

\medterm{Dual-Energy CT}-- CT using two different energy spectra. Material differentiation, virtual non-contrast
images, gout detection.

\medterm{Dubin-Johnson syndrome}-- A benign, autosomal recessive disorder of conjugated hyperbilirubinemia caused by
mutations in the ABCC2 gene encoding the multidrug resistance-associated protein 2
(MRP2), which transports conjugated bilirubin from hepatocytes into bile.

\medterm{Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy}-- An X-linked recessive neuromuscular disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene
(DMD gene) on chromosome Xp21.2, resulting in absent or severely reduced dystrophin
protein, which is essential for maintaining the structural integrity of the sarcolemma
during muscle contraction.

\medterm{Ductal-Dependent Lesions}-- Congenital heart defects that require the ductus arteriosus to remain patent for
adequate systemic or pulmonary blood flow. Includes pulmonary atresia, critical aortic
stenosis, coarctation, hypoplastic left heart syndrome, and transposition of the great
arteries.

\medterm{Ductus Arteriosus}-- A fetal blood vessel connecting the pulmonary artery to the descending aorta, allowing
blood to bypass the non-functioning fetal lungs. In utero, the ductus arteriosus is kept
patent by prostaglandin E2 and low oxygen tension.

\medterm{Duloxetine}-- A serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) used for major depressive disorder,
generalized anxiety disorder, diabetic peripheral neuropathy, fibromyalgia, chronic
musculoskeletal pain (including osteoarthritis), and stress urinary incontinence.

\medterm{Dumping Syndrome}-- Rapid gastric emptying of hypertonic contents into the small bowel after gastrectomy or
gastric bypass surgery. Early dumping occurs within thirty minutes with vasomotor
symptoms including flushing, palpitations, diarrhea, and dizziness from fluid shifts
into the intestinal lumen.

\medterm{Duodenal Atresia}-- A congenital obstruction of the duodenum caused by failure of recanalization during the
8th-10th week of gestation. Associated with Down syndrome (30\%), polyhydramnios (due to
impaired fetal swallowing of amniotic fluid), and other anomalies (VACTERL, congenital
heart disease, annular pancreas, malrotation).

\medterm{Duodenal perforation}-- Full-thickness breach of the duodenal wall, most commonly caused by penetrating peptic
ulcer disease, trauma, or iatrogenic injury during endoscopy. Presents with sudden
severe epigastric pain, board-like abdominal rigidity, and peritonitis. Free air under
the diaphragm on upright chest radiograph is diagnostic.

\medterm{Duodenal Ulcer}-- Peptic ulcer in the duodenal mucosa strongly associated with H. pylori infection.
Presents with epigastric pain relieved by food typically occurring several hours after
meals and at night. Complications include hemorrhage, perforation, and gastric outlet
obstruction. Diagnosis is by upper endoscopy.

\medterm{Duodenitis}-- Inflammation of the duodenal mucosa, caused by Helicobacter pylori infection,
acid-related disease, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, infection, coeliac disease,
or inflammatory bowel disease. Symptoms may include epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting,
and gastrointestinal bleeding.

\medterm{Duodenum}-- Shortest first small intestinal segment (25 cm), C-shaped around the pancreatic head.
Four parts. Descending part has the major duodenal papilla for bile/pancreatic duct
entry via the hepatopancreatic ampulla. Brunner's glands produce alkaline mucus. Most
common peptic ulcer site. Primary iron and calcium absorption site.

\medterm{Duplex Surveillance}-- Scheduled clinical and ultrasound monitoring of an artery, vein, graft, stent, aneurysm,
or dialysis access to detect restenosis, thrombosis, endoleak, or other failure before
symptoms or limb threat develop.

\medterm{Duplex Ultrasound}-- An ultrasound examination that combines grayscale imaging of anatomy with Doppler assessment of blood flow. It can evaluate
vessels, organs, and selected soft tissues.

\medterm{Duplication}-- Presence of extra copy of chromosomal segment. Can cause gene dosage effects. Examples:
Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1A (PMP22 duplication).

\medterm{Dupuyten's contracture}-- A fibrosing disorder of the palm that causes progressive flexion contractures of one or more
fingers.

\medterm{Dupuytren contracture}-- A benign, progressive fibroproliferative disorder of the palmar fascia causing nodules,
cords, and contractures of the fingers, most commonly affecting the ring and small
fingers.

\medterm{Dura Mater}-- The outermost, thickest meningeal layer, a tough fibrous membrane. In the cranium it is
two layers: periosteal (outer, adherent to skull) and meningeal (inner), which separate
to form dural venous sinuses (e.g., superior sagittal, cavernous). Dural folds: falx
cerebri, tentorium cerebelli, falx cerebelli.

\medterm{Durable Power of Attorney}-- A legal authorization allowing a designated person to act or make decisions for another person when specified conditions are
met. In healthcare, it may appoint a healthcare proxy.

\medterm{Dural Venous Sinuses}-- Venous channels between dural layers, draining brain blood and CSF from the subarachnoid
space into the internal jugular veins. Major sinuses: superior sagittal, transverse,
sigmoid, cavernous, straight. Valveless with bidirectional flow possible.

\medterm{DVT}-- Deep venous thrombosis, the formation of a blood clot in a deep vein, most commonly in the lower extremities.

\medterm{DVT Prevention}-- Measures used to reduce the risk of deep venous thrombosis, such as early movement, mechanical compression, and anticoagulant medicine when clinically appropriate.

\medterm{Dwarfism}-- Short stature resulting from a medical condition, most commonly achondroplasia (the most
common form of skeletal dysplasia, caused by gain-of-function mutation in FGFR3,
presenting with rhizomelic limb shortening, macrocephaly, frontal bossing, and trident
hands).

\medterm{DWI FLAIR Mismatch}-- Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) MRI
sequences are used together to estimate the age of an ischemic stroke lesion and guide
treatment decisions.

\medterm{Dx}-- A common abbreviation for diagnosis or diagnostic assessment.

\medterm{DXA Scan (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry)}-- An imaging test that estimates bone mineral density, commonly at the lumbar spine and hip.
It is used to assess fracture risk and osteoporosis. T-scores are generally used for
postmenopausal women and older men, whereas Z-scores and clinical context are important
in younger people.

\medterm{Dying}-- The process of progressive physical decline that occurs as a person approaches death, often accompanied by psychological, social, and spiritual changes.

\medterm{Dysarthria}-- A motor speech disorder resulting from neurological impairment of the muscular
components of speech production, including the lips, tongue, palate, vocal cords, and
respiratory muscles.

\medterm{Dysautonomia}-- Dysfunction of the autonomic nervous system that can affect blood pressure, heart rate, sweating,
temperature regulation, digestion, bladder function, or sexual function. It may be
primary or secondary to another disease, medication, or injury.

\medterm{Dysbiosis}-- An alteration in microbiome composition, diversity, or function relative to a healthy
state, permitting overgrowth of pathobionts or loss of beneficial microbes. Causes
include antibiotics, dietary change, stress, and infection. It is associated with
inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, allergy, and Clostridioides difficile colitis.

\medterm{Dyscalculia}-- A persistent difficulty with understanding or manipulating numerical quantities and
performing arithmetic that is not explained by inadequate instruction alone. It may
occur as a developmental learning disorder or result from acquired brain injury,
particularly involving dominant parietal networks.

\medterm{Dyscrasia}-- An historic term broadly referring to any abnormal condition of the blood or bone
marrow, now used primarily in the context of plasma cell dyscrasias (also called
monoclonal gammopathies).

\medterm{Dysentery}-- Severe diarrheal illness with blood and mucus in stool, typically caused by Shigella,
Salmonella, Campylobacter, or Entamoeba histolytica. Presents with frequent bloody
stools, abdominal cramps, tenesmus, and fever. Diagnosis: stool culture, microscopy,
PCR.

\medterm{Dysesthesia}-- An unpleasant, abnormal sensation produced by a normally non-painful stimulus, or an
unpleasant, exaggerated response to a normal stimulus. It is distinguished from
paresthesia (which is simply an abnormal sensation that may or may not be unpleasant) by
its consistently unpleasant quality.

\medterm{Dysgeusia}-- Distortion, reduction, or loss of the normal sense of taste. It may result from oral or
nasal disease, viral infection, neurological injury, nutritional deficiency, systemic
illness, or medication exposure and may occur with altered smell, dry mouth, or mucosal
inflammation.

\medterm{Dysgraphia}-- A developmental or acquired impairment of written expression involving handwriting,
spelling, written composition, or the motor and language processes required for writing.
It may occur alone or with other learning, language, attention, or neurological
disorders.

\medterm{Dyslexia}-- A specific learning disorder with neurobiological origin, characterized by difficulties
with accurate and fluent word recognition, spelling, and decoding ability, despite
adequate intelligence, motivation, and educational opportunity. Affects approximately
5--10\% of the population.

\medterm{Dyslipidemia}-- An abnormal concentration or composition of circulating lipoproteins, including elevated
low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, reduced high-density
lipoprotein cholesterol, or mixed abnormalities.

\medterm{Dysmenorrhea}-- Painful menstruation, often causing cramping pelvic pain. See Menstrual Cramps.

\medterm{Dysmenorrhoea}-- Painful menstruation, classified as primary (no identifiable pelvic pathology) or
secondary (underlying structural or inflammatory condition).

\medterm{Dyspareunia}-- Painful sexual intercourse.

\medterm{Dyspepsia}-- Chronic or recurrent pain or discomfort centered in the upper abdomen. Organic causes:
peptic ulcer disease, GERD, gastritis, gastric cancer. Functional dyspepsia is diagnosed
after exclusion of organic pathology with normal upper endoscopy. Rome IV criteria
define subtypes: epigastric pain syndrome and postprandial distress syndrome.

\medterm{Dysphagia}-- Difficulty swallowing, classified as oropharyngeal (difficulty initiating a swallow,
choking, coughing, nasal regurgitation) or esophageal (sensation of food sticking
retrosternally).

\medterm{Dysphagia (Geriatric)}-- Difficulty swallowing, common in elderly. Causes: neurological (stroke, Parkinson's),
structural (cancer, stricture), muscular (presbyesophagus), medications. Symptoms:
coughing with eating, choking, pocketing food, weight loss, aspiration. Assessment:
clinical exam, videofluoroscopy, endoscopy.

\medterm{Dysphagia Diet}-- A texture- and consistency-modified diet prescribed after assessment of swallowing
safety. Liquids and foods may be thickened or altered to reduce aspiration risk while
maintaining adequate energy, protein, hydration, and patient acceptability.

\medterm{Dysphagia Rehabilitation}-- Therapy to improve safe swallowing and nutrition in a person with dysphagia. It may include
swallow exercises, compensatory techniques, diet modification, and education based on
the cause and swallowing assessment.

\medterm{Dysphasia}-- A language disorder caused by damage to the language-dominant hemisphere of the brain
(typically left), involving difficulty with expression, comprehension, reading, or
writing. Dysphasia (often used interchangeably with aphasia): partial loss of language
function.

\medterm{Dysphonia}-- An abnormal voice quality encompassing hoarseness, breathiness, roughness, vocal
fatigue, pitch breaks, and strained or whispered speech. It is organic when due to
structural laryngeal pathology (nodules, polyps, laryngitis, laryngeal cancer) and
functional when due to voice misuse, muscle tension dysphonia, or psychogenic causes.

\medterm{Dysplasia}-- Disordered cellular growth and maturation characterized by structural abnormalities
within a tissue, representing a pre-neoplastic condition that may progress to invasive
carcinoma.

\medterm{Dysplastic Nevus}-- Atypical nevus: a melanocytic lesion with clinical and histologic atypia, larger (>5-6
mm), with irregular borders, variable color (pink-tan-brown), and a macular/papular
component; often multiple in the dysplastic nevus syndrome (familial atypical
mole-melanoma, FAMMM).

\medterm{Dyspnea}-- The subjective sensation of difficult or uncomfortable breathing. See Shortness of Breath. It may result from cardiopulmonary,
metabolic, neuromuscular, or psychological causes.

\medterm{Dyspnea on Exertion}-- Shortness of breath precipitated by physical activity, a cardinal symptom of cardiac and
pulmonary disease. Cardiac causes: heart failure, coronary artery disease (angina
equivalent), valvular disease; pulmonary: COPD, asthma, ILD, anemia, deconditioning.
Quantified by NYHA functional class (heart failure) and mMRC scale (dyspnea).

\medterm{Dyspnoea}-- The subjective sensation of difficult or labored breathing. See Shortness of Breath.

\medterm{Dysthymia (Persistent Depressive Disorder)}-- A chronic form of depression with depressed mood for most of the day, more days than
not, for \(\geq 2\) years (\(\geq 1\) year in children/adolescents). Symptoms: poor appetite or
overeating, insomnia or hypersomnia, low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration,
and hopelessness.

\medterm{Dystonia}-- A movement disorder characterized by sustained or intermittent muscle contractions
causing abnormal, often repetitive, movements and postures.

\medterm{Dystrophic Calcification}-- The deposition of calcium phosphate salts in damaged, necrotic, or degenerating tissues
despite normal serum calcium levels. It occurs in areas of tissue injury, inflammation,
or chronic degeneration where the local tissue environment favors calcium precipitation.

\medterm{Dysuria}-- Painful, burning, or uncomfortable sensation experienced during urination. Most commonly
indicates inflammation or infection of the lower urinary tract. Causes in females: acute
cystitis, urethritis (Chlamydia trachomatis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae), vaginitis (Candida,
Trichomonas), and vulvar atrophy.

\medterm{Dacryocystocele}-- A cystic swelling caused by blockage and dilation of the tear-duct system, usually near the inner corner of the eye.

\medterm{Demyelination}-- Loss or damage of the myelin sheath that normally insulates nerve fibers and speeds nerve signaling.

\medterm{Dental Calculus}-- Hardened dental plaque formed when mineral salts accumulate on teeth.

\medterm{Denys-Drash Syndrome}-- A rare genetic disorder associated with abnormal kidney development, nephropathy, and an increased risk of Wilms tumor.

\medterm{Dermatographic Urticaria}-- A form of hives in which scratching or pressure causes raised, itchy lines on the skin.

\medterm{Deuteranopia}-- Red-green color blindness caused by absence or severe dysfunction of green-sensitive cone photopigments.

\medterm{Diabetic Angiopathy}-- Blood-vessel damage caused by diabetes, affecting small or large vessels.

\medterm{Diastolic Murmur}-- A heart murmur heard while the heart relaxes between contractions.

\medterm{Diphtheria Toxin}-- A toxin produced by certain Corynebacterium diphtheriae bacteria that damages cells and tissues.

\medterm{Distal Pancreatectomy}-- Surgical removal of the body or tail of the pancreas, usually with preservation of the pancreatic head.

\medterm{DNA Damage}-- A chemical or structural change in DNA that can interfere with gene function or replication.

\medterm{Duane Retraction Syndrome}-- A congenital eye-movement disorder in which abnormal eye-muscle innervation limits horizontal movement and may cause the eye to retract.

\medterm{Duodenal Obstruction}-- Partial or complete blockage that prevents contents from passing through the duodenum.

\medterm{Dysfibrinogenemia}-- An inherited or acquired disorder in which fibrinogen is abnormal and blood clotting may be impaired or excessive.

\medterm{D2 Receptor}-- A dopamine receptor involved in movement, motivation, reward, and some psychiatric disorders.

\medterm{De Quervain's Tendonitis}-- Painful inflammation or narrowing of the tendons on the thumb side of the wrist.

\medterm{Declarative Memory}-- Memory for facts and events that can be consciously recalled.

\medterm{Decongestant}-- A medicine that reduces swelling of the nasal lining and relieves nasal congestion.

\medterm{Deep Sleep}-- The deepest stage of non-rapid-eye-movement sleep, during which arousal is difficult and physical restoration occurs.

\medterm{Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome}-- A circadian-rhythm disorder in which sleep and waking occur substantially later than desired.

\medterm{Dementia Pugilistica}-- A progressive brain disorder associated with repeated head trauma, historically described in some professional boxers.

\medterm{Demineralization}-- Loss of mineral from a tissue, such as tooth enamel or bone, which may weaken the tissue.

\medterm{Dendrites}-- Branching extensions of a nerve cell that receive signals from other cells.

\medterm{Dendritic Cells}-- Immune cells that capture and present antigens to lymphocytes, helping initiate adaptive immune responses.

\medterm{Dermal Papilla}-- A structure at the base of a hair follicle that supplies signals and nutrients important for hair growth.

\medterm{Detrusor Instability}-- Involuntary bladder-muscle contractions that can cause urgency and urge incontinence.

\medterm{Detrusor Muscle}-- The smooth muscle in the bladder wall that contracts to expel urine.

\medterm{Detumescence}-- The return of an erectile tissue to its non-erect state.

\medterm{Diastolic Blood Pressure}-- The pressure in the arteries while the heart relaxes between contractions.

\medterm{Diastolic Heart Failure}-- Heart failure in which impaired ventricular relaxation or increased stiffness limits filling of the heart.

\medterm{Dietary Fiber}-- Plant material that is not fully digested and supports bowel function and other aspects of health.

\medterm{Digital Rectal Examination}-- Examination of the rectum and nearby structures using a lubricated, gloved finger.

\medterm{Digitalis}-- A group of cardiac glycosides, including digoxin, that can increase cardiac contraction and affect heart rhythm.

\medterm{Dihydrotestosterone}-- A potent androgen formed from testosterone that contributes to prostate growth and male sexual development.

\medterm{Direct Transmission}-- Spread of an infectious organism through immediate contact or short-range respiratory droplets from an infected source.

\medterm{Discoid Lupus Erythematosus}-- A chronic form of cutaneous lupus that causes persistent, inflamed skin plaques that may scar.

\medterm{Diverticulum}-- A pouch-like projection from the wall of a hollow organ, especially the intestine.

\medterm{Dorsal Kyphosis}-- Excessive forward curvature of the upper spine, often associated with vertebral fractures or degenerative change.

\medterm{Double-Blind Study}-- A clinical study in which neither participants nor the investigators assessing outcomes know the assigned treatment group.

\medterm{Dry-Powder Inhaler}-- A device that delivers powdered medicine to the lungs when the user inhales through it.

\medterm{Dual-Energy X-Ray Absorptiometry}-- An imaging test that measures bone mineral density, commonly abbreviated DEXA or DXA.

\medterm{Dupuytren's Disease}-- Thickening and shortening of tissue in the palm that progressively bends one or more fingers toward the palm.

\medterm{Delayed Sleep Phase}-- A circadian-rhythm disorder in which a person falls asleep and wakes later than desired; also called delayed sleep-wake phase disorder.

\medterm{Dermatographia}-- A form of physical urticaria in which scratching or pressure causes raised, red, itchy lines on the skin.

\medterm{Desmoid Tumors}-- Rare, locally aggressive tumors arising from connective tissue that do not usually spread to distant organs.

\medterm{Desmoplastic Small Round Cell Tumors}-- Rare aggressive tumors that usually arise in the abdomen or pelvis and primarily affect children and young adults.

\medterm{Diabetic Coma}-- A state of profound unconsciousness caused by a severe diabetes-related disturbance in blood glucose or metabolism.

\medterm{Diabetic Hypoglycemia}-- An abnormally low blood glucose level in a person with diabetes, often caused by insulin or other glucose-lowering medicines.

\medterm{Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma}-- A fast-growing cancer of B lymphocytes and the most common type of non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

\medterm{Diffuse Midline Glioma}-- An aggressive brain or spinal-cord tumor arising in midline structures, often in children.

\medterm{Dislocated Shoulder}-- Displacement of the upper arm bone from the shoulder joint.

\medterm{Dissociative Disorders}-- Mental health disorders involving disruption in the normal integration of memory, identity, awareness, emotion, perception, or behavior.

\medterm{Double Uterus}-- A congenital condition in which the uterus develops as two separate cavities or two uteruses.

\medterm{Double-Outlet Right Ventricle}-- A congenital heart defect in which both major arteries arise mainly from the right ventricle.

\medterm{Dry Macular Degeneration}-- The more common form of age-related macular degeneration, involving gradual damage to the macula without abnormal blood-vessel growth.

\medterm{Dry Eyes}-- Irritation or discomfort caused by inadequate tear production or excessive tear evaporation.

\medterm{Dry Mouth}-- A condition in which the mouth does not produce enough saliva; also called xerostomia.

\medterm{Dural Arteriovenous Fistulas}-- Abnormal connections between arteries and veins in the dura mater surrounding the brain or spinal cord.

\medterm{Dyshidrosis}-- A skin disorder causing small, intensely itchy blisters on the palms, sides of the fingers, or soles.

\medterm{Dysthymia}-- A former term for persistent depressive disorder, a chronic condition with long-lasting depressive symptoms.

\medterm{Ductal Carcinoma In Situ}-- A noninvasive breast cancer in which abnormal cells remain within the milk ducts; commonly abbreviated DCIS.
