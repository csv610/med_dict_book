\chapter{Pocket Dictionary: E Terms}

\medterm{E Coli Enteritis}-- Intestinal infection caused by pathogenic strains of Escherichia coli. Depending on the
strain, it may cause watery or bloody diarrhea, abdominal cramps, vomiting, or systemic
complications such as hemolytic uremic syndrome.

\medterm{E Coli Infection}-- Infections caused by Escherichia coli, a gram-negative enteric bacillus. Causes: UTI
(most common—cystitis, pyelonephritis), diarrhea (enterotoxigenic ETEC—traveler's
diarrhea; enteropathogenic; enterohemorrhagic EHEC O157:H7—bloody diarrhea, HUS;
enteroinvasive), neonatal meningitis, sepsis, and intra-abdominal infections.

\medterm{Ear}-- Organ of hearing and balance, divided into external, middle, and inner ear. External:
auricle (pinna), external auditory canal, tympanic membrane. Middle: air-filled cavity
in temporal bone, auditory ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes), Eustachian tube (to
nasopharynx).

\medterm{Ear Barotrauma}-- Injury to the ear caused by a pressure difference between the middle ear and the
surrounding environment, commonly during air travel or diving. It may cause ear pain,
fullness, hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, or tympanic-membrane rupture.

\medterm{Ear canal}-- A tube leading from the eardrum to the outer ear.

\medterm{Ear Diseases}-- Any disorder affecting the structures of the ear--external ear (pinna, ear canal),
middle ear (tympanic membrane, ossicles, Eustachian tube), or inner ear (cochlea,
vestibular apparatus).

\medterm{Ear Infection}-- Infection or inflammation of the ear, commonly involving the external canal or middle
ear. It may cause ear pain, fever, reduced hearing, discharge, or irritability in
children. Also called otitis.

\medterm{Ear Infections}-- Infections of the ear, most commonly affecting the middle ear (otitis media) or the
outer ear canal (otitis externa). They are often caused by bacteria or viruses and
present with ear pain, drainage, hearing loss, and sometimes fever.

\medterm{Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT)}-- A medical specialty (otolaryngology) focused on the diagnosis and treatment of disorders
affecting the ears, nose, throat, head, and neck. Conditions managed include hearing
loss, sinusitis, tonsillitis, voice disorders, and head and neck cancers.

\medterm{Earache}-- Pain in or around the ear, caused by otitis, blockage, trauma, dental disease,
temporomandibular-joint disorders, or referred throat pathology. Persistent, severe, or
discharge-associated pain requires evaluation.

\medterm{Earache (Otalgia)}-- Pain originating from the ear, a common symptom with diverse causes. Otalgia is
classified as primary (originating within the ear itself) or secondary/referred (pain
perceived in the ear but arising from another site).

\medterm{Eardrum}-- A thin membrane separating the ear canal and middle ear.

\medterm{Early Decelerations}-- A fetal heart rate pattern with a gradual decrease (onset to nadir >30 seconds) that
mirrors the contraction and returns to baseline as the contraction ends. Caused by fetal
head compression during contractions (vagal response). A benign, physiologic finding
associated with normal fetal acid-base status. No intervention required.

\medterm{Early Detection}-- Testing asymptomatic individuals for disease. See screening.

\medterm{Early Intervention}-- Rehabilitation for infants, toddlers with developmental delays or disabilities.

\medterm{Earwax}-- Cerumen, a waxy secretion produced by the ceruminous glands in the outer third of the
external auditory canal. Earwax lubricates and protects the skin of the ear canal,
trapping dust, debris, and microorganisms to prevent them from reaching the tympanic
membrane.

\medterm{Eating Disorder}-- A group of conditions characterized by abnormal eating habits and distorted body image,
including anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder, and
avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID). Anorexia: severe calorie restriction,
intense fear of weight gain, and body image disturbance with dangerously low weight.

\medterm{Ebola}-- Ebola virus disease (EVD): a severe, often fatal viral hemorrhagic fever caused by Ebola
virus (Filoviridae, RNA), transmitted by direct contact with blood/body fluids of
infected humans/animals. Incubation 2-21 days.

\medterm{Ebola virus}-- A filovirus that causes Ebola virus disease, a severe hemorrhagic fever. The virus is
transmitted through contact with infected body fluids. The incubation period is 2-21
days. Symptoms include fever, headache, myalgia, and hemorrhage. Diagnosis is made by
PCR and antigen detection.

\medterm{Ebola Virus Disease}-- See Ebola.

\medterm{Ebstein Anomaly}-- A rare congenital heart defect accounting for less than 1 percent of all congenital
heart disease, characterized by apical displacement of the septal and posterior leaflets
of the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle, resulting in atrialization of the
proximal right ventricle and a small functional right ventricular chamber.

\medterm{Ecchymosis}-- An ecchymosis is a flat, purple or blue discoloration of the skin larger than 10
millimeters caused by extravasation of blood from damaged blood vessels into the
subcutaneous tissue. Unlike petechiae, which result from platelet disorders, ecchymoses
may result from coagulopathies, trauma, or vascular fragility.

\medterm{Eccrine Gland}-- A coiled sweat gland distributed throughout the skin that secretes a watery fluid
directly onto the skin surface through a duct. Eccrine sweating supports
thermoregulation and is stimulated by sympathetic cholinergic fibres; the glands are
especially numerous on the palms, soles, and forehead.

\medterm{ECG/EKG Machine}-- Records electrical activity of heart. 12-lead ECG standard. Components: electrodes,
leads, amplifier, recorder. Essential for cardiac diagnosis.

\medterm{Echinocandins}-- A class of antifungal agents that inhibit beta-1,3-glucan synthase, an enzyme essential
for synthesis of beta-1,3-glucan, a major component of the fungal cell wall. This unique
mechanism targets a structure absent in mammalian cells, resulting in favorable toxicity
profiles.

\medterm{Echinococcal cysts}-- See Echinococcosis.

\medterm{Echinococcosis}-- See Cystic echinococcosis.

\medterm{Echinococcus granulosus}-- A tapeworm that causes hydatid disease (cystic echinococcosis). The adult worm infects
dogs, and humans are accidental intermediate hosts. Infection occurs through ingestion
of eggs from dog feces. Larval cysts form in the liver and lungs, causing
space-occupying lesions.

\medterm{Echinocytes (Burr Cells)}-- Red blood cells with uniform, small, evenly spaced serrated or spiky membrane
projections along the entire erythrocyte circumference. Most commonly caused by
End-Stage Renal Disease (Uremia), pyruvate kinase deficiency, severe hypokalemia, or in
vitro artifact (crenation due to glass slides or prolonged storage).

\medterm{Echo}-- Ultrasound imaging of the heart, the primary modality for assessing cardiac structure
and function. See echocardiography.

\medterm{Echocardiogram}-- An ultrasound examination of the heart that shows its chambers, valves, wall motion, and
blood flow. It is used to evaluate heart failure, valve disease, congenital defects, and
other cardiac disorders.

\medterm{Echocardiography}-- Ultrasound imaging of the heart, the primary modality for assessing cardiac structure
and function. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is the most common approach;
transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) provides better views of posterior structures
(left atrium, mitral valve, aorta).

\medterm{Echogenic Bowel}-- Increased echogenicity of the fetal bowel relative to adjacent bone (iliac wing).
Identified in 0.1-0.5\1 of second-trimester ultrasounds.

\medterm{Echogenic Intracardiac Focus (EIF)}-- A small bright spot (calcification) in the fetal heart, typically in the left ventricle
(papillary muscle). Seen in 3-5\1 of normal pregnancies. In isolation, it is a normal
variant with minimal aneuploidy risk. However, EIF is more common in trisomy 21 fetuses
(10-30\1), particularly when combined with other soft markers.

\medterm{Echolalia}-- The involuntary, automatic repetition of words or phrases spoken by another person, a
speech phenomenon observed in various neurologic and psychiatric conditions. Immediate
echolalia involves repeating sounds immediately after hearing them; delayed echolalia
involves repeating phrases heard hours, days, or weeks earlier.

\medterm{Echovirus}-- A group of enteroviruses that commonly cause asymptomatic or mild infection but may also
cause aseptic meningitis, encephalitis, rash, respiratory illness, myocarditis, or
neonatal sepsis. Transmission is mainly faecal-oral or respiratory, and diagnosis is
usually by nucleic-acid testing or viral culture in selected cases.

\medterm{Eclampsia}-- The occurrence of generalized tonic-clonic seizures in a patient with preeclampsia,
representing a medical emergency. Complications include aspiration, placental abruption,
disseminated intravascular coagulation, and maternal/fetal death.

\medterm{ECOG Performance Status}-- A five-point scale describing functional impairment, from 0 for fully active to 5 for
death. It is widely used in clinical trials, prognostic assessment, and decisions about
systemic anticancer treatment.

\medterm{Ecthyma gangrenosum}-- A severe cutaneous infection caused by Pseudomonas aeruginosa, characterized by
painless, hemorrhagic vesicles that rapidly progress to necrotic ulcers with black
eschars. It occurs almost exclusively in immunocompromised or neutropenic patients and
results from bacterial invasion of blood vessel walls.

\medterm{Ectoderm}-- The outermost of the three primary germ layers in an embryo, which develops into the
skin, nervous system, and sensory organs.

\medterm{Ectodermal Dysplasia}-- A group of inherited disorders affecting tissues derived from the embryonic ectoderm,
including hair, teeth, nails, skin, and sweat glands. Findings vary but may include
sparse hair, abnormal teeth, reduced sweating, heat intolerance, and recurrent
infections.

\medterm{Ectopia lentis}-- Displacement or malposition of the crystalline lens from its normal position, most
commonly due to trauma but also associated with systemic conditions including Marfan
syndrome (superior displacement), homocystinuria (inferior displacement), and
Weill-Marchesani syndrome.

\medterm{Ectopic ACTH Syndrome}-- Adrenocorticotropic hormone secretion by non-pituitary tumors causing Cushing syndrome.
Most commonly caused by small cell lung cancer and bronchial carcinoid tumors. Other
sources include thymic carcinoid, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, and medullary
thyroid carcinoma.

\medterm{Ectopic Cushing Syndrome}-- Hypercortisolism caused by an extrapituitary tumor producing adrenocorticotropic hormone
or, rarely, corticotropin-releasing hormone. It may cause hypertension, diabetes,
hypokalemia, muscle weakness, infection risk, and rapid features of cortisol excess.

\medterm{Ectopic Heartbeat}-- A heartbeat originating outside the normal sinoatrial-node rhythm. Premature atrial or
ventricular beats may feel like a skipped beat, flutter, or sudden thump and can occur
in healthy people or with structural heart disease.

\medterm{Ectopic Pregnancy}-- Implantation of a fertilized ovum outside the uterine cavity, most commonly in the
fallopian tube (95\1). Risk factors: prior ectopic, PID, tubal surgery, IUD, smoking,
and assisted reproductive technology. Presents with lower abdominal pain, amenorrhea,
and vaginal bleeding. Rupture causes hemodynamic instability.

\medterm{Ectopic thyroid}-- Thyroid tissue located outside its normal pretracheal position, most commonly along the
path of thyroid descent from the foramen cecum to the normal cervical location. The most
common site is the base of the tongue (lingual thyroid).

\medterm{Ectopic Thyroid Tissue}-- Thyroid tissue located outside the normal pretracheal position resulting from failure of
normal thyroid descent during embryological development. Most commonly found at the base
of the tongue termed lingual thyroid. May be the only functioning thyroid tissue.
Symptoms include dysphagia, dysphonia, and upper airway obstruction.

\medterm{Ectrodactyly}-- A congenital limb malformation characterized by a deep median cleft of the hand or foot
with missing central digits, resulting in a claw-like or lobster-claw appearance. Also
known as split hand/foot malformation (SHFM). Most cases are autosomal dominant with
incomplete penetrance, linked to mutations in TP63, DLX5, or WNT10B genes.

\medterm{Ectropion}-- Outward turning of the eyelid margin, most often the lower lid, exposing the conjunctiva
and causing tearing, irritation, dryness, and incomplete eyelid closure. It may be
involutional, paralytic, cicatricial, or mechanical; persistent exposure can damage the
cornea.

\medterm{Eczema}-- A group of inflammatory skin conditions characterized by pruritic, erythematous, scaly
patches. Atopic dermatitis is the most common form. Nummular eczema presents as
coin-shaped lesions. Dyshidrotic eczema affects palms and soles with vesicles. Eczema
herpeticum is a disseminated HSV infection in eczema patients, a medical emergency.

\medterm{Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)}-- A chronic inflammatory skin condition characterized by dry, itchy, and inflamed patches
of skin. It is driven by a combination of genetic predisposition, immune system
dysfunction, and environmental triggers.

\medterm{Eczema herpeticum}-- A widespread viral skin infection caused by herpes simplex virus occurring in patients
with pre-existing skin barrier defects, most commonly atopic dermatitis. It presents
with monomorphic, umbilicated vesicles, pustules, and hemorrhagic crusted erosions,
often with fever and lymphadenopathy.

\medterm{Edema}-- Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues. Types: pitting (press leaves
indentation), non-pitting (myxedema, lymphedema). Causes: heart failure, kidney disease,
liver cirrhosis, venous insufficiency, lymphatic obstruction, medications (calcium
channel blockers, NSAIDs).

\medterm{Edema (historically Dropsy)}-- An historic term for edema, referring to the abnormal accumulation of serous fluid in
the tissues or body cavities.

\medterm{Edentulism}-- Complete or partial absence of natural teeth. It can impair mastication, speech,
nutrition, facial support, and quality of life and may be managed with removable, fixed,
or implant-supported prostheses.

\medterm{Edward Syndrome}-- Trisomy 18. Features: severe intellectual disability, clenched hands, rocker-bottom
feet, cardiac defects, feeding difficulties. Most die within first year.

\medterm{Edwards Syndrome}-- Trisomy 18, the second most common autosomal trisomy in live births (1 in 5,000). Caused
by complete trisomy 18 (94\1), mosaicism, or partial trisomy (translocation).

\medterm{EFAST Examination}-- Extended focused assessment with sonography in trauma, a bedside ultrasound examination
for intraperitoneal or pericardial free fluid and pneumothorax or hemothorax. It rapidly
informs trauma resuscitation but does not exclude all internal injuries when negative.

\medterm{Effacement}-- Thinning (shortening) of the cervix during labor, expressed as a percentage (0-100
percent), as the cervix is taken up into the lower uterine segment, enabling dilation.
Occurs with cervical dilation in the first stage of labor, driven by contractions and
the presenting part.

\medterm{Effective Dose}-- A radiation-protection quantity that estimates the population-averaged stochastic health
risk from nonuniform exposure by weighting absorbed doses to different organs and
tissues. It is not a patient-specific prediction of cancer risk.

\medterm{Effective Half-Life}-- The time required for the activity of a radionuclide in a tissue or body to fall by one
half through both physical radioactive decay and biologic elimination. It determines the
duration of radiation exposure in that compartment.

\medterm{Effective Reproduction Number (Rt)}-- The average number of secondary infections caused by an infectious person at a
particular time, accounting for existing immunity, behavior, interventions, and
population structure. Sustained transmission generally requires Rt above 1.

\medterm{Efficacy}-- The maximum therapeutic effect a drug or intervention can produce under ideal conditions
(e.g., in a clinical trial). Efficacy is determined by the drug's mechanism of action
and the receptor-effector system; it is measured by the Emax (maximal effect) on the
dose-response curve.

\medterm{EGD}-- Esophagogastroduodenoscopy: upper endoscopy visualizing the esophagus, stomach, and
duodenum using a flexible endoscope. Indications: dyspepsia with alarm features,
dysphagia, GI bleeding (diagnosis and therapy—injection, clips, banding, cautery),
biopsy (celiac, H.

\medterm{EGFR inhibition}-- A targeted cancer therapy using monoclonal antibodies or small-molecule tyrosine kinase
inhibitors that block the epidermal growth factor receptor signaling pathway, inhibiting
tumor cell proliferation, survival, and metastasis. Used in colorectal cancer, non-small
cell lung cancer, and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma.

\medterm{Egophony}-- An abnormal vocal sound heard during auscultation in which spoken ``E'' sounds are heard
as ``A'' (the ``E-to-A'' change). It is a sign of lung consolidation, as in lobar
pneumonia. The consolidated lung tissue conducts high-frequency sounds better than
air-filled lung.

\medterm{Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome}-- A group of hereditary connective tissue disorders caused by defects in collagen
synthesis or processing.

\medterm{EHR}-- Electronic Health Record. Broader than EMR, includes population health.

\medterm{Ehrlichia and Anaplasma}-- Obligate intracellular bacteria transmitted by ticks that cause ehrlichiosis and
anaplasmosis. Ehrlichia chaffeensis causes human monocytic ehrlichiosis. Anaplasma
phagocytophilum causes human granulocytic anaplasmosis. The organisms infect white blood
cells (Ehrlichia) or granulocytes (Anaplasma).

\medterm{Ehrlichiosis}-- A tick-borne bacterial infection caused by Ehrlichia chaffeensis (human monocytic
ehrlichiosis) and E. ewingii (granulocytic). Endemic in the southeastern and
south-central US. Presents with fever, headache, myalgia, malaise, and
thrombocytopenia/leukopenia. Rash less common than RMSF.

\medterm{Eisenmenger Syndrome}-- Reversal of a left-to-right cardiac shunt due to irreversible pulmonary hypertension,
resulting in right-to-left shunting and cyanosis. It occurs in untreated congenital
heart defects with large left-to-right shunts (VSD, ASD, PDA) that cause progressive
pulmonary vascular disease.

\medterm{Eisenmenger's syndrome}-- See Eisenmenger Syndrome.

\medterm{Ejaculation}-- The expulsion of seminal fluid from the male urethra during sexual climax, a reflex
coordinated by the sympathetic nervous system (T10-L2) and involving contraction of the
epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, and bulbospongiosus muscle.

\medterm{Ejection Click}-- A high-pitched sound occurring at the beginning of systole, caused by opening of a
stenotic semilunar valve. Aortic ejection click is heard in bicuspid aortic valve and
aortic stenosis with pliable leaflets. Pulmonary ejection click is heard in pulmonary
stenosis and disappears as the valve becomes more calcified and immobile.

\medterm{Ejection Fraction}-- The percentage of blood ejected from the left ventricle during systole, calculated as
(stroke volume / end-diastolic volume) x 100. Normal EF is 55-70\1. Heart failure with
reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF): EF 40\1 or less; heart failure with preserved
ejection fraction (HFpEF): EF 50\1 or greater.

\medterm{Elastic Cartilage}-- Flexible cartilage containing abundant elastic fibers in its matrix. It supports
structures that require shape retention with flexibility, including the external ear,
epiglottis, and auditory tube.

\medterm{Elastography}-- Imaging tissue stiffness. Used for liver fibrosis assessment, breast lesion
characterization, thyroid nodule evaluation.

\medterm{Elbow Joint}-- A synovial hinge complex comprising the humeroulnar and humeroradial joints. It
primarily permits flexion and extension, while the proximal radioulnar component permits
forearm rotation.

\medterm{Elder Abuse}-- Intentional or neglectful act causing harm to older person. Types: physical, emotional,
sexual, financial, neglect. Risk factors: social isolation, caregiver stress, cognitive
impairment, dependency. Mandatory reporting required. Assessment: direct question,
observation, collateral history.

\medterm{Elderly}-- Person aged 65 or older. Divisions: young-old (65-74), middle-old (75-84), old-old
(85+). Heterogeneous population with varying health status, needs, preferences.

\medterm{Electrical Cardioversion}-- Delivery of a synchronized electrical shock timed to the R wave to terminate selected
tachyarrhythmias, such as atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, or certain
supraventricular tachycardias. Sedation, thromboembolic risk, and the patient's
hemodynamic status must be assessed.

\medterm{Electrical Injury}-- Tissue damage caused by passage of electrical current through the body. Severity depends
on voltage, current (alternating vs. direct), pathway, duration, and resistance.
High-voltage (>1000V): extensive internal tissue damage (muscle necrosis, compartment
syndrome, rhabdomyolysis) with relatively small skin burns (entry/exit wounds).

\medterm{Electrical Stimulation}-- TENS, NMES for pain, muscle re-education, spasticity.

\medterm{Electrocardiogram}-- A recording of the heart's electrical activity through electrodes placed on the skin. It
helps detect arrhythmias, conduction abnormalities, myocardial ischemia or infarction,
and some structural heart conditions. Also called ECG or EKG.

\medterm{Electrocardiogram (ECG / EKG)}-- A recording of the heart's electrical activity using electrodes placed on the body
surface. A standard 12-lead ECG provides views of the heart from different angles: limb
leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF) and precordial leads (V1-V6).

\medterm{Electrocoagulation}-- A surgical technique using high-frequency electrical current to destroy tissue or stop
bleeding by coagulating proteins, also known as diathermy or cauterization.

\medterm{Electroconvulsive Therapy}-- A neuromodulation treatment involving the induction of a generalized seizure under
general anesthesia using electrical current applied to the scalp. It is the most
effective treatment for severe depression, especially with psychotic features,
catatonia, or suicidal ideation.

\medterm{Electrocution}-- Injury from electrical current. Types: low voltage (<1000V), high voltage (>1000V).
Effects: cardiac arrhythmias, muscle contractions, burns, neurological damage.
Management: ABCs, cardiac monitoring, tetanus prophylaxis, wound care, check for
compartment syndrome.

\medterm{Electroencephalogram}-- EEG: a recording of electrical activity of the brain via scalp electrodes, reflecting
cortical neuronal activity. Uses: diagnosis and classification of seizures/epilepsy
(interictal epileptiform discharges, spikes/waves), status epilepticus monitoring,
assessment of encephalopathy, sleep disorders, and determination of brain death.

\medterm{Electroencephalogram (EEG)}-- Recording of the brain's electrical activity from scalp electrodes. Background rhythms:
alpha (8-13 Hz, relaxed, eyes closed, occipital), beta (13-30 Hz, alert/active), theta
(4-8 Hz, drowsy/light sleep, pathological in awake adults), delta (<4 Hz, deep sleep,
pathological in awake).

\medterm{Electroencephalography}-- A non-invasive neurophysiological test that records the electrical activity of the brain
using electrodes placed on the scalp according to the international 10-20 system.

\medterm{Electroencephalography (EEG)}-- Recording of electrical activity from scalp electrodes. Used for seizure disorders,
encephalopathy, brain death.

\medterm{Electrolyte Balance}-- Homeostasis of sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, phosphate. Regulated by
kidneys, hormones (aldosterone, ADH, PTH). Imbalance causes neuromuscular, cardiac,
metabolic dysfunction.

\medterm{Electrolyte Imbalance}-- Abnormal serum concentration of electrolytes (sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium,
phosphate, chloride). Hyponatremia (<135 mEq/L): most common electrolyte disorder;
causes include SIADH, heart failure, hypovolemia, and polydipsia. Hypernatremia (>145
mEq/L): free water deficit from poor intake, diabetes insipidus, or diuretics.

\medterm{Electrolytes}-- Ions in body fluids that carry an electrical charge and are essential for normal
physiologic function.

\medterm{Electromagnetic Navigation Bronchoscopy}-- A guidance system for bronchoscopy that uses electromagnetic fields to navigate to
peripheral lung lesions. It allows biopsy of lesions not visible on standard
bronchoscopy. It is more accurate than conventional transbronchial biopsy for peripheral
lesions. It may reduce the need for CT-guided biopsy.

\medterm{Electromyography}-- Suite of diagnostic procedures designed to evaluate the physiological function of the
lower urinary tract (bladder and urethral sphincter) during filling, storage, and
emptying phases. See urodynamic testing.

\medterm{Electromyography (EMG)}-- Recording of electrical activity from muscles. Used for neuropathy, myopathy,
radiculopathy.

\medterm{Electron Transport Chain}-- Series of protein complexes (I-IV) and mobile carriers (ubiquinone, cytochrome c) in the
inner mitochondrial membrane that transfer electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen,
pumping protons to create electrochemical gradient used by ATP synthase. Complex I (NADH
dehydrogenase): NADH -> ubiquinone.

\medterm{Electrophysiology Study (EPS)}-- Invasive cardiac catheterization with multielectrode catheters placed in the heart to
map the cardiac conduction system. Measures: sinus node function (SNRT, SACT), AV node
function (AH interval, HV interval), and inducibility of arrhythmias (programmed
electrical stimulation).

\medterm{Electrosurgical Unit (ESU)}-- A generator that delivers high-frequency electrical energy through an active electrode
to cut tissue or achieve hemostasis. Monopolar and bipolar configurations differ in
current pathway and safety considerations; burns, unintended tissue injury, and
interference with implanted devices are possible complications.

\medterm{Elephantiasis}-- Massive enlargement of a body part, typically a limb or the scrotum, caused by chronic
lymphedema from obstruction of lymphatic vessels. The most common cause worldwide is
lymphatic filariasis, a mosquito-borne parasitic infection by Wuchereria bancrofti,
Brugia malayi, or Brugia timori.

\medterm{Elevation}-- Superior movement of a body part, such as raising the mandible or shoulder girdle.

\medterm{Elimination Half-Life}-- Time for plasma concentration to decrease by 50\1. Determines dosing interval,
accumulation, withdrawal from exposure. Affected by renal/hepatic function, age, drug
interactions.

\medterm{Embolectomy}-- Surgical removal of embolus from blood vessel. Indications: acute arterial occlusion,
pulmonary embolism. Techniques: Fogarty catheter, open surgical extraction. Often
combined with thrombolysis.

\medterm{Embolectomy (Vascular)}-- See Embolectomy Catheter.

\medterm{Embolism}-- An embolism is the occlusion of a blood vessel by a detached mass that has traveled
through the bloodstream from its site of origin. The most common form is
thromboembolism, where a fragment of a thrombus dislodges and travels to a distant site
through the venous or arterial circulation.

\medterm{Embolization}-- Deliberate occlusion of a blood vessel or vascular malformation using materials such as
coils, particles, glue, or plugs. It is used to control hemorrhage, treat aneurysms or
malformations, reduce tumor vascularity, or induce organ infarction in selected
therapies.

\medterm{Embolization (Vascular)}-- See Embolization Device.

\medterm{Embolization Therapy}-- Image-guided occlusion of vessels. Used for hemorrhage control, tumor devascularization,
aneurysm treatment, varices.

\medterm{Embryo}-- The developing human organism from fertilization (conception) through the eighth week of
gestation (10th week by last menstrual period).

\medterm{Emergence Agitation}-- A state of confusion, restlessness, and inconsolable crying during emergence from
anesthesia, occurring in 10 to 80 percent of pediatric patients and 5 to 10 percent of
adults. Risk factors include sevoflurane induction, short procedures, parental
separation, and preexisting behavioral disorders.

\medterm{Emergency Contraception}-- Postcoital contraception to prevent pregnancy after unprotected intercourse or
contraceptive failure. Levonorgestrel (Plan B): effective within 72 hours, available
over the counter. Ulipristal acetate (Ella): effective within 120 hours, prescription
only. Copper IUD: most effective emergency contraception, placed within 5 days.

\medterm{Emergency Department}-- Hospital department providing immediate medical care for acute illnesses and injuries.
Staffed by emergency physicians and supported by specialists. Key principles: triage
(prioritize by severity), stabilization, diagnosis, treatment, disposition.

\medterm{Emergency Procedures}-- Spill response, contamination control, radiation exposure.

\medterm{Emerging Infectious Disease}-- New or reemerging infectious diseases. Examples: COVID-19, Zika, Ebola, MERS.

\medterm{Emesis (Vomiting)}-- The forceful expulsion of gastric contents through the mouth, a complex reflex
coordinated by the vomiting center in the medulla oblongata.

\medterm{Emetics}-- Reflex expulsion of gastric contents. See vomiting.

\medterm{Emission}-- The discharge or release of a substance, usually a fluid. In male reproduction, emission
refers to the movement of semen into the posterior urethra in preparation for
ejaculation.

\medterm{Emotional Eating}-- Eating in response to emotions rather than hunger. Stress, boredom, sadness triggers.
Cognitive behavioral therapy may help.

\medterm{Emphysema}-- A form of COPD characterized by permanent, abnormal enlargement of airspaces distal to
the terminal bronchioles, accompanied by destruction of their walls. Centriacinar
emphysema affects the respiratory bronchioles and is associated with smoking.

\medterm{Emphysematous aortitis due to Clostridium septicum}-- A rare, life-threatening infection of the aortic wall by Clostridium septicum,
characterized by gas formation within the aortic wall visible on imaging. It typically
occurs in immunocompromised patients and may present with sepsis, back pain, or
aortoenteric fistula.

\medterm{Emphysematous cholecystitis}-- A severe, gas-forming infection of the gallbladder wall caused by anaerobic organisms,
most commonly Clostridium species and Escherichia coli, occurring predominantly in
diabetic men. Gas within the gallbladder wall or lumen is visible on imaging. It is a
surgical emergency requiring urgent cholecystectomy and broad-spectrum antibiotics.

\medterm{Emphysematous hepatitis}-- A rare, life-threatening gas-forming infection of the liver parenchyma, most commonly
caused by Clostridium species or mixed enteric organisms in immunocompromised or
diabetic patients. Imaging reveals gas within the liver parenchyma.

\medterm{Emphysematous prostatitis}-- A rare, severe gas-forming infection of the prostate gland, most commonly caused by
Escherichia coli in diabetic or immunocompromised patients. It presents with fever,
perineal pain, urinary retention, and sepsis. Imaging shows gas within the prostatic
parenchyma.

\medterm{Emphysematous Pyelonephritis}-- Severe necrotizing infection of renal parenchyma with gas formation. Almost exclusively
in diabetic patients. Presents with high fever, flank pain, and sepsis. CT shows gas
within renal parenchyma. A urological emergency requiring aggressive antibiotics and
usually nephrectomy. Mortality is high even with treatment.

\medterm{Empty Calories}-- Foods providing energy but few nutrients. Sugary drinks, candies, alcohol. Contribute to
obesity, nutrient deficiency.

\medterm{Empty Sella Syndrome}-- Herniation of subarachnoid space into the sella turcica, compressing the pituitary gland

\medterm{Empyema}-- Pus in the pleural space. CT shows peripheral enhancement (split pleura sign). Treatment

long-term peritoneal dialysis but also seen after abdominal surgery, radiation, and

\medterm{Endocardial Cushion Defect}-- A congenital heart defect caused by abnormal development of the atrioventricular

\medterm{Endocarditis}-- Infection of the endocardial surface of the heart, most commonly involving the valves.

\medterm{Endocardium}-- The innermost layer of the heart wall, a thin membrane of endothelial cells and

\medterm{Endocrine Disrupting Chemicals}-- Environmental chemicals that interfere with endocrine function including bisphenol A,

\medterm{Endocrinology}-- The branch of medicine concerned with the study, diagnosis, and treatment of disorders

\medterm{Endoderm}-- The innermost germ layer in an embryo, which develops into the linings of the digestive

\medterm{Endometriosis}-- A chronic, estrogen-dependent gynecologic disorder characterized by the presence of

implanted on the pelvic peritoneum, ovaries, uterosacral ligaments, and rectovaginal

\medterm{Endometritis}-- Infection of the endometrium, the most common cause of puerperal fever after cesarean

\medterm{Endometrium}-- The lining of the uterus.

\medterm{Endophthalmitis}-- A severe, sight-threatening intraocular inflammation involving the vitreous cavity,

\medterm{Endorphin}-- Endogenous opioid peptides (beta-endorphin, enkephalins, dynorphins) produced by the

\medterm{Erectile Disorder}-- A sexual dysfunction characterized by persistent or recurrent difficulty achieving or

\medterm{Erysipelas}-- A superficial form of cellulitis involving the upper dermis and superficial lymphatics,

\medterm{Erythema Nodosum}-- The most common form of panniculitis, characterized by painful, erythematous, tender

\medterm{Erythrasma}-- A superficial infection caused by Corynebacterium minutissimum in moist intertriginous

\medterm{Erythrocyte}-- A mature red blood cell specialized to transport oxygen and carbon dioxide through the

\medterm{Erythrocytosis}-- An increased red-cell mass, usually reflected by elevated haemoglobin and haematocrit.

\medterm{Erythrodermic psoriasis}-- A severe, life-threatening variant of psoriasis characterised by widespread erythema and

\medterm{Erythromelalgia}-- Episodic burning pain, redness, and warmth of the extremities triggered by warmth or

\medterm{Esophageal Atresia}-- A congenital anomaly where the esophagus ends in a blind pouch, often with a

\medterm{Ewing Sarcoma}-- A highly malignant small round blue cell tumor of bone and soft tissue, representing the
second most common primary bone malignancy in children and adolescents (peak age 10-20
years), with a strong predilection for males and Caucasian populations.

\medterm{Exacerbation of COPD}-- An acute worsening of respiratory symptoms beyond normal day-to-day variations,
requiring change in medication. It is commonly triggered by respiratory infections
(viral, bacterial), air pollution, or non-compliance. Treatment includes increased
bronchodilators, systemic corticosteroids, antibiotics if indicated, and oxygen therapy.

\medterm{Examination}-- Systematic assessment of a patient's health status. Components: inspection, palpation,
percussion, auscultation (IPPA). General survey: appearance, posture, gait, vital signs.
Focused examination based on chief complaint.

\medterm{Exanthema}-- A widespread skin eruption, usually macular or papular, associated with an acute
systemic illness. Viral infections are common causes, but drug reactions, bacterial
disease, immune disorders, and other systemic conditions may also produce exanthema;
fever, mucosal involvement, blistering, or skin pain may indicate a severe process.

\medterm{Exanthematous pustulosis}-- See acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis. A severe drug-induced eruption with
acute onset of hundreds of small, non-follicular, sterile pustules on an erythematous
base, often accompanied by fever and neutrophilia. It typically resolves within two
weeks after withdrawal of the offending medication.

\medterm{Exchange Transfusion}-- Emergent procedure to rapidly lower severe hyperbilirubinemia by removing small volumes
of the infant's blood and replacing with compatible donor blood. Used when phototherapy
fails or bilirubin approaches toxic levels (greater than 25 mg/dL in term infants or
above exchange threshold on Bhutani curve).

\medterm{Excision}-- The surgical removal of a piece of tissue, an organ, or a lesion. Excisional biopsy
removes the entire lesion with a margin of normal tissue, used for definitive diagnosis
and treatment of small tumors (suspected melanoma, breast lump, lymph node).

\medterm{Excisional Biopsy}-- Surgical removal of entire lesion. Diagnosis and treatment.

\medterm{Excitation-Contraction Coupling}-- The sequence of events linking an action potential to muscle contraction. In skeletal
muscle: the action potential travels down the T-tubule, triggering calcium release from
the sarcoplasmic reticulum via ryanodine receptors (calcium-induced calcium release
mediated by voltage-gated dihydropyridine receptors).

\medterm{Excoriation Disorder}-- Recurrent skin picking resulting in skin lesions. The individual has made repeated
attempts to decrease or stop the skin picking. The skin picking causes clinically
significant distress or impairment. It is not attributable to a medical condition or
substance. Common sites include face, arms, and hands.

\medterm{Excretion}-- Removal of substance from body. Routes: renal (urine), hepatic (bile), pulmonary (gas),
sweat, breast milk. Important for toxicity and drug clearance.

\medterm{Exercise Intolerance}-- Reduced ability to perform physical activity at the expected level because of exertional
fatigue, breathlessness, chest discomfort, leg pain, or other symptoms. Causes include
cardiac, pulmonary, vascular, metabolic, neuromuscular, haematological, and
deconditioning states.

\medterm{Exercise Stress Test}-- A noninvasive cardiac diagnostic test that evaluates the cardiovascular response to
graded physical exertion, primarily used to diagnose coronary artery disease, assess
functional capacity, and guide prognosis.

\medterm{Exercise-Induced Arrhythmias}-- Catecholamine-sensitive ventricular tachycardia and right ventricular outflow tract VT
can be triggered by exercise. HCM-related sudden death risk increases during vigorous
exercise. Exercise-induced myocardial ischemia may trigger ventricular arrhythmias in
CAD.

\medterm{Exercise-Induced Asthma}-- Bronchoconstriction occurring during or shortly after physical exertion, causing cough,
wheezing, chest tightness, or shortness of breath. It is associated with airway
hyperresponsiveness and may occur in people with or without persistent asthma.

\medterm{Exercise-Induced Bronchoconstriction}-- Airway narrowing during or after exercise, presenting with cough, wheeze, and dyspnea.
It occurs in up to 90\1 of asthmatics and is triggered by heat and water loss from the
airways. Prevention includes pre-exercise SABA.

\medterm{Exhaled Nitric Oxide}-- A non-invasive measure of airway eosinophilic inflammation. FeNO > 25 ppb in adults
suggests eosinophilic airway inflammation and predicts response to inhaled
corticosteroids. It is useful for diagnosing eosinophilic asthma, monitoring treatment
response, and differentiating asthma from other causes of cough.

\medterm{Exhibitionistic Disorder}-- A paraphilic disorder involving recurrent, intense sexual arousal from exposing one's
genitals to an unsuspecting person. The individual has acted on these urges or they
cause marked distress or interpersonal difficulty. Duration \(\)6 months. Onset
typically before age 18. Treatment: CBT and SSRIs.

\medterm{Exhumation}-- The lawful disinterment and removal of a buried body or remains for re-examination,
re-testing, or identification. Indications: new evidence, suspicion of poisoning or foul
play, DNA testing, paternity/maternity testing, exhumation of a wrongly buried body, or
repatriation.

\medterm{Existential Distress}-- See psychological.

\medterm{Exit Wound}-- A skin defect produced where a projectile leaves the body, typically larger, irregular,
and stellate, with everted edges, no abrasion ring or soot. Size depends on deformation,
yaw, and fragmentation; small non-deformed bullets may produce shored exits, and an exit
may be absent if the projectile is retained.

\medterm{Exocytosis}-- Process by which intracellular vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane, releasing their
contents into the extracellular space and incorporating membrane proteins/lipids into
the cell membrane. The SNARE complex (v-SNARE on vesicles and t-SNARE on target
membrane) mediates vesicle docking and fusion.

\medterm{Exon}-- A segment of a DNA or RNA molecule containing information coding for a protein or
peptide sequence.

\medterm{Exophthalmos}-- Abnormal protrusion of the eyeball (globe) from the orbit, most commonly caused by
thyroid eye disease (Graves ophthalmopathy--an autoimmune condition associated with
hyperthyroidism). The proptosis results from inflammation, edema, and fibrosis of the
extraocular muscles and proliferation of orbital fat within the fixed bony orbit.

\medterm{Exotoxin}-- A heat-labile, protein toxin secreted or released by bacteria (mostly gram-positive;
also some gram-negative), often encoded on plasmids or phages, that acts at distant
sites.

\medterm{Exotoxins}-- Proteins secreted by bacteria that have specific toxic effects on host cells. Exotoxins
are generally more potent than endotoxins and have specific mechanisms of action. A-B
toxins consist of an active (A) component and a binding (B) component. Examples include
diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, and botulinum toxin.

\medterm{Exotropia}-- A type of strabismus in which one eye deviates outward (toward the temple). Intermittent
exotropia: most common type, often noticed when tired or daydreaming, can be controlled
with fusion. Constant exotropia: requires treatment to prevent amblyopia. Congenital
exotropia: rare, requires early evaluation.

\medterm{Expectorants}-- Medications that promote the clearance of mucus from the respiratory tract by increasing
sputum volume and reducing its viscosity, facilitating productive cough. The most common
expectorant is guaifenesin (glyceryl guaiacolate), which increases respiratory tract
fluid secretion and reduces sputum adhesiveness.

\medterm{Expectoration}-- The act of coughing up and spitting out mucus, sputum, or phlegm from the respiratory
tract; sputum production.

\medterm{Expiratory Grunting}-- A forced exhalation against a partially closed glottis, creating an audible sound. It is
an end-expiratory maneuver that generates intrinsic PEEP, helping to maintain alveolar
recruitment and improve oxygenation. It is commonly seen in children with respiratory
distress.

\medterm{Explantation}-- Surgical removal of an implanted device or prosthesis. It may be required for infection,
malfunction, intolerance, migration, fracture, tissue injury, or completion of a
temporary therapy.

\medterm{Exploratory Laparotomy}-- Open surgical entry into the abdominal cavity to diagnose or treat suspected
intra-abdominal pathology, such as hemorrhage, perforation, ischemia, or obstruction,
when less invasive evaluation is inadequate or inappropriate.

\medterm{Exposure Route}-- Path by which substance enters body. Routes: inhalation, ingestion, dermal, injection,
ocular. Determines absorption rate and toxicity.

\medterm{Expressivity}-- Degree to which a genotype is expressed in phenotype. Variable expressivity: same
genotype produces different phenotypic severity in different individuals. Examples:
neurofibromatosis type 1, Marfan syndrome.

\medterm{Extension}-- Movement that increases the angle between two bones or straightens a joint.

\medterm{Extensively Drug-Resistant TB}-- TB resistant to isoniazid, rifampin, fluoroquinolones, and at least one injectable
agent. Very limited treatment options and poor outcomes.

\medterm{Extensor Digitorum}-- Posterior forearm muscle from lateral epicondyle via the common extensor tendon,
dividing into 4 tendons inserting on extensor expansions of digits 2-5. Innervated by
posterior interosseous nerve (C7-C8). Extends MCP joints, assists PIP/DIP extension.
Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow) is an overuse injury of its common origin.

\medterm{External Auditory Canal}-- Tube from the concha to the tympanic membrane, approximately 2.5 cm long in adults.
Lateral third is cartilaginous; medial two-thirds are bony (temporal bone). Skin-lined
with ceruminous glands (producing cerumen/earwax) and hair follicles. Cerumen has
antimicrobial and insect-repellent properties.

\medterm{External Beam Radiation Therapy}-- Radiation from outside body. 3D conformal, IMRT, stereotactic body radiation therapy
(SBRT).

\medterm{External Cephalic Version (ECV)}-- A procedure to manually rotate a breech or transverse fetus to a cephalic (head-down)
presentation from outside the abdomen. Performed after 36-37 weeks (term, with adequate
amniotic fluid). Success rate: 30-80\1 (higher with prior vaginal delivery, transverse
lie, adequate fluid, and non-anterior placenta).

\medterm{External Fixation}-- Stabilization of a fracture or limb using pins or wires attached to an external frame.
It may provide temporary damage-control stabilization or definitive treatment,
particularly when soft-tissue injury or contamination limits internal fixation.

\medterm{External Iliac Artery}-- Larger common iliac terminal branch, continuing as the femoral artery below the inguinal
ligament. Gives off inferior epigastric and deep circumflex iliac arteries. Related to
external iliac vein medially and ureter anteriorly. Commonly used for cardiac
catheterization and endovascular access.

\medterm{External Jugular Vein}-- Formed by posterior auricular and retromandibular vein divisions, crossing the
sternocleidomastoid superficially to drain into the subclavian vein. Visible through the
skin; used for peripheral IV access when other sites are unavailable. Distended in right
heart failure and SVC obstruction. A useful physical examination landmark.

\medterm{External Oblique Muscle}-- Most superficial flat abdominal muscle from outer lower eight ribs to linea alba, pubic
tubercle, and iliac crest. Innervated by T7-T12. Contralateral trunk rotation and
flexion; compresses abdomen. Forms the inguinal ligament from its free inferior border.
Superficial inguinal ring is an opening in its aponeurosis.

\medterm{External Resorption}-- Loss of tooth structure from outside. Inflammatory, replacement, surface, cervical
resorption. Treatment depends on type and extent.

\medterm{Extracellular Fluid (ECF)}-- The fluid outside cells, including plasma and interstitial fluid. Sodium, chloride, and
bicarbonate are its principal effective solutes; ECF volume and composition are
regulated by the kidneys, cardiovascular system, and endocrine mechanisms.

\medterm{Extracellular Matrix}-- A complex network of proteins and carbohydrates secreted by cells that provides
structural and chemical support to surrounding cells.

\medterm{Extracellular pathogens}-- Microorganisms that survive and replicate outside host cells. Extracellular bacteria
include Streptococcus, Staphylococcus, and Escherichia coli. These pathogens are
typically killed by antibodies and complement. They produce toxins and enzymes to damage
host tissues.

\medterm{Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation}-- A temporary mechanical circulatory support system that provides cardiac and/or
respiratory support. VA-ECMO (veno-arterial) provides both cardiac and respiratory
support; VV-ECMO (veno-venous) provides respiratory support only.

\medterm{Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL)}-- Non-invasive treatment method for urinary calculi that uses focused acoustic shock waves
generated outside the body to fragment kidney or ureteral stones into smaller
gravel-like pieces (dust/sand) that pass spontaneously in urine.

\medterm{Extranodal natural killer T-cell lymphoma}-- An aggressive lymphoma arising from natural killer cells, most commonly involving the
nasal cavity and upper aerodigestive tract. It is strongly associated with Epstein-Barr
virus infection.

\medterm{Extraocular Muscle Function Testing}-- Assessment of the six extraocular muscles innervated by CN III (medial rectus:
adduction; superior rectus: elevation; inferior rectus: depression; inferior oblique:
extorsion), CN IV (superior oblique: intorsion and depression), and CN VI (lateral
rectus: abduction).

\medterm{Extraocular Muscles}-- Six muscles controlling eye movement: four rectus (superior, inferior, medial, lateral)
and two oblique (superior, inferior).

\medterm{Extrapulmonary manifestation of tuberculosis}-- See Tuberculosis (TB).

\medterm{Extrapyramidal Symptoms}-- Drug-induced movement disorders caused by D2 receptor blockade in the nigrostriatal
pathway.

\medterm{Extrauterine Pregnancy}-- A pregnancy in which the fertilized ovum implants outside the uterine cavity, an
alternative term for ectopic pregnancy. By far the most common site is the fallopian
tube (ampulla 70\1, isthmus 12\1, fimbria 11\1, cornual/interstitial 2--4\1).

\medterm{Extravasation}-- Leakage of urine outside urinary tract. Causes: trauma, surgery, obstruction.
Pseudovesical extravasation (rectus sheath), extraperitoneal, intraperitoneal.
Treatment: catheter drainage, surgical repair.

\medterm{Exudate}-- An exudate is an inflammatory fluid with high protein content and abundant cells,
resulting from increased vascular permeability during inflammation. Exudates are
typically caused by bacterial infections, autoimmune diseases, viral infections, or
malignancy.

\medterm{Exudative Pleural Effusion}-- From local pleural disease. Causes include pneumonia, malignancy, TB, and PE. Elevated
protein, LDH, and cell count.

\medterm{Eye}-- The organ of vision, a spherical structure (approximately 24 mm diameter) in the bony
orbit. Three layers: fibrous (sclera, cornea), vascular/uveal (choroid, ciliary body,
iris), neural (retina). Anterior segment: cornea, anterior chamber (aqueous humor),
iris/pupil, lens.

\medterm{Eye Disorders}-- The sensory ability to perceive light and interpret visual information processed by the
eyes and brain, enabling recognition of shapes, colors, depth, and motion. See Vision.

\medterm{Eye Floaters}-- Spots, threads, or cobweb-like shapes that move across the visual field, usually caused
by opacities in the vitreous. Sudden new floaters, flashes, or a curtain-like shadow
require urgent evaluation for retinal tear or detachment. See also Floaters.

\medterm{Eye Health}-- The maintenance of optimal vision and prevention of ocular disease through regular eye
examinations, protective measures, and timely treatment. Common conditions affecting eye
health include refractive errors, cataracts, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, and
age-related macular degeneration.

\medterm{Eye Irritation}-- Chemical injury to eye. Immediate irrigation with water or saline for 15-20 minutes.
Alkali burns more dangerous than acid. Ophthalmology consultation.

\medterm{Eye Pain}-- Pain in or around the eye caused by surface irritation, infection, trauma, inflammation,
glaucoma, or orbital disease. Severe pain with vision loss, photophobia, trauma, or
nausea requires urgent assessment.

\medterm{Eye Redness}-- Red discoloration of the eye caused by dilation or inflammation of conjunctival or
deeper ocular blood vessels. Causes range from irritation and conjunctivitis to
keratitis, uveitis, and acute glaucoma.

\medterm{Eye Trauma}-- Ocular injuries ranging from corneal abrasions to globe rupture. Chemical burns:
irrigate immediately with saline or water for 30 minutes. Penetrating injuries: shield
eye, no pressure, emergent ophthalmology consultation. Foreign bodies: remove
superficial items; embedded objects require surgical removal.

\medterm{Ezetimibe}-- A cholesterol absorption inhibitor that blocks intestinal absorption of dietary and
biliary cholesterol at the Niemann-Pick C1-like 1 (NPC1L1) transporter in the small
intestine.

\medterm{Eosinophilic Bronchitis}-- A cause of chronic cough in which eosinophilic inflammation affects the airways without the
variable airflow obstruction typical of asthma. Cough-variant asthma and reflux may mimic it;
diagnosis relies on clinical assessment and airway inflammation testing.

\medterm{Eosinophilic Fasciitis}-- A rare inflammatory disorder causing painful swelling, induration, and progressive tightening of
the fascia and skin, usually in the limbs. Blood eosinophilia may occur. Diagnosis often
requires imaging or deep biopsy to distinguish it from scleroderma and other causes.

\medterm{Eggshell Nail}-- A nail that becomes unusually thin, soft, flexible, and sometimes whitish or concave. It may be associated
with nutritional deficiency, chronic illness, aging, or repeated trauma; persistent nail changes
should be evaluated with the skin and other symptoms in mind.

\medterm{Endometrial Hyperplasia}-- Abnormal overgrowth of the lining of the uterus, often related to prolonged estrogen exposure without enough
progesterone. It may cause heavy, irregular, or postmenopausal bleeding; atypical forms can precede endometrial
cancer and require gynecologic evaluation, often including endometrial sampling.
