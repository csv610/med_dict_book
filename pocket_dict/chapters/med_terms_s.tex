disorder, hematologic disorder, immunologic disorder. Diagnosis: ANA positive plus
clinical/immunologic criteria.

\medterm{Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)} Systemic lupus erythematosus is an autoimmune disease that can inflame skin, joints, kidneys, blood cells, nervous system, heart, or other organs.
\medterm{Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)} A chronic autoimmune disease that can affect skin, joints, kidneys, blood, nervous system, heart, lungs, and other organs.
\medterm{Systemic Mastocytosis} A disorder in which excessive mast cells accumulate in one or more organs and release inflammatory chemicals.

\medterm{Systemic Scleroderma} Systemic sclerosis, an autoimmune connective-tissue disease causing vascular dysfunction and fibrosis of skin and internal organs, commonly affecting the esophagus, lungs, kidneys, and heart.

\medterm{Systemic Sclerosis} Systemic sclerosis, also known as scleroderma, is a chronic autoimmune disease
characterized by progressive fibrosis of the skin and internal organs, vasculopathy, and
immune dysfunction.

\synonyms
scleroderma, is a chronic autoimmune disease.

\medterm{Systemic Therapy} Treatment that reaches the whole body, including chemotherapy, endocrine therapy,
targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, as opposed to local therapies such as surgery and
radiation.

\medterm{Systemic Vascular Resistance} The resistance to blood flow offered by the systemic circulation, determined mainly by
arteriolar tone. It is calculated from the pressure gradient across the systemic
circulation and cardiac output and is a major determinant of arterial pressure.

\medterm{Systemic Venous} Systemic venous refers to veins returning deoxygenated blood from the body to the right side of the heart, excluding the pulmonary circulation.

\medterm{Systole} The phase of the cardiac cycle during which a chamber contracts and ejects blood.
\medterm{Systolic} Systolic means relating to contraction of the heart, especially the pressure in arteries when the ventricles eject blood.

\medterm{Systolic Murmurs} Murmurs occurring between S1 and S2. Ejection systolic murmurs (crescendo-decrescendo)
are caused by flow across semilunar valves (aortic stenosis, pulmonary stenosis) or
increased flow across normal valves.

\medterm{Systolic Pressure} The maximum arterial pressure during ventricular contraction, recorded as the upper number of a blood-pressure reading.

\medterm{Sz} Sz is an informal abbreviation for seizure; clinical documentation should identify the seizure type and relevant features clearly.

\medterm{SéZary Syndrome} The leukemic variant of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, characterized by erythroderma,
lymphadenopathy, pruritus, and circulating atypical lymphocytes (Sézary cells).

\medterm{Serous Borderline Ovarian Tumor} An ovarian serous epithelial tumor with hierarchical papillae and cellular atypia but no destructive stromal invasion; extraovarian implants may occur.

\medterm{Serous Fluid Cytology} Cytologic examination of pleural, pericardial, or peritoneal fluid to assess for malignant cells, infection, and reactive inflammation. Cell-block preparation and ancillary tests may improve diagnostic accuracy.
\medterm{Serous Tubal Intraepithelial Carcinoma (STIC)} A noninvasive high-grade serous lesion of the fallopian-tube fimbria, considered a precursor or early manifestation of many tubo-ovarian high-grade serous carcinomas.

\medterm{Small Cell Carcinoma Of The Ovary, Hypercalcemic Type} A rare, highly aggressive ovarian tumor in young patients, often associated with alterations in SMARCA4 and sometimes presenting with hypercalcemia.

\medterm{Struma Ovarii} A specialized mature ovarian teratoma composed predominantly of thyroid tissue; it may be hormonally active or develop malignant thyroid-type transformation.
