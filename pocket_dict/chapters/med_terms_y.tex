\medterm{YAG Capsulotomy} A laser procedure that makes an opening in the cloudy posterior capsule behind an artificial lens after cataract surgery. It treats posterior capsule opacification, which can blur vision or cause glare.

\medterm{YAG Laser Surgery} An eye procedure using a neodymium-doped yttrium-aluminum-garnet (Nd:YAG) laser to cut or alter tissue. Uses include opening a cloudy lens capsule after cataract surgery and making a peripheral opening in the iris for some forms of angle-closure glaucoma.

\medterm{Yawn} An involuntary behavior consisting of a deep inhalation followed by a slower exhalation, often with stretching of the jaw and facial muscles. Yawning is common with fatigue but excessive or unusual yawning can occur with some medications or neurologic conditions.

\medterm{Yaws} A chronic skin infection caused by \textit{Treponema pallidum} subspecies \textit{pertenue} and spread by direct contact with an infectious skin lesion. It can cause papillomas and ulcers, followed in some cases by damage to the skin, bone, or cartilage.

\medterm{Yaws Papilloma} A raised, often painless, raspberry-like skin lesion of early yaws, caused by \textit{Treponema pallidum} subspecies \textit{pertenue}. It may become ulcerated and is also called a mother yaw or frambesioma.

\medterm{Y-Box-Binding Protein 1} A protein that binds DNA and RNA and helps regulate gene activity, RNA processing, and protein production.

\medterm{Y Chromosome} One of the two human sex chromosomes. It carries genes involved in testis development and other functions. A biological father passes it to a child who inherits his Y chromosome; sex-chromosome patterns and body development vary.

\medterm{Y-Chromosome Microdeletion} A small missing section of the Y chromosome, often in an azoospermia-factor (AZF) region. It can impair sperm production, causing a very low sperm count or no sperm in the ejaculate.

\medterm{Yeast} A type of fungus made of single cells. Some yeasts normally live on or in the body, while others can cause infections of the mouth, skin, vagina, lungs, blood, or other organs.

\medterm{Yeast Infection} An infection caused by yeast, most often \textit{Candida} species. It may affect the mouth, skin, genital tract, or bloodstream; symptoms depend on the site. Yeast may also be present without causing disease, so detection alone does not establish infection.

\medterm{Yeast Infection In Women And Men} A lay term for symptomatic Candida infection of genital tissues. Vulvovaginal candidiasis can cause vulvar itching, soreness, and vaginal discharge; candidal balanitis causes redness, itching, or irritation of the glans penis. These conditions are not usually acquired through sexual intercourse and are not generally classified as sexually transmitted infections.

\medterm{Yeast Infections} See \medref{Yeast Infection}.
\medterm{Yeast Present} A laboratory report showing yeast in a specimen. This may mean harmless colonization, contamination, or infection; the meaning depends on the symptoms, specimen, and type of yeast.

\medterm{Yeast Vulvitis} Inflammation of the vulva associated with a Candida yeast infection. It may cause itching, burning, redness, swelling, and soreness; other conditions can cause similar symptoms.

\medterm{Yellow Cartilage} A flexible type of cartilage containing many elastic fibers. It is found in the outer ear, the epiglottis, parts of the voice box, and the tube connecting the middle ear to the throat.

\synonyms

\medterm{Yellow Fever} A serious viral hemorrhagic disease caused by the yellow fever virus, transmitted to humans through the bites of infected mosquitoes in tropical areas of Africa and South America. While many people experience mild illness or recover after initial symptoms of fever, chills, headache, back pain, and nausea, some progress to a severe toxic phase. This phase causes liver damage that produces jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes, giving the disease its name), bleeding from the mouth, nose, or stomach, and kidney failure.

\synonyms
Flavivirus Hemorrhagic Fever; Yellow Jack

\medterm{Yellow Fever Vaccine} A live, weakened-virus vaccine that provides long-lasting protection after one dose for most people. Rare serious adverse events include vaccine-associated neurotropic disease (YEL-AND) and viscerotropic disease (YEL-AVD), in which the vaccine virus affects internal organs.

\synonyms
17D Vaccine

\medterm{Yellow Fever Virus} A mosquito-borne flavivirus that causes yellow fever, which ranges from a mild febrile illness to severe disease with jaundice, bleeding, shock, or organ failure.

\medterm{Yellowish Palms/Soles} Alternate name; see \medref{Carotenemia}.

\medterm{Yellow Ligament} A series of elastic bands connecting the back parts of neighboring bones in the spine. They help support the spine and surround the spinal canal.

\synonyms
Ligamentum Flavum; Flavum Ligament

\medterm{Yellow Marrow} Bone marrow containing mostly fat instead of blood-forming cells. It is common in the hollow centers of adult long bones and can change back toward blood-forming marrow when the body needs more blood cells.

\synonyms
Fatty Bone Marrow; Medulla Ossium Flava

\medterm{Yellow Nail Syndrome} A rare multisystem disorder classically defined by a triad of symptoms: slow-growing, thickened, yellow-to-green nails that lack cuticles and may separate from the nail bed; chronic respiratory problems, such as persistent cough, chronic bronchitis, or fluid accumulation around the lungs (pleural effusions); and swelling (lymphedema), typically affecting the legs, caused by impaired lymphatic fluid drainage.

\synonyms

\medterm{Yellow-Nail Syndrome} Alternate spelling; see \\textit{Yellow Nail Syndrome}.

\medterm{Yellow Nail Syndrome Triad} The three main features of yellow nail syndrome: slow-growing yellow or thickened nails, swelling from lymph-fluid buildup, and long-term breathing or lung problems.

\synonyms
YNS Triad; Lymphedema-Pleural Effusion-Yellow Nails

\medterm{Yellow Spot} The macula, a small yellowish area near the center of the retina. It contains the fovea, which provides the sharp central vision used for fine detail.

\synonyms
Macula Lutea; Anatomical Macula

\medterm{Yellow Tongue} Yellow discoloration or coating of the tongue that may result from oral hygiene, smoking, dry mouth, diet, medicines, or an underlying illness.

\medterm{Yergason Test} A shoulder examination performed with the elbow bent and the forearm turned palm-down. The person turns the palm up and rotates the arm outward against resistance; pain over the biceps tendon in the front of the shoulder may suggest biceps tendon disease, but the test alone cannot confirm a diagnosis.

\medterm{Yersinia} A group of bacteria that can cause intestinal illness, swollen lymph nodes in the abdomen, or plague. Different species spread through contaminated food or water, infected animals, or fleas.

\medterm{Yersinia Enterocolitica} A bacterium that causes an intestinal infection known as yersiniosis, most commonly contracted by consuming undercooked pork products, unpasteurized milk, or contaminated water. The bacteria infect the lining of the small intestine and surrounding lymph nodes, causing fever, watery or bloody diarrhea, and sharp abdominal pain that often localizes to the lower right side of the belly, frequently mimicking the symptoms of acute appendicitis.

\synonyms
Yersinia Enteritis; Yersiniosis Pathogen

\medterm{Yersinia Enterocolitica Pseudoappendicitis} A Yersinia infection that causes pain in the lower right abdomen and swollen lymph nodes near the intestine, making it resemble appendicitis. It can also cause fever and diarrhea, often after contaminated food such as undercooked pork.

\synonyms
Yersinia Mesenteric Adenitis; Pseudoappendiceal Syndrome

\medterm{Yersinia Infection} An intestinal infection caused mainly by \textit{Yersinia enterocolitica} or \textit{Y. pseudotuberculosis}. It can cause diarrhea, abdominal pain, fever, or vomiting; swollen lymph nodes in the abdomen may cause pain that resembles appendicitis. It is distinct from plague, which is caused by \textit{Y. pestis}.

\medterm{Yersinia Outer Proteins} Effector proteins made by disease-causing \textit{Yersinia} bacteria and delivered into host cells by a type III secretion system. They disrupt cell signaling and immune defenses, helping the bacteria evade destruction.

\synonyms
Yops; Yersinia Effector Proteins; Yop Virulence Factors

\medterm{Yersinia Pestis} A species of bacterium that causes plague, a severe and historically devastating zoonotic infectious disease. The bacteria naturally circulate among wild rodents and are most commonly transmitted to humans through the bites of infected rodent fleas, though they can also spread through contact with infected animal tissues or via airborne droplets. Depending on how the infection enters the body, it can cause swollen, painful lymph nodes (bubonic plague), spread through the bloodstream (septicemic plague), or infect the lungs (pneumonic plague), causing severe fever and rapid illness.

\synonyms
Plague Bacillus; Pasteurella Pestis

\medterm{Yersinia Pestis Pneumonic Plague} Plague involving the lungs, caused by \textit{Yersinia pestis}. It may follow inhalation of infectious respiratory droplets or spread from plague elsewhere in the body. It can cause fever, cough, chest pain, breathing difficulty, and rapidly worsening lung failure.

\synonyms
Pneumonic Plague

\medterm{Yersinia Pseudotuberculosis} A bacterium usually acquired from contaminated food or water. It can cause fever, diarrhea, and pain from swollen lymph nodes in the abdomen, sometimes resembling appendicitis.

\medterm{Yersinia Reactive Arthritis} Joint inflammation that develops after a Yersinia intestinal infection, usually one to four weeks later. It often affects a few joints, especially in the legs, without bacteria being present inside the joints.

\medterm{Yersiniosis} See \medref{Yersinia Infection}.

\medterm{Yew Poisoning} Poisoning after ingesting yew leaves, seeds, or other toxic parts of the plant. Taxine alkaloids can cause vomiting, dizziness, seizures, dangerous heart rhythms, collapse, and death. The fleshy red covering of the seed is nontoxic, but the seed inside is poisonous, especially if chewed.

\medterm{Y-Linked Gene} A gene located on the Y chromosome outside its pseudoautosomal regions. \synonyms

\medterm{Y-Linked Inheritance} Inheritance of a condition caused by a gene on the Y chromosome. A biological father passes the gene to children who inherit his Y chromosome, but not to children who inherit his X chromosome.

\medterm{Y-Maze Test} A test used mainly in laboratory animals to study movement, exploration, and spatial working memory. The animal moves through three arms of a Y-shaped maze, and the pattern of movement is recorded.

\synonyms

\medterm{Yolk Sac} A temporary structure that supports an early embryo before the placenta is fully working. It helps form the first blood cells and part of the early intestine and can be seen on early pregnancy ultrasound.

\medterm{Yolk Sac Tumor} A rare, fast-growing malignant germ cell tumor that originates from primitive reproductive cells, most commonly developing in the testes of young boys or the ovaries of young girls, though it can occasionally arise in the tailbone, chest, or brain. The tumor cells resemble the structures of an early embryonic yolk sac and typically produce and secrete elevated amounts of alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) into the bloodstream, which serves as a valuable marker for diagnosis and monitoring.

\medterm{Yolk Sac Tumor Pathology} A malignant germ-cell tumor with variable reticular, microcystic, papillary, glandular, or solid patterns. Schiller-Duval bodies, hyaline globules, and alpha-fetoprotein expression may support the diagnosis.

\medterm{Yolk Sac Tumor Schiller-Duval Bodies} A characteristic microscope finding in yolk sac tumors. It looks somewhat like a tiny kidney filter, with tumor cells arranged around a small blood vessel. The finding helps confirm the diagnosis when considered with other test results.

\synonyms
