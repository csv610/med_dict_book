
\medterm{Y Chromosome} The sex chromosome that typically carries the SRY gene and other genes involved in
testicular development and male sex differentiation. It is present in most individuals
with a typical 46,XY karyotype and is transmitted through the paternal line.

\medterm{Y-Linked Inheritance} Y-linked inheritance involves a gene on the Y chromosome and typically passes from an affected father to his sons, not daughters.

\medterm{YAG Capsulotomy} A laser procedure that creates an opening in an opaque posterior lens capsule after cataract
surgery. It can improve vision when posterior capsule opacification is causing visual symptoms.

\medterm{YAG Laser Capsulotomy} A laser procedure that creates an opening in a cloudy posterior lens capsule after cataract surgery, restoring the passage of light and improving vision.

\medterm{YAG Laser Surgery} YAG laser surgery uses a yttrium-aluminum-garnet laser to cut, vaporize, or treat tissue, including posterior-capsule clouding after cataract surgery.

\medterm{Yarrowia} A genus of yeast that can rarely cause opportunistic infection, particularly in people who are immunocompromised or have indwelling medical devices. Identification and treatment depend on species and susceptibility testing.

\medterm{Yarrowia Lipolytica} A yeast species in the genus Yarrowia that is primarily environmental but has rarely been reported as an opportunistic human pathogen, including bloodstream or device-associated infection.

\medterm{Yawn} An involuntary behavior consisting of a deep inhalation followed by a slower exhalation, often with stretching of the jaw and facial muscles. Yawning is common with fatigue but excessive or unusual yawning can occur with some medications or neurologic conditions.

\medterm{Yawning} Repeated involuntary opening of the mouth with a deep inhalation and subsequent exhalation. It is usually associated with sleepiness or boredom; persistent excessive yawning may warrant assessment for medication effects or an underlying disorder.

\medterm{Yawning - Excessive} Excessive yawning is unusually frequent or difficult-to-control yawning. It may accompany sleep deprivation, excessive daytime sleepiness, medication effects, vasovagal reactions, or less commonly neurologic or cardiopulmonary disease; persistent unexplained episodes merit clinical evaluation.

\medterm{Yaws} Yaws is a chronic infection caused by Treponema pallidum subsp. pertenue and spread mainly through close skin contact. It can cause an initial papilloma or ulcer and later destructive lesions of skin, bone, and cartilage; antibiotics can cure the infection.

\medterm{Yaws (Frambesia)} A chronic, non-venereal treponematosis caused by Treponema pallidum subspecies pertenue,
closely related to the syphilis spirochete (T. pallidum pallidum).

\synonyms
Frambesia

\medterm{Yd.} An abbreviation for yard, a unit of length equal to 0.9144 meter. In clinical documentation, the unit should be written clearly to avoid confusion with other abbreviations.

\medterm{Yeast} Yeasts are unicellular fungi that reproduce mainly by budding or, in some species, fission; medically important examples include Candida, Cryptococcus, and Malassezia, which may colonize humans or cause opportunistic infection.

\medterm{Yeast Infection} Vulvovaginal candidiasis: infection by Candida albicans (most common), causing vulvar
and vaginal itching, burning, thick white 'cottage cheese' discharge, dyspareunia, and
erythema; more common in pregnancy, diabetes, antibiotic use, and immunosuppression.
Diagnosis: clinical; wet mount (budding yeast/hyphae), KOH, or culture.

\medterm{Yeast Infection (Vaginal)} An overgrowth of Candida in the vagina causing itching, irritation, redness, and thick white discharge.

\synonyms
Candidiasis, Vaginal
Vaginal Candidiasis

\medterm{Yeast Infection In Women And Men} Genital yeast infection is usually caused by Candida and can cause itching, soreness, redness, and thick discharge in women or irritation and rash on the penis in men. Recurrent, severe, atypical, or treatment-resistant symptoms need confirmation and evaluation for contributing conditions.

\synonyms
Candidal Vulvovaginitis (Yeast Infection In Women And Men)

\medterm{Yeast Oral (Thrush)} Alternate index label for Thrush.

\medterm{Yeast Present} A laboratory report indicating that yeast forms were detected in a specimen. The finding may represent colonization, contamination, or infection and must be interpreted with symptoms, specimen type, and organism identification.

\medterm{Yellow Fever} Yellow fever is an acute mosquito-borne infection caused by yellow fever virus. Fever, headache, muscle aches, nausea, and vomiting may be followed by jaundice, bleeding, shock, and organ failure; vaccination is the main preventive measure.

\medterm{Yellow Fever Virus} A flavivirus transmitted mainly by infected mosquitoes that can cause yellow fever, ranging from a mild febrile illness to jaundice, bleeding, shock, and organ failure.

\medterm{Yellow Nail Syndrome} A rare disorder characterized by slow-growing yellow dystrophic nails, chronic lymphedema, and respiratory disease.
Nail changes may include thickening, excessive curvature, loss of the cuticle, and separation of the nail plate from the nail bed.
Respiratory manifestations can include recurrent sinusitis, chronic cough, pleural effusions, or bronchiectasis, although the complete triad is not always present.
Diagnosis is clinical and supported by characteristic nail findings and evaluation for lymphatic or pulmonary complications; management is directed at the affected systems.
\medterm{Yellow Tongue} Yellow discoloration or coating of the tongue that may result from oral hygiene, smoking, dry mouth, diet, medicines, or an underlying illness.

\medterm{Yellow-Nail Syndrome} A rare triad of yellow, thickened, slow-growing nails, lymphedema, and chronic
respiratory disease including bronchiectasis and pleural effusions. The nail changes
result from impaired lymphatic drainage affecting nail growth. It typically affects
middle-aged adults.

\medterm{Yergason Test} A shoulder examination maneuver used to assess the long head of the biceps tendon and transverse humeral ligament by resisted supination and external rotation.

\medterm{Yersinia} Yersinia is a genus of Gram-negative bacteria that includes Y. pestis, Y. enterocolitica, and Y. pseudotuberculosis; human disease ranges from enterocolitis and pseudoappendicitis to plague.

\medterm{Yersinia Aldovae} An environmental Yersinia species rarely isolated from humans; its significance depends on recovery from a compatible infection rather than colonization or contamination.

\medterm{Yersinia Bercovieri} An uncommon environmental or enteric Yersinia species with limited evidence of human pathogenicity; clinical significance depends on the specimen and symptoms.

\medterm{Yersinia Enterocolitica} A gram-negative coccobacillus of the family Enterobacteriaceae, a common cause of
enterocolitis and mesenteric lymphadenitis, especially in children and young adults in
temperate climates.

\medterm{Yersinia Frederiksenii} An environmental Yersinia species occasionally recovered from food, water, or clinical specimens; confirmed human infection is uncommon.

\medterm{Yersinia Infection} Infection caused by Yersinia bacteria, commonly producing diarrhea, abdominal pain, fever, or vomiting.
Some cases mimic appendicitis; severe illness, dehydration, bloody diarrhea, or persistent symptoms
requires medical assessment.

\medterm{Yersinia Intermedia} An environmental Yersinia species that is uncommon in human disease; interpretation of an isolate depends on the site, symptoms, and evidence of invasive infection.

\medterm{Yersinia Kristensenii} An uncommon Yersinia species found in environmental or animal sources and rarely associated with human infection.

\medterm{Yersinia Mollaretii} An uncommon Yersinia species mainly identified in environmental or animal samples; human pathogenicity is not well established.

\medterm{Yersinia Pestis} A gram-negative rod that causes plague, a severe and potentially fatal infection; see \textit{Plague}. The
organism is transmitted by flea bites or direct contact with infected animals. Bubonic
plague presents with painful lymphadenopathy (buboes). Pneumonic plague involves the
lungs and is transmitted by respiratory droplets.

\medterm{Yersinia Pseudotuberculosis} Yersinia pseudotuberculosis is a Gram-negative bacterium acquired mainly from contaminated food or water that causes enterocolitis, fever, and mesenteric lymphadenitis resembling appendicitis; systemic infection is uncommon.

\medterm{Yersinia Rohdei} An uncommon Yersinia species associated mainly with environmental or animal sources and rarely reported in human clinical specimens.

\medterm{Yersinia Ruckeri} A bacterium that causes enteric redmouth disease in fish; human infection is extremely rare and should be assessed in the context of a compatible exposure and specimen.

\medterm{Yersiniosis} Infection caused by Yersinia bacteria, most often Y. enterocolitica or Y. pseudotuberculosis, producing diarrhea, abdominal pain, fever, or pseudoappendicitis.

\medterm{Yew Poisoning} Yew poisoning follows ingestion of yew plant material, especially leaves or seeds containing taxine compounds. Toxicity can cause nausea, abdominal symptoms, dizziness, seizures, dangerous slowing or disruption of the heart rhythm, cardiovascular collapse, and death.

\medterm{Yips} Sudden involuntary movements or muscle spasms that interfere with a skilled task, often in athletes or musicians.

\medterm{Yoga} A mind-body practice combining physical postures, breathing techniques, and meditation or relaxation. It may support flexibility, balance, stress reduction, and symptom management, but exercises should be adapted to individual health conditions.

\medterm{Yoga For Health} Yoga for health combines physical postures, breathing exercises, and meditation or relaxation. It may improve flexibility, balance, strength, stress, and some chronic symptoms when adapted to the person; instructors should account for injuries, pregnancy, and medical limitations.

\medterm{Yohimbine} An alpha-2 adrenergic antagonist derived from yohimbe-related compounds and studied for erectile dysfunction; evidence is limited and adverse effects may include anxiety, high blood pressure, palpitations, and seizures.

\medterm{Yokenella} A genus of Gram-negative bacteria in the family Enterobacterales. Yokenella species are uncommon human pathogens but can cause opportunistic infections, including wound or bloodstream infections.

\medterm{Yokenella Regensburgei} A Gram-negative bacterium that rarely causes human infection, including wound, urinary, or bloodstream infection, usually in people with underlying illness or impaired immunity. Treatment is guided by culture and antimicrobial susceptibility results.

\medterm{Yolk Sac} An extraembryonic structure that supports the early embryo before the placenta is fully functional.
It contributes to early blood-cell formation and development of the primitive gut and is
assessed on early pregnancy ultrasound.

\medterm{Yolk Sac Tumor} A malignant ovarian germ-cell tumor that often occurs in children or young adults and produces alpha-fetoprotein; it requires prompt treatment.
\medterm{Yolk Stalk} The embryonic connection between the yolk sac and the developing midgut, also called the vitelline duct. Persistence of this structure can result in a Meckel diverticulum or other vitelline duct anomaly.

\medterm{Yolk-Sac Tumor} A malignant germ-cell tumor that commonly occurs in children and produces
alpha-fetoprotein.


\medterm{Your Baby And The Flu} Infants with influenza can develop fever, cough, poor feeding, vomiting, diarrhea, breathing difficulty, or unusual sleepiness. Care focuses on prompt medical advice, hydration, monitoring for respiratory distress, and age-appropriate antiviral treatment when indicated; influenza vaccination is preventive for eligible family members and children.

\medterm{Your Baby In  The Birth Canal} A baby in the birth canal is progressing through the maternal pelvis during labor. Clinicians assess fetal position, descent, heart rate, and the progress of cervical dilation to identify normal vaginal-birth progress or complications requiring intervention.

\medterm{Your Cancer Care Team} A cancer care team is the group of oncology professionals who coordinate diagnosis, treatment, symptom control, rehabilitation, psychosocial support, and follow-up. It may include medical, surgical, and radiation oncologists, nurses, pharmacists, social workers, dietitians, and palliative-care specialists.

\medterm{Your Cancer Diagnosis - Do You Need A Second Opinion?} A second opinion after a cancer diagnosis is an independent review of pathology, imaging, staging, and treatment options by another qualified clinician or cancer center. It can confirm the diagnosis, identify alternatives, and help the patient make an informed treatment decision without usually delaying urgent care.

\medterm{Your Cancer Survivorship Care Plan} A cancer survivorship care plan summarizes completed treatment, possible late effects, recommended surveillance, preventive care, rehabilitation, and communication between oncology and primary-care clinicians. It is updated as health needs change after active treatment.

\medterm{Your Child And The Flu} Children with influenza may have fever, cough, sore throat, fatigue, headache, muscle aches, vomiting, or diarrhea. Supportive care includes fluids and symptom monitoring; urgent assessment is needed for breathing difficulty, dehydration, confusion, severe weakness, or worsening after improvement.


\medterm{Your Diet After Gastric Bypass Surgery} Diet after gastric bypass surgery progresses from liquids to pureed, soft, and regular-textured foods in small portions as healing permits. Long-term care emphasizes protein, fluids separated from meals, vitamin and mineral supplements, avoidance of dumping-triggering foods, and monitoring for nutritional deficiencies.

\medterm{Ytterbium} A chemical element with the symbol Yb and atomic number 70. Its compounds have specialized industrial and research uses; medical relevance depends on the specific compound and exposure.

\medterm{Yttrium} A chemical element with the symbol Y and atomic number 39. The radioactive isotope yttrium-90 is used in selected radiation treatments, including some targeted therapies and radiosynovectomy procedures.

\medterm{Yttrium-90} A radioactive beta-emitting isotope with a physical half-life of about 64 hours, used in selected targeted radiation treatments such as liver radioembolization and some radiosynovectomy procedures.
