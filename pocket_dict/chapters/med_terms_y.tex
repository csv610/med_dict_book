\chapter{Pocket Dictionary: Y Terms}

\medterm{Y Chromosome}-- The sex chromosome that typically carries the SRY gene and other genes involved in
testicular development and male sex differentiation. It is present in most individuals
with a typical 46,XY karyotype and is transmitted through the paternal line.

\medterm{YAG Capsulotomy}-- YAG laser posterior capsulotomy is a minimally invasive laser procedure used to treat
posterior capsule opacification, the most common late complication of cataract surgery,
in which the posterior lens capsule becomes opacified by proliferation of residual lens
epithelial cells, causing decreased vision.

\medterm{Yaws}-- A chronic infectious disease caused by Treponema pallidum pertenue and transmitted
mainly through skin-to-skin contact in children. It begins with a skin lesion and may
progress to destructive lesions of skin, bone, and cartilage if untreated.

\medterm{Yaws (Frambesia)}-- A chronic, non-venereal treponematosis caused by Treponema pallidum subspecies pertenue,
closely related to the syphilis spirochete (T. pallidum pallidum).

\medterm{Yeast}-- Unicellular fungi that reproduce by budding (blastoconidia formation) or fission. The
clinically most important yeasts are Candida species (Candida albicans, C. glabrata, C.
parapsilosis, C. tropicalis, C. krusei, C. auris) and Cryptococcus species (C.
neoformans, C. gattii).

\medterm{Yeast Infection}-- Vulvovaginal candidiasis: infection by Candida albicans (most common), causing vulvar
and vaginal itching, burning, thick white 'cottage cheese' discharge, dyspareunia, and
erythema; more common in pregnancy, diabetes, antibiotic use, and immunosuppression.
Diagnosis: clinical; wet mount (budding yeast/hyphae), KOH, or culture.

\medterm{Yellow Fever}-- A mosquito-borne viral hemorrhagic fever caused by yellow fever virus (Flavivirus),
endemic in tropical Africa and South America. Incubation 3-6 days. Presents with fever,
myalgia, headache, and vomiting. A proportion of patients progress to toxic phase with
jaundice, hemorrhagic fever, and multi-organ failure (hepatorenal).

\medterm{Yellow-nail syndrome}-- A rare triad of yellow, thickened, slow-growing nails, lymphedema, and chronic
respiratory disease including bronchiectasis and pleural effusions. The nail changes
result from impaired lymphatic drainage affecting nail growth. It typically affects
middle-aged adults.

\medterm{Yersinia Enterocolitica}-- A gram-negative coccobacillus of the family Enterobacteriaceae, a common cause of
enterocolitis and mesenteric lymphadenitis, especially in children and young adults in
temperate climates.

\medterm{Yersinia pestis}-- A gram-negative rod that causes plague, a severe and potentially fatal infection. The
organism is transmitted by flea bites or direct contact with infected animals. Bubonic
plague presents with painful lymphadenopathy (buboes). Pneumonic plague involves the
lungs and is transmitted by respiratory droplets.

\medterm{Yolk Sac}-- The first extraembryonic structure visible on ultrasound at 5-6 weeks gestation. It
provides early nutrition to the embryo before the placental circulation is established,
produces blood cells until the fetal liver takes over, and forms the primitive gut. A
normal yolk sac is 3-6 mm in diameter.

\medterm{Yolk-sac tumor}-- A malignant germ-cell tumor that commonly occurs in children and produces
alpha-fetoprotein.

\medterm{Yttrium-90}-- Beta emitter, half-life 64 hours. Used in radioembolization (Y-90 microspheres) for
liver cancer. Bremsstrahlung imaging possible.

\medterm{Yersinia Infection}-- Infection caused by Yersinia bacteria, commonly producing diarrhea, abdominal pain, fever, or vomiting.
Some cases mimic appendicitis; severe illness, dehydration, bloody diarrhea, or persistent symptoms
requires medical assessment.
