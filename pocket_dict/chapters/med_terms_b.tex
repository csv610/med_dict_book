\chapter{Pocket Dictionary: B Terms}

\medterm{B Cell (B Lymphocyte)}-- A key cellular component of adaptive humoral immunity that matures in the bone marrow.
Upon antigen recognition via B-cell receptor (BCR/surface IgM) and T-cell help
(CD40/CD40L interaction), B cells proliferate and differentiate into antibody-secreting
plasma cells and long-lived memory B cells.

\medterm{B Chromosome}-- A supernumerary chromosome that is not part of the standard chromosome complement and is
not required for normal development. It may occur in certain plants, animals, or tumors
and varies in number between cells.

\medterm{B-Cell Receptor (BCR)}-- The membrane-bound immunoglobulin complex on a B lymphocyte that recognizes native
antigen. BCR signaling, with appropriate co-stimulation, promotes B-cell activation,
proliferation, differentiation, and antibody production.

\medterm{B-Lynch Suture}-- A surgical technique for controlling postpartum hemorrhage due to uterine atony
unresponsive to medical management. A compressive suture placed around the uterus to
mechanically compress the uterine walls and reduce blood flow. The suture creates a
brace-like effect. Can be combined with other compression sutures (Cho, Hayman).

\medterm{Babesiosis}-- A tick-borne malaria-like illness caused by Babesia species (B. microti in the US, B.
divergens in Europe), transmitted by Ixodes ticks (same vector as Lyme disease).
Parasites infect red blood cells, causing hemolytic anemia.

\medterm{Babinski Reflex}-- An extensor plantar response present from birth to approximately 2 years. When the
lateral sole is stroked, the great toe dorsiflexes and other toes fan outward.
Disappears as the corticospinal tract myelinates. Persistence beyond age 2 may indicate
neurologic abnormality.

\medterm{Babinski Sign}-- The Babinski sign is a pathological reflex elicited by stroking the lateral aspect of
the sole of the foot from the heel to the metatarsal heads with a blunt object. A normal
response (flexor plantar response) is plantar flexion (curling downward) of all toes.

\medterm{Bacillary Angiomatosis}-- A vascular proliferative disease caused by Bartonella henselae or B. quintana, occurring
in immunocompromised patients (HIV/AIDS, transplant). Presents with erythematous
papules, nodules, or pedunculated lesions resembling Kaposi sarcoma or pyogenic
granuloma. Can involve skin, liver (peliosis hepatis), spleen, bone, and lymph nodes.

\medterm{Baclofen}-- A muscle relaxant that reduces muscle spasticity by activating GABA-B receptors in the spinal
cord. It is used for conditions such as multiple sclerosis and spinal-cord injury. Drowsiness,
weakness, dizziness, and confusion can occur; it should not be stopped suddenly after regular use
because withdrawal can be dangerous.

\medterm{Bacilluria}-- The presence of bacilli in the urine, usually referring to rod-shaped bacteria detected
by microscopy or culture. Its clinical significance depends on symptoms, quantitative
culture, and the organism identified.

\medterm{Back Pain}-- One of the most common medical complaints, affecting up to 80 percent of the population
at some point in life, and the leading cause of disability worldwide. Back pain is
classified by duration: acute (less than 4 weeks), subacute (4-12 weeks), and chronic
(greater than 12 weeks).

\medterm{Backache}-- Pain or discomfort located anywhere in the posterior trunk from neck to buttocks, a
symptom rather than a disease. Causes include musculoskeletal strain (most common), disc
herniation, spinal stenosis, facet arthropathy, sacroiliac joint dysfunction, and
referred pain from renal, pancreatic, or vascular sources.

\medterm{Bacteremia}-- The presence of viable bacteria in the bloodstream, a serious condition that can be
transient (after dental procedures, toothbrushing), intermittent (undrained abscess), or
continuous (endovascular infection like endocarditis). Bacteremia triggers systemic
inflammatory responses ranging from fever and leukocytosis to septic shock.

\medterm{Bacteria}-- Single-celled microscopic organisms lacking a nucleus, which can exist independently or
in colonies; some are beneficial to health (microbiome) while others cause infectious
disease.

\medterm{Bacterial adherence}-- The process by which bacteria attach to host cell surfaces, a critical first step in
colonisation and infection. Adherence is mediated by adhesins, which are surface
structures that bind to specific receptors on host cells. Adhesins include pili,
fimbriae, outer membrane proteins, and capsules.

\medterm{Bacterial biofilms}-- Structured communities of bacteria attached to surfaces and embedded in a self-produced
matrix of extracellular polymeric substances. Biofilms are a major cause of chronic
infections, particularly on medical devices including catheters, prosthetic joints, and
heart valves.

\medterm{Bacterial capsule}-- A polysaccharide layer surrounding the bacterial cell wall that protects against
phagocytosis and complement-mediated killing. Capsules are major virulence factors for
many pathogens including Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and
Klebsiella pneumoniae.

\medterm{Bacterial cell wall}-- The rigid outer layer of bacteria that provides structural integrity and determines cell
shape. The cell wall is composed primarily of peptidoglycan, a polymer of sugars
cross-linked by short peptides.

\medterm{Bacterial classification}-- The organisation of bacteria into taxonomic groups based on genetic, phenotypic, and
evolutionary relationships. Bacteria are classified using a hierarchical system: domain,
phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species.

\medterm{Bacterial conjugation}-- The transfer of genetic material between bacteria through direct cell-to-cell contact
via a pilus. Conjugation is mediated by conjugative plasmids that encode the genes for
transfer. The donor cell (F+) forms a conjugative pilus that attaches to the recipient
cell (F-).

\medterm{Bacterial Conjunctivitis}-- An infection of the conjunctival mucous membrane caused by various bacterial pathogens,
presenting with a purulent or mucopurulent discharge that causes the eyelids to become
matted together, particularly upon waking, conjunctival injection, and a gritty or
foreign body sensation.

\medterm{Bacterial dermatitis}-- Inflammation of the skin caused by bacterial infection, typically presenting with
erythema, edema, warmth, pain, and purulent discharge. Common causative organisms
include Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus pyogenes, and the condition may arise de
novo or as a superinfection of pre-existing dermatitis.

\medterm{Bacterial dormancy}-- A state of reduced metabolic activity that allows bacteria to survive unfavourable
conditions. Dormant forms include spores, persisters, and viable but non-culturable
cells. Spores are highly resistant structures formed by Bacillus and Clostridium.
Persisters are phenotypic variants that are tolerant to antibiotics.

\medterm{Bacterial Endocarditis}-- See Infective Endocarditis.

\medterm{Bacterial Endocarditis Vegetations}-- Vegetations in bacterial endocarditis are complex masses composed of bacteria, fibrin,
and platelets that form on heart valve surfaces or endocardium. The pathogenesis begins
with bacteremia seeding a site of endothelial damage or a previously abnormal valve
surface.

\medterm{Bacterial endospores}-- Highly resistant dormant structures formed by certain gram-positive bacteria including
Bacillus and Clostridium species. Endospores form during nutrient deprivation and can
survive for decades. They are resistant to heat, radiation, chemicals, and desiccation.

\medterm{Bacterial endotoxin}-- Lipopolysaccharide from the outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria that activates
innate immunity and can contribute to fever, hypotension, and septic shock.

\medterm{Bacterial exotoxin}-- A secreted bacterial protein that produces specific toxic effects in host cells or
tissues.

\medterm{Bacterial flagella}-- Thread-like appendages composed of flagellin protein that provide motility to bacteria.
Flagella are composed of three parts: the filament, hook, and basal body. The basal body
is embedded in the cell wall and functions as a rotary motor.

\medterm{Bacterial Gastroenteritis}-- Acute inflammation of the stomach and intestines caused by pathogenic bacteria such as
Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella, or pathogenic Escherichia coli. It commonly causes
diarrhea, abdominal cramps, fever, and sometimes bloody stools or vomiting.

\medterm{Bacterial genetics}-- The study of genes and genetic processes in bacteria. Bacteria have a single circular
chromosome and may contain plasmids, which are extrachromosomal DNA elements. Bacterial
genes are organized into operons, which are clusters of genes regulated as a unit.

\medterm{Bacterial growth phases}-- The stages of bacterial growth in culture. The lag phase is when bacteria adapt to their
environment. The log (exponential) phase is when bacteria multiply at their maximum
rate. The stationary phase is when nutrient depletion and waste accumulation limit
growth. The death phase is when bacteria die.

\medterm{Bacterial Infections}-- Illnesses caused by pathogenic bacteria proliferating in the body, ranging from mild
sore throats and ear infections to life-threatening sepsis and meningitis. They are
diagnosed through cultures, PCR testing, or other microbiological methods.

\medterm{Bacterial Meningitis}-- Infection of the meninges by bacteria, a medical emergency. In neonates: Group B
Streptococcus, E. coli, Listeria. In older children: Neisseria meningitidis,
Streptococcus pneumoniae. Presents with fever, headache, neck stiffness, altered
consciousness, and Kernig/Brudzinski signs. Diagnosis by lumbar puncture.

\medterm{Bacterial metabolism}-- The chemical processes that occur within bacteria to maintain life. Bacteria have
diverse metabolic capabilities including aerobic and anaerobic respiration,
fermentation, and photosynthesis. Metabolic pathways are targets for antimicrobial
drugs.

\medterm{Bacterial morphology}-- The shape and arrangement of bacteria as observed under microscopy. Basic shapes include
cocci (spheres), bacilli (rods), and spirilla (spirals). Cocci can be arranged in pairs
(diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), or tetrads. Bacilli can
be single, paired, or arranged in chains.

\medterm{Bacterial Overgrowth}-- Excessive proliferation of bacteria in the small intestine, commonly called
small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth. It may result from impaired intestinal motility,
anatomical stasis, blind loops, strictures, diverticula, or loss of the normal
ileocaecal barrier.

\medterm{Bacterial pathogenesis}-- The mechanisms by which bacteria cause disease. Pathogenesis involves adhesion,
invasion, toxin production, and immune evasion. Adhesins mediate attachment to host
cells. Invasins allow penetration of tissues. Toxins damage host cells. Immune evasion
mechanisms include capsules, biofilms, and intracellular survival.

\medterm{Bacterial pili}-- Hair-like appendages on the bacterial surface composed of pilin protein. Pili are
shorter and thicker than flagella. Type I pili mediate adherence to host cells and are
important virulence factors. Type IV pili are involved in twitching motility and DNA
uptake during transformation.

\medterm{Bacterial Pneumonia}-- See Pneumonia.

\medterm{Bacterial Spore}-- A metabolically dormant, highly resistant structure produced by certain bacteria,
notably Bacillus and Clostridioides species, in response to environmental stress. Spores
resist desiccation, heat, and many disinfectants and require validated sterilization for
reliable destruction.

\medterm{Bacterial Toxin}-- A poisonous molecule produced by bacteria that damages host cells or disrupts normal
physiology. Exotoxins are secreted or released from viable bacteria, whereas endotoxin
refers principally to lipopolysaccharide from the outer membrane of gram-negative
bacteria.

\medterm{Bacterial toxins}-- Substances produced by bacteria that cause damage to host cells. There are two major
categories: exotoxins and endotoxins. Exotoxins are proteins secreted by bacteria that
have specific toxic effects. Endotoxins are lipopolysaccharide components of
gram-negative bacterial outer membranes released during cell lysis.

\medterm{Bacterial Tracheitis}-- See Tracheitis.

\medterm{Bacterial transduction}-- The transfer of bacterial DNA from one bacterium to another through bacteriophages
(bacterial viruses).

\medterm{Bacterial transformation}-- The uptake of free DNA from the environment by competent bacteria. Transformation occurs
naturally in some bacterial species including Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus
influenzae, and Neisseria. Competence is the ability to take up DNA and is regulated by
specific genes.

\medterm{Bacterial Vaginosis}-- BV: replacement of normal vaginal lactobacilli by an overgrowth of mixed anaerobes
(Gardnerella vaginalis, Mobiluncus, etc.), the most common cause of vaginal discharge in
reproductive-age women. Risk: new/multiple partners, douching, smoking.

\medterm{Bacterial virulence factors}-- Molecules produced by bacteria that contribute to their ability to cause disease.
Virulence factors include adhesins, invasins, toxins, capsules, and enzymes. Adhesins
mediate attachment to host cells. Toxins damage host cells directly. Capsules protect
against phagocytosis. Enzymes degrade host tissues.

\medterm{Bactericide}-- An agent that kills bacteria rather than merely inhibiting their growth
(bacteriostatic). Bactericidal antibiotics include beta-lactams (penicillins,
cephalosporins, carbapenems), aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, metronidazole, and
vancomycin.

\medterm{Bacteriology}-- The branch of microbiology concerned with the study of bacteria, including their
morphology, genetics, physiology, ecology, and clinical significance.

\medterm{Bacteriophage}-- A virus that infects and replicates within bacteria.

\medterm{Bacteriophage therapy}-- The use of bacterial viruses (bacteriophages) to treat bacterial infections.
Bacteriophages are viruses that specifically infect and lyse bacteria. They have narrow
specificity for target bacteria and do not affect normal flora.

\medterm{Bacteriophages}-- Viruses that specifically infect and replicate within bacteria, also known as phages.
Each phage type typically infects a narrow range of bacterial strains. Phages attach to
bacterial surface receptors and inject their genome, hijacking bacterial machinery to
produce progeny phages that lyse the host cell.

\medterm{Bacterium}-- A unicellular prokaryote lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, possessing a
single circular chromosome and typically a peptidoglycan cell wall.

\medterm{Bacteriuria}-- The presence of bacteria in urine, detected by urinalysis or culture. It may occur with
urinary symptoms, as in a urinary-tract infection, or without symptoms, as in
asymptomatic bacteriuria; interpretation depends on specimen quality, bacterial
quantity, patient context, and the presence of inflammation or symptoms.

\medterm{Bag-Mask Ventilation}-- Manual ventilation of a non-breathing or inadequately breathing patient using a
self-inflating bag (AMBU) attached to a mask sealed over the face. Effective technique:
correct mask size, jaw thrust/head extension, two-person technique (two-hand seal),
adequate tidal volumes with ETCO2 monitoring.

\medterm{Bagassosis}-- An occupational hypersensitivity pneumonitis (extrinsic allergic alveolitis) caused by
inhalation of dust from moldy bagasse—the fibrous residue of sugarcane after extraction.
Caused by thermophilic actinomycetes (Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula) that colonize
stored bagasse.

\medterm{Bainbridge Reflex}-- An increase in heart rate produced by stretch of the atrial walls, especially during
increased venous return. The reflex helps limit venous congestion by adjusting cardiac
output.

\medterm{Baker Cyst}-- A fluid-filled cyst in the popliteal fossa, resulting from herniation of the synovial
membrane through the posterior knee joint capsule or from a communication between the
knee joint and the gastrocnemius-semimembranosus bursa.

\medterm{Baker's cyst}-- A fluid-filled synovial cyst arising from the gastrocnemius-semimembranosus bursa
posterior to the knee, typically caused by chronic knee joint effusion from conditions
such as osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or meniscal tears. It presents as a
palpable, tender mass in the popliteal fossa that may rupture causing calf swelling.

\medterm{Balance}-- The ability to maintain the body's center of mass over its base of support, relying on
input from the vestibular system, vision, and proprioception. It is essential for
walking, standing, and performing daily activities without falling. Age-related decline,
medications, and neurological disorders can impair balance, increasing fall risk.

\medterm{Balance Assessment}-- Tests: Berg Balance Scale, Tinetti, timed up and go, Romberg. Identifies fall risk.

\medterm{Balance Disorders and Vertigo}-- Balance disorders encompass conditions that cause dizziness, unsteadiness, or a
sensation of motion, often arising from dysfunction in the inner ear, brain, or sensory
pathways.

\medterm{Balance Impairment}-- The ability to maintain the body's center of mass over its base of support, relying on
input from the vestibular system, vision, and proprioception. See balance.

\medterm{Balance Training}-- Exercises improving postural stability and preventing falls. Components: static balance,
dynamic balance, reactive balance. Techniques: weight shifting, tandem stance,
single-leg stance, perturbations, balance boards. Essential for elderly, neurological
conditions.

\medterm{Balanitis}-- Inflammation of the glans penis, often involving the prepuce (balanoposthitis). Causes
include poor hygiene, irritants, infection, and dermatologic conditions such as lichen
planus, psoriasis, and lichen sclerosus. It may cause erythema, swelling, discharge,
pruritus, and discomfort; treatment depends on the cause.

\medterm{Balanoposthitis}-- Inflammation of both the glans penis and the prepuce. It may cause pain, erythema,
swelling, discharge, or difficulty retracting the foreskin and can result from
infection, irritation, or inflammatory skin disease.

\medterm{Baldness}-- The common term for hair loss or alopecia, most frequently referring to androgenetic
alopecia (male or female pattern hair loss). Affects up to 50\1 of men by age 50 and
25\1 of women by age 60. Driven by dihydrotestosterone (DHT)-mediated miniaturization of
hair follicles in susceptible individuals.

\medterm{Ballard Score}-- Clinical assessment of gestational age in newborns using 6 physical and 6 neuromuscular
maturity criteria. Physical parameters: skin texture, ear form and recoil, eye
appearance, breast tissue, and genitalia. Neuromuscular: posture, arm and leg recoil,
popliteal angle, scarf sign, and heel-to-ear.

\medterm{Ballism}-- A hyperkinetic movement disorder characterized by sudden, involuntary, large-amplitude,
flinging movements of a limb or limbs. It is often related to dysfunction of the
subthalamic region or connected basal-ganglia circuits.

\medterm{Ballistocardiography}-- The graphic recording of repetitive body movements produced by the ejection of blood
with each heartbeat. It has been used to study cardiac mechanical activity and
circulation.

\medterm{Balloon (Vascular)}-- Inflatable balloon for angioplasty, stent deployment. See Balloon Catheter.

\medterm{Balloon Aortic Valvuloplasty}-- Catheter-based procedure using balloon inflation to dilate a stenotic aortic valve.
Provides temporary hemodynamic improvement in severe AS but restenosis is common within
6-12 months. Used as a bridge to TAVR or surgical AVR in hemodynamically unstable
patients, or as palliation in patients not eligible for definitive valve replacement.

\medterm{Balloon Catheter}-- Inflatable balloon for angioplasty, stent deployment. Non-compliant balloons for
high-pressure dilation.

\medterm{Band Keratopathy}-- A horizontal band of calcium deposition in the superficial cornea, typically across the
interpalpebral area. It may occur with chronic ocular inflammation, hypercalcemia, renal
disease, or longstanding eye disease and can impair vision.

\medterm{Bandage}-- Material for wound covering or support. Types: gauze, adhesive, elastic, tubular.

\medterm{Bandage Scissors}-- Blunt-tipped scissors for cutting bandages.

\medterm{Bankart Lesion}-- Injury to inferior glenohumeral ligament/labrum from shoulder dislocation. Associated
with recurrent instability. Treatment: surgical repair (arthroscopic/open).

\medterm{Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba Syndrome}-- Autosomal dominant PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS) caused by loss-of-function
mutations in PTEN (10q23), allelic to Cowden syndrome.

\medterm{Banti Disease}-- A historical term for congestive splenomegaly associated with portal hypertension and
hypersplenism. Modern evaluation usually identifies the underlying hepatic,
portal-venous, or hematologic cause.

\medterm{Banti Syndrome}-- Historical eponym for non-cirrhotic portal hypertension with splenomegaly and
hypersplenism, originally described by Guido Banti as "splenic anemia with portal
hypertension but without hepatic cirrhosis." Causes include extrahepatic portal vein
thrombosis, congenital hepatic fibrosis, schistosomiasis, nodular regenerative

\medterm{Barbiturate}-- A class of CNS depressants derived from barbituric acid that enhance GABA-A receptor
activity by increasing the duration of chloride channel opening (as opposed to
benzodiazepines, which increase the frequency). At low doses: anxiolysis and sedation.
At moderate doses: hypnosis and anesthesia.

\medterm{Bare Stent (Vascular)}-- See Bare Stent.

\medterm{Bariatric Surgery}-- Weight loss procedures and surgery, collectively known as bariatric surgery, are
invasive interventions for severe obesity that produce substantial and sustained weight
loss by altering gastrointestinal anatomy and physiology.

\medterm{Barium}-- A metallic element (atomic number 56, symbol Ba) whose sulfate salt (barium sulfate) is
used as a radiocontrast agent due to its high atomic number, which effectively absorbs
X-rays.

\medterm{Barium Enema}-- Fluoroscopic imaging of the large intestine after retrograde filling with barium
contrast. Double-contrast (barium + air) provides better mucosal detail. Indications:
colorectal cancer screening (historical, now CT colonography preferred), evaluation of
colonic obstruction, diverticulosis, and postsurgical anatomy.

\medterm{Barium Study}-- X-ray imaging using barium sulfate contrast. Barium swallow, barium enema, upper GI
series. Evaluates GI tract morphology and function.

\medterm{Barium Swallow}-- Fluoroscopic examination of the esophagus after ingestion of barium contrast. Used to
evaluate esophageal anatomy, motility, strictures, and structural abnormalities. The
study demonstrates mucosal detail, filling defects, strictures, and motility disorders.
A barium follow-through extends the examination to the small bowel.

\medterm{Barium-133}-- Calibration source. Half-life 10.5 years. Used for energy calibration.

\medterm{Barognosis}-- The ability to recognize and distinguish the weight of objects by handling them, with
intact primary sensation. Impaired barognosis indicates a cortical sensory deficit.

\medterm{Baroreceptor Reflex}-- The rapid neural mechanism for short-term blood pressure regulation, involving
baroreceptors (mechanoreceptors) in the carotid sinus (innervated by CN IX) and aortic
arch (innervated by CN X). When blood pressure rises, baroreceptors increase their
firing rate, signaling the cardiovascular center in the medulla.

\medterm{Barotrauma}-- Lung injury from excessive pressure during ventilation, causing alveolar rupture
(pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum). Risk factors: high pressures, high PEEP. Prevented by
lung-protective ventilation.

\medterm{Barrett Esophagus}-- Metaplastic transformation of the normal stratified squamous epithelium of the distal
esophagus to specialized intestinal-type columnar epithelium containing goblet cells,
occurring as a consequence of chronic gastroesophageal reflux disease.

\medterm{Barrett Esophagus Surveillance}-- Systematic endoscopic monitoring of patients with Barrett esophagus to detect dysplasia
and early esophageal adenocarcinoma. Patients without dysplasia undergo surveillance
every three to five years with four-quadrant biopsies every two centimeters throughout
the Barrett segment.

\medterm{Barrier Creams}-- Topical preparations applied to the skin to create a physical protective layer against
irritants, moisture, and pathogens. Common formulations include zinc oxide, petrolatum,
dimethicone, and titanium dioxide.

\medterm{Barthel Index}-- Standardized measure of functional independence in activities of daily living (ADL).
Scores 0-100 based on 10 items: feeding, bathing, grooming, dressing, bowel control,
bladder control, toilet use, transfers, mobility, stair climbing. <40: severe
dependency; 41-60: moderate dependency; >60: mild dependency.

\medterm{Bartholin Cyst}-- A cyst of the Bartholin gland (paired mucus-secreting glands at the posterolateral
introitus) due to obstruction of the duct; if infected, a Bartholin abscess.

\medterm{Bartholin Gland}-- One of two small glands near the vaginal opening that produce lubricating mucus.
Blockage of its duct can cause a painless cyst; infection can produce a painful abscess with swelling,
redness, and difficulty walking or sitting. Recurrent or atypical masses require gynecologic assessment.

\medterm{Bartonellosis}-- A group of infections caused by Bartonella species. Cat scratch disease (B. henselae):
regional lymphadenopathy after cat scratch/bite, fever, inoculation papule. Bacillary
angiomatosis (B. henselae, B. quintana): vascular proliferative lesions in
immunocompromised (HIV), red papules/nodules on skin, bone, and viscera. Oroya fever (B.

\medterm{Bartter Syndrome}-- Inherited autosomal recessive disorder of the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle
causing salt wasting, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis, and hypercalciuria. Results from
mutations in genes encoding the NKCC2 transporter or its regulatory subunits. Presents
in childhood with failure to thrive, polyuria, and polydipsia.

\medterm{Basal Cell Carcinoma}-- The most common malignant skin tumor, arising from basal keratinocyte-lineage cells and
associated with cumulative ultraviolet exposure and Hedgehog-pathway abnormalities. It
commonly presents as a pearly papule or nodule with telangiectasia, ulceration, or a
rolled border.

\medterm{Basal Ganglia}-- Deep gray matter nuclei in the cerebral hemispheres for motor control, cognition, and
emotion. Key structures: caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus (internal/external
segments), subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra. Direct pathway facilitates movement;
indirect inhibits it.

\medterm{Basal Ganglia Dysfunction}-- Abnormal function of the deep cerebral nuclei that regulate movement, cognition, and
procedural learning. It may cause tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, chorea, dystonia, or
abnormal involuntary movements, as in Parkinson disease and Huntington disease.

\medterm{Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)}-- Energy expenditure at rest for basic physiological functions. Accounts for 60-75\1 of
total energy expenditure. Affected by age, sex, body composition, thyroid status,
genetics. Measured by indirect calorimetry.

\medterm{Base}-- A substance that accepts protons (H+) or donates electron pairs (Lewis base). In aqueous
solution, bases increase OH- concentration (pH >7). Strong bases (NaOH, KOH) dissociate
completely; weak bases (NH3, HCO3-, protein side chains) partially.

\medterm{Base Excision Repair}-- The repair pathway for small, non-helix-distorting base lesions including oxidized bases
(8-oxoguanine), deaminated bases (uracil from cytosine deamination), alkylated bases,
and abasic (AP) sites.

\medterm{Basement Membrane}-- A thin, delicate layer of extracellular matrix that anchors epithelial tissue to the
underlying connective tissue.

\medterm{Basic Life Support (BLS)}-- Level of medical care used for victims of life-threatening illnesses or injuries until
full medical care can be provided.

\medterm{Basic Metabolic Panel}-- A panel of blood tests measuring serum glucose, sodium, potassium, chloride,
bicarbonate, BUN, and creatinine. Also includes calcium in some institutions. Uses:
assess hydration status, renal function, acid-base balance, and glucose control.

\medterm{Basic Reproduction Number (R0)}-- The expected number of secondary infections generated by one typical infectious person
in a completely susceptible population, under specified conditions. It is a population
and context parameter, not a fixed biological constant.

\medterm{Basilar artery aneurysm}-- Dilation of the basilar artery, most commonly fusiform, often associated with
atherosclerosis or hypertension. May compress cranial nerves or brainstem structures
causing facial pain, double vision, or hemiparesis. Rupture causes subarachnoid
hemorrhage with high morbidity.

\medterm{Basophil}-- A rare (<1 percent of leukocytes) granulocyte with large, basophilic granules containing
histamine, heparin, and leukotrienes. Basophils bear high-affinity IgE receptors
(Fc-epsilon-RI) and, like mast cells, release histamine and mediators during immediate
hypersensitivity (allergic) reactions.

\medterm{Battered Child Syndrome}-- A pattern of repeated, non-accidental physical injury inflicted on a child by a
caregiver, formally described by Kempe in 1962. Features include fractures of differing
ages, subdural and retinal hemorrhages, inconsistent histories, and delayed
presentation; mandatory reporting is required in most jurisdictions.

\medterm{Bauhin Valve}-- The ileocecal valve, a mucosal sphincter at the junction of the terminal ileum and cecum
that helps regulate passage of intestinal contents and limits reflux from the colon.

\medterm{BCG Vaccine}-- Bacille Calmette-Guerin, a live attenuated vaccine derived from Mycobacterium bovis,
used for TB prevention. Efficacy: 60-80\1 against disseminated TB and TB meningitis in
children; variable efficacy against pulmonary TB. Indications: part of routine childhood
immunization in high-TB-burden countries.

\medterm{Beals Syndrome}-- A congenital connective-tissue disorder characterized by congenital contractures,
arachnodactyly, kyphoscoliosis, and crumpled ears. It is usually associated with
pathogenic variants affecting fibrillin-2.

\medterm{Beam Hardening Artifact}-- CT artifact from dense structures. Reduced by beam hardening correction algorithms.

\medterm{Beau Lines}-- Transverse depressions or grooves across the nail plate caused by temporary interruption
of nail-matrix growth. They may follow severe illness, fever, trauma, malnutrition,
chemotherapy, or systemic inflammatory disease.

\medterm{Beau's Lines}-- Transverse grooves or depressions across a nail caused by temporary interruption of
nail-matrix growth. They may follow severe illness, trauma, nutritional stress, or
systemic disease and grow distally with the nail.

\medterm{Bechterew Disease}-- Historical eponym for ankylosing spondylitis (also known as Marie-Strümpell disease),
describing the chronic inflammatory arthritis of the axial skeleton first reported by
Vladimir Bekhterev (Russian, 1892) and Adolf Strümpell (German, 1884).

\medterm{Beck Triad}-- Classic diagnostic clinical triad of Cardiac Tamponade: (1) Hypotension, (2) Distended
neck veins (elevated JVP), and (3) Muffled / distant heart sounds. See Cardiac
Tamponade.

\medterm{Becker Muscular Dystrophy}-- An X-linked recessive neuromuscular disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene,
similar to Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), but with in-frame mutations that allow
production of some functional dystrophin protein, albeit reduced in quantity or abnormal
in size. The result is a milder clinical phenotype compared to DMD.

\medterm{Becker’s nevus}-- A benign hamartomatous lesion appearing as a unilateral, hyperpigmented, often hairy
patch, typically developing in adolescence on the shoulder or upper trunk of males. It
results from a localized androgen-dependent increase in dermal smooth muscle and
pigmentation. It is cosmetically concerning but carries no malignant potential.

\medterm{Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome}-- An overgrowth disorder associated with 11p15.5 imprinting defects. Features: macrosomia,
macroglossia, omphalocele, visceromegaly, and neonatal hypoglycemia. Increased risk of
embryonal tumors (Wilms tumor, hepatoblastoma).

\medterm{Becquerel (Bq)}-- The SI unit of radioactive activity, equal to one nuclear transformation per second.
Administered activity is commonly reported in megabecquerels or gigabecquerels in
nuclear medicine.

\medterm{Bedbug Bites}-- Cutaneous reactions to bites of Cimex lectularius (bedbugs), nocturnal blood-feeding
insects. Bites are painless but cause erythematous, pruritic papules, often in a linear
arrangement (breakfast, lunch, dinner sign) or clusters on exposed skin (face, neck,
arms, hands). Delayed hypersensitivity develops over days.

\medterm{Bedpan}-- Bedside toilet for patients.

\medterm{Bedsore}-- Pressure ulcer (decubitus): localized skin/soft tissue injury from prolonged pressure
over bony prominences (sacrum, heels, occiput, trochanters), usually in immobile
patients.

\medterm{Bedwetting}-- Involuntary urination during sleep after the age at which nighttime bladder control is
usually established. It may be primary or secondary to urinary infection, constipation,
diabetes, sleep disorders, stress, or other medical conditions and should be assessed
when persistent, new, or accompanied by daytime symptoms.

\medterm{Beers Criteria}-- List of potentially inappropriate medications in elderly. Updated periodically. Guides
deprescribing.

\medterm{Behavioral Rehabilitation}-- Behavioral interventions for chronic conditions: pain management, addiction, adjustment.

\medterm{Behcet Disease}-- A chronic, relapsing systemic vasculitis characterized by recurrent oral ulcers, genital
ulcers, and ocular inflammation, with a distribution along the ancient Silk Road from
the Mediterranean to East Asia.

\medterm{Belching}-- The release of swallowed air or gas from the stomach through the mouth. Frequent
belching may occur with aerophagia, gastroesophageal reflux, functional dyspepsia, or
carbonated-drink consumption.

\medterm{Bell Palsy}-- Acute, idiopathic, unilateral facial nerve (CN VII) paralysis of lower motor neuron
type, with an incidence of approximately 20-30 per 100,000 person-years.

\medterm{Bell's Palsy}-- Idiopathic facial nerve paralysis. Acute onset unilateral facial weakness. May follow
viral infection. Treatment: corticosteroids within 72 hours, eye protection. Most
recover spontaneously within 3-6 months.

\medterm{Bence Jones Protein}-- Monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains (free kappa or lambda) detectable in urine of
patients with monoclonal gammopathies, most characteristically multiple myeloma but also
Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia, MGUS, and primary amyloidosis (AL).

\medterm{Bence-Jones Protein}-- A monoclonal immunoglobulin light chain found in urine or blood, classically associated
with plasma-cell disorders such as multiple myeloma. Detection contributes to evaluation
of monoclonal gammopathy and kidney injury.

\medterm{Bends}-- A manifestation of decompression sickness caused by gas bubbles forming in tissues or
blood after a rapid reduction in ambient pressure. It commonly produces deep joint or
muscle pain but may also involve the nervous system or lungs.

\medterm{Beneficence and Non-Maleficence}-- Core biomedical ethical principles guiding clinical practice and medical research.
Beneficence requires healthcare providers to act in the best interest of the patient,
taking positive steps to promote health, prevent harm, and relieve suffering.

\medterm{Benign Intracranial Hypertension}-- An older term for idiopathic intracranial hypertension, a disorder of raised
intracranial pressure without an intracranial mass or hydrocephalus. Headache and
papilledema are typical, and untreated disease may threaten vision.

\medterm{Benign Mixed Tumor}-- See Pleomorphic Adenoma.

\medterm{Benign Neoplasm}-- Benign neoplasms are proliferations of cells that remain confined to their site of
origin, grow by expansion rather than invasion, and do not metastasize to distant sites.
They typically grow slowly, pushing aside adjacent normal tissue and often becoming
enclosed by a fibrous pseudocapsule formed from compressed connective tissue.

\medterm{Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)}-- A common peripheral vertigo from dislodged otoconia (canalithiasis) within the
semicircular canals, most often the posterior canal. Brief episodes of rotational
vertigo lasting under one minute provoked by changes in head position.

\medterm{Benign Positional Vertigo}-- Brief episodes of spinning dizziness triggered by changes in head position, usually
caused by displaced calcium carbonate crystals in a semicircular canal. It is also
called benign paroxysmal positional vertigo or BPPV.

\medterm{Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia}-- Non-malignant enlargement of the prostate gland due to hyperplasia of stromal and
glandular elements, affecting >50\1 of men by age 60 and >90\1 by age 85. Presents with
lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS): hesitancy, weak stream, intermittency, straining,
incomplete emptying, urgency, frequency, and nocturia.

\medterm{Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)}-- Non-cancerous enlargement of prostate gland causing lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS)
in aging men. Prevalence: ~50\1 at age 60, ~90\1 at age 85. Symptoms: hesitancy, weak
stream, urgency, frequency, nocturia, incomplete emptying. Diagnosis: DRE, PSA,
uroflowmetry, post-void residual.

\medterm{Benign Prostatic Obstruction (BPO)}-- Bladder-outlet obstruction caused by benign prostatic enlargement or increased prostatic
smooth-muscle tone. It can produce weak stream, hesitancy, intermittency, straining,
incomplete emptying, retention, infection, and upper-tract deterioration.

\medterm{Benign Tumor}-- Non-cancerous growth, slow-growing, well-differentiated, localized, does not invade or
metastasize. Examples: lipoma, fibroma, adenoma. May cause problems by compression or
hormone secretion.

\medterm{Bent Spine Syndrome}-- A marked, usually reducible forward flexion of the trunk caused by weakness or abnormal
contraction of axial extensor muscles. It is also called camptocormia and may accompany
neuromuscular or movement disorders.

\medterm{Benzene}-- Solvent, industrial chemical. Chronic exposure: aplastic anemia, leukemia (especially
AML). Acute: CNS depression, arrhythmias. Prevention: substitution, ventilation,
monitoring.

\medterm{Benzo(a)pyrene}-- Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon from combustion. Carcinogenic (lung, skin cancer). Found
in tobacco smoke, charred meat, air pollution.

\medterm{Benzodiazepine Use Disorder}-- A problematic pattern of benzodiazepine use leading to significant impairment.
Benzodiazepines include alprazolam, clonazepam, diazepam, and lorazepam. Dependence can
develop rapidly with regular use. Withdrawal can be life-threatening, presenting with
anxiety, insomnia, tremor, seizures, and psychosis.

\medterm{Benzodiazepine Withdrawal}-- Symptoms from cessation or reduction of prolonged benzodiazepine use. Early symptoms
include anxiety, insomnia, tremor, and diaphoresis. Severe withdrawal can include
seizures, psychosis, and delirium. Withdrawal severity depends on the half-life of the
benzodiazepine, duration of use, and dose.

\medterm{Benzodiazepines}-- A class of drugs that enhance the effect of the inhibitory neurotransmitter
gamma-aminobutyric acid at the GABA-A receptor, increasing the frequency of chloride
channel opening and producing anxiolytic, sedative, anticonvulsant, and muscle relaxant
effects.

\medterm{Bereavement}-- Natural grief response to the death of a loved one, marked by sadness, yearning, and
preoccupation with the deceased, experienced in waves that diminish over weeks to months
with preserved self-esteem. Distinct from major depression by its wave-like quality and
preserved self-worth.

\medterm{Bereavement reaction}-- A normal emotional response to the death of a loved one, involving grief that generally
follows a variable course without necessarily indicating a mental disorder.

\medterm{Berger Disease}-- Historical name for IgA nephropathy named after Jean Berger who first characterized
mesangial IgA deposition in 1968. Characterized by recurrent gross hematuria triggered
by mucosal infections with intervals of microscopic hematuria and proteinuria. Generally
benign course in most patients but subset progresses to CKD.

\medterm{Beriberi}-- A nutritional deficiency disease caused by inadequate thiamine (vitamin B1), occurring
in two forms. Wet beriberi presents with high-output heart failure, peripheral
vasodilation, edema, and tachycardia due to impaired cardiac energy metabolism.

\medterm{Bernard–Soulier syndrome}-- An inherited platelet-adhesion disorder caused by deficiency or dysfunction of the
glycoprotein Ib-IX-V complex, producing mucocutaneous bleeding and giant platelets.

\medterm{Berry Aneurysm}-- A small, rounded saccular aneurysm arising at an arterial branch point in the circle of
Willis. Rupture is a major cause of nontraumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage.

\medterm{Berylliosis}-- A granulomatous lung disease from beryllium exposure. Chronic berylliosis resembles
sarcoidosis. Diagnosis requires beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test.

\medterm{Beta Blockers}-- A class of medications that block the effects of adrenaline on the body's beta
receptors. They slow down the heart rate and reduce blood pressure, making them widely
used for treating heart conditions, high blood pressure, and anxiety.

\medterm{Beta-Adrenergic Blocker}-- A drug that antagonizes beta-adrenergic receptors, reducing heart rate, myocardial
contractility, and renin release. It is used in selected patients with hypertension,
angina, arrhythmias, post-myocardial infarction disease, and heart failure; abrupt
withdrawal may worsen ischemia.

\medterm{Beta-Blocker Overdose}-- Bradycardia, hypotension, hypoglycemia, bronchospasm (non-selective). Treatment:
atropine, glucagon (1-10 mg IV), high-dose insulin euglycemia therapy, calcium,
vasopressors, pacing if refractory.

\medterm{Beta-Blockers}-- Beta-adrenergic blocking agents, commonly known as beta-blockers, are topical
medications used in the treatment of glaucoma that lower intraocular pressure by
reducing the production of aqueous humor by the ciliary body, primarily through blockade
of beta-2 adrenergic receptors.

\medterm{Beta-Lactam Antibiotics}-- A large class of antimicrobial agents characterized by a beta-lactam ring in their
molecular structure, which is essential for their bactericidal activity.

\medterm{Beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations}-- Combinations of beta-lactam antibiotics with beta-lactamase inhibitors that extend
activity against beta-lactamase producing organisms. The major combinations include
amoxicillin-clavulanate, ampicillin-sulbactam, piperacillin-tazobactam,
cefoperazone-sulbactam, and ceftolozane-tazobactam.

\medterm{Beta-Oxidation}-- The catabolic process by which fatty acid molecules are broken down in the mitochondria
(and peroxisomes) to generate acetyl-CoA, NADH, and FADH2.

\medterm{Beta-thalassemia}-- An inherited hemoglobin disorder with deficient or absent beta-globin chain synthesis,
ranging from mild trait to transfusion-dependent major disease with iron overload,
extramedullary hematopoiesis, and skeletal changes.

\medterm{Bezoar}-- See Gastric Bezoar.

\medterm{BI-RADS}-- Breast Imaging-Reporting and Data System. Standardizes mammography reporting.
Categories: 0 (incomplete), 1 (negative), 2 (benign), 3 (probably benign), 4
(suspicious), 5 (highly suggestive of malignancy), 6 (known biopsy-proven malignancy).

\medterm{Bicarbonate}-- The principal extracellular buffer anion, formed from carbon dioxide and water and
regulated chiefly by the lungs and kidneys. The bicarbonate-carbonic-acid system helps
maintain arterial pH; serum bicarbonate is commonly used to assess metabolic components
of acid-base disorders.

\medterm{Biceps Brachii Muscle}-- Two-headed anterior arm muscle. Long head from supraglenoid tubercle, short head from
coracoid process, both inserting on the radial tuberosity. Innervated by
musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C6). Most powerful elbow flexor in supination; supinates
forearm; weakly flexes shoulder. Distal rupture causes Popeye deformity.

\medterm{Biceps Reflex}-- A tendon reflex produced by tapping the biceps tendon, causing elbow flexion through the
musculocutaneous nerve and predominantly C5--C6 spinal segments. It helps assess the
corresponding peripheral and central pathways.

\medterm{Bicipital Groove}-- The intertubercular groove of the humerus that transmits the tendon of the long head of
the biceps brachii, with the transverse humeral ligament and adjacent rotator-cuff
structures contributing to tendon stability.

\medterm{Bicipital Tuberosity}-- The radial tuberosity, a roughened prominence on the proximal radius where the biceps
brachii tendon inserts and produces flexion and supination of the forearm.

\medterm{Bicuspid Aortic Valve}-- The most common congenital cardiac anomaly, present in 1-2\1 of the population. The
aortic valve has two leaflets instead of three, predisposing to aortic stenosis (usually
by age 50-60), aortic regurgitation, and infective endocarditis. Associated with
aortopathy (aortic root dilation and dissection risk) and coarctation of the aorta.

\medterm{Bicuspid Valve}-- A heart valve with two leaflets rather than the usual three. The term most often refers
to congenital bicuspid aortic valve, which can cause aortic stenosis, regurgitation,
infective endocarditis, or ascending-aortic disease.

\medterm{Bietti's Crystalline Dystrophy}-- A rare autosomal recessive chorioretinal dystrophy characterized by tiny crystalline
deposits in the retina and cornea with progressive chorioretinal atrophy, presenting
with night blindness and progressive visual field loss beginning in early adulthood.

\medterm{Biguanide}-- A class of oral antidiabetic drugs derived from guanidine, with metformin being the only
biguanide in current clinical use (phenformin and buformin were withdrawn due to lactic
acidosis risk). Metformin is the first-line pharmacotherapy for type 2 diabetes
mellitus.

\medterm{Bilateral peritonsillar abscesses}-- Suppurative infection in the peritonsillar space on both sides, a rare complication of
acute tonsillitis presenting with severe sore throat, dysphagia, trismus, and muffled
hot potato voice. Bilateral involvement risks acute upper airway obstruction and
requires urgent needle aspiration or incision and drainage.

\medterm{Bile Acids}-- Substances produced in the liver that aid digestion of fats and absorption of
fat-soluble vitamins. In pregnancy, elevated bile acids (\(\)10 \(\)mol/L) indicate
intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (obstetric cholestasis), a liver disorder
characterized by pruritus (itching, typically palms and soles) and elevated serum bile

\medterm{Bile Duct}-- A channel that carries bile from the liver and gallbladder to the duodenum. The right
and left hepatic ducts unite as the common hepatic duct; union with the cystic duct
forms the common bile duct, which usually joins the pancreatic duct before entering the
duodenum at the major duodenal papilla.

\medterm{Bile Reflux}-- Reflux of duodenal contents (bile acids, lysolecithin, pancreatic enzymes) into the
stomach and esophagus, caused by incompetence of the pyloric sphincter, prior gastric
surgery (Billroth II, Roux-en-Y, vagotomy with pyloroplasty), cholecystectomy, or
functional gastroparesis.

\medterm{Bile Salt Emulsification}-- Bile salts (derived from cholesterol in the liver) are amphipathic molecules with
hydrophobic and hydrophilic regions that act as biological detergents, emulsifying
dietary lipids into smaller droplets (micelles). This increases the surface area of
lipid substrate available for pancreatic lipase action.

\medterm{Bile Salt Malabsorption}-- Condition characterized by impaired reabsorption of bile acids in the terminal ileum
leading to excessive bile acids entering the colon where they stimulate water and
electrolyte secretion causing chronic watery diarrhea.

\medterm{Biliary Atresia}-- A progressive, obliterative cholangiopathy of the extrahepatic bile ducts in neonates,
leading to biliary obstruction, cholestasis, and ultimately biliary cirrhosis if
untreated.

\medterm{Biliary Cirrhosis}-- See Primary Biliary Cholangitis.

\medterm{Biliary Colic}-- Episodic right-upper-quadrant or epigastric pain caused by transient obstruction of the
cystic duct, usually by a gallstone. Pain is typically steady, may follow meals, and
resolves when the obstruction passes; fever and persistent inflammatory findings suggest
a complication.

\medterm{Biliary Dyskinesia}-- Functional gallbladder disorder with impaired gallbladder ejection fraction in the
absence of gallstones. Presents with biliary colic-like right upper quadrant pain.
Diagnosed by CCK-HIDA scan showing ejection fraction less than thirty-five percent. The
diagnosis requires exclusion of other causes of biliary-type pain.

\medterm{Biliary Fever}-- Fever associated with infection or inflammation of the biliary tract, classically acute
ascending cholangitis. It may occur with right-upper-quadrant pain and jaundice and can
progress rapidly to sepsis.

\medterm{Biliary Stent}-- Tube placed in bile duct to relieve obstruction. Types: plastic (short-term, cheaper,
higher occlusion rate), self-expanding metal (longer-term, patent longer). Indications:
malignant obstruction (pancreatic cancer, cholangiocarcinoma), benign stricture.
Complications: migration, occlusion, cholangitis, pancreatitis.

\medterm{Biliary Tract Injuries}-- Bile duct injury (BDI) occurs in 0.3-0.5\1 of laparoscopic cholecystectomies. Strasberg
classification: A (cystic duct leak/minor hepatic duct leak), B (occlusion of aberrant
right hepatic duct), C (transection of aberrant right hepatic duct without ligation), D
(lateral injury to major duct), E (major duct injury: E1-E5 based on level).

\medterm{Bilious Vomiting}-- Vomiting of green/yellow, bilious material in the newborn or infant, defined as emerald
green or yellow vomitus that contains bile.

\medterm{Bilirubin}-- A yellow pigment formed mainly from heme breakdown. Unconjugated bilirubin is
transported bound to albumin to the liver, where it is conjugated with glucuronic acid
and excreted in bile; accumulation causes jaundice.

\medterm{Bilirubin Encephalopathy}-- Neurologic injury caused by deposition of unconjugated bilirubin in the brain,
especially the basal ganglia. Severe neonatal hyperbilirubinemia can produce acute
lethargy, abnormal tone, feeding difficulty, seizures, and permanent kernicterus.

\medterm{Bilirubin Metabolism}-- Bilirubin is the breakdown product of heme from senescent red blood cells, metabolized
primarily by the liver through a series of enzymatic steps. Heme is converted to
biliverdin by heme oxygenase, then to unconjugated bilirubin by biliverdin reductase.
Unconjugated bilirubin is lipophilic and transported bound to albumin to the liver.

\medterm{Bilirubinuria}-- The presence of conjugated bilirubin in urine. It indicates that water-soluble bilirubin
has entered the circulation, often because of hepatocellular disease or biliary
obstruction.

\medterm{Binet-Simon Scale}-- An early developmental assessment used to estimate intellectual functioning in children
by comparing performance with age-based tasks. Modern evaluation uses standardized,
validated developmental and cognitive instruments.

\medterm{Binge Eating Disorder}-- Recurrent episodes of eating large quantities of food with a sense of lack of control,
without regular compensatory behavior. Associated with obesity, depression, and anxiety.
Diagnosis by DSM-5 criteria. Treatment: cognitive behavioral therapy, interpersonal
therapy, and SSRIs. Weight management with dietary counseling and exercise.

\medterm{Binocular visual loss}-- Reduction in vision affecting both eyes simultaneously, typically resulting from
bilateral optic neuropathy, chiasmal lesions, or diffuse bilateral retinal disease
rather than monocular ocular pathology. Evaluation requires neuroimaging and visual
field testing to localise the lesion along the visual pathway.

\medterm{Bioabsorbable Stent (Vascular)}-- See Bioabsorbable Stent.

\medterm{Bioavailability}-- The fraction of an administered drug that reaches the systemic circulation in its
unchanged active form. It is expressed as a percentage and is determined by the route of
administration and the extent of first-pass metabolism. For intravenous administration,
bioavailability is 100 percent by definition.

\medterm{Bioburden}-- The number and type of viable microorganisms contaminating a surface, medical device,
tissue, or pharmaceutical product; a measure of contamination. Bioburden testing
assesses the level of microbial contamination (total viable count) before sterilization
or in quality control.

\medterm{Biochemical Assessment}-- Blood tests: albumin, prealbumin, transferrin, electrolytes, vitamins, minerals,
glucose, lipids. Reflect nutritional status and metabolic function.

\medterm{Biocompatibility}-- Ability of material to function without adverse response. ISO 10993 standards for
biocompatibility testing.

\medterm{Biodegradable Implant}-- An implant designed to perform a temporary function and then degrade into products that
can be metabolized or eliminated by the body. Degradation rate, local tissue response,
and mechanical strength must be matched to the intended clinical use.

\medterm{Biodistribution}-- The time-dependent distribution of a radiopharmaceutical among organs, tissues, blood,
and excretory pathways. It determines image quality, target-to-background contrast,
therapeutic delivery, and radiation exposure to normal organs.

\medterm{Bioequivalence}-- The property of two drug products (brand-name and generic) that have the same rate and
extent of absorption when administered at the same molar dose under similar conditions,
resulting in comparable bioavailability and therapeutic effect.

\medterm{Bioethics}-- Interdisciplinary study of the ethical, legal, philosophical, and social implications of
advancements in medicine, biology, healthcare technology, and biotechnology.

\medterm{Bioethics (Geriatric)}-- Ethical considerations in elderly care: autonomy vs. paternalism, informed consent
capacity, end-of-life decisions, resource allocation, elder abuse. Principles: respect
for autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice. Advance directives: living will,
durable power of attorney for healthcare.

\medterm{Biofilm}-- An organized community of microorganisms attached to a surface and embedded in a
self-produced extracellular polymeric matrix (polysaccharides, proteins, DNA).

\medterm{Biofilm-associated infections}-- Infections caused by bacteria growing in biofilms on medical devices or tissues.
Biofilms are structured communities of bacteria embedded in extracellular polymeric
substances. Bacteria in biofilms are more resistant to antibiotics and host immune
responses.

\medterm{Biologic Therapy}-- Treatment with biologic agents targeting specific immune pathways, used in autoimmune
and inflammatory disease.

\medterm{Biologic Therapy in Dermatology}-- Treatment with targeted biological agents, usually monoclonal antibodies or receptor
proteins, that inhibit selected immune pathways. Biologics are used for diseases such as
psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, hidradenitis suppurativa, and urticaria; infection risk,
vaccination status, screening, and monitoring must be considered.

\medterm{Biologic Variation}-- Natural fluctuation in a measurable analyte within an individual over time and variation
between individuals. It should be considered when interpreting serial results and
deciding whether a change is clinically meaningful.

\medterm{Biological Half-Life}-- Time required for the human body to eliminate half the administered quantity of a drug
or radiopharmaceutical through metabolic and excretory processes. Different from
physical half-life (radioactive decay) and effective half-life (combined biological and
physical elimination). Formula: 1/T\1effective = 1/T\1physical + 1/T\1biological.

\medterm{Biomarker}-- Measurable indicator of disease. Cancer biomarkers: diagnostic, prognostic, predictive.

\medterm{Biomarker Testing}-- Analysis of DNA/RNA for mutations, fusions, expression. See molecular testing.

\medterm{Biomechanics}-- Study of movement mechanics. Gait analysis, joint forces, muscle activation. Applied to
rehabilitation device design.

\medterm{Biomolecule}-- Any organic molecule produced by living organisms, essential for structure, function,
and regulation of biological processes.

\medterm{Biomonitoring}-- Measurement of substances in body. Examples: blood glucose, drug levels, toxicants.

\medterm{Biophysical Profile}-- A non-stress test combined with ultrasound assessment of five fetal parameters, each
scored 0 or 2 for a total of 0-10. Parameters include non-stress test (fetal heart rate
reactivity), fetal breathing movements, fetal tone, fetal movement, and amniotic fluid
volume.

\medterm{Biophysical Profile (BPP)}-- Antepartum fetal assessment combining ultrasound and NST. Components (2 points each):
NST reactivity (reactive), fetal breathing movements (\(\)1 episode of 30 seconds in 30
minutes), fetal movement (\(\)3 discrete movements in 30 minutes), fetal tone (\(\)1
episode of extension/flexion), and amniotic fluid volume (AFI \(\)5 cm).

\medterm{Biopsy}-- Removal of tissue for histological examination to diagnose disease. Types: needle biopsy
(fine needle aspiration, core needle), excisional, incisional, punch, endoscopic. Sites:
breast, lung, liver, lymph node, bone marrow. Complications: bleeding, infection,
seeding (rare).

\medterm{Biopsy Needle}-- A needle designed to obtain cells or tissue for cytologic or histopathologic
examination. Fine-needle aspiration retrieves cells, whereas core biopsy obtains a
tissue cylinder that preserves architecture.

\medterm{Biosensor}-- A device that combines a biological recognition element with a physical or chemical
transducer to detect and measure an analyte. Medical examples include sensors for
glucose, pathogens, hormones, and cardiac biomarkers.

\medterm{Biosimilar}-- A biologic medicine highly similar to an approved reference biologic, with no clinically
meaningful differences in safety, purity, or potency for its approved uses. Because
biologics are complex, biosimilarity is established through a comparative evidence
program rather than simple chemical identity.

\medterm{Biot Breathing}-- An irregular breathing pattern with random periods of normal breathing interspersed with
apneic episodes, seen in brainstem lesions. It is less organized than Cheyne-Stokes
breathing and indicates medullary damage.

\medterm{Biotelemetry}-- The remote measurement and transmission of physiologic data from a person or animal.
Clinical applications include ambulatory cardiac rhythm monitoring, temperature
monitoring, and intensive-care observation.

\medterm{Bioterrorism Agents}-- Category A: anthrax (Bacillus anthracis), smallpox (variola), botulism (Clostridium
botulinum), plague (Yersinia pestis), tularemia (Francisella tularensis), viral
hemorrhagic fevers (Ebola, Marburg, Lassa, Machupo). Anthrax: inhalational (mediastinal
widening, hemorrhagic mediastinitis), cutaneous (eschar), GI.

\medterm{Biotinidase Deficiency}-- An autosomal recessive inborn error of biotin recycling caused by BTD gene mutations,
resulting in inability to cleave biotin from biocytin (proteolytic degradation product
of biotin-dependent carboxylases).

\medterm{Biotransformation}-- The process by which drugs and other xenobiotics are chemically modified by metabolic
enzymes, primarily in the liver, to facilitate elimination from the body.
Biotransformation occurs in two phases.

\medterm{Biotrauma}-- Inflammatory response to ventilator-induced lung injury with systemic cytokine release.
Can lead to multi-organ dysfunction. Minimized by lung-protective ventilation
strategies.

\medterm{BiPAP}-- Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure, a form of non-invasive ventilation delivering two
levels of pressure: IPAP (inspiratory) for ventilation and EPAP (expiratory) for
alveolar recruitment. It is particularly effective for COPD exacerbations with
hypercapnic respiratory failure. It reduces work of breathing and improves gas exchange.

\medterm{BiPAP Machine}-- Bi-level positive airway pressure. Two pressure settings.

\medterm{Biparietal Diameter (BPD)}-- The transverse diameter of the fetal skull measured at the level of the thalami and
cavum septum pellucidum. Used for dating (14-26 weeks, ±7-10 days) and assessing growth.
Normal growth: ~2-4 mm per week. BPD is less reliable after 26 weeks and in abnormal
fetal head shapes (dolichocephaly, brachycephaly).

\medterm{Bipolar Disorder}-- A mood disorder characterized by episodes of mania/hypomania and depression. Bipolar I:
at least one manic episode (elevated mood, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep,
pressured speech, increased goal-directed activity, risky behavior) lasting \(\)1 week
or requiring hospitalization.

\medterm{Bipolar I Disorder}-- A mood disorder defined by at least one manic episode lasting at least seven days or
requiring hospitalization. A manic episode is a distinct period of abnormally elevated,
expansive, or irritable mood and increased goal-directed activity or energy.

\medterm{Bipolar II Disorder}-- A mood disorder characterized by at least one hypomanic episode and at least one major
depressive episode, with no history of a full manic episode. The depressive episodes in
bipolar II tend to be more frequent and longer-lasting than in bipolar I, leading to
greater cumulative disability.

\medterm{Bird Fancier's Lung}-- HP from avian proteins in bird droppings or feathers. Common among pigeon breeders.
Chronic exposure leads to progressive fibrosis.

\medterm{Bird Flu}-- Influenza caused by avian-adapted influenza A viruses. Human infection is uncommon but
may cause severe respiratory disease, and public-health assessment depends on exposure
history, testing, and infection-control measures.

\medterm{Birt-Hogg-Dube Syndrome}-- Autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutations in the FLCN gene (17p11.2) encoding
folliculin.

\medterm{Birth Control}-- Contraception; see Contraception. Methods to prevent unintended pregnancy: hormonal
contraceptives (pill, patch, ring, injection, implant), intrauterine devices (copper,
levonorgestrel), barrier methods (condoms, diaphragm), emergency contraception,
fertility awareness, and sterilization.

\medterm{Birthmarks}-- Congenital or early-appearing marks on the skin, including vascular lesions such as
hemangiomas and port-wine stains and pigmented lesions such as nevi and café-au-lait
macules. Most are benign, but changes in appearance warrant assessment.

\medterm{Bisacodyl}-- A stimulant laxative (diphenylmethane derivative) that stimulates colonic motility and
enhances water/electrolyte secretion, producing a bowel movement within 6-12 hours
(oral) or 15-60 minutes (suppository). Used for constipation, bowel preparation, and
evacuation before procedures.

\medterm{Bishop Score}-- A pre-induction cervical scoring system that predicts the success of labor induction.
Five components: cervical dilatation (0-3), effacement (0-3), station (-3 to +2, scored
0-3), consistency (firm, medium, soft; scored 0-2), and position (posterior, mid,
anterior; scored 0-2). Total score: 0-13.

\medterm{Bismuth Salicylate}-- A compound combining bismuth subsalicylate with salicylic acid that provides
antimicrobial and antisecretory effects, used for diarrhea and gastric upset but capable
of causing salicylate toxicity in overdose.

\medterm{Bismuth Subsalicylate}-- An antidiarrheal and anti-ulcer agent (e.g., Pepto-Bismol) with antimicrobial,
anti-inflammatory, and coating actions. Used for traveler's diarrhea (prophylaxis and
treatment), dyspepsia, Helicobacter pylori eradication (quadruple therapy), and symptoms
of indigestion.

\medterm{Benzocaine}-- A topical local anesthetic used for short-term relief of pain or itching on the skin or in the
mouth and throat. It can rarely cause methemoglobinemia, especially in young children or after excessive
use; seek urgent care for blue or gray skin, shortness of breath, fatigue, or rapid heartbeat. Do not
use oral products in children younger than two years unless specifically directed by a clinician.

\medterm{Bisoprolol}-- A beta-1 selective blocker used to treat high blood pressure, angina, and some forms of heart
failure. It slows the heart rate and reduces cardiac workload. Dizziness, fatigue, slow pulse, and
worsening breathing symptoms may occur; it should generally be tapered rather than stopped abruptly.

\medterm{Bisphenol A (BPA)}-- Endocrine disruptor in plastics. Estrogenic effects, associated with reproductive
disorders, obesity, diabetes. Avoid BPA-containing products, especially for children.

\medterm{Bitemporal Hemianopia}-- Loss of vision in the temporal (lateral) half of both visual fields due to lesions that
cross the optic chiasm, damaging the decussating fibers from the nasal retina of each
eye (which carry temporal field information).

\medterm{Bitewing}-- A dental radiograph that records the crowns of the upper and lower teeth and the
adjacent alveolar bone in one image. It is particularly useful for detecting
interproximal caries and assessing crestal bone levels.

\medterm{Bitewing Radiograph}-- An intraoral radiograph showing the crowns of the maxillary and mandibular posterior
teeth and the alveolar crest. It is particularly useful for detecting interproximal
caries and assessing crestal bone levels.

\medterm{Bitot's Spots}-- Foamy, whitish, triangular patches on the conjunctiva, usually near the temporal limbus,
associated classically with vitamin A deficiency. They may accompany night blindness and
xerosis of the conjunctiva or cornea.

\medterm{BK Virus Nephropathy}-- Polyomavirus infection in transplant recipients. Viral cytopathic changes on biopsy.
Reduce immunosuppression as primary treatment. Can lead to graft loss. Monitor viral
loads in blood for early detection.

\medterm{Black Hairy Tongue}-- A benign elongation and dark discoloration of the filiform papillae on the dorsal tongue
caused by impaired desquamation and trapping of debris or pigment. Predisposing factors
include smoking, poor oral hygiene, dry mouth, and some exposures.

\medterm{Black Lung}-- Coal workers' pneumoconiosis caused by prolonged inhalation of coal-mine dust. It ranges
from simple anthracosis to progressive massive fibrosis and may cause cough,
breathlessness, and permanent lung impairment.

\medterm{Black Stain}-- Extrinsic staining along gumline. Associated with poor hygiene. Polishing removes.

\medterm{Black Triangle}-- Triangular space between teeth at gumline. Caused by papilla loss, tooth shape, bone
level. Treatment: orthodontics, restorative, papilla reconstruction.

\medterm{Blackheads}-- Open comedones formed when a hair follicle becomes blocked with sebum and keratin and
the surface material oxidizes. They are a common non-inflammatory lesion of acne,
especially on the face, chest, and back.

\medterm{Bladder Cancer}-- Urothelial carcinoma. Risk factors: smoking, occupational exposures.

\medterm{Bladder Carcinoma}-- Malignant neoplasm originating from the urothelium lining the urinary bladder, with
transition cell carcinoma (urothelial carcinoma) accounting for over 90 percent of
cases. Predominant risk factor: cigarette smoking (responsible for ~50\1 of cases) and
occupational exposure to aromatic amines or aniline dyes.

\medterm{Bladder diverticula}-- Outpouchings of the bladder mucosa through the muscular wall, often acquired from
chronic bladder outlet obstruction or congenital in origin. Acquired diverticula are
associated with benign prostatic hyperplasia and may cause urinary stasis, recurrent
infections, and stone formation.

\medterm{Bladder Diverticulum}-- Outpouching of bladder wall. Congenital or acquired (obstruction). Asymptomatic or
causes urinary stasis, stones, infection. Treatment: address obstruction,
diverticulectomy if symptomatic.

\medterm{Bladder Exstrophy}-- A rare congenital abdominal wall defect where the bladder is exposed on the outside of
the body due to failure of the lower abdominal wall and bladder to close during
embryonic development. Part of the exstrophy-epispadias complex. Associated with pubic
bone diastasis, external genital anomalies, and potential bladder dysfunction.

\medterm{Bladder Fistula}-- An abnormal connection between the bladder and another organ or the skin, most often the
vagina, bowel, or uterus. It may cause continuous urine leakage, recurrent infections, gas or stool in
the urine, or unusual vaginal wetness. Treatment depends on the cause and may require catheter drainage
and surgical repair.

\medterm{Bladder Health and Disorders}-- Bladder health involves the proper storage and voluntary release of urine through
coordinated function of the detrusor muscle and sphincters. Common disorders include
urinary tract infections, overactive bladder, stress incontinence, and interstitial
cystitis.

\medterm{Bladder Outlet Obstruction}-- Blockage of urine flow at the bladder neck or urethra, commonly caused by benign
prostatic enlargement, urethral stricture, stones, tumors, or pelvic-organ prolapse. It
may cause weak stream, hesitancy, retention, hydronephrosis, infection, and renal
impairment.

\medterm{Bladder Pain Syndrome}-- Chronic bladder-related pain, pressure, or discomfort associated with urinary symptoms
in the absence of infection or another identifiable cause. It is a diagnosis of
exclusion and is managed with multimodal, patient-centered treatment.

\medterm{Bladder Perforation}-- A tear or hole in the bladder wall caused by trauma, pelvic surgery, instrumentation,
severe inflammation, or obstruction. Urine may leak into surrounding tissues, causing abdominal pain,
distention, blood in the urine, or infection. It requires urgent medical assessment and may need catheter
drainage or surgical repair.

\medterm{Bladder Problems}-- A range of conditions affecting urine storage and voiding, including incontinence,
overactive bladder, urinary retention, and recurrent infections. Causes may include
neurological damage, pelvic floor weakness, prostate enlargement, or bladder outlet
obstruction.

\medterm{Bladder Spasm}-- An involuntary contraction of the bladder muscle that can cause sudden urgency, cramping,
urine leakage, or pain. It may occur with infection, catheter use, bladder irritation, neurological
conditions, or after surgery. Treatment addresses the cause and may include bladder training or medicines.

\medterm{Bladder Retraining}-- See urology.

\medterm{Bladder Stone}-- Calculus forming in bladder. Causes: obstruction, infection, foreign body, stasis.
Symptoms: dysuria, hematuria, interrupted stream. Treatment: removal, address underlying
cause.

\medterm{Bladder Stones}-- Calculi that form in or migrate into the urinary bladder. They may cause lower abdominal
pain, dysuria, urinary frequency, hematuria, recurrent infection, or intermittent
urinary obstruction.

\medterm{Blalock-Taussig Operation}-- A surgical systemic-to-pulmonary shunt that increases pulmonary blood flow in selected
cyanotic congenital heart disease. The modern modified procedure commonly uses a
prosthetic conduit rather than direct sacrifice of a subclavian artery.

\medterm{Blast Crisis}-- Aggressive, terminal phase of myeloid (chiefly CML) or, less commonly, lymphoid
malignancies in which immature blasts dominate the marrow and peripheral blood, behaving
like acute leukemia. CML blast crisis is defined by 20\1 blasts in peripheral blood or
bone marrow or by the presence of extramedullary blastic infiltrates (chloromas).

\medterm{Blast Injury}-- Tissue injury caused by an explosion, classified as primary injury from the pressure
wave, secondary injury from projectiles, tertiary injury from displacement or impact,
and quaternary injury from burns, inhalation, toxins, or crush. Multiple mechanisms may
coexist.

\medterm{Blastic plasmacytoid dendritic-cell neoplasm}-- A rare, aggressive hematologic malignancy derived from plasmacytoid dendritic cells. It
often involves skin, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and central nervous system, with a poor
prognosis.

\medterm{Blastocyst}-- An early stage in embryonic development, consisting of a hollow sphere of cells that
implants into the uterine wall.

\medterm{Blastoma}-- Generic descriptor for embryonal (primitive) malignant tumors arising from precursor
("blast") cells that fail to differentiate normally.

\medterm{Blastomyces dermatitidis}-- A dimorphic fungus that causes blastomycosis, a systemic pyogranulomatous infection
acquired by inhalation of conidia from soil in endemic regions such as the Ohio and
Mississippi River valleys. Cutaneous manifestations include verrucous or ulcerated
plaques, and pulmonary involvement is common.

\medterm{Blastomycosis}-- A fungal infection caused by Blastomyces dermatitidis, endemic to the Ohio and
Mississippi River valleys and Great Lakes region. Inhalation of conidia causes pulmonary
infection. Pulmonary: acute pneumonia (often misdiagnosed as bacterial), chronic
pneumonia with nodules and cavitation.

\medterm{Bleeding}-- The escape of blood from the vascular system, which may be external (visible hemorrhage
from a wound, orifice, or natural opening) or internal (bleeding into a body cavity,
organ, or tissue).

\medterm{Bleeding Disorders}-- A heterogeneous group of conditions characterized by abnormal hemostasis, resulting from
defects in blood vessels, platelets, or coagulation factors.

\medterm{Bleeding Esophageal Varices}-- Life-threatening hemorrhage from dilated submucosal veins in the lower esophagus,
occurring as a complication of portal hypertension, most commonly from cirrhosis.
Varices form when the portal venous pressure gradient exceeds 10 mmHg (the threshold for
variceal development).

\medterm{Bleeding into a thyroid nodule}-- Hemorrhage within a thyroid nodule, presenting with sudden onset of neck pain and rapid
enlargement of a pre-existing or new thyroid mass. It may mimic thyroid malignancy
clinically and on imaging. Ultrasound shows a complex cystic lesion with internal
echoes. Fine needle aspiration confirms the benign nature of the hemorrhagic cyst.

\medterm{Blennorrhea}-- A mucopurulent discharge from a mucous membrane, especially the urethra or conjunctiva.
The finding requires clinical assessment for infectious and noninfectious causes rather
than treatment based on the symptom alone.

\medterm{Blepharitis}-- Chronic inflammation of the eyelid margins, one of the most common ophthalmic
conditions, classified into anterior blepharitis (affecting the eyelash follicle and
base) and posterior blepharitis (affecting the meibomian glands).

\medterm{Blepharoconjunctivitis}-- Inflammation involving the eyelid margins and conjunctiva. It may cause crusting,
itching, redness, tearing, irritation, and recurrent symptoms associated with infection,
allergy, or eyelid-gland dysfunction.

\medterm{Blepharoplasty}-- Surgical reconstruction or reshaping of the eyelids, often involving removal or
repositioning of excess skin, muscle, or orbital fat. It may be functional, cosmetic, or
reconstructive.

\medterm{Blepharospasm}-- Involuntary, bilateral tonic-clonic spasms of the eyelid muscles causing forced eye
closure that can be functionally disabling. Most often essential (idiopathic) but can
follow ocular irritation or brainstem lesions; first-line treatment is local botulinum
toxin injection.

\medterm{Blind Loop Syndrome}-- Malabsorption syndrome due to stasis and bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) in a non-propulsive
("blind") segment of small bowel, classically caused by surgical bypass loops (Billroth
II, end-to-side anastomosis), strictures (Crohn disease, radiation, adhesions),
diverticulosis, or impaired motility (diabetes, scleroderma).

\medterm{Blinding}-- Concealing group assignment. Single-blind: participants. Double-blind: participants and
investigators. Triple-blind: participants, investigators, analysts.

\medterm{Blindness}-- Severe or complete loss of vision in one or both eyes, caused by disease or injury
affecting the cornea, lens, retina, optic nerve, visual pathways, or visual cortex. It
may be congenital or acquired, sudden or progressive, and reversible or permanent;
sudden visual loss is an ophthalmic emergency.

\medterm{Blood Alcohol Concentration}-- The concentration of ethanol in blood, commonly expressed as mass per volume or as a
percentage. Interpretation depends on sampling, timing, metabolism, tolerance,
co-ingestants, and local standards; clinical impairment cannot be inferred from a single
value without context.

\medterm{Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC)}-- Measure of ethanol in blood. Legal limit for driving: 0.08\1 (varies by jurisdiction).
Metabolism: zero-order (15-20 mg/dL/hour), liver breakdown. Units: g/dL or mg/dL.

\medterm{Blood Alcohol Level}-- Measurement of ethanol concentration in blood. Legal limit for driving: 0.08 g/dL (80
mg/dL) in most US states. Impaired: 0.04-0.08 g/dL. Severe intoxication: 0.15-0.30 g/dL.
Potentially fatal: >0.30 g/dL. With chronic alcoholism: marked tolerance (may appear
functional at 0.30+). Elimination rate: 15-25 mg/dL/hour.

\medterm{Blood Clot}-- A mass of coagulated blood within a blood vessel or body cavity. A clot may be
protective when it stops bleeding, but an abnormal thrombus can obstruct blood flow or
embolize to the lungs, brain, heart, or other organs.

\medterm{Blood Clots}-- Gel-like masses formed when blood coagulates inside a blood vessel or injured tissue.
Clots may stop bleeding but can cause deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, stroke,
heart attack, or arterial ischemia when they obstruct circulation.

\medterm{Blood Coagulation}-- The cascade of enzymatic reactions converting circulating fibrinogen into an insoluble
fibrin clot.

\medterm{Blood Coagulation Disorder}-- A disorder in which clot formation is deficient, excessive, or inappropriately
regulated, causing abnormal bleeding or thrombosis. Causes include inherited factor
deficiencies, platelet disorders, liver disease, vitamin deficiencies, immune
conditions, and acquired systemic disease.

\medterm{Blood Culture}-- Inoculation of blood into culture media to detect bacteremia or fungemia. Two sets (two
bottles each: aerobic + anaerobic) from separate venipuncture sites, 20-30 mL total.
Indications: fever of unknown origin, suspected sepsis, infective endocarditis, and
immunocompromised with fever.

\medterm{Blood Culture Contamination}-- Introduction of skin or environmental microorganisms into a blood-culture bottle during
collection or handling rather than true bloodstream infection. It can lead to
unnecessary antimicrobial treatment and should be assessed using the organism, number of
positive sets, time to positivity, and clinical findings.

\medterm{Blood Film (Peripheral Blood Smear)}-- A stained microscopic preparation of peripheral blood used to assess erythrocyte,
leukocyte, and platelet morphology and to detect abnormal cells, parasites, blasts, and
red-cell fragments.

\medterm{Blood Flow}-- The volume of blood passing through a vessel, organ, or the systemic circulation per
unit time, typically expressed in mL/min. Governed by the hemodynamic equivalent of
Ohm's law: flow = pressure gradient / resistance. Total cardiac output (~5 L/min at
rest) equals systemic blood flow.

\medterm{Blood Gas (Venous)}-- Sampling of venous blood (typically from a peripheral vein or central line) for pH,
PvCO2, HCO3, and base excess. Less painful than ABG, avoids arterial puncture risks.
Limitations: no PaO2 or SpO2 data; peripheral venous gases in shock may not reflect
arterial pH. VBG pH is typically 0.03-0.05 lower than ABG; PvCO2 is 3-8 mmHg higher.

\medterm{Blood Glucose Monitoring}-- Blood sugar monitoring involves measuring glucose levels in the blood to manage
diabetes, typically using a glucometer with finger-stick testing or continuous glucose
monitors that track interstitial glucose in real time.

\medterm{Blood Group}-- The classification of blood based on the presence or absence of specific antigens
(agglutinogens) on the surface of red blood cells. The ABO system has four groups: A (A
antigen), B (B antigen), AB (both A and B), and O (neither A nor B). The Rh system:
RhD-positive (85\1 of population) or RhD-negative (15\1).

\medterm{Blood Loss}-- The volume of blood lost during surgery or trauma, estimated by weighing swabs, suction
contents, and hemodynamic monitoring, guiding fluid and transfusion management.

\medterm{Blood Pressure}-- The force exerted by blood against arterial walls, measured in millimeters of mercury
(mmHg). Systolic pressure (peak during ventricular ejection, normally approximately 120
mmHg) and diastolic pressure (minimum during ventricular relaxation, normally
approximately 80 mmHg).

\medterm{Blood Products}-- Blood products used in anesthesia include packed red blood cells (increase
oxygen-carrying capacity, each unit raises hemoglobin by approximately 1 g/dL), fresh

\medterm{Blood Smear}-- Peripheral blood film: a thin blood smear on a slide stained (Wright/Giemsa) and
examined microscopically for cell morphology, aiding diagnosis.

\medterm{Blood Spatter Analysis}-- The forensic interpretation of bloodstain patterns to reconstruct events surrounding a
violent incident. Types: passive (drops, pools, flows), transfer (contact patterns), and
impact/expirated spatter.

\medterm{Blood Sugar Test}-- Measurement of blood glucose to detect or monitor diabetes and other disorders of
glucose regulation. Testing may be performed fasting, randomly, or after a glucose load,
using a laboratory sample or a point-of-care meter.

\medterm{Blood Transfusion}-- The administration of whole blood or blood components (packed red blood cells,
platelets, fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate) to restore or maintain adequate
circulating blood volume and oxygen-carrying capacity.

\medterm{Blood Urea Nitrogen}-- BUN: serum urea, the end product of protein catabolism, filtered and partially
reabsorbed by the tubules. Used with creatinine to assess renal function and volume
status.

\medterm{Blood Viscosity}-- The internal friction of blood that resists flow, determined primarily by hematocrit
(red cell concentration), with smaller contributions from plasma proteins and
temperature. Normal whole-blood viscosity is approximately 3-4 times that of water.

\medterm{Blood-Borne Disease}-- Disease transmitted via blood. Examples: HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C.

\medterm{Blood-Brain Barrier}-- The blood-brain barrier is a highly selective semipermeable membrane that separates the
circulating blood from the brain extracellular fluid, consisting of endothelial cells
connected by tight junctions, astrocyte foot processes, and a basement membrane.

\medterm{Bloom Syndrome}-- Rare autosomal recessive chromosomal instability syndrome caused by biallelic mutations
in BLM (15q26.1) encoding a RecQ helicase.

\medterm{Blount Disease}-- A growth disorder of the proximal tibia causing progressive bowing of the legs, usually
involving the medial tibial physis. It may occur in infants or adolescents and can lead
to gait abnormality, limb-length discrepancy, and early knee degeneration.

\medterm{Blue Baby}-- Generic term for an infant with cyanosis (bluish skin, lips, nail beds) from arterial
oxygen desaturation.

\medterm{Blue Bloater}-- A clinical phenotype of chronic bronchitis in COPD. Patients present with chronic cough,
sputum production, cyanosis, right heart failure, and airway-centric inflammation. They
tend to be younger, have more hypercapnia, and less emphysema on imaging.

\medterm{Blunt Abdominal Trauma}-- Trauma to the abdomen without penetrating injury. Mechanisms: motor vehicle collisions
(most common), falls, assaults. Commonly injured organs: spleen (most common, left upper
quadrant), liver (right upper quadrant), kidneys (flank), bowel (seatbelt sign, delayed
perforation), and pancreas (epigastric).

\medterm{Blunt Force Trauma}-- Injury produced when a blunt object strikes the body, or the body strikes a blunt
surface, without a sharp edge penetrating the skin. Patterns include abrasions,
contusions, lacerations with tissue bridging, and fractures; patterned injuries may
reproduce the impacting object.

\medterm{BMI (Body Mass Index)}-- Weight in kg divided by height in meters squared. Screening tool for weight categories:
underweight (<18.5), normal (18.5-24.9), overweight (25-29.9), obese (>= 30).
Limitations: does not distinguish muscle from fat, may misclassify athletes, elderly,
certain ethnic groups.

\medterm{BNP}-- B-type natriuretic peptide. Marker of heart failure. Released from ventricles in
response to stretch.

\medterm{Bochdalek Hernia}-- A congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) occurring through the posterolateral foramen of
Bochdalek, resulting from failure of the pleuroperitoneal membrane to close by the 8th
week of gestation. The left side is affected in 85\1 of cases (the right
pleuroperitoneal canal closes earlier).

\medterm{Body Dysmorphic Disorder}-- Preoccupation with one or more perceived defects or flaws in physical appearance that
are not observable or appear slight to others. The individual performs repetitive
behaviors in response to appearance concerns such as mirror checking, excessive
grooming, skin picking, reassurance seeking, and comparing.

\medterm{Body Identification}-- The process of establishing the identity of a deceased person, essential in forensic and
mass-disaster contexts.

\medterm{Body Lice}-- Infestation of clothing and skin by the human body louse, causing intense itching,
excoriations, and sometimes secondary bacterial infection. Treatment requires hygiene,
laundering or heat treatment of clothing and bedding, and appropriate antiparasitic
measures.

\medterm{Body Plethysmography}-- Technique for measuring lung volumes (TLC, RV, FRC) using a sealed chamber. Gold
standard for TLC measurement. Useful for diagnosing restriction and hyperinflation.

\medterm{Body Ringworm}-- Dermatophyte infection of the glabrous skin, also called tinea corporis. It typically
produces an expanding annular scaly lesion with a more active border and central
clearing.

\medterm{Body Temperature}-- The balance between heat production (metabolism, muscle activity) and heat loss
(radiation, conduction, convection, evaporation). See Temperature (Body Temperature).

\medterm{Boeck's Sarcoid}-- An older eponym for sarcoidosis, a multisystem granulomatous disease characterized by
noncaseating granulomas and commonly involving the lungs, intrathoracic lymph nodes,
skin, or eyes.

\medterm{Boerhaave Syndrome}-- Transmural spontaneous rupture of the esophagus typically occurring after forceful
vomiting in the setting of a full stomach. Most commonly involves the left
posterolateral wall of the distal esophagus. Presents with severe chest pain,
subcutaneous emphysema, mediastinal emphysema, and rapidly progressive sepsis.

\medterm{Bohn Nodules}-- Small white cysts on gingiva of newborns. Epithelial remnants. Resolve spontaneously.

\medterm{Bohr Effect}-- The decrease in hemoglobin's oxygen affinity (right shift of the dissociation curve)
caused by decreased pH (increased H+) and increased PCO2. In metabolically active
tissues, CO2 production and lactic acid accumulation lower the local pH, causing
hemoglobin to release more oxygen precisely where it is needed most.

\medterm{Boil}-- Furuncle: an acute, deep infection of a hair follicle (folliculitis that extends to the
dermis) by Staphylococcus aureus, forming a painful, red, swollen nodule with central
purulent core that may spontaneously drain.

\medterm{Boil (Furuncle)}-- A painful, deep-seated infection of a hair follicle caused most commonly by
Staphylococcus aureus, extending into the surrounding subcutaneous tissue. Presents as a
tender, erythematous, fluctuant nodule that eventually discharges pus. When multiple
adjacent follicles coalesce, it forms a carbuncle, often with systemic symptoms.

\medterm{Boils}-- Painful, pus-filled infections of a hair follicle and surrounding skin, usually caused
by Staphylococcus aureus. Recurrent or large boils may require incision, drainage,
culture, and evaluation for risk factors such as diabetes or household transmission.

\medterm{Bone}-- A rigid, mineralised connective tissue that forms the skeleton, providing structural
support, protection of vital organs, hematopoiesis (bone marrow), and mineral storage
(calcium, phosphate). Bone composition: organic matrix (90\1 type I collagen) and
inorganic mineral (hydroxyapatite, calcium phosphate).

\medterm{Bone Cement (Polymethylmethacrylate—PMMA)}-- Acrylic polymer used to fix prosthetic joints to bone. Mixes as dough, cures
exothermically. Used in hip/knee arthroplasty, vertebroplasty, cranial reconstruction.
Complications: cement implantation syndrome (hypotension, hypoxia), infection,
loosening.

\medterm{Bone Densitometry}-- Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry measures bone mineral density at the lumbar spine and
hip. Results are expressed as T-score comparing to young adult mean and Z-score
comparing to age-matched controls. T-score of minus 1.0 to minus 2.5 indicates
osteopenia. T-score of minus 2.5 or less indicates osteoporosis.

\medterm{Bone Density}-- Measurement of bone mineral content (BMC) and density (BMD). Assessed by DEXA scan (gold
standard). T-score: compares to young adult reference. Normal: >-1.0; osteopenia: -1.0
to -2.5; osteoporosis: <-2.5. Fracture risk increases with decreasing BMD. Screening:
women >65, men >70, or younger with risk factors.

\medterm{Bone Density Screening}-- Systemic skeletal disease characterized by low bone mass and microarchitectural
deterioration. See osteoporosis.

\medterm{Bone Graft}-- Bone tissue or a bone-substitute material used to fill a defect, promote fusion, support
reconstruction, or enhance fracture healing. Autograft provides osteogenic cells and
biologic signals; allograft and synthetic substitutes provide variable osteoconductive
and osteoinductive properties.

\medterm{Bone Graft (Dental)}-- Material placed to augment alveolar bone. Types: autograft, allograft, xenograft,
alloplast. Used for implants, ridge preservation.

\medterm{Bone Health}-- Systemic skeletal disease characterized by low bone mass and microarchitectural
deterioration. See osteoporosis.

\medterm{Bone Infection}-- See Osteomyelitis.

\medterm{Bone Marrow}-- Vascular tissue within the medullary cavities and cancellous bone. Red marrow produces
blood cells, whereas yellow marrow primarily stores fat and can revert to red marrow
when demand increases.

\medterm{Bone Marrow Aspiration}-- A procedure that withdraws liquid marrow, usually from the posterior iliac crest, for
cytologic, flow-cytometric, cytogenetic, molecular, and microbiologic evaluation. It
complements the core biopsy, which assesses marrow architecture.

\medterm{Bone Marrow Aspiration and Biopsy}-- Sampling of bone marrow, typically from the posterior iliac crest. Aspiration: liquid
marrow for cytology (differential, morphology), flow cytometry, cytogenetics, and MRD.
Biopsy: core of bone for cellularity, architecture, fibrosis, and infiltrative disease
(lymphoma, metastatic cancer, granulomas).

\medterm{Bone Marrow Biopsy}-- A procedure sampling bone marrow (typically posterior iliac crest) via aspiration
(cytology, flow cytometry, cultures) and trephine core biopsy (histology, architecture,
fibrosis, cellularity).

\medterm{Bone Marrow Depression}-- See myelosuppression.

\medterm{Bone marrow hyperplasia}-- Increased cellularity of the bone marrow resulting from accelerated hematopoiesis,
typically compensating for chronic hemolytic anemia, hemorrhage, or systemic hypoxia.

\medterm{Bone marrow necrosis}-- Death of hematopoietic and stromal cells within the bone marrow, often associated with
sickle cell crisis, malignancy, or infections, presenting with severe bone pain and
cytopenias.

\medterm{Bone Metastases}-- Bone metastases: most common from breast, prostate, lung, kidney, thyroid. Solitary vs
multiple. Clinical: pain (worse at night), pathologic fracture, hypercalcemia, spinal
cord compression.

\medterm{Bone Metastasis}-- Common in breast, prostate, lung, thyroid, kidney. Pain, fractures, hypercalcemia.
Treatment: radiation, bisphosphonates, surgery.

\medterm{Bone Scan}-- Skeletal imaging using Tc-99m MDP. Detects osteoblastic activity. Sensitivity: high;
specificity: low. Used for metastasis detection, fracture, infection, metabolic bone
disease. "Hot spots" indicate increased uptake; "cold spots" indicate decreased uptake.

\medterm{Bone Scan (Nuclear Scintigraphy)}-- Nuclear medicine imaging using Tc-99m MDP (methylene diphosphonate) that accumulates in
areas of increased bone turnover. Three phases: perfusion (immediate), blood pool (5
min), and delayed (2-3 hours).

\medterm{Bone Turnover Markers}-- Biochemical markers reflecting the rate of bone remodeling. Formation markers include
bone-specific alkaline phosphatase, osteocalcin, and N-terminal and C-terminal
propeptides of type I procollagen. Resorption markers include C-terminal and N-terminal
telopeptides of type I collagen and deoxypyridinoline.

\medterm{Bones and Joints}-- Bones and joints form the skeletal system, providing structural support, protection of
organs, and enabling movement through articulations between bones. Joints are classified
as fibrous, cartilaginous, or synovial, with synovial joints offering the greatest range
of motion.

\medterm{Bony Exostosis}-- A benign localized bony protuberance, including torus palatinus of the palate and torus
mandibularis of the mandible. Treatment is usually unnecessary unless the lesion causes
trauma, interferes with speech or prosthesis, or requires diagnostic clarification.

\medterm{Borderline Personality Disorder}-- A pattern of instability in interpersonal relationships, self-image, and affects, with
marked impulsivity.

\medterm{Bordetella pertussis}-- A Gram-negative coccobacillus and the causative agent of whooping cough, a highly
contagious respiratory infection. It produces pertussis toxin, which inhibits G-protein
signaling in respiratory epithelial cells, and tracheal cytotoxin, which damages
ciliated epithelium.

\medterm{Bornholm Disease}-- An acute epidemic pleurodynia caused most often by coxsackieviruses. It produces sudden
attacks of severe chest or upper-abdominal muscle pain, fever, and sometimes headache or
vomiting.

\medterm{Borrelia}-- A genus of spiral-shaped bacteria (spirochetes) transmitted by arthropod vectors, most
notably ticks and lice. The major human pathogen is Borrelia burgdorferi sensu lato, the
causative agent of Lyme disease, transmitted by Ixodes ticks.

\medterm{Borrelia burgdorferi}-- See Lyme Disease.

\medterm{Borreliosis}-- Infection caused by bacteria of the genus Borrelia. Human disease includes Lyme
borreliosis and relapsing fever, with manifestations varying according to species,
stage, and organ involvement.

\medterm{Bosniak Classification}-- System classifying renal cysts by CT features. Categories I-II benign, IIF follow-up,
III-IV likely malignant.

\medterm{Bot fly}-- A parasitic fly of the family Oestridae whose larvae cause myiasis in humans and
animals. Dermatobia hominis, the human bot fly, deposits eggs on blood-sucking insects;
larvae penetrate the skin and develop subcutaneously, causing a painful boil-like lesion
with a central breathing pore. Treatment is manual extraction of the larva.

\medterm{Botryomycosis}-- A chronic bacterial infection characterized by granule-forming lesions that can resemble
a fungal infection. It most often affects skin and subcutaneous tissue but may involve
viscera in susceptible patients.

\medterm{Botulism}-- A rare, potentially fatal neuroparalytic disease caused by botulinum toxin, a potent
presynaptic neurotoxin produced by Clostridium botulinum, an anaerobic, spore-forming,
gram-positive bacillus.

\medterm{Bouchard's Node}-- A hard, palpable enlargement of the proximal interphalangeal joint caused by
osteoarthritis. It reflects osteophyte formation and may be associated with pain,
stiffness, and reduced finger motion.

\medterm{Bougienage}-- The use of a bougie to dilate or explore a narrowed tubular structure, such as the
esophagus or urethra. It is a procedure that requires assessment of the cause and risks
of the stricture.

\medterm{Boutonneuse Fever}-- A tick-borne rickettsial infection, usually caused by Rickettsia conorii, characterized
by fever, headache, rash, and often a dark inoculation eschar called a tache noire.

\medterm{Bouveret Syndrome}-- Rare variant of gallstone ileus where a large gallstone erodes through a
cholecystoduodenal fistula and impacts in the duodenum or pylorus causing gastric outlet
obstruction. Represents approximately two to three percent of gallstone ileus cases.
Presents with nausea, vomiting, epigastric pain, and anorexia.

\medterm{Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy}-- A fatal transmissible prion disease of cattle causing progressive neurologic
degeneration. It is related to variant Creutzfeldt--Jakob disease in humans through
exposure to contaminated tissue.

\medterm{Bowel Incontinence}-- The involuntary loss of solid or liquid stool through the anus, also known as fecal
incontinence, affecting approximately 2-15 percent of the general population and
increasing in prevalence with age.

\medterm{Bowel infarction}-- Ischemic necrosis of bowel tissue from occlusive or non-occlusive mesenteric ischemia,
presenting with severe abdominal pain out of proportion to examination, bloody stools,
and peritoneal signs. Causes include mesenteric arterial embolism or thrombosis, venous
thrombosis, and low-flow states.

\medterm{Bowel ischemia}-- Reduced blood flow to the intestines causing mucosal injury, most commonly from
mesenteric atherosclerosis, low-flow states, or mesenteric venous thrombosis. Presents
with abdominal pain, bloody stools, and elevated lactate. Severe cases progress to
transmural necrosis.

\medterm{Bowel Obstruction}-- Blockage of intestinal lumen. X-ray, CT diagnosis. Differentiate small vs. large bowel
obstruction. Mechanical vs. paralytic ileus.

\medterm{Bowel Program}-- See gastroenterology.

\medterm{Bowenoid papulosis}-- A low-grade squamous intraepithelial neoplasia of the anogenital skin caused by human
papillomavirus infection, typically types 16 and 18, presenting as multiple pigmented
papules on the genital mucosa. Histologically it resembles squamous cell carcinoma in
situ but follows a benign clinical course.

\medterm{Bowlegs}-- Outward bowing of the legs causing the knees to remain apart when the feet and ankles
are together, also called genu varum. It is common in early childhood but requires
assessment when severe, asymmetric, painful, or persistent beyond the expected age.

\medterm{Bowman Capsule}-- Double-walled epithelial cup surrounding the glomerular capillaries forming the
beginning of the nephron. The visceral layer is composed of podocytes whose foot
processes interdigitate to form filtration slits. The parietal layer is composed of
simple squamous epithelium.

\medterm{Bowman's Gland}-- A serous gland in the olfactory mucosa whose secretion helps dissolve odorant molecules
and cleanse the olfactory surface. It is located in the lamina propria beneath the
olfactory epithelium.

\medterm{Boxed Warning}-- The most prominent safety warning required in prescribing information for a medicine
when there is a serious or life-threatening risk. It communicates major hazards and does
not necessarily mean that the medication is never appropriate.

\medterm{BPH}-- Abbreviation for Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia.

\medterm{Brachial Artery}-- The continuation of the axillary artery, running from the lower border of teres major to
the cubital fossa, where it divides into radial and ulnar arteries. Lies medial to the
biceps; palpated for blood pressure and brachial pulse. Branches: profunda brachii (deep
brachial), superior/inferior ulnar collaterals.

\medterm{Brachial Plexopathy}-- Dysfunction of the brachial plexus causing pain, weakness, sensory loss, or paralysis in
the shoulder and upper limb. Causes include trauma, compression, tumors, inflammation,
and radiation injury.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus}-- Nerve network (C5-T1) formed by roots, trunks, divisions, and cords, giving rise to
upper limb nerves. Roots unite into superior/middle/inferior trunks, dividing into
anterior/posterior divisions, recombining into lateral/posterior/medial cords. Major
branches: musculocutaneous, axillary, radial, median, ulnar.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Cords}-- Formed by divisions recombining behind the pectoralis minor. Lateral cord (anterior
divisions of upper/middle trunks), posterior cord (all three posterior divisions),
medial cord (anterior division of lower trunk). Named for relationship to the axillary
artery. Lateral cord: lateral pectoral nerve, contributes to median nerve.

adducted, elbow extended, forearm pronated). Klumpke's palsy (C8-T1) affects the hand

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Roots}-- Ventral rami of C5-T1 emerging from intervertebral foramina, passing between anterior
and middle scalene muscles (interscalene triangle). C5-C6 form the upper trunk, C7
continues as middle trunk, C8-T1 form the lower trunk. Root injuries cause specific
dermatome/myotome deficits. C5 avulsion causes loss of deltoid/biceps function.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Trunks}-- Formed in the posterior neck triangle. Upper (C5-C6), middle (C7), and lower (C8-T1)
trunks each divide into anterior/posterior divisions behind the clavicle. Upper trunk is
most commonly injured in birth injuries (Erb-Duchenne palsy), causing waiter's tip
position.

\medterm{Brachialis Muscle}-- Flat strong muscle deep to the biceps, from distal anterior humerus to ulnar tuberosity
and coronoid process. Innervated by musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C6). Primary elbow flexor
regardless of forearm position ("workhorse" of elbow flexion). Less visible than biceps
but significantly contributes to arm strength.

\medterm{Brachiocephalic Trunk}-- The first and largest branch of the aortic arch. It ascends to the right
sternoclavicular joint, where it divides into the right common carotid and right
subclavian arteries.

\medterm{Brachytherapy}-- Internal radiation therapy. Sources placed inside or near tumor. Used for prostate,
cervical, breast cancer.

\medterm{Brachytherapy Seed}-- Radioactive source implanted directly into tumor. I-125 or Pd-103 seeds for prostate
cancer. Permanent or temporary implantation.

\medterm{Bradycardia}-- A heart rate <60 beats per minute. May be physiologic (athletes) or pathologic due to
sinus node dysfunction, AV block, or medication effects. Symptomatic bradycardia causes
fatigue, dizziness, syncope, or heart failure. Evaluation includes ECG, Holter
monitoring, and assessment of reversible causes.

\medterm{Bradycardia of Prematurity}-- Heart rate less than 100 beats per minute in a premature infant, often associated with
apnea and oxygen desaturation. May be caused by vagal stimulation, reflux, or immaturity
of the cardiac conduction system. Treatment: gentle stimulation, caffeine, and
addressing underlying causes.

\medterm{Bradykinesia}-- Slowness and reduced amplitude of voluntary movement, often accompanied by difficulty
initiating or sustaining repetitive actions. It is a cardinal feature of parkinsonism
and may be associated with rigidity, resting tremor, postural instability, reduced arm
swing, masked facial expression, and a shuffling gait.

\medterm{Bradypnea}-- Abnormally slow respiratory rate (adult <12 breaths/min). Causes:
opioid/benzodiazepine/sedative overdose, central nervous system depression (stroke,
trauma, increased ICP), hypothyroidism, hypothermia, and metabolic (e.g., hypokalemia).
May lead to hypoventilation, hypercapnia, and respiratory failure.

\medterm{Brain}-- The central organ of the nervous system, enclosed in the cranial cavity and composed of
the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem. The brain receives, processes, and integrates
sensory information; initiates and coordinates motor responses; and serves as the seat
of cognition, emotion, memory, and consciousness.

\medterm{Brain Abscess}-- A focal, purulent infection within the brain parenchyma, most commonly occurring in the
frontal or temporal lobes.

\medterm{Brain Concussion}-- A mild traumatic brain injury caused by a blow, jolt, or force that temporarily changes
brain function. Symptoms may include headache, dizziness, confusion, memory difficulty, nausea, and
sensitivity to light or noise, sometimes without loss of consciousness. Worsening headache, repeated
vomiting, seizure, weakness, or increasing drowsiness requires urgent assessment.

\medterm{Brain Death}-- The irreversible cessation of all brain and brainstem function, including consciousness
and brainstem reflexes, with absent respiratory drive—a legal definition of death.

\medterm{Brain Edema}-- Swelling caused by excess fluid in brain tissue, which can raise pressure inside the skull.
Causes include head injury, stroke, infection, tumor, bleeding, and severe metabolic disturbances.
Symptoms may include headache, vomiting, confusion, seizures, weakness, or reduced consciousness; severe
cases are medical emergencies.

\medterm{Brain Health}-- Brain health encompasses the ability to perform cognitive functions such as memory,
learning, decision-making, and emotional regulation across the lifespan. Key factors
include cardiovascular health, nutrition, sleep, social engagement, cognitive
stimulation, and management of chronic conditions like hypertension and diabetes.

\medterm{Brain Herniation}-- Displacement of brain tissue across rigid intracranial compartments or through the
foramen magnum due to mass effect or raised ICP. It can compress the brainstem and
cranial nerves and is a life-threatening neurosurgical emergency.

\medterm{Brain Injuries}-- Brain injuries range from mild concussions to severe traumatic brain injury caused by
falls, motor vehicle accidents, sports impacts, or penetrating trauma. Symptoms depend
on injury location and severity, including headache, confusion, memory loss, seizures,
and altered consciousness.

\medterm{Brain Injury}-- See Brain Injuries.

\medterm{Brain Metastases}-- The most common intracranial neoplasms, occurring in approximately ten to forty percent
of patients with systemic cancer, and are more common than primary brain tumors.

\medterm{Brain Metastasis}-- Common in lung, breast, melanoma. Symptoms: headache, seizures, focal deficits.
Treatment: surgery, radiation, stereotactic radiosurgery.

\medterm{Brain Monitoring}-- Brain monitoring during anesthesia assesses the depth of hypnosis and neurological
function. Bispectral index (BIS) processes the frontal EEG into a number from 0
(isoelectric) to 100 (fully awake), with 40 to 60 recommended for general anesthesia.
BIS monitoring reduces awareness risk and may reduce anesthetic requirements.

\medterm{Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)}-- A cardiac neurohormone released from the ventricles in response to volume overload and
wall stress. BNP <100 pg/mL makes heart failure unlikely; >400 pg/mL suggests heart
failure. NT-proBNP: <125 pg/mL (<75 years) or <450 pg/mL (>75 years) excluding HF. Also
prognostic: rising levels indicate worsening HF and higher mortality.

\medterm{Brain SPECT}-- Cerebral perfusion imaging using Tc-99m HMPAO or ECD. Used for epilepsy localization,
dementia differentiation, cerebrovascular disease.

\medterm{Brain Tumor}-- An abnormal growth of cells within the brain or its surrounding structures, classified
as primary (originating from brain tissue or its coverings) or secondary/metastatic
(spread from a cancer elsewhere in the body, which are more common, accounting for
approximately 50 percent of all brain tumors).

\medterm{Brain Ventricles}-- The four interconnected fluid-filled cavities within the brain that produce and contain
cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

\medterm{Brainstem}-- Connects cerebrum to spinal cord, with midbrain (superior), pons (middle), and medulla
oblongata (inferior). Contains cranial nerve nuclei (III-XII), ascending/descending
tracts, and vital autonomic centers for cardiac, respiratory, and vasomotor function.
Reticular formation regulates consciousness and sleep-wake cycles.

\medterm{Branchial Arch}-- One of the paired embryonic arches that contribute to development of facial, pharyngeal,
laryngeal, muscular, skeletal, vascular, and neural structures of the head and neck.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft}-- An embryonic groove between adjacent branchial arches. Persistence of cleft remnants can
produce congenital branchial cysts, sinuses, or fistulas, most commonly along the
lateral neck.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft Anomaly}-- A congenital remnant of the branchial apparatus presenting as a cyst, sinus, or fistula
in the head and neck. Recurrent infection or drainage from a persistent opening warrants
imaging and specialist management.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft Cyst}-- Congenital cystic mass arising from incomplete obliteration of branchial cleft apparatus
during embryogenesis.

\medterm{Braxton Hicks Contractions}-- Irregular, painless uterine contractions occurring throughout pregnancy, becoming more
frequent in the third trimester. Usually last 30-60 seconds and are felt as a tightening
of the abdomen. Distinguished from true labour contractions by their irregularity, lack
of cervical change, and resolution with rest or hydration.

\medterm{BRCA}-- BReast CAncer gene: BRCA1 and BRCA2 are tumor suppressor genes involved in DNA
double-strand break repair via homologous recombination. Mutations in these genes confer
a significantly increased lifetime risk of breast cancer (up to 70-80\1 for BRCA1,
40-70\1 for BRCA2) and ovarian cancer (up to 40-50\1 for BRCA1, 10-20\1 for BRCA2).

\medterm{BRCA1 and BRCA2}-- Tumor-suppressor genes involved in homologous-recombination DNA repair. Germline
pathogenic variants increase susceptibility to breast, ovarian, fallopian-tube, primary
peritoneal, prostate, and pancreatic cancers and may guide surveillance and treatment.

\medterm{Breakbone Fever}-- A traditional name for dengue fever, reflecting the severe myalgia and arthralgia that
may accompany the acute viral illness.

\medterm{Breast Abscess}-- A localized collection of pus within the breast tissue, most commonly a complication of
lactational mastitis. Presents as a fluctuant, painful mass with overlying erythema and
systemic symptoms. Diagnosis by ultrasound. Treatment requires incision and drainage
plus antibiotics.

\medterm{Breast Cancer}-- Most common cancer in women. Risk factors: age, genetics, hormones, lifestyle.
Screening: mammography. Treatment: surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, hormone therapy,
targeted therapy.

\medterm{Breast Cancer Genetics}-- Hereditary breast cancer: BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations (lifetime risk 45-85\1). Other genes:
TP53 (Li-Fraumeni), PTEN (Cowden), PALB2, CHEK2. Genetic counseling and testing
indicated for early onset, bilateral, male breast cancer, Ashkenazi Jewish heritage.

\medterm{Breast Engorgement}-- Overfilling of the breasts with milk, causing bilateral pain, firmness, warmth, and
nipple flattening. Occurs 3-5 days postpartum. Pathophysiology: vascular congestion and
edema before milk secretion is established.

\medterm{Breast Health}-- Breast health involves routine self-awareness, clinical examinations, and mammographic
screening to detect abnormalities such as lumps, pain, or skin changes that may indicate
benign conditions or breast cancer. Risk factors include genetics, age, hormone
exposure, and lifestyle factors.

\medterm{Breast Infection}-- Infection and inflammation of breast tissue, most commonly mastitis during
breastfeeding. It causes localized pain, warmth, redness, swelling, and sometimes fever;
an untreated infection may progress to a breast abscess.

\medterm{Breast Lump}-- A localized thickening, mass, or swelling in breast tissue caused by a cyst,
fibroadenoma, infection, trauma, or malignancy. New, persistent, hard, fixed, or
changing lumps require clinical and often imaging assessment.

\medterm{Breast MRI}-- Magnetic resonance imaging of the breast with IV gadolinium contrast.

\medterm{Breast Pain}-- Pain or tenderness in one or both breasts, caused by hormonal changes, cysts, infection,
trauma, medications, or musculoskeletal disease. Persistent focal pain or an associated
lump requires assessment.

\medterm{Breast Surgery}-- Breast cancer surgery: breast-conserving therapy (BCT: lumpectomy + radiation)
equivalent survival to mastectomy for early stage. Margins: "no ink on tumor" for
invasive cancer; 2mm for DCIS.

\medterm{Breast Ultrasound}-- Ultrasound imaging of the breast. Indications: evaluating palpable masses
(distinguishing solid vs. cystic), characterizing mammographic abnormalities, guiding
biopsy (core needle, fine needle, or vacuum-assisted), evaluating implant integrity, and
imaging young/dense breast tissue.

\medterm{Breastfeeding}-- The feeding of an infant with breast milk, recommended exclusively for the first 6
months and continued with complementary foods to 2 years and beyond. Benefits: infant
(nutrition, immune protection—IgA, reduced infection/allergy/obesity, bonding) and
maternal (uterine involution, contraception, reduced breast/ovarian cancer).

\medterm{Breath Sounds}-- The sounds produced by airflow through the respiratory tract during breathing, assessed
by auscultation with a stethoscope over the chest wall.

\medterm{Breath Stacking}-- A technique using a manual resuscitation bag or glossopharyngeal breathing to deliver
successive breaths without exhaling, stacking tidal volumes to achieve higher lung
volumes. It improves cough effectiveness and secretion clearance in patients with weak
respiratory muscles.

\medterm{Breath-Holding Spells}-- A benign paroxysmal phenomenon in young children (6 months to 6 years), provoked by
frustration, pain, or fear. Cyanotic type (more common): child cries, forcefully
exhales, becomes cyanotic, and may lose consciousness briefly.

\medterm{Breech Delivery}-- Vaginal delivery of a breech presentation. Types: assisted breech (the baby delivers
spontaneously to the umbilicus, then the operator assists delivery of the shoulders and
head) and total breech extraction (the operator delivers the entire baby by grasping the
feet and pulling).

\medterm{Breech Presentation}-- A fetal presentation where the buttocks or feet are positioned to deliver first. Types
include frank breech (hips flexed, knees extended, most common), complete breech (hips
and knees flexed), and footling breech (one or both feet presenting). Associated with
increased risk of cord prolapse, head entrapment, and birth trauma.

\medterm{Bremsstrahlung}-- Braking radiation produced when beta particles decelerate in matter. Continuous
spectrum. Detected in I-131 imaging (poor quality). Used for Y-90 bremsstrahlung SPECT.

\medterm{Brenner Tumor}-- Rare ovarian epithelial neoplasm (1-2\1 of ovarian tumors) composed of nests of
transitional (urothelial-like) epithelial cells embedded in a dense fibromatous stroma.
Most are unilateral, small (< 2 cm), benign (90\1) and incidental; borderline
(proliferative) and malignant variants are uncommon.

\medterm{Breslow Thickness}-- Microstaging measurement of melanoma invasion depth from the top of the granular layer
of the epidermis to the deepest point of tumor invasion, in millimeters. Most important
prognostic factor in primary cutaneous melanoma, used in the AJCC TNM staging system (T
category).

\medterm{Bridge (Dental)}-- Fixed prosthetic replacing missing teeth. Retained by adjacent teeth (abutments).
Requires preparation of abutments.

\medterm{Brief Psychotic Disorder}-- A disturbance characterized by the sudden onset of at least one psychotic symptom:

behavior. The disturbance lasts at least one day but less than one month, after which
the individual fully returns to premorbid functioning.

\medterm{Brief Resolved Unexplained Event (BRUE)}-- A sudden, brief, and now-resolved episode in an infant involving abnormal breathing,
skin color, muscle tone, or responsiveness, with no explanation after the initial
evaluation. Risk assessment is based on age, history, examination findings, and
recurrence; higher-risk events require targeted investigation.

diagnosis specifies the underlying renal lesion.

prowazekii infection years after the original illness. It is usually milder than the
initial infection but can transmit to body lice.

\medterm{Broca Aphasia}-- Acquired language disorder of expressive speech (motor aphasia) caused by lesion of the

\medterm{Broca's Aphasia}-- A nonfluent language disorder caused by dysfunction of the dominant inferior frontal
language region. Speech is effortful and reduced, while comprehension is relatively
preserved compared with repetition and fluent language production.

production, language formulation, and aspects of syntax. Lesions may cause expressive
aphasia.

\medterm{Broken Heart Syndrome}-- See Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy.

\medterm{Bromhidrosis}-- Excessive or unpleasant body odor produced when microbial metabolism acts on sweat or
skin secretions. It is often prominent in the axillae or feet and may be associated with
hyperhidrosis or local skin disease.

\medterm{Bromocriptine}-- An ergot dopamine agonist (D2) used for hyperprolactinemia (prolactinomas,
galactorrhea), acromegaly (adjunct), Parkinson disease (less common now), and postpartum
breast engorgement (historical). Also used for neuroleptic malignant syndrome.

\medterm{Bromoderma}-- A skin eruption or dermatitis attributed to exposure to bromine or bromide compounds. It
may present with acneiform, pustular, or other inflammatory lesions.

\medterm{Bronchi}-- The major airways conducting air from the trachea into the lungs. The trachea bifurcates
at the carina (T4-T5) into right and left main bronchi. The right main bronchus is
wider, shorter, and more vertical—aspirated foreign bodies preferentially lodge here.

\medterm{Bronchial Artery}-- An artery arising from the systemic circulation that supplies the walls of the bronchi,
connective tissue of the lungs, and visceral pleura. It differs from the pulmonary
arteries, which carry blood for gas exchange.

\medterm{Bronchial Biopsy}-- Tissue sampling of the bronchial wall during bronchoscopy. It is used to diagnose
inflammatory conditions (sarcoidosis, eosinophilic bronchitis), infections, and
malignancy. Endobronchial biopsy of visible lesions and transbronchial biopsy of
parenchymal lesions provide complementary diagnostic information.

\medterm{Bronchial Carcinoid}-- Pulmonary neuroendocrine tumor arising from Kulchitsky cells of the bronchial mucosa;
subclassified as typical carcinoid (TC, low-grade, $<3$ mitoses/$2\,\mathrm{mm}^2$, no necrosis,

intermediate-grade, 2-10 mitoses or necrosis present, higher recurrence/ metastasis).

\medterm{Bronchial Stenosis}-- Narrowing of a bronchus from trauma, infection, inflammation, or prior intervention. It
causes localized wheezing, recurrent infections, and impaired secretion clearance.
Diagnosis is by bronchoscopy. Treatment includes balloon dilation, stenting, or surgical
resection.

\medterm{Bronchial Tree}-- The branching airway system formed by the trachea, main bronchi, lobar and segmental
bronchi, bronchioles, and terminal airways leading toward the alveoli.

\medterm{Bronchial Vein}-- A vein draining portions of the bronchial walls and larger airways. It empties partly
into the azygos system and partly into pulmonary veins, creating a small physiologic
venous admixture.

\medterm{Bronchiectasis}-- Permanent, abnormal dilation of bronchi caused by destruction of the bronchial wall from
chronic airway inflammation and infection. Causes include CF, immune deficiency, primary
ciliary dyskinesia, and post-infectious damage. Features include chronic cough, copious
purulent sputum, and recurrent hemoptysis.

\medterm{Bronchiole}-- Small airways (less than 2 mm diameter) without cartilage, branching from segmental
bronchi to terminal bronchioles (conducting zone) to respiratory bronchioles
(transitional, alveoli bud from walls) to alveolar ducts to alveolar sacs to alveoli.

\medterm{Bronchiolectasis}-- Permanent abnormal dilation of bronchioles, usually caused by chronic inflammation,
infection, or obstruction. It may accompany bronchiectasis and contribute to impaired
mucus clearance and recurrent infection.

\medterm{Bronchiolitis}-- Lower respiratory tract infection causing inflammation and obstruction of small airways.
Most common cause is respiratory syncytial virus. Affects infants under 12 months.
Presents with wheezing, tachypnea, nasal flaring, and retractions. Diagnosis is
clinical.

\medterm{Bronchiolitis Obliterans}-- An uncommon, often irreversible obstructive small-airway disease caused by inflammation
and fibrotic narrowing or obliteration of the bronchioles. Causes include severe
infection, inhalational injury, autoimmune disease, and complications after lung or
bone-marrow transplantation.

\medterm{Bronchitis}-- Inflammation of the bronchial mucosa. Acute bronchitis is typically viral, presents with
cough (often productive), and resolves spontaneously. Cough with sputum production for
at least 3 months in 2 consecutive years, often due to smoking.

\medterm{Bronchoalveolar Lavage}-- Diagnostic procedure during bronchoscopy where saline is instilled and aspirated.
Analyzed for cell counts, microbiology, and cytology. Useful for Pneumocystis pneumonia,
pulmonary alveolar proteinosis, and sarcoidosis diagnosis.

\medterm{Bronchodilator Response}-- The change in spirometric airflow after administration of a short-acting bronchodilator.
A significant response can support variable airflow limitation, but a negative response
does not exclude asthma and a positive response is not specific to one disease.

\medterm{Bronchodilator Reversibility}-- Improvement in FEV1 > 12\1 and > 200 mL after short-acting bronchodilator. Supports
asthma diagnosis. Some COPD patients show partial reversibility.

\medterm{Bronchoesophageal fistula}-- Abnormal communication between the bronchial tree and the esophagus, most commonly
acquired from malignancy, mediastinal infection, or tuberculous lymphadenitis.
Congenital forms are associated with esophageal atresia.

\medterm{Bronchogenic Cyst}-- A congenital cyst derived from abnormal budding of the ventral foregut. It is typically
found in the middle mediastinum or near the carina. It is usually asymptomatic but may
cause compression of adjacent structures. Treatment is surgical resection if
symptomatic.

\medterm{Bronchophony}-- Increased transmission of spoken sounds to the stethoscope over consolidated lung
tissue. It is normally heard over the central airways. Excessive bronchophony over
peripheral lung fields indicates consolidation. It is part of the constellation of
physical examination findings in pneumonia.

\medterm{Bronchopleural Fistula}-- An abnormal communication between the bronchial tree and pleural space. It is a
complication of pneumonectomy, lung surgery, or empyema. It causes persistent air leak,
subcutaneous emphysema, and respiratory compromise. Treatment includes chest tube
drainage, bronchoscopic intervention, and surgical repair.

\medterm{Bronchopneumonia}-- Patchy consolidation centered on bronchioles across multiple lobes. Caused by S. aureus,
gram-negative bacteria, and Pseudomonas.

\medterm{Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia}-- A chronic lung disease of premature infants, resulting from lung injury from mechanical
ventilation, oxygen toxicity, and inflammation. Most commonly occurs in infants born at
less than 28 weeks gestation with birth weight less than 1500 grams.

\medterm{Bronchoscopy}-- Endoscopic examination of the tracheobronchial tree. Flexible bronchoscopy: most common,
for diagnosis (lung mass, persistent cough, hemoptysis, suspected infection, ILD) and
therapy (foreign body removal, bronchial washing, biopsy, BAL). Rigid bronchoscopy: for
massive hemoptysis, large foreign bodies, and stent placement.

\medterm{Bronchospasm}-- Involuntary constriction of bronchial smooth muscle causing airway obstruction,
wheezing, and increased airway pressures. Triggers include airway manipulation, volatile
anesthetics (sevoflurane is bronchodilating; desflurane can cause bronchospasm),
histamine-releasing drugs (morphine, atracurium), and reactive airway disease.

\medterm{Bronchostenosis}-- Narrowing of a bronchus that restricts airflow. Causes include scarring, tumors, foreign
bodies, inflammation, external compression, or postinfectious and postprocedural
changes.

\medterm{Bronchus}-- One of the two main branches of the trachea that conduct air into the lungs. The right
main bronchus is wider, shorter, and more vertical than the left, making it more
susceptible to aspiration. Each main bronchus divides into lobar bronchi (secondary),
segmental bronchi (tertiary), and progressively smaller branches (bronchioles).

\medterm{Bronze Diabetes}-- Historical eponym for hereditary hemochromatosis (HFE-related HH, C282Y homozygous
mutation), characterized by the classic triad of skin pigmentation ("bronze"),
cirrhosis, and diabetes mellitus from pancreatic iron deposition. Also known as
"Troisier syndrome" or pigment cirrhosis.

\medterm{Brow Presentation}-- A fetal presentation where the fetal head is in a military position between full flexion
(vertex) and hyperextension (face). The presenting diameter is the largest
(occipitofrontal, ~13.5 cm). Incidence: 1:1000-1:2000. Vaginal delivery is impossible
with a persistent brow in a term fetus unless it converts to vertex or face.

\medterm{Brown Lung Disease}-- An occupational airway disease associated with inhalation of cotton, flax, or hemp dust,
classically causing byssinosis. Symptoms may include chest tightness, cough, wheezing,
and work-related airflow limitation.

\medterm{Brown Sequard Syndrome}-- Brown-Sequard syndrome (hemisection of the spinal cord) results from a lesion affecting
one half of the spinal cord, producing a characteristic pattern of ipsilateral and
contralateral neurological deficits.

\medterm{Brown Stain}-- Extrinsic staining from chromogenic bacteria, diet, tobacco. Removed by prophylaxis.

\medterm{Brudzinski Sign}-- Flexion of the hips and knees that occurs involuntarily when the neck is passively
flexed. It is a meningeal irritation sign that may occur in meningitis but lacks
sufficient sensitivity or specificity to establish the diagnosis alone.

\medterm{Brugada Syndrome}-- A genetic channelopathy causing abnormal sodium channel function (SCN5A mutation). ECG
pattern: coved ST elevation >2mm in V1-V3 (type 1) followed by negative T wave. Patients
are at risk for ventricular fibrillation and sudden cardiac death, often at rest or
during sleep. More common in young Southeast Asian men.

\medterm{Bruise}-- A contusion: localized hemorrhage into tissues due to blunt force trauma rupturing small
blood vessels, with intact skin.

\medterm{Bruising}-- Ecchymosis/contusion: a purplish-blue discoloration from blood extravasation into
skin/subcutaneous tissue following trauma; color changes with age (red-blue purple
green/yellow—bilirubin breakdown).

\medterm{Bruits}-- Turbulent blood flow sounds heard with stethoscope. Indicates stenosis.

\medterm{Brunner's Gland}-- A mucus-secreting gland in the submucosa of the duodenum. Its alkaline secretion helps
protect the mucosa from gastric acid and provides conditions favorable for intestinal
enzymes.

\medterm{Bruxism}-- Grinding or clenching teeth, often during sleep. Causes: stress, occlusion, sleep
disorders. Consequences: tooth wear, fractures, muscle pain, TMJ disorders. Treatment:
occlusal guard, stress management, behavior modification.

\medterm{Buccal Gland}-- A minor salivary gland located in the cheek. It secretes mucus into the oral cavity to
lubricate the buccal mucosa and assist formation and handling of the food bolus.

\medterm{Budd-Chiari Syndrome}-- Obstruction of hepatic venous outflow at any level from small hepatic venules to the
inferior vena cava junction with the right atrium. The most common predisposing
condition is myeloproliferative disorders accounting for forty to fifty percent of
cases. Other risk factors include thrombophilia and oral contraceptive use.

\medterm{Buerger Disease}-- See thromboangiitis obliterans.

\medterm{Buerger's Disease}-- Thromboangiitis obliterans, a nonatherosclerotic inflammatory disease of small and
medium arteries and veins, strongly associated with tobacco exposure. It causes distal
ischemia, pain, ulcers, and sometimes gangrene.

\medterm{Buffer}-- A solution that resists pH change upon addition of acid or base, composed of a weak acid
and its conjugate base (or weak base and conjugate acid). Buffer capacity is maximal
when pH = pKa ± 1.

\medterm{Bulbar Conjunctiva}-- The bulbar conjunctiva is the portion of the conjunctival membrane that covers the
anterior surface of the sclera, extending from the limbus (the junction of the cornea
and sclera) to the fornices where it reflects onto the inner surface of the eyelids.

\medterm{Bulbar Paralysis}-- Weakness or paralysis of muscles supplied by the lower cranial nerves, causing
dysarthria, dysphagia, impaired gag or cough, and sometimes respiratory compromise.
Causes include brainstem disease, motor-neuron disease, and neuromuscular disorders.

\medterm{Bulimia Nervosa}-- An eating disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of binge eating followed by
compensatory behaviors (purging, laxatives, excessive exercise). Self-esteem overly
influenced by body shape and weight.

\medterm{Bullae}-- Large (>1 cm) fluid-filled blisters; see also vesicle (<1 cm).

\medterm{Bullet Track}-- The path a projectile traverses through the body, defined by the entry wound, internal
course (through organs, vessels, bones), and exit wound (or retained bullet).

\medterm{Bullous Impetigo}-- A superficial staphylococcal skin infection caused by exfoliative toxins that produce
fragile, flaccid bullae and honey-coloured or varnish-like crusts, especially in infants
and young children.

\medterm{Bullous Pemphigoid}-- An autoimmune subepidermal blistering disease affecting elderly patients. It presents
with tense blisters on erythematous or normal-appearing skin, often preceded by pruritic
urticarial plaques. Autoantibodies target BP180 and BP230 (hemidesmosome proteins).

\medterm{Bumetanide}-- A potent loop diuretic inhibiting the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb
of the loop of Henle. Used for edema in heart failure, cirrhosis, and renal disease, and
for hypertension in renal impairment. Approximately 40 times more potent than
furosemide; more bioavailable orally, so equivalent IV-to-oral dosing is possible.

\medterm{Bundle Branch Block}-- Delay or block in the electrical conduction through the bundle branches. Left Bundle
Branch Block (LBBB): QRS >120 ms, broad notched R waves in lateral leads (I, V5-V6),
deep S waves in V1-V2. Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB): QRS >120 ms, rSR' pattern in
V1-V2, wide S waves in I, V5-V6.

\medterm{Bundle of His}-- A narrow band of specialized cardiac tissue that conducts electrical impulses from the
AV node through the fibrous cardiac skeleton to the interventricular septum. It divides
into the right and left bundle branches. The bundle of His is the only electrical
connection between atria and ventricles through the fibrous skeleton.

\medterm{Bunion}-- A bony prominence (exostosis) at the medial aspect of the first metatarsal head, causing
lateral deviation of the great toe (hallux valgus). The deformity is characterized by a
structural abnormality at the first metatarsophalangeal joint, with the first metatarsal
deviating medially and the proximal phalanx deviating laterally.

\medterm{Bunionectomy}-- Surgical correction of hallux valgus (bunion) deformity, indicated for symptomatic
bunions refractory to conservative measures (proper footwear, orthoses, toe spacers,
NSAIDs). Procedure chosen based on severity (hallux valgus angle and intermetatarsal
angle) and patient factors (age, activity, comorbidities).

\medterm{Buphthalmos}-- Enlargement of the eye caused by elevated intraocular pressure during early childhood,
when the ocular coats are still distensible. It is classically associated with
congenital or infantile glaucoma.

\medterm{Bupropion}-- An atypical antidepressant that inhibits dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake. Used for
major depressive disorder, seasonal affective disorder, and smoking cessation (Zyban).
Low sexual side effects and weight neutrality; energizing. Also used off-label for ADHD.

\medterm{Burkitt Lymphoma}-- A highly aggressive B-cell lymphoma characterized by MYC translocation (t(8;14) IGH-MYC,
t(8;22) IGL-MYC, t(2;8) IGK-MYC) and a "starry sky" morphology (tingible body
macrophages). Three variants: endemic (African, EBV+, jaw/facial bones), sporadic
(abdominal, ileocecal), immunodeficiency-associated (HIV).

\medterm{Burn}-- See Burns.

\medterm{Burn (Oral)}-- Thermal or chemical injury to oral mucosa. Food burn common. Treatment: symptomatic,
healing usually rapid.

\medterm{Burn Surgery}-- Burn assessment: rule of 9s (adult), Lund-Browder (children). Depth: superficial
(epidermis), superficial partial (dermis, blanch, blister, painful), deep partial
(deeper dermis, white, sluggish capillary refill, less painful), full thickness (all
dermis, white/charred, anesthetic).

\medterm{Burning Mouth Syndrome}-- A chronic oral pain disorder characterized by burning, scalding, or tingling discomfort
of the mouth, often involving the tongue, lips, or palate, without a sufficient visible
mucosal abnormality.

\medterm{Burns}-- Tissue damage from thermal, chemical, electrical, or radiation sources. Classification:
first-degree (epidermis, erythema), second-degree (partial thickness, blisters),
third-degree (full thickness, eschar).

\medterm{Bursectomy}-- Surgical excision of a bursa, usually performed for persistent painful bursitis,
recurrent infection, or another structural problem that has not responded to appropriate
conservative management.

\medterm{Bursitis}-- Inflammation of a bursa, a fluid-filled sac that reduces friction between tendons,
muscles, bones, and skin. Bursae are lined by synovial cells and normally contain a thin
layer of lubricating synovial fluid.

\medterm{Button magnets in the nasal cavity}-- Ingestion or insertion of small magnets into the nose, posing a risk of nasal septal
perforation or necrosis if multiple magnets are involved, requiring urgent removal.

\medterm{Bypass}-- Surgical creation of alternative route around blocked vessel. Uses vein or prosthetic
graft.

\medterm{Bypass (Vascular)}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein
(saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). See Bypass Graft.

\medterm{Bypass Graft}-- Surgical procedure creating detour around blocked vessel using autologous vein
(saphenous) or prosthetic graft (PTFE, Dacron). Indications: peripheral arterial
disease, coronary artery disease, aortic aneurysm. Long-term patency depends on graft
type, inflow/outflow, patient factors.

\medterm{Bypass Surgery}-- A surgical conduit that reroutes flow around an obstructed or diseased segment, applied
to coronary arteries (CABG), peripheral arteries (autologous vein or prosthetic bypass),
and the gastrointestinal tract (gastric bypass). Principal long-term concerns are
conduit patency, graft infection, and anastomotic stenosis.

\medterm{Byssinosis}-- An occupational obstructive airway disease caused by inhalation of textile fibers,
especially cotton, flax, or hemp dust. It may cause chest tightness and cough that
worsen during or after work exposure.

\medterm{Biliary Tract}-- The network of ducts that carries bile from the liver and gallbladder to the small intestine. Blockage or
inflammation can cause abdominal pain, jaundice, infection, or pancreatitis.

\medterm{Barrel Chest}-- A rounded, enlarged appearance of the chest caused by increased lung volume or changes in the chest wall.
It may occur with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, severe asthma, or other long-standing
lung conditions.

\medterm{Beau's Lines}-- Horizontal grooves or depressions across a fingernail or toenail caused by temporary interruption of nail
growth. They may follow severe illness, injury, high fever, nutritional deficiency, or local trauma;
the timing of the groove can help estimate when the event occurred.

\medterm{Back Injury}-- Damage to the muscles, ligaments, joints, discs, vertebrae, or nerves of the back caused by trauma, lifting, or repetitive
strain. Pain and stiffness are common; weakness, numbness, loss of bladder or bowel control, or severe pain
after major trauma requires urgent assessment.

\medterm{Bleeding Gums}-- Bleeding from the gum tissue, often caused by plaque-related gingivitis, periodontitis, trauma, or vigorous brushing.
It can also occur with blood disorders or medicines that affect clotting. Persistent bleeding, swelling,
loose teeth, or spontaneous bleeding requires dental or medical evaluation.

\medterm{Body Aches}-- Generalized muscle or body pain that may occur with viral infections, fever, exertion, inflammation, medication effects, or
other systemic illness. Severe pain with weakness, dark urine, breathing difficulty, chest pain, or high fever
requires prompt medical assessment.

\medterm{Bone Cancer}-- Cancer that begins in bone, such as osteosarcoma or chondrosarcoma, or cancer that has spread to bone from another organ.
Possible features include persistent localized bone pain, swelling, or a fracture after minor trauma. Diagnosis
requires imaging and tissue evaluation; treatment depends on the type and extent of disease.

\medterm{Bone Fracture}-- A partial or complete break in a bone caused by injury, repeated stress, or weakened bone. Pain, swelling, bruising,
deformity, and inability to use the limb may occur. Open fractures, loss of circulation or sensation, or major
deformity require emergency care.

\medterm{Breast Cyst}-- A fluid-filled sac within breast tissue, usually benign and sometimes tender or larger before menstruation. Examination and
imaging distinguish a cyst from a solid lesion; persistent, growing, bloody, or newly discovered breast lumps
should be assessed by a clinician.

\medterm{Breast Discharge}-- Fluid released from the nipple without breastfeeding. It may be caused by hormonal changes, infection, benign duct disease,
or cancer. Bloody, clear, spontaneous, one-sided, or persistent discharge, especially with a lump or skin change,
requires prompt breast evaluation.
