
\medterm{B Cell (B Lymphocyte)} A key cellular component of adaptive humoral immunity that matures in the bone marrow.
Upon antigen recognition via B-cell receptor (BCR/surface IgM) and T-cell help
(CD40/CD40L interaction), B cells proliferate and differentiate into antibody-secreting
plasma cells and long-lived memory B cells.

\synonyms
B Lymphocyte

\medterm{B Chromosome} A supernumerary chromosome that is not part of the standard chromosome complement and is
not required for normal development. It may occur in certain plants, animals, or tumors
and varies in number between cells.

\medterm{B-Cell Receptor} The membrane-bound immunoglobulin complex on a B lymphocyte that recognizes native
antigen. BCR signaling, with appropriate co-stimulation, promotes B-cell activation,
proliferation, differentiation, and antibody production.

\medterm{B-Lynch Suture} A surgical technique for controlling postpartum hemorrhage due to uterine atony
unresponsive to medical management. A compressive suture placed around the uterus to
mechanically compress the uterine walls and reduce blood flow. The suture creates a
brace-like effect. Can be combined with other compression sutures (Cho, Hayman).

\medterm{Babesiosis} A tick-borne infection caused by Babesia parasites, commonly B. microti in the United States and B. divergens in Europe. The parasites infect red blood cells and can cause fever, fatigue, haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, jaundice, and haemoglobinuria; severe disease is more likely in people without a spleen or with impaired immunity.

\medterm{Babinski Reflex} An extensor plantar response present from birth to approximately 2 years. When the
lateral sole is stroked, the great toe dorsiflexes and other toes fan outward.
Disappears as the corticospinal tract myelinates. Persistence beyond age 2 may indicate
neurologic abnormality.

\medterm{Babinski Sign} The Babinski sign is a pathological reflex elicited by stroking the lateral aspect of
the sole of the foot from the heel to the metatarsal heads with a blunt object. A normal
response (flexor plantar response) is plantar flexion (curling downward) of all toes.

\medterm{Bacillary Angiomatosis} A vascular proliferative disease caused by Bartonella henselae or B. quintana, occurring
in immunocompromised patients (HIV/AIDS, transplant). Presents with erythematous
papules, nodules, or pedunculated lesions resembling Kaposi sarcoma or pyogenic
granuloma. Can involve skin, liver (peliosis hepatis), spleen, bone, and lymph nodes.

\medterm{Bacilluria} The presence of bacilli in the urine, usually referring to rod-shaped bacteria detected
by microscopy or culture. Its clinical significance depends on symptoms, quantitative
culture, and the organism identified.

\medterm{Back Injury} Damage to the muscles, ligaments, joints, discs, vertebrae, or nerves of the back caused by trauma, lifting, or repetitive
strain. Pain and stiffness are common; weakness, numbness, loss of bladder or bowel control, or severe pain
after major trauma requires urgent assessment.

\medterm{Back Pain} One of the most common medical complaints, affecting up to 80 percent of the population
at some point in life, and the leading cause of disability worldwide. Back pain is
classified by duration: acute (less than 4 weeks), subacute (4-12 weeks), and chronic
(greater than 12 weeks).

\synonyms
Pain, Back

\medterm{Backache} Pain or discomfort located anywhere in the posterior trunk from neck to buttocks, a
symptom rather than a disease. Causes include musculoskeletal strain (most common), disc
herniation, spinal stenosis, facet arthropathy, sacroiliac joint dysfunction, and
referred pain from renal, pancreatic, or vascular sources.

\medterm{Bacteremia} The presence of viable bacteria in the bloodstream, a serious condition that can be
transient (after dental procedures, toothbrushing), intermittent (undrained abscess), or
continuous (endovascular infection like endocarditis). Bacteremia triggers systemic
inflammatory responses ranging from fever and leukocytosis to septic shock.

\medterm{Bacteria} Single-celled microscopic organisms lacking a nucleus, which can exist independently or
in colonies; some are beneficial to health (microbiome) while others cause infectious
disease.

\medterm{Bacterial Adherence} The process by which bacteria attach to host cell surfaces, a critical first step in
colonisation and infection. Adherence is mediated by adhesins, which are surface
structures that bind to specific receptors on host cells. Adhesins include pili,
fimbriae, outer membrane proteins, and capsules.

\medterm{Bacterial Biofilms} Structured communities of bacteria attached to surfaces and embedded in a self-produced
matrix of extracellular polymeric substances. Biofilms are a major cause of chronic
infections, particularly on medical devices including catheters, prosthetic joints, and
heart valves.

\medterm{Bacterial Capsule} A polysaccharide layer surrounding the bacterial cell wall that protects against
phagocytosis and complement-mediated killing. Capsules are major virulence factors for
many pathogens including Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and
Klebsiella pneumoniae.

\medterm{Bacterial Cell Wall} The rigid outer layer of bacteria that provides structural integrity and determines cell
shape. The cell wall is composed primarily of peptidoglycan, a polymer of sugars
cross-linked by short peptides.

\medterm{Bacterial Classification} The organisation of bacteria into taxonomic groups based on genetic, phenotypic, and
evolutionary relationships. Bacteria are classified using a hierarchical system: domain,
phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species.

\medterm{Bacterial Conjugation} The transfer of genetic material between bacteria through direct cell-to-cell contact
via a pilus. Conjugation is mediated by conjugative plasmids that encode the genes for
transfer. The donor cell (F+) forms a conjugative pilus that attaches to the recipient
cell (F-).

\medterm{Bacterial Conjunctivitis} An infection of the conjunctival mucous membrane caused by various bacterial pathogens,
presenting with a purulent or mucopurulent discharge that causes the eyelids to become
matted together, particularly upon waking, conjunctival injection, and a gritty or
foreign body sensation.

\medterm{Bacterial Dermatitis} Inflammation of the skin caused by bacterial infection, typically presenting with
erythema, edema, warmth, pain, and purulent discharge. Common causative organisms
include Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus pyogenes, and the condition may arise de
novo or as a superinfection of pre-existing dermatitis.

\medterm{Bacterial Dormancy} A state of reduced metabolic activity that allows bacteria to survive unfavourable
conditions. Dormant forms include spores, persisters, and viable but non-culturable
cells. Spores are highly resistant structures formed by Bacillus and Clostridium.
Persisters are phenotypic variants that are tolerant to antibiotics.

\medterm{Bacterial Endocarditis} Infection of the heart's inner lining or valves by bacteria, usually producing fever and a new
or changing heart murmur. It can cause valve damage and embolic complications.

\medterm{Bacterial Endocarditis Vegetations} Vegetations in bacterial endocarditis are complex masses composed of bacteria, fibrin,
and platelets that form on heart valve surfaces or endocardium. The pathogenesis begins
with bacteremia seeding a site of endothelial damage or a previously abnormal valve
surface.

\medterm{Bacterial Endospores} Highly resistant dormant structures formed by certain gram-positive bacteria including
Bacillus and Clostridium species. Endospores form during nutrient deprivation and can
survive for decades. They are resistant to heat, radiation, chemicals, and desiccation.

\medterm{Bacterial Flagella} Thread-like appendages composed of flagellin protein that provide motility to bacteria.
Flagella are composed of three parts: the filament, hook, and basal body. The basal body
is embedded in the cell wall and functions as a rotary motor.

\medterm{Bacterial Gastroenteritis} Acute inflammation of the stomach and intestines caused by pathogenic bacteria such as
Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella, or pathogenic Escherichia coli. It commonly causes
diarrhea, abdominal cramps, fever, and sometimes bloody stools or vomiting.

\medterm{Bacterial Genetics} The study of genes and genetic processes in bacteria. Bacteria have a single circular
chromosome and may contain plasmids, which are extrachromosomal DNA elements. Bacterial
genes are organized into operons, which are clusters of genes regulated as a unit.

\medterm{Bacterial Growth Phases} The stages of bacterial growth in culture. The lag phase is when bacteria adapt to their
environment. The log (exponential) phase is when bacteria multiply at their maximum
rate. The stationary phase is when nutrient depletion and waste accumulation limit
growth. The death phase is when bacteria die.

\medterm{Bacterial Infections} Illnesses caused by pathogenic bacteria proliferating in the body, ranging from mild
sore throats and ear infections to life-threatening sepsis and meningitis. They are
diagnosed through cultures, PCR testing, or other microbiological methods.

\medterm{Bacterial Meningitis} Infection of the meninges by bacteria, a medical emergency. In neonates: Group B Streptococcus, E. coli, Listeria. In older children: Neisseria meningitidis, Streptococcus pneumoniae. Presents with fever, headache, neck stiffness, altered consciousness, and Kernig/Brudzinski signs.

\medterm{Bacterial Metabolism} The chemical processes that occur within bacteria to maintain life. Bacteria have
diverse metabolic capabilities including aerobic and anaerobic respiration,
fermentation, and photosynthesis. Metabolic pathways are targets for antimicrobial
drugs.

\medterm{Bacterial Morphology} The shape and arrangement of bacteria as observed under microscopy. Basic shapes include
cocci (spheres), bacilli (rods), and spirilla (spirals). Cocci can be arranged in pairs
(diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), or tetrads. Bacilli can
be single, paired, or arranged in chains.

\medterm{Bacterial Overgrowth} Excessive proliferation of bacteria in the small intestine, commonly called
small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth. It may result from impaired intestinal motility,
anatomical stasis, blind loops, strictures, diverticula, or loss of the normal
ileocaecal barrier.

\synonyms
small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth.

\medterm{Bacterial Pathogenesis} The mechanisms by which bacteria cause disease. Pathogenesis involves adhesion, invasion, toxin production, and immune evasion. Adhesins mediate attachment to host cells. Invasins allow penetration of tissues. Toxins damage host cells.

\medterm{Bacterial Pili} Hair-like appendages on the bacterial surface composed of pilin protein. Pili are
shorter and thicker than flagella. Type I pili mediate adherence to host cells and are
important virulence factors. Type IV pili are involved in twitching motility and DNA
uptake during transformation.

\medterm{Bacterial Pneumonia} Infection of the lung tissue by bacteria, causing inflammation and air-space filling. It may cause
fever, cough, sputum, chest pain, and shortness of breath.

\medterm{Bacterial Spore} A metabolically dormant, highly resistant structure produced by certain bacteria,
notably Bacillus and Clostridioides species, in response to environmental stress. Spores
resist desiccation, heat, and many disinfectants and require validated sterilization for
reliable destruction.

\medterm{Bacterial Toxins} Substances produced by bacteria that cause damage to host cells. There are two major
categories: exotoxins and endotoxins. Exotoxins are proteins secreted by bacteria that
have specific toxic effects. Endotoxins are lipopolysaccharide components of
gram-negative bacterial outer membranes released during cell lysis.

\medterm{Bacterial Tracheitis} Bacterial infection and inflammation of the trachea, often causing fever, a harsh cough, noisy
breathing, and potentially rapid airway obstruction.

\medterm{Bacterial Transduction} The transfer of bacterial DNA from one bacterium to another through bacteriophages
(bacterial viruses).

\medterm{Bacterial Transformation} The uptake of free DNA from the environment by competent bacteria. Transformation occurs
naturally in some bacterial species including Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus
influenzae, and Neisseria. Competence is the ability to take up DNA and is regulated by
specific genes.

\medterm{Bacterial Vaginosis} BV: replacement of normal vaginal lactobacilli by an overgrowth of mixed anaerobes
(Gardnerella vaginalis, Mobiluncus, etc.), the most common cause of vaginal discharge in
reproductive-age women. Risk: new/multiple partners, douching, smoking.

\medterm{Bacterial Virulence Factors} Molecules produced by bacteria that contribute to their ability to cause disease. Virulence factors include adhesins, invasins, toxins, capsules, and enzymes. Adhesins mediate attachment to host cells. Toxins damage host cells directly. Capsules protect against phagocytosis.

\medterm{Bactericide} An agent that kills bacteria rather than merely inhibiting their growth
(bacteriostatic). Bactericidal antibiotics include beta-lactams (penicillins,
cephalosporins, carbapenems), aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, metronidazole, and
vancomycin.

\medterm{Bacteriology} The branch of microbiology concerned with the study of bacteria, including their
morphology, genetics, physiology, ecology, and clinical significance.

\medterm{Bacteriophage} A virus that infects and replicates within bacteria.

\medterm{Bacteriophage Therapy} The investigational use of bacteriophages, viruses that infect bacteria, to treat selected
bacterial infections. Their activity is usually limited to susceptible bacterial strains.

\medterm{Bacterium} A unicellular prokaryote lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, possessing a
single circular chromosome and typically a peptidoglycan cell wall.

\medterm{Bacteriuria} The presence of bacteria in urine, detected by urinalysis or culture. It may occur with
urinary symptoms, as in a urinary-tract infection, or without symptoms, as in
asymptomatic bacteriuria; interpretation depends on specimen quality, bacterial
quantity, patient context, and the presence of inflammation or symptoms.

\medterm{Bag-Mask Ventilation} Manual ventilation of a non-breathing or inadequately breathing patient using a
self-inflating bag (AMBU) attached to a mask sealed over the face. Effective technique:
correct mask size, jaw thrust/head extension, two-person technique (two-hand seal),
adequate tidal volumes with ETCO2 monitoring.

\medterm{Bagassosis} An occupational hypersensitivity pneumonitis (extrinsic allergic alveolitis) caused by
inhalation of dust from moldy bagasse—the fibrous residue of sugarcane after extraction.
Caused by thermophilic actinomycetes (Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula) that colonize
stored bagasse.

\medterm{Bainbridge Reflex} An increase in heart rate produced by stretch of the atrial walls, especially during
increased venous return. The reflex helps limit venous congestion by adjusting cardiac
output.

\medterm{Baker Cyst} A fluid-filled cyst in the popliteal fossa, resulting from herniation of the synovial
membrane through the posterior knee joint capsule or from a communication between the
knee joint and the gastrocnemius-semimembranosus bursa.

\synonyms
Bakers Cyst (Baker Cyst)
Popliteal Cyst (Baker Cyst)

\medterm{Baker's Cyst} A fluid-filled synovial cyst arising from the gastrocnemius-semimembranosus bursa
posterior to the knee, typically caused by chronic knee joint effusion from conditions
such as osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or meniscal tears. It presents as a
palpable, tender mass in the popliteal fossa that may rupture causing calf swelling.

\medterm{Balamuthia Mandrillaris Infection} A rare, usually fatal infection caused by the free-living amoeba Balamuthia mandrillaris. It may
begin as a skin lesion and progress to granulomatous amoebic encephalitis with headache, confusion, seizures, or
focal neurologic deficits. Diagnosis is difficult and requires specialized testing; urgent infectious-disease and
neurologic evaluation is essential.

\medterm{Balance} The ability to maintain the body's center of mass over its base of support, relying on
input from the vestibular system, vision, and proprioception. It is essential for
walking, standing, and performing daily activities without falling. Age-related decline,
medications, and neurological disorders can impair balance, increasing fall risk.

\medterm{Balance Assessment} An evaluation of steadiness and the ability to maintain safe posture during standing and movement. It may include standardized
tests and helps identify mobility or fall risk.

\medterm{Balance Disorders And Vertigo} Balance disorders encompass conditions that cause dizziness, unsteadiness, or a
sensation of motion, often arising from dysfunction in the inner ear, brain, or sensory
pathways.

\medterm{Balance Impairment} The ability to maintain the body's center of mass over its base of support, relying on
input from the vestibular system, vision, and proprioception. See balance.

\medterm{Balance Training} Exercises improving postural stability and preventing falls. Components: static balance,
dynamic balance, reactive balance. Techniques: weight shifting, tandem stance,
single-leg stance, perturbations, balance boards. Essential for elderly, neurological
conditions.

\medterm{Balanitis} Inflammation of the glans penis, often involving the prepuce (balanoposthitis). Causes
include poor hygiene, irritants, infection, and dermatologic conditions such as lichen
planus, psoriasis, and lichen sclerosus. It may cause erythema, swelling, discharge,
pruritus, and discomfort; treatment depends on the cause.

\medterm{Balanoposthitis} Inflammation of both the glans penis and the prepuce. It may cause pain, erythema,
swelling, discharge, or difficulty retracting the foreskin and can result from
infection, irritation, or inflammatory skin disease.

\medterm{Baldness} Commonly used term for substantial hair loss, especially androgenetic alopecia, also called pattern
hair loss. In this condition, genetically susceptible hair follicles gradually become smaller under the influence of
androgens.

\synonyms
pattern.

\medterm{Ballard Score} Clinical assessment of gestational age in newborns using 6 physical and 6 neuromuscular
maturity criteria. Physical parameters: skin texture, ear form and recoil, eye
appearance, breast tissue, and genitalia. Neuromuscular: posture, arm and leg recoil,
popliteal angle, scarf sign, and heel-to-ear.

\medterm{Ballism} A hyperkinetic movement disorder characterized by sudden, involuntary, large-amplitude,
flinging movements of a limb or limbs. It is often related to dysfunction of the
subthalamic region or connected basal-ganglia circuits.

\medterm{Ballistocardiography} The graphic recording of repetitive body movements produced by the ejection of blood
with each heartbeat. It has been used to study cardiac mechanical activity and
circulation.

\medterm{Balloon (Vascular)} An inflatable catheter component used to widen a narrowed blood vessel or expand a vascular stent.

\synonyms
Vascular

\medterm{Balloon Aortic Valvuloplasty} Catheter-based procedure using balloon inflation to dilate a stenotic aortic valve.
Provides temporary hemodynamic improvement in severe AS but restenosis is common within
6-12 months. Used as a bridge to TAVR or surgical AVR in hemodynamically unstable
patients, or as palliation in patients not eligible for definitive valve replacement.

\medterm{Balloon Catheter} A catheter with an inflatable tip used to widen a narrowed vessel, expand a stent, or dilate another body passage.

\medterm{Band Keratopathy} A horizontal band of calcium deposition in the superficial cornea, typically across the
interpalpebral area. It may occur with chronic ocular inflammation, hypercalcemia, renal
disease, or longstanding eye disease and can impair vision.

\medterm{Bandage} Material applied to cover, protect, compress, or support an injured body part. Examples include gauze, adhesive, elastic, and tubular
bandages.

\medterm{Bandage Scissors} Blunt-tipped scissors for cutting bandages.

\medterm{Bankart Lesion} A tear of the anterior-inferior glenoid labrum, usually caused by anterior shoulder dislocation and associated with recurrent instability.

\medterm{Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba Syndrome} Autosomal dominant PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS) caused by loss-of-function
mutations in PTEN (10q23), allelic to Cowden syndrome.

\medterm{Banti Disease} A historical term for congestive splenomegaly associated with portal hypertension and
hypersplenism. Modern evaluation usually identifies the underlying hepatic,
portal-venous, or hematologic cause.

\medterm{Banti Syndrome} A historical term for portal hypertension with splenomegaly and hypersplenism. Modern evaluation
usually identifies an underlying hepatic, portal-venous, or hematologic disorder.

\medterm{Barbiturate} A class of CNS depressants derived from barbituric acid that enhance GABA-A receptor
activity by increasing the duration of chloride channel opening (as opposed to
benzodiazepines, which increase the frequency). At low doses: anxiolysis and sedation.
At moderate doses: hypnosis and anesthesia.

\medterm{Bare Stent (Vascular)} A metal mesh tube placed inside a narrowed vessel without a drug coating to keep the vessel open.

\synonyms
Vascular

\medterm{Bariatric Surgery} Weight loss procedures and surgery, collectively known as bariatric surgery, are
invasive interventions for severe obesity that produce substantial and sustained weight
loss by altering gastrointestinal anatomy and physiology.

\medterm{Barium} A metallic element (atomic number 56, symbol Ba) whose sulfate salt (barium sulfate) is
used as a radiocontrast agent due to its high atomic number, which effectively absorbs
X-rays.

\medterm{Barium Enema} Fluoroscopic imaging of the large intestine after retrograde filling with barium
contrast. Double-contrast (barium + air) provides better mucosal detail. Indications:
colorectal cancer screening (historical, now CT colonography preferred), evaluation of
colonic obstruction, diverticulosis, and postsurgical anatomy.

\medterm{Barium Study} X-ray imaging using barium sulfate contrast. Barium swallow, barium enema, upper GI
series. Evaluates GI tract morphology and function.

\medterm{Barium Swallow} Fluoroscopic examination of the esophagus after ingestion of barium contrast. Used to
evaluate esophageal anatomy, motility, strictures, and structural abnormalities. The
study demonstrates mucosal detail, filling defects, strictures, and motility disorders.
A barium follow-through extends the examination to the small bowel.

\medterm{Barium-133} Calibration source. Half-life 10.5 years. Used for energy calibration.

\medterm{Barognosis} The ability to recognize and distinguish the weight of objects by handling them, with
intact primary sensation. Impaired barognosis indicates a cortical sensory deficit.

\medterm{Baroreceptor Reflex} The rapid neural mechanism for short-term blood pressure regulation, involving
baroreceptors (mechanoreceptors) in the carotid sinus (innervated by CN IX) and aortic
arch (innervated by CN X). When blood pressure rises, baroreceptors increase their
firing rate, signaling the cardiovascular center in the medulla.

\medterm{Bouchard Nodes} Bony enlargements of the proximal interphalangeal joints, usually associated with osteoarthritis.

\medterm{Buffy Coat} The thin layer of white blood cells and platelets that forms between plasma and red blood cells after centrifugation of whole blood.

\medterm{Barotrauma} Lung injury from excessive pressure during ventilation, causing alveolar rupture
(pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum). Risk factors: high pressures, high PEEP. Prevented by
lung-protective ventilation.

\medterm{Barrel Chest} A rounded, enlarged appearance of the chest caused by increased lung volume or changes in the chest wall.
It may occur with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, severe asthma, or other long-standing
lung conditions.

\medterm{Barrett Esophagus} Metaplastic transformation of the normal stratified squamous epithelium of the distal
esophagus to specialized intestinal-type columnar epithelium containing goblet cells,
occurring as a consequence of chronic gastroesophageal reflux disease.

\medterm{Barrett Esophagus Surveillance} Periodic endoscopic evaluation of Barrett esophagus to detect dysplasia or early
esophageal adenocarcinoma. The interval and biopsy protocol depend on the presence and
grade of dysplasia and current clinical guidelines.

\medterm{Barrier Creams} Topical preparations applied to the skin to create a physical protective layer against
irritants, moisture, and pathogens. Common formulations include zinc oxide, petrolatum,
dimethicone, and titanium dioxide.

\medterm{Barthel Index} A standardized scale that measures assistance needed for activities of daily living, including
feeding, bathing, dressing, toileting, transfers, mobility, and stair use. Scores are
interpreted according to the version of the scale and the clinical setting.

\medterm{Bartholin Cyst} A cyst of the Bartholin gland (paired mucus-secreting glands at the posterolateral
introitus) due to obstruction of the duct; if infected, a Bartholin abscess.

\medterm{Bartholin Gland} One of two small glands near the vaginal opening that produce lubricating mucus.
Blockage of its duct can cause a painless cyst; infection can produce a painful abscess with swelling,
redness, and difficulty walking or sitting. Recurrent or atypical masses require gynecologic assessment.

\medterm{Bartonellosis} A group of infections caused by Bartonella species, including cat-scratch disease, bacillary
angiomatosis, and, in some regions, bloodstream infection with fever and anemia. The
clinical features depend on the species and the patient's immune status.

\medterm{Bartter Syndrome} Inherited autosomal recessive disorder of the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle
causing salt wasting, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis, and hypercalciuria. Results from
mutations in genes encoding the NKCC2 transporter or its regulatory subunits. Presents
in childhood with failure to thrive, polyuria, and polydipsia.

\medterm{Basal Cell Carcinoma} The most common malignant skin tumor, arising from basal keratinocyte-lineage cells and
associated with cumulative ultraviolet exposure and Hedgehog-pathway abnormalities. It
commonly presents as a pearly papule or nodule with telangiectasia, ulceration, or a
rolled border.

\medterm{Basal Ganglia} Deep gray matter nuclei in the cerebral hemispheres for motor control, cognition, and
emotion. Key structures: caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus (internal/external
segments), subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra. Direct pathway facilitates movement;
indirect inhibits it.

\medterm{Basal Ganglia Dysfunction} Abnormal function of the deep cerebral nuclei that regulate movement, cognition, and
procedural learning. It may cause tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, chorea, dystonia, or
abnormal involuntary movements, as in Parkinson disease and Huntington disease.

\medterm{Basal Metabolic Rate} Energy expenditure at rest for basic physiological functions. Accounts for 60-75\% of
total energy expenditure. Affected by age, sex, body composition, thyroid status,
genetics. Measured by indirect calorimetry.

\medterm{Base} A substance that accepts protons (H+) or donates electron pairs (Lewis base). In aqueous
solution, bases increase OH- concentration (pH >7). Strong bases (NaOH, KOH) dissociate
completely; weak bases (NH3, HCO3-, protein side chains) partially.

\medterm{Base Excision Repair} The repair pathway for small, non-helix-distorting base lesions including oxidized bases
(8-oxoguanine), deaminated bases (uracil from cytosine deamination), alkylated bases,
and abasic (AP) sites.

\medterm{Basement Membrane} A thin, delicate layer of extracellular matrix that anchors epithelial tissue to the
underlying connective tissue.

\medterm{Basic Life Support} Immediate care for a person with cardiac arrest or respiratory failure, including recognition, emergency activation, chest
compressions, ventilation, and defibrillation when indicated.

\medterm{Basic Metabolic Panel} A panel of blood tests measuring serum glucose, sodium, potassium, chloride,
bicarbonate, BUN, and creatinine. Also includes calcium in some institutions. Uses:
assess hydration status, renal function, acid-base balance, and glucose control.

\medterm{Basic Reproduction Number} The expected number of secondary infections generated by one typical infectious person
in a completely susceptible population, under specified conditions. It is a population
and context parameter, not a fixed biological constant.

\medterm{Basilar Artery Aneurysm} Dilation of the basilar artery, most commonly fusiform, often associated with
atherosclerosis or hypertension. May compress cranial nerves or brainstem structures
causing facial pain, double vision, or hemiparesis. Rupture causes subarachnoid
hemorrhage with high morbidity.

\medterm{Basophil} A rare (<1 percent of leukocytes) granulocyte with large, basophilic granules containing
histamine, heparin, and leukotrienes. Basophils bear high-affinity IgE receptors
(Fc-epsilon-RI) and, like mast cells, release histamine and mediators during immediate
hypersensitivity (allergic) reactions.

\medterm{Bath Salts Abuse And Addiction} Misuse of synthetic cathinones, stimulant drugs that may cause agitation, paranoia, hallucinations,
seizures, dangerously high temperature, cardiovascular complications, and dependence. Severe agitation, chest pain,
seizure, hyperthermia, or altered consciousness requires emergency care. Treatment is supportive and may require
addiction and mental-health services.

\medterm{Battered Child Syndrome} A pattern of repeated, non-accidental physical injury inflicted on a child by a
caregiver, formally described by Kempe in 1962. Features include fractures of differing
ages, subdural and retinal hemorrhages, inconsistent histories, and delayed
presentation; mandatory reporting is required in most jurisdictions.

\medterm{Bauhin Valve} The ileocecal valve, a mucosal sphincter at the junction of the terminal ileum and cecum
that helps regulate passage of intestinal contents and limits reflux from the colon.

\medterm{BCG Vaccine} A live attenuated vaccine derived from Mycobacterium bovis that helps prevent severe forms of
tuberculosis, especially in children. Its use depends on national tuberculosis-risk
policies.

\medterm{Beals Syndrome} A congenital connective-tissue disorder characterized by congenital contractures,
arachnodactyly, kyphoscoliosis, and crumpled ears. It is usually associated with
pathogenic variants affecting fibrillin-2.

\medterm{Beam Hardening Artifact} CT artifact from dense structures. Reduced by beam hardening correction algorithms.

\medterm{Beau Lines} Transverse depressions or grooves across the nail plate caused by temporary interruption
of nail-matrix growth. They may follow severe illness, fever, trauma, malnutrition,
chemotherapy, or systemic inflammatory disease.

\medterm{Beau's Lines} Transverse grooves or depressions across a fingernail or toenail caused by temporary interruption of
nail-matrix growth. They may follow severe illness, injury, high fever, nutritional stress, or
local trauma; the timing of the groove can help estimate when the event occurred.

\medterm{Bechterew Disease} Historical eponym for ankylosing spondylitis (also known as Marie-Strümpell disease),
describing the chronic inflammatory arthritis of the axial skeleton first reported by
Vladimir Bekhterev (Russian, 1892) and Adolf Strümpell (German, 1884).

\synonyms
Marie-Strümpell disease),.

\medterm{Beck Triad} Classic diagnostic clinical triad of Cardiac Tamponade: (1) Hypotension, (2) Distended
neck veins (elevated JVP), and (3) Muffled / distant heart sounds. See Cardiac
Tamponade.

\medterm{Becker Muscular Dystrophy} An X-linked recessive neuromuscular disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene,
similar to Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), but with in-frame mutations that allow
production of some functional dystrophin protein, albeit reduced in quantity or abnormal
in size. The result is a milder clinical phenotype compared to DMD.

\medterm{Becker’s Nevus} A benign hamartomatous lesion appearing as a unilateral, hyperpigmented, often hairy
patch, typically developing in adolescence on the shoulder or upper trunk of males. It
results from a localized androgen-dependent increase in dermal smooth muscle and
pigmentation. It is cosmetically concerning but carries no malignant potential.

\medterm{Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome} An overgrowth disorder associated with 11p15.5 imprinting defects. Features: macrosomia,
macroglossia, omphalocele, visceromegaly, and neonatal hypoglycemia. Increased risk of
embryonal tumors (Wilms tumor, hepatoblastoma).

\medterm{Becquerel (Bq)} The SI unit of radioactive activity, equal to one nuclear transformation per second.
Administered activity is commonly reported in megabecquerels or gigabecquerels in
nuclear medicine.

\medterm{Bladder Carcinoma} A malignant tumor arising in the urinary bladder, most often from the urothelial lining.

\medterm{Bladder Diverticula} Outpouchings of the bladder mucosa through the muscular wall, often acquired from

\medterm{Bladder Fistula} An abnormal connection between the bladder and another organ or the skin, most often the

\medterm{Bladder Health And Disorders} Bladder health involves the proper storage and voluntary release of urine through

\medterm{Bladder Outlet Obstruction} Blockage of urine flow at the bladder neck or urethra, commonly caused by benign

\medterm{Bladder Pain Syndrome} Chronic bladder-related pain, pressure, or discomfort associated with urinary symptoms

exclusion and is managed with multimodal, patient-centered treatment.

\medterm{Bladder Perforation} A tear or hole in the bladder wall caused by trauma, pelvic surgery, instrumentation,
severe inflammation, or obstruction. Urine may leak into surrounding tissues, causing abdominal pain,
distention, blood in the urine, or infection. It requires urgent medical assessment and may need catheter
drainage or surgical repair.

\medterm{Bladder Problems} A range of conditions affecting urine storage and voiding, including incontinence,
overactive bladder, urinary retention, and recurrent infections. Causes may include

\medterm{Bladder Spasm} An involuntary contraction of the bladder muscle that can cause sudden urgency, cramping,

\medterm{Bladder Stone} A mineral deposit formed in or entering the urinary bladder. It may cause painful urination, blood in the urine, interrupted flow, or recurrent infection; treatment addresses the stone and its cause.

\medterm{Blast Injury} Tissue injury caused by an explosion, classified as primary injury from the pressure

\medterm{Blastic Plasmacytoid Dendritic-Cell Neoplasm} A rare, aggressive hematologic malignancy derived from plasmacytoid dendritic cells. It

\medterm{Blastocyst} An early stage in embryonic development, consisting of a hollow sphere of cells that

\medterm{Blastoma} Generic descriptor for embryonal (primitive) malignant tumors arising from precursor

\medterm{Blastomyces Dermatitidis} A dimorphic fungus that causes blastomycosis, a systemic pyogranulomatous infection

Mississippi River valleys. Cutaneous manifestations include verrucous or ulcerated
plaques, and pulmonary involvement is common.

\medterm{Blastomycosis} A fungal infection caused by Blastomyces dermatitidis, endemic to the Ohio and

\medterm{Bleeding} The escape of blood from the vascular system, which may be external (visible hemorrhage

\medterm{Bleeding Disorders} A heterogeneous group of conditions characterized by abnormal hemostasis, resulting from
defects in blood vessels, platelets, or coagulation factors.

\medterm{Bleeding Esophageal Varices} Life-threatening hemorrhage from dilated submucosal veins in the lower esophagus,

\medterm{Bleeding Gums} Bleeding from the gum tissue, often caused by plaque-related gingivitis, periodontitis, trauma, or vigorous brushing.

\medterm{Bleeding Into A Thyroid Nodule} Hemorrhage within a thyroid nodule, presenting with sudden onset of neck pain and rapid

echoes. Fine needle aspiration confirms the benign nature of the hemorrhagic cyst.

\medterm{Blennorrhea} A mucopurulent discharge from a mucous membrane, especially the urethra or conjunctiva.

than treatment based on the symptom alone.

\medterm{Blepharitis} Chronic inflammation of the eyelid margins, one of the most common ophthalmic

\medterm{Blepharoplasty} Surgical reconstruction or reshaping of the eyelids, often involving removal or

\medterm{Band Cell} An immature neutrophil with a curved, unsegmented nucleus. An increased number in blood may occur during acute infection or inflammation.

\medterm{Blind Loop Syndrome} Malabsorption syndrome due to stasis and bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) in a non-propulsive

\medterm{Blinding} Concealing group assignment. Single-blind: participants. Double-blind: participants and

\medterm{Blindness} Severe or complete loss of vision in one or both eyes, caused by disease or injury
affecting the cornea, lens, retina, optic nerve, visual pathways, or visual cortex. It
may be congenital or acquired, sudden or progressive, and reversible or permanent;
sudden visual loss is an ophthalmic emergency.

\medterm{Blood Alcohol Concentration} The concentration of ethanol in blood, commonly expressed as mass per volume or as a
percentage. Interpretation depends on sampling, timing, metabolism, tolerance,
co-ingestants, and local standards; clinical impairment cannot be inferred from a single

\medterm{Blood Alcohol Level} Measurement of ethanol concentration in blood. The clinical effects depend on the
concentration, timing, tolerance, co-ingestants, and individual factors; a single level does not by itself determine
impairment or prognosis.

\medterm{Blood Clot} A mass of coagulated blood within a blood vessel or body cavity. A clot may be
protective when it stops bleeding, but an abnormal thrombus can obstruct blood flow or
embolize to the lungs, brain, heart, or other organs.

\medterm{Blood Coagulation} The cascade of enzymatic reactions converting circulating fibrinogen into an insoluble
fibrin clot.

\medterm{Blood Coagulation Disorder} A disorder in which clot formation is deficient, excessive, or inappropriately

deficiencies, platelet disorders, liver disease, vitamin deficiencies, immune
conditions, and acquired systemic disease.

\medterm{Blood Culture} A laboratory test in which blood is placed in culture bottles to detect bacteria or fungi in the
bloodstream. It is used when bloodstream infection, sepsis, or infective endocarditis is suspected.

\medterm{Blood Culture Contamination} Introduction of skin or environmental microorganisms into a blood-culture bottle during
collection or handling rather than true bloodstream infection. It can lead to
unnecessary antimicrobial treatment and should be assessed using the organism, number of
positive sets, time to positivity, and clinical findings.

\medterm{Blood Film (Peripheral Blood Smear)} A stained microscopic preparation of peripheral blood used to assess erythrocyte,
leukocyte, and platelet morphology and to detect abnormal cells, parasites, blasts, and
red-cell fragments.

\medterm{Blood Flow} The volume of blood passing through a vessel, organ, or the systemic circulation per
unit time, typically expressed in mL/min. Governed by the hemodynamic equivalent of
Ohm's law: flow = pressure gradient / resistance. Total cardiac output (~5 L/min at
rest) equals systemic blood flow.

\medterm{Blood Gas (Venous)} Sampling of venous blood (typically from a peripheral vein or central line) for pH,

\medterm{Blood Glucose Monitoring} Blood sugar monitoring involves measuring glucose levels in the blood to manage

\medterm{Blood Group} The classification of blood based on the presence or absence of specific antigens

\medterm{Blood In Semen} Hematospermia, or the presence of blood in the ejaculate. It is often temporary and related to inflammation,

\medterm{Hematospermia} Blood in the semen, often temporary and associated with infection, inflammation, trauma, or a recent urologic procedure. Persistent or recurrent bleeding, pain, fever, urinary symptoms, or bleeding in an older adult requires medical evaluation.

\medterm{Blood Loss} The volume of blood lost during surgery or trauma, estimated by weighing swabs, suction

\medterm{Blood Pressure} The force exerted by blood against arterial walls, measured in millimeters of mercury

\medterm{Blood Products} Components prepared from donated blood for transfusion, including red blood cells, platelets, plasma, and

\medterm{Blood Smear} Peripheral blood film: a thin blood smear on a slide stained (Wright/Giemsa) and

\medterm{Blood Spatter Analysis} The forensic interpretation of bloodstain patterns to reconstruct events surrounding a

impact/expirated spatter.

\medterm{Blood Sugar Test} Measurement of blood glucose to detect or monitor diabetes and other disorders of

\medterm{Blood Transfusion} The administration of whole blood or blood components (packed red blood cells,

\medterm{Blood Urea Nitrogen} BUN: serum urea, the end product of protein catabolism, filtered and partially

\medterm{Blood Viscosity} The internal friction of blood that resists flow, determined primarily by hematocrit

temperature. Normal whole-blood viscosity is approximately 3-4 times that of water.

\medterm{Blood-Borne Disease} An infection that can spread through contact with infected blood or other body fluids. Examples include HIV and hepatitis B and C.

\medterm{Blood-Brain Barrier} The blood-brain barrier is a highly selective semipermeable membrane that separates the

\medterm{Bloom Syndrome} Rare autosomal recessive chromosomal instability syndrome caused by biallelic mutations

\medterm{Blount Disease} A growth disorder of the proximal tibia causing progressive bowing of the legs, usually

\medterm{Blue Bloater} A clinical phenotype of chronic bronchitis in COPD. Patients present with chronic cough,

\medterm{Blunt Abdominal Trauma} Trauma to the abdomen without penetrating injury. Mechanisms: motor vehicle collisions

\medterm{Blunt Force Trauma} Injury produced when a blunt object strikes the body, or the body strikes a blunt

\medterm{BMI (Body Mass Index)} Weight in kg divided by height in meters squared. Screening tool for weight categories:

\medterm{Bocavirus Infection} Infection caused by human bocavirus, a respiratory virus that most often affects young children. It may cause

\medterm{Bochdalek Hernia} A congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) occurring through the posterolateral foramen of

\medterm{Body Aches} Generalized muscle or body pain that may occur with viral infections, fever, exertion, inflammation, medication effects, or

\medterm{Body Dysmorphic Disorder} Preoccupation with one or more perceived defects or flaws in physical appearance that

\medterm{Body Identification} The process of establishing the identity of a deceased person, essential in forensic and

\medterm{Body Lice} Infestation of clothing and skin by the human body louse, causing intense itching,

\medterm{Bone} A rigid, mineralised connective tissue that forms the skeleton, providing structural

\medterm{Bowenoid Papulosis} A low-grade squamous intraepithelial neoplasia of the anogenital skin caused by human

\medterm{Bowlegs} Outward bowing of the legs causing the knees to remain apart when the feet and ankles

\medterm{Bowman Capsule} Double-walled epithelial cup surrounding the glomerular capillaries forming the

\medterm{Bowman's Gland} A serous gland in the olfactory mucosa whose secretion helps dissolve odorant molecules

olfactory epithelium.

\medterm{Boxed Warning} The most prominent safety warning required in prescribing information for a medicine
when there is a serious or life-threatening risk. It communicates major hazards and does
not necessarily mean that the medication is never appropriate.

\medterm{BPH} Abbreviation for benign prostatic hyperplasia, a noncancerous enlargement of the prostate that may obstruct urine flow.

\medterm{Brachial Artery} The continuation of the axillary artery, running from the lower border of teres major to
the cubital fossa, where it divides into radial and ulnar arteries. Lies medial to the
biceps; palpated for blood pressure and brachial pulse. Branches: profunda brachii (deep
brachial), superior/inferior ulnar collaterals.

\medterm{Brachial Plexopathy} Dysfunction of the brachial plexus causing pain, weakness, sensory loss, or paralysis in
the shoulder and upper limb. Causes include trauma, compression, tumors, inflammation,
and radiation injury.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus} Nerve network (C5-T1) formed by roots, trunks, divisions, and cords, giving rise to

anterior/posterior divisions, recombining into lateral/posterior/medial cords. Major
branches: musculocutaneous, axillary, radial, median, ulnar.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Cords} Formed by divisions recombining behind the pectoralis minor. Lateral cord (anterior
divisions of upper/middle trunks), posterior cord (all three posterior divisions),
medial cord (anterior division of lower trunk). Named for relationship to the axillary
artery. Lateral cord: lateral pectoral nerve, contributes to median nerve.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Injuries} Injuries to the network of nerves supplying the shoulder and upper limb, caused by traction, compression,
or penetrating trauma. They may produce pain, weakness, sensory loss, or paralysis; the
pattern and severity depend on which roots, trunks, or cords are affected.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Injury} Damage to the brachial plexus nerves during birth, most commonly from shoulder dystocia
or excessive traction. Erb's palsy (C5-C6) presents with waiter's tip position (arm
adducted, elbow extended, forearm pronated). Klumpke's palsy (C8-T1) affects the hand
and may cause Horner syndrome. Most cases resolve spontaneously.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Roots} Ventral rami of C5-T1 emerging from intervertebral foramina, passing between anterior
and middle scalene muscles (interscalene triangle). C5-C6 form the upper trunk, C7
continues as middle trunk, C8-T1 form the lower trunk. Root injuries cause specific
dermatome/myotome deficits. C5 avulsion causes loss of deltoid/biceps function.

\medterm{Brachial Plexus Trunks} Formed in the posterior neck triangle. Upper (C5-C6), middle (C7), and lower (C8-T1)
trunks each divide into anterior/posterior divisions behind the clavicle. Upper trunk is
most commonly injured in birth injuries (Erb-Duchenne palsy), causing waiter's tip
position.

\medterm{Brachialis Muscle} Flat strong muscle deep to the biceps, from distal anterior humerus to ulnar tuberosity
and coronoid process. Innervated by musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C6). Primary elbow flexor
regardless of forearm position ("workhorse" of elbow flexion). Less visible than biceps
but significantly contributes to arm strength.

\medterm{Brachiocephalic Trunk} The first and largest branch of the aortic arch. It ascends to the right
sternoclavicular joint, where it divides into the right common carotid and right
subclavian arteries.

\medterm{Brachytherapy} Radiation treatment in which a radioactive source is placed inside or near a tumor to deliver a concentrated dose.

\medterm{Brachytherapy Seed} Radioactive source implanted directly into tumor. I-125 or Pd-103 seeds for prostate
cancer. Permanent or temporary implantation.

\medterm{Bradycardia} A heart rate <60 beats per minute. May be physiologic (athletes) or pathologic due to
sinus node dysfunction, AV block, or medication effects. Symptomatic bradycardia causes
fatigue, dizziness, syncope, or heart failure. Evaluation includes ECG, Holter
monitoring, and assessment of reversible causes.

\synonyms
Slow Heartbeat

\medterm{Bradycardia Of Prematurity} Heart rate less than 100 beats per minute in a premature infant, often associated with
apnea and oxygen desaturation. May be caused by vagal stimulation, reflux, or immaturity
of the cardiac conduction system. Treatment: gentle stimulation, caffeine, and
addressing underlying causes.

\medterm{Bradykinesia} Slowness and reduced amplitude of voluntary movement, often accompanied by difficulty
initiating or sustaining repetitive actions. It is a cardinal feature of parkinsonism
and may be associated with rigidity, resting tremor, postural instability, reduced arm
swing, masked facial expression, and a shuffling gait.

\medterm{Bradypnea} Abnormally slow respiratory rate (adult <12 breaths/min). Causes:
opioid/benzodiazepine/sedative overdose, central nervous system depression (stroke,
trauma, increased ICP), hypothyroidism, hypothermia, and metabolic (e.g., hypokalemia).
May lead to hypoventilation, hypercapnia, and respiratory failure.

\medterm{Brain} The central organ of the nervous system, enclosed in the cranial cavity and composed of
the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem. The brain receives, processes, and integrates
sensory information; initiates and coordinates motor responses; and serves as the seat
of cognition, emotion, memory, and consciousness.

\medterm{Brain Abscess} A focal, purulent infection within the brain parenchyma, most commonly occurring in the
frontal or temporal lobes.

\medterm{Brain Concussion} A mild traumatic brain injury caused by a blow, jolt, or force that temporarily changes
brain function. Symptoms may include headache, dizziness, confusion, memory difficulty, nausea, and
sensitivity to light or noise, sometimes without loss of consciousness. Worsening headache, repeated
vomiting, seizure, weakness, or increasing drowsiness requires urgent assessment.

\medterm{Brain Death} The irreversible cessation of all brain and brainstem function, including consciousness
and brainstem reflexes, with absent respiratory drive—a legal definition of death.

\medterm{Brain Edema} Swelling caused by excess fluid in brain tissue, which can raise pressure inside the skull.
Causes include head injury, stroke, infection, tumor, bleeding, and severe metabolic disturbances.
Symptoms may include headache, vomiting, confusion, seizures, weakness, or reduced consciousness; severe
cases are medical emergencies.

\medterm{Brain Health} Brain health encompasses the ability to perform cognitive functions such as memory,
learning, decision-making, and emotional regulation across the lifespan. Key factors
include cardiovascular health, nutrition, sleep, social engagement, cognitive
stimulation, and management of chronic conditions like hypertension and diabetes.

\medterm{Brain Herniation} Displacement of brain tissue across rigid intracranial compartments or through the
foramen magnum due to mass effect or raised ICP. It can compress the brainstem and
cranial nerves and is a life-threatening neurosurgical emergency.

\medterm{Brain Injuries} Brain injuries range from mild concussions to severe traumatic brain injury caused by
falls, motor vehicle accidents, sports impacts, or penetrating trauma. Symptoms depend
on injury location and severity, including headache, confusion, memory loss, seizures,
and altered consciousness.

\medterm{Brain Injury} Damage to the brain caused by trauma, lack of oxygen, stroke, infection, toxins, or other disease. Effects
depend on the location and severity of injury.

\medterm{Brain Metastases} Cancer deposits that have spread to the brain from another primary site. They may cause headache, seizures, or focal neurologic deficits.

\synonyms
Secondary Brain Cancer

\medterm{Brain Metastasis} A deposit of cancer that has spread to the brain from another primary site. It may cause headache, seizures, or focal neurologic deficits; treatment depends on the number, location, and biology of the lesions.

\medterm{Brain Monitoring} Brain monitoring during anesthesia assesses the depth of hypnosis and neurological
function. Bispectral index (BIS) processes the frontal EEG into a number from 0
(isoelectric) to 100 (fully awake), with 40 to 60 recommended for general anesthesia.
BIS monitoring reduces awareness risk and may reduce anesthetic requirements.

\medterm{Brain Natriuretic Peptide} A hormone released mainly by the heart ventricles in response to myocardial wall stress.
BNP and NT-proBNP can support the assessment of suspected heart failure, but results are
affected by age, kidney function, body size, rhythm, and other illnesses and must be
interpreted with the clinical findings.

\medterm{Brain SPECT} Cerebral perfusion imaging using Tc-99m HMPAO or ECD. Used for epilepsy localization,
dementia differentiation, cerebrovascular disease.

\medterm{Brain Tumor} An abnormal growth of cells within the brain or its surrounding structures, classified
as primary (originating from brain tissue or its coverings) or secondary/metastatic
(spread from a cancer elsewhere in the body, which are more common, accounting for
approximately 50 percent of all brain tumors).

\medterm{Brain Ventricles} The four interconnected fluid-filled cavities within the brain that produce and contain
cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

\medterm{Brainstem} Connects cerebrum to spinal cord, with midbrain (superior), pons (middle), and medulla
oblongata (inferior). Contains cranial nerve nuclei (III-XII), ascending/descending
tracts, and vital autonomic centers for cardiac, respiratory, and vasomotor function.
Reticular formation regulates consciousness and sleep-wake cycles.

\medterm{Branchial Arch} One of the paired embryonic arches that contribute to development of facial, pharyngeal,
laryngeal, muscular, skeletal, vascular, and neural structures of the head and neck.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft} An embryonic groove between adjacent branchial arches. Persistence of cleft remnants can
produce congenital branchial cysts, sinuses, or fistulas, most commonly along the
lateral neck.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft Anomaly} A congenital remnant of the branchial apparatus presenting as a cyst, sinus, or fistula
in the head and neck. Recurrent infection or drainage from a persistent opening warrants
imaging and specialist management.

\medterm{Branchial Cleft Cyst} Congenital cystic mass arising from incomplete obliteration of branchial cleft apparatus
during embryogenesis.

\medterm{Braxton Hicks Contractions} Irregular, painless uterine contractions occurring throughout pregnancy, becoming more

\medterm{BRCA} BRCA1 and BRCA2 are tumor-suppressor genes involved in repair of DNA double-strand breaks. Pathogenic

\medterm{BRCA1 And BRCA2} Tumor-suppressor genes involved in homologous-recombination DNA repair. Germline

\medterm{Breakbone Fever} A traditional name for dengue fever, reflecting the severe myalgia and arthralgia that

\medterm{Breast Abscess} A localized collection of pus within the breast tissue, most commonly a complication of

\medterm{Breast Cancer} A malignant tumor arising in breast tissue. Risk, screening, and treatment depend on age, inherited and acquired biology, stage, and the person’s circumstances.

\medterm{Breast Density} The relative proportion of fibroglandular tissue compared with fat in a breast on mammography.

\medterm{Breast Cancer Genetics} The study of inherited variants that increase breast-cancer risk. Important genes and

\medterm{Breast Cyst} A fluid-filled sac within breast tissue, usually benign and sometimes tender or larger before menstruation. Examination and

\medterm{Breast Discharge} Fluid released from the nipple without breastfeeding. It may be caused by hormonal changes, infection, benign duct disease,

\medterm{Breast Engorgement} Overfilling of the breasts with milk, causing bilateral pain, firmness, warmth, and

\medterm{Breast Health} Breast health involves routine self-awareness, clinical examinations, and mammographic

benign conditions or breast cancer. Risk factors include genetics, age, hormone

\medterm{Breast Infection} Infection and inflammation of breast tissue, most commonly mastitis during

\medterm{Breast Lump} A localized thickening, mass, or swelling in breast tissue caused by a cyst,

\medterm{Breast MRI} Magnetic resonance imaging used to evaluate breast tissue. Contrast may be administered when clinically indicated.

\medterm{Breast Pain} Pain or tenderness in one or both breasts, caused by hormonal changes, cysts, infection,

\medterm{Breath-Holding Spells} A benign paroxysmal phenomenon in young children (6 months to 6 years), provoked by

\medterm{Breech Presentation} A fetal presentation where the buttocks or feet are positioned to deliver first. Types

\medterm{Bremsstrahlung} Braking radiation produced when beta particles decelerate in matter. Continuous

\medterm{Brenner Tumor} Rare ovarian epithelial neoplasm (1-2\% of ovarian tumors) composed of nests of

\medterm{Breslow Thickness} Microstaging measurement of melanoma invasion depth from the top of the granular layer

\medterm{Brief Resolved Unexplained Event} A sudden, brief, and now-resolved episode in an infant involving abnormal breathing,

\medterm{Bright's Disease} A historical term encompassing several forms of kidney disease, particularly glomerular

diagnosis specifies the underlying renal lesion.

\medterm{Brill's Disease} A recrudescent form of epidemic typhus caused by reactivation of latent Rickettsia

\medterm{Brittle Asthma} An uncommon term for severe asthma with unusually unpredictable and difficult-to-control airflow or attacks despite
appropriate treatment. The term is not a universally standardized diagnostic category;

\medterm{Brittle Diabetes} Labile, unstable type 1 diabetes mellitus characterized by unpredictable, large,

hypoglycemia (often with ketoacidosis) despite optimal adherence, significantly

\medterm{Broca Aphasia} Acquired language disorder of expressive speech (motor aphasia) caused by lesion of the
posterior inferior frontal gyrus (Broca area, Brodmann area 44/45) of the dominant
(usually left) hemisphere, most commonly from middle cerebral artery (MCA) superior
division infarction.

\medterm{Broca's Aphasia} A nonfluent language disorder caused by dysfunction of the dominant inferior frontal
language region. Speech is effortful and reduced, while comprehension is relatively
preserved compared with repetition and fluent language production.

\medterm{Broca's Area} A language-related region of the dominant inferior frontal gyrus involved in speech
production, language formulation, and aspects of syntax. Lesions may cause expressive
aphasia.

\medterm{Broken Finger} A fracture of one of the finger bones caused by trauma, crushing, or twisting. Pain, swelling, bruising, deformity,
and reduced movement may occur. Assessment should check alignment, tendon function, sensation, and circulation;
some fractures require reduction, splinting, or surgery.

\synonyms
Pinky Broken Finger (Broken Finger)

\medterm{Broken Foot} A fracture of one or more bones of the foot, commonly caused by a fall, direct impact, twisting injury, or repetitive
stress. Pain, swelling, bruising, and difficulty bearing weight are typical. Deformity, an open wound, numbness,
or inability to walk requires urgent evaluation and imaging.

\medterm{Broken Heart Syndrome} A temporary weakening of the heart muscle, often triggered by severe emotional or physical stress.
It can resemble a heart attack but usually occurs without an obstructing coronary clot.

\medterm{Broken Toe} A fracture of a toe bone, usually after direct impact or crushing. Pain, swelling, bruising, and tenderness are common;
deformity, an open injury, numbness, or impaired circulation requires prompt assessment. Treatment may include

\synonyms
Toe Broken (Broken Toe)

\medterm{Bromhidrosis} Excessive or unpleasant body odor produced when microbial metabolism acts on sweat or
skin secretions. It is often prominent in the axillae or feet and may be associated with
hyperhidrosis or local skin disease.

\medterm{Bromoderma} A skin eruption or dermatitis attributed to exposure to bromine or bromide compounds. It
may present with acneiform, pustular, or other inflammatory lesions.

\medterm{Bronchi} The major airways conducting air from the trachea into the lungs. The trachea bifurcates
at the carina (T4-T5) into right and left main bronchi. The right main bronchus is
wider, shorter, and more vertical—aspirated foreign bodies preferentially lodge here.

\medterm{Bronchial Artery} An artery arising from the systemic circulation that supplies the walls of the bronchi,
connective tissue of the lungs, and visceral pleura. It differs from the pulmonary
arteries, which carry blood for gas exchange.

\medterm{Bronchial Biopsy} Tissue sampling of the bronchial wall during bronchoscopy. It is used to diagnose
inflammatory conditions (sarcoidosis, eosinophilic bronchitis), infections, and
malignancy. Endobronchial biopsy of visible lesions and transbronchial biopsy of
parenchymal lesions provide complementary diagnostic information.

\medterm{Bronchial Carcinoid} Pulmonary neuroendocrine tumor arising from Kulchitsky cells of the bronchial mucosa;
subclassified as typical carcinoid (TC, generally lower grade, with fewer mitoses and no
necrosis) and atypical carcinoid (AC, generally higher grade, with more mitoses or necrosis
and greater risk of recurrence or metastasis).

\medterm{Bronchial Stenosis} Narrowing of a bronchus from trauma, infection, inflammation, or prior intervention. It
causes localized wheezing, recurrent infections, and impaired secretion clearance.
Diagnosis is by bronchoscopy. Treatment includes balloon dilation, stenting, or surgical
resection.

\medterm{Bronchial Tree} The branching airway system formed by the trachea, main bronchi, lobar and segmental
bronchi, bronchioles, and terminal airways leading toward the alveoli.

\medterm{Bronchial Vein} A vein draining portions of the bronchial walls and larger airways. It empties partly
into the azygos system and partly into pulmonary veins, creating a small physiologic
venous admixture.

\medterm{Bronchiectasis} Permanent, abnormal dilation of bronchi caused by destruction of the bronchial wall from
chronic airway inflammation and infection. Causes include CF, immune deficiency, primary
ciliary dyskinesia, and post-infectious damage. Features include chronic cough, copious
purulent sputum, and recurrent hemoptysis.

\medterm{Bronchiole} Small airways (less than 2 mm diameter) without cartilage, branching from segmental
bronchi to terminal bronchioles (conducting zone) to respiratory bronchioles
(transitional, alveoli bud from walls) to alveolar ducts to alveolar sacs to alveoli.

\medterm{Bronchiolectasis} Permanent abnormal dilation of bronchioles, usually caused by chronic inflammation,
infection, or obstruction. It may accompany bronchiectasis and contribute to impaired
mucus clearance and recurrent infection.

\medterm{Bronchiolitis} Lower respiratory tract infection causing inflammation and obstruction of small airways.
Most common cause is respiratory syncytial virus. Affects infants under 12 months.
Presents with wheezing, tachypnea, nasal flaring, and retractions. Diagnosis is
clinical.

\medterm{Bronchiolitis Obliterans} An uncommon, often irreversible obstructive small-airway disease caused by inflammation
and fibrotic narrowing or obliteration of the bronchioles. Causes include severe
infection, inhalational injury, autoimmune disease, and complications after lung or
bone-marrow transplantation.

\medterm{Bronchitis} Inflammation of the bronchial mucosa. Acute bronchitis is typically viral, presents with
cough (often productive), and resolves spontaneously. Cough with sputum production for
at least 3 months in 2 consecutive years, often due to smoking.

\medterm{Bronchoalveolar Lavage} Diagnostic procedure during bronchoscopy where saline is instilled and aspirated.
Analyzed for cell counts, microbiology, and cytology. Useful for Pneumocystis pneumonia,
pulmonary alveolar proteinosis, and sarcoidosis diagnosis.

\medterm{Bronchodilator Response} The change in spirometric airflow after administration of a short-acting bronchodilator.
A significant response can support variable airflow limitation, but a negative response
does not exclude asthma and a positive response is not specific to one disease.

\medterm{Bronchodilator Reversibility} Improvement in airflow measurements after an inhaled bronchodilator. A measurable
increase may support variable airway obstruction, but no single response proves or
excludes asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

\medterm{Bronchoesophageal Fistula} Abnormal communication between the bronchial tree and the esophagus, most commonly
acquired from malignancy, mediastinal infection, or tuberculous lymphadenitis.
Congenital forms are associated with esophageal atresia.

\medterm{Bronchogenic Cyst} A congenital cyst derived from abnormal budding of the ventral foregut. It is typically
found in the middle mediastinum or near the carina. It is usually asymptomatic but may
cause compression of adjacent structures. Treatment is surgical resection if
symptomatic.

\medterm{Bronchophony} Increased transmission of spoken sounds to the stethoscope over consolidated lung
tissue. It is normally heard over the central airways. Excessive bronchophony over
peripheral lung fields indicates consolidation. It is part of the constellation of
physical examination findings in pneumonia.

\medterm{Bronchopleural Fistula} An abnormal communication between the bronchial tree and pleural space. It is a
complication of pneumonectomy, lung surgery, or empyema. It causes persistent air leak,
subcutaneous emphysema, and respiratory compromise. Treatment includes chest tube
drainage, bronchoscopic intervention, and surgical repair.

\medterm{Bronchopneumonia} Patchy consolidation centered on bronchioles across multiple lobes. Caused by S. aureus,
gram-negative bacteria, and Pseudomonas.

\medterm{Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia} A chronic lung disease of premature infants, resulting from lung injury from mechanical
ventilation, oxygen toxicity, and inflammation. Most commonly occurs in infants born at
less than 28 weeks gestation with birth weight less than 1500 grams.

\medterm{Bronchoscopy} Endoscopic examination of the tracheobronchial tree. Flexible bronchoscopy: most common,
for diagnosis (lung mass, persistent cough, hemoptysis, suspected infection, ILD) and
therapy (foreign body removal, bronchial washing, biopsy, BAL). Rigid bronchoscopy: for
massive hemoptysis, large foreign bodies, and stent placement.

\medterm{Bronchospasm} Involuntary constriction of bronchial smooth muscle causing airway obstruction,
wheezing, and increased airway pressures. Triggers include airway manipulation, volatile
anesthetics (sevoflurane is bronchodilating; desflurane can cause bronchospasm),
histamine-releasing drugs (morphine, atracurium), and reactive airway disease.

\medterm{Bronchostenosis} Narrowing of a bronchus that restricts airflow. Causes include scarring, tumors, foreign
bodies, inflammation, external compression, or postinfectious and postprocedural
changes.

\medterm{Bronchus} One of the two main branches of the trachea that conduct air into the lungs. The right
main bronchus is wider, shorter, and more vertical than the left, making it more
susceptible to aspiration. Each main bronchus divides into lobar bronchi (secondary),
segmental bronchi (tertiary), and progressively smaller branches (bronchioles).

\medterm{Bronze Diabetes} Historical eponym for hereditary hemochromatosis (HFE-related HH, C282Y homozygous
mutation), characterized by the classic triad of skin pigmentation ("bronze"),
cirrhosis, and diabetes mellitus from pancreatic iron deposition. Also known as
"Troisier syndrome" or pigment cirrhosis.

\synonyms
"Troisier syndrome" or pigment cirrhosis.

\medterm{Brow Presentation} A fetal presentation where the fetal head is in a military position between full flexion
(vertex) and hyperextension (face). The presenting diameter is the largest
(occipitofrontal, ~13.5 cm). Incidence: 1:1000-1:2000. Vaginal delivery is impossible
with a persistent brow in a term fetus unless it converts to vertex or face.

\medterm{Brown Lung Disease} An occupational airway disease associated with inhalation of cotton, flax, or hemp dust,
classically causing byssinosis. Symptoms may include chest tightness, cough, wheezing,
and work-related airflow limitation.

\medterm{Brown Sequard Syndrome} Brown-Sequard syndrome (hemisection of the spinal cord) results from a lesion affecting
one half of the spinal cord, producing a characteristic pattern of ipsilateral and
contralateral neurological deficits.

\medterm{Brown Stain} Extrinsic staining from chromogenic bacteria, diet, tobacco. Removed by prophylaxis.

\medterm{Brucellosis} A zoonotic infection caused by Brucella bacteria, transmitted through contact with infected animals
or consumption of unpasteurized animal products. It commonly causes fever, sweats,
fatigue, and joint or muscle pain.

\medterm{Brudzinski Sign} Flexion of the hips and knees that occurs involuntarily when the neck is passively
