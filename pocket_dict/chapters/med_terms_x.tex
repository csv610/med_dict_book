
\medterm{X Chromosome} One of the two human sex chromosomes, carrying genes involved in sex development and
many non-sex-specific biological functions. Individuals typically have one or two X
chromosomes, and dosage is partly regulated by X-chromosome inactivation.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{XXX syndrome} Alternate index label; see \textit{Triple X syndrome} (trisomy X), a sex-chromosome variation in which a female has an additional X chromosome.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Inactivation} Process inactivating one X chromosome in female mammals, forming Barr body. Occurs early
in development, random in most tissues. Ensures dosage compensation.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Linked Agammaglobulinemia} An inherited immunodeficiency, usually caused by a pathogenic variant in the BTK
gene, that prevents normal B-cell maturation. It causes very low immunoglobulin levels and recurrent bacterial or
enteroviral infections, usually beginning in infancy or early childhood.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Linked Inheritance} Transmission of a pathogenic variant on the X chromosome. Phenotypes differ by sex,
X-inactivation, and variant mechanism; fathers do not transmit an X-linked variant to
their sons.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-linked retinoschisis} An inherited vitreoretinal dystrophy caused by mutations in the RS1 gene, characterized
by splitting of the retinal nerve fibre layer producing a spoke-wheel pattern of cystic
spaces at the macula and peripheral retinal schisis.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-ray} An imaging method that uses ionizing radiation to create pictures of internal body structures.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Ray (Plain Radiography)} An imaging method that uses X-rays to create a two-dimensional projection of internal
structures. It is used to assess bones, the chest, abdomen, teeth, and other selected body regions.



\textbf{Synonyms}: Plain Radiography

\medterm{X-ray (Radiography)} Projectional imaging using ionizing radiation. Dense structures (bone, contrast) appear
white; air appears black. Fast, inexpensive, widely available. Two-dimensional
projection of three-dimensional structures. Mammography: specialized breast X-ray for
screening.



\textbf{Synonyms}: Radiography

\medterm{X-ray Attenuation} Reduction in the intensity of an X-ray beam as it passes through tissue or another material.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-ray Shield} Lead apron, thyroid shield for radiation protection during imaging.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Rays (Röntgen Rays)} Electromagnetic radiation used in radiography and other imaging techniques to create images by measuring differences in tissue attenuation.



\textbf{Synonyms}: Röntgen Rays

\medterm{Xanax} A brand name for alprazolam, a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor activity and produces
anxiolytic and sedative effects. It can cause impaired coordination, dependence, and
respiratory depression, especially when combined with other sedatives.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xanthelasma} Xanthelasma palpebrarum are soft, yellow, lipid-filled papules or plaques on the medial
canthus of the eyelids (upper more commonly than lower), the most common form of
cutaneous xanthoma. They are composed of foam cells (lipid-laden macrophages) in the
superficial dermis.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitor} A class of medications that inhibit xanthine oxidase, the enzyme that catalyzes the
final steps of purine metabolism: hypoxanthine to xanthine and xanthine to uric acid. By
inhibiting this enzyme, XOIs reduce uric acid production and lower serum uric acid
levels, used for management of chronic gout and tumor lysis syndrome.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xanthogranulomatous} Chronic destructive renal infection with replacement of parenchyma by lipid-laden
macrophages. See xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xanthogranulomatous Pyelonephritis} A chronic destructive kidney infection in which renal tissue is replaced by
lipid-laden macrophages. It is often associated with obstruction or stones and may require antibiotics, drainage, surgery,
or nephrectomy depending on the extent of damage.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xanthoma} Cutaneous deposits of lipids (cholesterol, triglycerides) in the dermis or tendons,
presenting as yellow-orange papules, nodules, or plaques.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xanthomatosis} A disorder in which fatty deposits called xanthomas develop in the skin, tendons, or organs. It may be
associated with inherited or acquired lipid disorders and can signal increased cardiovascular
or organ-specific risk requiring medical evaluation.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xenobiotic} Foreign chemical substance in body. Drugs, pollutants, pesticides, industrial chemicals.
Metabolized by liver enzymes.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xenograft} Tissue or a biological material transplanted between different species, often used as a temporary graft or tissue substitute.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xenograft (Heterograft)} A surgical graft taken from a donor of a different species (xenos = foreign, Greek).
Xenografts are used as temporary biological dressings or as tissue replacements.



\textbf{Synonyms}: Heterograft

\medterm{Xeroderma} Abnormally dry skin that may cause scaling, roughness, itching, or cracking. It can result from environmental exposure,
skin disease, age, or medicines.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xeroderma Pigmentosum} A rare autosomal recessive disorder of nucleotide-excision DNA repair that causes extreme
sun sensitivity and a greatly increased risk of skin and eye damage and cancers. It is
caused by pathogenic variants in genes involved in DNA repair.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xenophobia} An intense or persistent fear of unfamiliar people, cultures, or situations; when clinically significant, it may be considered a specific phobia.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xerophthalmia} Dryness of the conjunctiva and cornea, often caused by vitamin A deficiency. Severe disease can damage
the cornea and impair vision.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xerosis} Abnormally dry skin, common in elderly patients and winter months. It results from
decreased sebaceous and sweat gland activity, impaired skin barrier function, and
environmental factors. Treatment includes regular emollient application (cream or
ointment rather than lotion), gentle cleansing, and humidification.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xerostomia} The sensation of dry mouth caused by reduced or altered saliva production. It may result from
medicines, dehydration, salivary-gland disease, radiation, or systemic illness and can impair speaking, swallowing,
taste, and dental health.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{Xerostomia (Dry Mouth)} A subjective sensation of oral dryness caused by reduced or altered saliva production.
It may result from medicines, dehydration, salivary-gland disease, radiation, or systemic
illness and can contribute to difficulty speaking, swallowing, and dental decay.



\textbf{Synonyms}: Dry Mouth

\medterm{Xylitol Toxicity} Poisoning caused by excessive xylitol exposure. It can cause marked hypoglycemia in dogs and may cause
gastrointestinal symptoms in people.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Linked Adrenoleukodystrophy} An inherited disorder of peroxisomal fatty-acid metabolism, usually affecting boys, that can cause progressive damage to the adrenal glands, nervous system, and myelin.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Linked Ichthyosis} An inherited skin disorder caused by deficiency of steroid sulfatase, producing generalized dry, dark, plate-like scaling that often begins in infancy or early childhood.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Linked Severe Combined Immunodeficiency} A severe inherited immune disorder, usually caused by defects affecting the interleukin-2 receptor gamma chain, resulting in profound T-cell dysfunction and vulnerability to serious infections.

\textbf{Synonyms}:

\medterm{X-Linked Hypophosphatemia} An inherited disorder of phosphate regulation, commonly caused by excess fibroblast growth factor 23, leading to renal phosphate wasting, rickets or osteomalacia, bone pain, and growth abnormalities.

\textbf{Synonyms}:
