\medterm{Dry Mouth Xerostomia} Alternate name; see \textit{Xerostomia}.

\synonyms
Asialia; Hyposalivation; Oral Dryness

\medterm{Factor X} A blood-clotting protein that helps start the final steps of clot formation. Too little factor X can cause prolonged or unexpected bleeding and is assessed with clotting tests and other blood studies.

\medterm{Heterograft} Alternate name; see \textit{Xenograft}.

\synonyms
Heterologous Graft; Xenotransplant; Heteroplastic Graft

\medterm{Plain Radiography} An imaging method that uses X-rays to create a two-dimensional projection of internal
structures. It is used to assess bones, the chest, abdomen, teeth, and other selected body regions.

\synonyms
Roentgenography; Projection Radiography

\medterm{Radiography} Projectional imaging that uses ionizing radiation to create a two-dimensional picture of the body. Bone and contrast material usually appear light, while air appears dark; the method is fast and useful for bones, lungs, teeth, and selected breast examinations.

\synonyms
Diagnostic Roentgenogram; X-Ray Imaging

\medterm{Roentgen Rays} Alternate name; see \textit{X-Ray}.

\synonyms
Roentgen Radiation; Electromagnetic X-Radiation

\medterm{Skeletal X-Ray} A skeletal X-ray series uses radiographs of selected bones and joints to evaluate fractures, alignment, bone density patterns, arthritis, infection, tumors, and developmental abnormalities.

\medterm{X Chromosome} One of the two types of human sex chromosomes. It carries genes involved in sex development as well as many other body functions. Most people have one or two X chromosomes.

\medterm{Xanthelasma} A benign, sharply demarcated, yellowish-orange subcutaneous plaque composed of lipid-laden, foamy histiocytes (macrophages containing esterified and free cholesterol) located within the superficial dermis of the eyelid, most commonly around the medial canthus of the upper and lower lids (xanthelasma palpebrarum). It is the most frequent form of cutaneous xanthoma. While approximately 50\% of affected individuals have normal plasma lipid levels, xanthelasma is an independent clinical marker of dyslipidemia (such as familial hypercholesterolemia), elevated LDL and ApoB levels, and an increased long-term risk of ischemic heart disease and systemic atherosclerosis. Management begins with cardiovascular risk and lipid profile evaluation, with cosmetic removal options including topical trichloroacetic acid (TCA) chemical peels, surgical excision, cryotherapy, or laser ablation (CO2 or pulsed-dye laser).

\synonyms
Xanthelasma Palpebrarum; Eyelid Xanthoma; Palpebral Xanthoma

\medterm{Xanthine} A natural breakdown product of substances found in cells and foods. It normally changes into uric acid, but can build up when a rare enzyme disorder blocks this pathway and may then contribute to kidney or urinary stones.

\medterm{Xanthine Oxidase} An enzyme that helps convert cell and food breakdown products into uric acid. Medicines used for gout can block this enzyme, lowering uric-acid production and helping prevent urate crystal deposits.

\medterm{Xanthochromia} The yellowish or amber discoloration of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) supernatant observed following centrifugation, resulting from the enzymatic breakdown of red blood cells into oxyhemoglobin, methemoglobin, and bilirubin. Spectrophotometric or visual detection of xanthochromia in CSF obtained $>6$--12 hours after symptom onset is diagnostic of subarachnoid hemorrhage.

\synonyms
CSF Xanthochromia; Cerebrospinal Fluid Yellowing

\medterm{Xanthogranulomatous} Describing inflammation in which tissue contains many immune cells filled with fat. This pattern can occur in long-lasting infections and other inflammatory conditions.

\medterm{Xanthogranulomatous Oophoritis} A rare long-lasting inflammation of an ovary, often linked to infection or endometriosis. It can form a mass that resembles ovarian cancer on scans and may cause pelvic pain, fever, or abnormal bleeding.

\medterm{Xanthogranulomatous Pyelonephritis} A severe, long-lasting kidney infection that destroys normal kidney tissue and replaces it with inflamed tissue. It is often associated with blockage or stones and may require antibiotics, drainage, or removal of the damaged kidney.

\medterm{Xanthoma} A yellow-orange papule, nodule, or plaque formed by deposits of fat-filled immune cells in the skin, tissue beneath the skin, or tendons. It may occur with high blood lipids, diabetes, cholestatic liver disease, or an inherited lipid disorder and warrants assessment of the underlying cause.

\medterm{Xanthoma Disseminatum} A rare, benign, non-Langerhans cell histiocytic disorder characterized by widespread, symmetric red-brown to yellow-orange papules and nodules localized to flexural and intertriginous skin, mucous membranes of the upper respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts, and sometimes involving the central nervous system to cause diabetes insipidus.

\synonyms
Montgomery Syndrome; Disseminate Xanthoma

\medterm{Xanthoma Eruptivum} A cutaneous manifestation of severe hypertriglyceridemia (often serum triglycerides $>1000\text{ mg/dL}$) characterized by the sudden appearance of crops of small (1--5 mm), yellow-orange papules on an erythematous base, located primarily over the buttocks, extensor surfaces of the limbs, and shoulders.

\synonyms
Eruptive Xanthoma; Eruptive Xanthomatosis

\medterm{Xanthoma Planum} Flat or slightly elevated yellowish macules or thin plaques composed of foam cell histiocytes, occurring in skin folds (intertriginous xanthomas), palmar creases (xanthoma striatum palmare, pathognomonic for dysbetalipoproteinemia/type III hyperlipoproteinemia), or generalized on the trunk and neck (associated with monoclonal gammopathies).

\synonyms
Plane Xanthoma; Xanthoma Striatum Palmare

\medterm{Xanthoma Tendinosum} Firm, slowly enlarging, non-tender subcutaneous nodules composed of lipid-laden macrophages embedded in tendons or ligaments, most commonly affecting the Achilles tendon and the extensor tendons of the hand and fingers. It is a classic clinical physical hallmark of severe heterozygous or homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia.

\synonyms
Tendon Xanthoma; Tendinous Xanthoma

\medterm{Xanthoma Tuberosum} Slowly enlarging, painless, firm, yellowish-red nodular lipid deposits occurring over extensor pressure areas, particularly the elbows, knees, heels, and buttocks. They are strongly associated with familial hypercholesterolemia and type III hyperlipoproteinemia.

\synonyms
Tuberous Xanthoma; Tuberous Xanthomatosis

\medterm{Xanthomatosis} A disorder in which fatty deposits called xanthomas develop in the skin, tendons, or organs. It may be
associated with inherited or acquired lipid disorders and can signal increased cardiovascular
or organ-specific risk requiring medical evaluation.

\medterm{Xanthopsia} A visual disturbance in which objects appear yellow or yellowish. It can occur with certain eye disorders, medication toxicity such as digoxin toxicity, or other neurologic or retinal conditions and warrants evaluation when new or persistent.

\medterm{Xanthosis} Yellow discoloration of skin or other tissues caused by carotene or another yellow substance. Unlike jaundice, it does not usually make the whites of the eyes yellow; the cause is assessed from the person’s diet and health.

\medterm{Xenobiotic} A chemical that does not naturally belong in the body, such as a medicine, pollutant, pesticide, or industrial compound. The body changes and removes it, but some resulting chemicals can be harmful.

\medterm{Xenobiotic Metabolism} The body’s processing of foreign chemicals, including medicines, pollutants, and toxins. Enzymes, especially in the liver, change them so they can be removed; the changed substances may remain active or become harmful.

\medterm{Xenobiotic Resistance} The cellular or organismal capacity to withstand the cytotoxic or therapeutic effects of foreign chemical substances, medications, or toxins. Mechanisms include enhanced metabolic breakdown (e.g., cytochrome P450 upregulation), increased cellular efflux (e.g., ATP-binding cassette transporters such as P-glycoprotein), or alteration of intracellular molecular targets.

\synonyms
Multidrug Resistance; Xenobiotic Tolerance

\medterm{Xenograft} Tissue, cells, or a biological material transplanted between different species, such as animal tissue used in a human. Rejection and infection are important risks, although some materials are used temporarily as tissue coverings or substitutes.

\medterm{Xenotransplantation} Transplanting living cells, tissues, or organs between different species, usually from an animal to a human. It may increase the supply of transplant material but carries major risks of immune rejection, infection, and poor organ function.

\medterm{Xeroderma} Alternate name; see \textit{Dry skin}.

\medterm{Xeroderma Pigmentosum} A rare inherited disorder in which cells cannot properly repair DNA damage caused by ultraviolet (UV) light. Children may develop severe sun sensitivity, sunburn, dry skin, and unusual freckling or changes in skin color after little exposure. The disorder greatly increases the risk of skin and eye-surface cancers and may cause painful light sensitivity, eye damage, or reduced vision. Some people also develop progressive nervous-system problems such as hearing loss, poor coordination, seizures, or difficulty speaking and swallowing. Most forms are inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern.

\medterm{Xerophthalmia} A progressive eye disease caused by severe vitamin A deficiency, and the leading preventable cause of blindness in children worldwide. Vitamin A is essential for seeing in low light and keeping the eye's surface moist and healthy. The disease starts with night blindness (difficulty seeing in dim light or darkness), followed by dry, hazy eyes and Bitot's spots (foamy, silvery-white patches on the white part of the eye). Without treatment, it progresses to keratomalacia, where the clear front window of the eye (cornea) softens, ulcerates, and bursts, causing permanent blindness. Prompt treatment with high-dose vitamin A capsules quickly cures the early stages and protects eyesight.
\synonyms{Vitamin A Deficiency Eye Disease; Ocular Xerosis; Keratomalacia}

\medterm{Xerophthalmic Ulceration} Severe progressive ulceration of the cornea resulting from severe vitamin A deficiency (classified as WHO stage X3A when involving less than one-third of the corneal area, and stage X3B when involving more). Untreated, it rapidly causes corneal liquefactive necrosis (keratomalacia) and permanent blindness.

\synonyms
Vitamin A Corneal Ulcer; Keratomalacic Ulceration

\medterm{Xerosis} Alternate name; see \textit{Dry skin}.

\medterm{Xerostomia} The sensation of dry mouth caused by reduced or altered saliva production. It may result from
medicines, dehydration, salivary-gland disease, radiation, or systemic illness and can impair speaking, swallowing,
taste, and dental health.

\medterm{X-Inactivation} A process in early development that switches off most genes on one of two X chromosomes in each cell. It helps balance gene activity, although some genes remain active and the pattern can differ between cells.

\medterm{Xiphisternal Joint} The synchondrosis articulation connecting the inferior border of the sternal body to the superior margin of the xiphoid process. It typically ossifies during middle to late adulthood and lies at the level of the ninth thoracic vertebra and the lower border of the thoracic cavity anteriorly.

\synonyms
Xiphisternal Synchondrosis; Xiphisternal Articulation

\medterm{Xiphoid Process} The small pointed lower part of the breastbone. Its shape varies between people and it provides attachment for some muscles and ligaments.

\medterm{Xiphoidalgia} A painful musculoskeletal syndrome characterized by localized tenderness and discomfort over the xiphoid process and xiphisternal joint, often radiating to the epigastrium, anterior chest wall, back, or shoulders. Symptoms are exacerbated by light palpation, forward bending, heavy lifting, or coughing.

\synonyms
Xiphoidodynia; Hypersensitive Xiphoid Syndrome; Xiphoid Syndrome

\medterm{X-Linked} Describing a gene or condition caused by a change on the X chromosome. The pattern differs between people with one X chromosome and those with two; fathers pass their X chromosome to daughters, not sons.

\medterm{X-Linked Adrenoleukodystrophy} An inherited disorder, usually affecting boys, in which certain fats build up and damage the adrenal glands and nervous system. It can cause behavior or learning changes, movement problems, seizures, or adrenal failure.

\medterm{X-Linked Agammaglobulinemia} An inherited immune disorder, usually affecting boys, in which the body cannot make enough antibodies. Repeated bacterial infections often begin in infancy or early childhood and are treated with antibody replacement and infection care.

\medterm{X-Linked Chronic Granulomatous Disease} The most common form of chronic granulomatous disease, caused by disease-causing variants in the \textit{CYBB} gene on the X chromosome that impair the phagocyte NADPH oxidase complex (gp91phox subunit). Phagocytes fail to generate reactive oxygen species (superoxide), predisposing boys to recurrent severe infections by catalase-positive bacteria and fungi, and inflammatory granuloma formation.

\synonyms
X-Linked CGD; CYBB Deficiency; Cytochrome b-245 Beta Deficiency

\medterm{X-Linked Emery-Dreifuss Muscular Dystrophy} A hereditary muscle disorder caused by mutations in the \textit{EMD} gene on the X chromosome, encoding the nuclear membrane protein emerin. It is characterized by the classic clinical triad of early-onset joint contractures (elbows, Achilles tendons, postcervical spine), slowly progressive humeroperoneal muscle weakness, and cardiac conduction abnormalities requiring pacemaker implantation.

\synonyms
EDMD1; Emerin Deficiency Muscular Dystrophy

\medterm{X-Linked Hypophosphatemia} An inherited disorder that causes the kidneys to lose too much phosphate. Low phosphate can lead to soft or poorly growing bones, bone pain, bowed legs, dental problems, and reduced height.

\medterm{X-Linked Ichthyosis} An inherited, usually X-linked recessive skin disorder caused by a variant or deletion affecting the STS gene and deficiency of steroid sulfatase. It usually affects males and causes widespread dry, dark, plate-like scales that often begin in infancy or early childhood.

\medterm{X-Linked Inheritance} Inheritance of a gene change located on the X chromosome. The effects depend on the gene, the type of change, and whether a person has one or two X chromosomes; fathers do not pass an X chromosome to sons.

\medterm{X-Linked Lymphoproliferative Disease Type 1} A primary immunodeficiency disorder caused by mutations in the \textit{SH2D1A} gene on the X chromosome, encoding the signaling lymphocyte activation molecule-associated protein (SAP). Hemizygous males exhibit inappropriate and uncontrolled immune responses to Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), resulting in fulminant infectious mononucleosis, hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), hypogammaglobulinemia, and malignant lymphoma.

\synonyms
XLP1; Duncan Disease; SAP Deficiency

\medterm{X-Linked Lymphoproliferative Syndrome} An inherited immune disorder, usually affecting boys, in which the body reacts abnormally to infections. Severe Epstein–Barr virus illness, persistent fever, liver or blood problems, lymphoma, and intestinal inflammation may occur.

\medterm{X-Linked Myotubular Myopathy} A rare inherited muscle disease, usually affecting boys, that causes severe weakness and low muscle tone from birth. Babies may have drooping eyelids, feeding difficulty, and dangerous weakness of the breathing muscles.

\synonyms
XLMTM; Myotubularin Deficiency

\medterm{X-Linked Retinitis Pigmentosa} A severe hereditary dystrophy of the retina caused most frequently by pathogenic variants in the \textit{RPGR} gene on the X chromosome. It causes early progressive nyctalopia (night blindness), constriction of visual fields (tunnel vision), attenuated retinal vessels, and bone-spicule retinal pigmentation, often progressing to legal blindness by early adulthood.

\synonyms
XLRP; X-Linked Pigmentary Retinopathy

\medterm{X-Linked Retinoschisis} An inherited eye disorder, usually affecting boys, in which layers of the retina separate. It can cause blurred central or distance vision, crossed eyes, bleeding, or retinal detachment in severe cases.

\medterm{X-Linked Severe Combined Immunodeficiency} A severe inherited immune disorder, usually affecting boys, in which T cells do not develop normally and antibody protection is poor. Babies are highly vulnerable to serious infections and need urgent specialist treatment.

\medterm{X-Linked Sideroblastic Anemia} An inherited microcytic, hypochromic anemia caused by mutations in the erythroid-specific delta-aminolevulinate synthase gene (\textit{ALAS2}) on the X chromosome. Defective heme synthesis leads to mitochondrial iron accumulation in developing red cell precursors, seen on bone marrow biopsy as ring sideroblasts stained with Prussian blue.

\synonyms
XLSA; ALAS2 Deficiency; Hereditary Sideroblastic Anemia

\medterm{X-Linked Spinal and Bulbar Muscular Atrophy} An adult-onset, slowly progressive lower motor neuron disease caused by an expanded CAG trinucleotide repeat in the androgen receptor (\textit{AR}) gene on the X chromosome. Affected males experience proximal muscle weakness, fasciculations (especially perioral and lingual), bulbar dysfunction (dysarthria, dysphagia), and signs of partial androgen insensitivity (gynecomastia, reduced fertility).

\synonyms
Kennedy Disease; SBMA; Bulbo-Spinal Muscular Atrophy

\medterm{X-Ray} An imaging test that uses a small amount of radiation to create a picture of structures inside the body. It is especially useful for bones, lungs, teeth, and some other tissues.

\medterm{X-Ray Attenuation} The reduction in strength of an X-ray beam as it passes through the body. Bone, soft tissue, and air reduce the beam by different amounts, creating the differences seen in the image.

\medterm{X-Ray Crystallography} An analytical biophysical technique that determines the three-dimensional atomic structure of crystallized biomolecules, such as proteins, nucleic acids, and macromolecular complexes. A beam of incident X-rays strikes the crystal, and the resulting diffraction pattern of scattered rays is measured to calculate electron density maps at atomic resolution.

\synonyms
X-Ray Crystal Diffraction Analysis; Macromolecular Crystallography

\medterm{X-Ray Shield} Protective material, usually lead or another radiation-attenuating substance, used to reduce exposure to scattered X-rays during imaging; shielding is applied according to the procedure and current radiation-safety guidance.

\medterm{X-Ray Therapy} Treatment that uses carefully aimed X-ray radiation to damage abnormal cells or relieve symptoms. It is used mainly for cancer and may be given from a machine outside the body or near the target.

\medterm{XX Male Syndrome} A difference in sex development in which a person with two X chromosomes develops typically male physical features, usually because a sex-development gene has moved onto an X chromosome. Infertility is common, and care is individualized.

\medterm{XXX Syndrome} Alternate name; see \textit{Triple X syndrome}, a sex-chromosome variation in which a female has an additional X chromosome.

\medterm{XXXX Syndrome} XXXX syndrome is a rare sex-chromosome variation in which a female has four X chromosomes; it may cause developmental or learning difficulties, distinctive physical features, and ovarian dysfunction, with severity varying widely.

\medterm{Xylitol} A sugar alcohol used as a sweetener and in some oral-care products. Large human ingestions may cause gastrointestinal symptoms; it is highly toxic to dogs.

\medterm{Xylose} A simple sugar found in some plants. A form called D-xylose has been used in tests of how well the small intestine absorbs nutrients and is also used in laboratory studies.

\medterm{Xylose Absorption Test} A specialized clinical diagnostic test used to evaluate the functional absorptive integrity of the proximal small bowel mucosa (duodenum and jejunum). A standard oral dose of D-xylose (a pentose absorbed passively without requiring pancreatic enzymes) is administered; low serum levels and reduced 5-hour urinary excretion indicate mucosal malabsorption (e.g., celiac disease, tropical sprue), whereas normal excretion in a patient with fat malabsorption points toward pancreatic exocrine insufficiency.

\synonyms
D-Xylose Absorption Test; D-Xylose Tolerance Test

\medterm{XYY Syndrome} A sex-chromosome variation in which a male has an extra Y chromosome. Many affected individuals have few or no obvious symptoms, though some have tall stature, learning difficulties, or developmental differences.
