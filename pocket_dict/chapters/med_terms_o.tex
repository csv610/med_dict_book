
\medterm{Obesity}-- Abnormal or excessive fat accumulation that presents a health risk. Defined by BMI:
$\geq30\,\mathrm{kg/m^2}$ (class I: 30-34.9, class II: 35-39.9, class III: $\geq40$). Major risk
factor for type 2 diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, sleep apnea,
osteoarthritis, certain cancers, and reduced life expectancy.

\medterm{Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome}-- Chronic daytime hypercapnia in obese individuals without other causes. Reduced chest
wall compliance and blunted respiratory drive. Often coexists with OSA. Treatment:
weight loss and BiPAP.

\medterm{Obex Region}-- The caudal tip of the fourth ventricle where it narrows into the central canal of the
spinal cord. It lies near the dorsal medulla and is an important anatomical landmark in
the floor of the ventricular system.

\medterm{Objective Response Rate}-- The proportion of patients whose tumor shrinks by a predefined amount (complete or
partial response) during treatment; a common efficacy endpoint in oncology trials.

\medterm{Oblique Plane}-- A plane that passes through the body or an organ at an angle other than a right angle
(diagonal plane).

\medterm{Obsessions}-- Recurrent, persistent, intrusive, and unwanted thoughts, urges, or images that are
experienced as intrusive and cause marked anxiety or distress. Common obsessions include
contamination fears, fears of harming oneself or others, need for symmetry, taboo
thoughts (sexual, religious, or aggressive), and doubt.

\medterm{Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder}-- Characterized by the presence of obsessions, compulsions, or both. Obsessions are
recurrent, persistent, intrusive, and unwanted thoughts, urges, or images that cause
marked anxiety or distress. The individual attempts to ignore or suppress them or
neutralize them with some other thought or action.

\medterm{Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder}-- A pattern of preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism, and mental and interpersonal
control, at the expense of flexibility and efficiency.

\medterm{Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injury}-- A third- or fourth-degree perineal tear involving the anal sphincter
complex during childbirth. It requires accurate examination, repair, follow-up, and assessment for bowel or continence
problems.

\medterm{Obstetric Ultrasound}-- Ultrasound imaging during pregnancy. First trimester: dating, viability. Second
trimester: anatomy survey. Third trimester: growth, position.

\medterm{Obstetrician}-- A medical doctor who specializes in the care of women during pregnancy, labor, and the
postpartum period (obstetrics). Obstetricians manage normal and high-risk pregnancies,
perform cesarean deliveries and assisted vaginal deliveries, and treat pregnancy
complications (pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, hemorrhage, preterm labor).

\medterm{Obstetrics}-- The branch of medicine and surgery concerned with the care of women during pregnancy,
childbirth, and the postpartum period.

\medterm{Obstructed Infected Urinary System}-- Urinary tract obstruction accompanied by infection, pyonephrosis, or sepsis. It is a
urologic emergency requiring immediate resuscitation, antimicrobial therapy, and urgent
decompression by nephrostomy or ureteral stent.

\medterm{Obstruction}-- Blockage of urinary flow. Ureteral, urethral, bladder outlet. Causes: stones, stricture,
tumor, BPH. Complications: hydronephrosis, infection, renal failure.

\medterm{Obstructive Jaundice}-- Jaundice caused by obstruction of bile flow from the liver to the duodenum, resulting in
accumulation of conjugated bilirubin in the blood. See Jaundice for complete overview.

\medterm{Obstructive Nephropathy}-- Kidney damage caused by blockage of urine flow anywhere from the kidney to the urethra. Causes
include stones, tumors, strictures, enlarged prostate, and congenital abnormalities; untreated
obstruction can lead to hydronephrosis, infection, and kidney failure.

\medterm{Obstructive parotitis}-- Inflammation of the parotid gland resulting from obstruction of the parotid duct, most
commonly by sialolithiasis, presenting with painful glandular swelling that worsens with
meals. The obstruction leads to stasis, secondary bacterial infection, and recurrent
episodes of swelling and tenderness.

\medterm{Obstructive Shock}-- A form of shock caused by physical obstruction of blood flow outside the heart, reducing
cardiac output despite normal cardiac function.

\medterm{Obstructive Sleep Apnea}-- Recurrent partial or complete upper-airway collapse during sleep, causing disrupted
breathing and often daytime symptoms. Evaluation may include sleep testing; treatment is
individualized and can include positive-airway-pressure therapy, weight management, oral
appliances, positional therapy, or surgery.

\medterm{Obstructive Uropathy}-- Urinary tract obstruction at any level from the renal pelvis to the urethra, leading to
impaired urine flow and potential renal damage. Causes include nephrolithiasis, benign
prostatic hyperplasia, ureteral strictures, tumors of the bladder, prostate, or cervix,
and external compression from lymphadenopathy or retroperitoneal fibrosis.

\medterm{Obturator}-- A structure that occludes an opening. In anatomy, the obturator foramen is a large
opening in the pelvic bone, closed by the obturator membrane. The obturator nerve
(L2--L4) innervates the adductor muscles of the thigh and provides sensory innervation
to the medial thigh. The obturator artery supplies the adductor compartment.

\medterm{Obturator hernia}-- Herniation of abdominal contents through the obturator canal, most commonly occurring in
thin elderly women with a history of multiple pregnancies. It is a rare cause of small
bowel obstruction and may present with the Howship-Romberg sign of medial thigh pain
from obturator nerve compression.

\medterm{Obtuse Marginal Artery}-- A branch of the circumflex coronary artery that runs along the obtuse margin of the
heart and supplies the lateral wall of the left ventricle. Its size and number vary with
coronary anatomy.

\medterm{Occipital Bone}-- Forms the posterior-inferior cranium surrounding the foramen magnum (spinal cord
passage). Has basilar, lateral, and squamous parts. Occipital condyles articulate with
C1 for nodding movement. The external occipital protuberance provides muscle attachment.

\medterm{Occipital Lobe}-- The most posterior of the four cerebral lobes, located behind the parieto-occipital
sulcus and above the tentorium cerebelli. The occipital lobe is the primary visual
processing center of the brain.

\medterm{Occipital Neuralgia}-- A paroxysmal, severe, stabbing or electric shock-like pain in the distribution of the
greater, lesser, or third occipital nerves (posterior scalp, from the nuchal line to the
vertex).

\medterm{Occiput Posterior Position}-- A fetal head position in which the occiput faces the maternal sacrum. It may
cause back pain or slow labor, although the fetus may rotate during labor.

\medterm{Occiput Transverse Position}-- The fetal head presenting with the sagittal suture oriented transversely. Usually the
normal descent position; most rotate to OA with contractions. When persistent, it can
cause protracted descent and prolonged second stage.

\medterm{Occlusal Guard}-- Appliance protecting teeth from attrition. Night guard for bruxism. Sports mouthguard.

\medterm{Occlusion}-- Complete blockage of vessel. May be natural (thrombosis) or iatrogenic (embolization).

\medterm{Occult Blood}-- Blood that is not visible to the naked eye and is detected by chemical or immunochemical testing. Fecal
occult-blood tests may be used in colorectal-cancer screening; the test type and collection
instructions determine whether dietary or medicine restrictions apply.

\medterm{Occult Gastrointestinal Bleeding}-- Chronic or intermittent gastrointestinal blood loss that is not visible to the patient
and may not produce melaena or haematochezia. It commonly presents with iron-deficiency
anaemia or a positive faecal blood test.

\medterm{Occupational Asthma}-- Asthma from workplace exposures to sensitizers (isocyanates, flour, latex) or irritants.
Improves away from work and worsens at work. Serial peak flow monitoring supports
diagnosis.

\medterm{Occupational COPD}-- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease associated with workplace exposure to dust, chemicals,
fumes, or other inhaled hazards. Symptoms and risk depend on the exposure and other factors, including smoking.

\medterm{Occupational Dermatoses}-- Work-related skin diseases. Occupational contact dermatitis accounts for 90 percent of
occupational skin diseases. Irritant contact dermatitis is more common (hands, wet work)
than allergic contact dermatitis. Occupations at risk include healthcare workers,
hairdressers, construction workers, food handlers, and metalworkers.

\medterm{Occupational Exposure}-- Radiation exposure to nuclear medicine staff. Monitored with dosimeters. Limits: 50
mSv/year whole body, 150 mSv/year lens, 500 mSv/year extremities.

\medterm{Occupational Exposure Limit}-- A regulatory or advisory concentration or dose limit intended to reduce worker exposure
to a hazardous substance over a defined averaging period. Limits vary by substance,
jurisdiction, route, and whether the value is a ceiling, short-term limit, or
time-weighted average.

\medterm{Occupational Health}-- Health of workers. Examples: workplace safety, occupational diseases, ergonomics.

\medterm{Occupational Hearing Loss}-- Permanent sensorineural hearing impairment caused by repeated or prolonged exposure to
workplace noise or ototoxic hazards. Prevention depends on reducing exposure and using
effective hearing protection.

\medterm{Occupational Medicine}-- The medical specialty concerned with the effects of work on health, prevention of work-related illness and injury, and safe return
to work.

\medterm{Occupational Rehabilitation}-- Services that help a person regain or adapt the skills needed for work after illness,
injury, or disability.

\medterm{Occupational Therapy}-- OT helps patients perform daily activities (ADLs, IADLs) through therapeutic activities,
adaptive equipment, environmental modification. Focus on upper extremity function,
cognition, sensory integration.

\medterm{Occupational Therapy Assessment}-- Evaluation of a person's ability to perform daily activities, work, and other meaningful
occupations, including factors affecting performance and the need for adaptive strategies or equipment.

\medterm{OCD}-- Obsessive-compulsive disorder, a condition involving recurrent unwanted thoughts or urges and/or repetitive behaviors or mental acts that cause distress or interfere with daily life.

\medterm{Ochronosis}-- A blue-black or brownish pigmentation of connective tissues (cartilage, skin, sclerae,
tendons) caused by accumulation of homogentisic acid and its oxidation products.

\medterm{Octreoscan}-- Nuclear medicine imaging using In-111 octreotide. Detects neuroendocrine tumors.

\medterm{Ocular Histoplasmosis Syndrome}-- Ocular disease following prior subclinical Histoplasma capsulatum infection,
characterized by punched-out peripheral chorioretinal scars, peripapillary atrophy, and
choroidal neovascularization that causes central vision loss. Active choroidal
neovascularization is treated with anti-VEGF therapy.

\medterm{Ocular Hypertension}-- Persistently elevated intraocular pressure without demonstrable glaucomatous optic-nerve
damage or corresponding visual-field loss. It increases future glaucoma risk and
requires individualized assessment of corneal thickness, optic-nerve appearance,
pressure, and other risk factors.

\medterm{Ocular myiasis}-- Infestation of the eye or ocular adnexa by fly larvae, which may involve the
conjunctiva, cornea, or intraocular structures, presenting with pain, irritation,
tearing, and visual disturbance. In severe cases, larvae can penetrate the globe causing
endophthalmitis and blindness.

\medterm{Ocular Pain}-- Pain in or around the eye caused by surface irritation, infection, inflammation, trauma, glaucoma, or orbital
disease. Severe pain with vision loss, light sensitivity, nausea, or injury requires urgent
ophthalmic assessment.

\medterm{Ocular Surface Disease}-- A group of disorders affecting the cornea, conjunctiva, eyelids, and tear film,
including dry eye, blepharitis, meibomian-gland dysfunction, and exposure-related
disease. Symptoms and signs reflect tear-film instability, inflammation, epithelial
injury, or abnormal lid function.

\medterm{Oculocerebrorenal Syndrome}-- An inherited peroxisomal disorder, also called Zellweger spectrum disorder, affecting
the brain, eyes, kidneys, liver, and other organs. Severe forms present neonatally with
hypotonia, seizures, feeding difficulty, distinctive facial features, and profound
developmental impairment.

\medterm{Odds Ratio}-- Ratio of odds of exposure in cases vs. controls. Used in case-control studies. OR
approximates RR for rare diseases.

\medterm{Odontogenic cutaneous fistula}-- An abnormal tract connecting the skin surface to a periapical dental infection,
typically presenting as a draining sinus tract on the chin, cheek, or submandibular
region. It results from chronic dental infection eroding through the bone and soft
tissue to the skin surface.

\medterm{Odontogenic Keratocyst}-- Aggressive cyst with high recurrence. Associated with nevoid basal cell carcinoma
syndrome (Gorlin syndrome). Treatment: enucleation, resection, or decompression.

\medterm{Odontoid Process}-- A tooth-like projection superiorly from the body of the axis (C2 vertebra). The odontoid
process projects into the atlas (C1) and is held in place by the transverse ligament of
the atlas, allowing rotation of the head (the atlantoaxial joint accounts for ~50\% of
cervical rotation).

\medterm{Odontoma}-- Hamartomatous dental tissue mass. Compound (multiple tooth-like structures) or complex
(amorphous calcified mass). Treatment: surgical removal.

\medterm{Odor Threshold}-- Lowest concentration detectable by smell. Important for warning properties of toxins.

\medterm{Odynophagia}-- Painful swallowing, characterized by a burning, squeezing, or sticking sensation behind
the sternum during deglutition. Strongly suggests mucosal inflammation or ulceration of
the esophagus.

\medterm{Oesophageal Ulcer}-- A mucosal ulceration of the oesophagus caused by gastro-oesophageal reflux, infection,
medication or corrosive injury, inflammatory disease, or malignancy. It may cause
odynophagia, dysphagia, chest pain, haematemesis, or melaena.

\medterm{Oesophageal Varices}-- Enlarged veins in the esophagus, usually caused by portal hypertension. They can rupture and cause
life-threatening bleeding.

\medterm{Oesophagitis}-- Inflammation of the oesophageal mucosa. Common causes include gastro-oesophageal reflux,
infection in immunocompromised patients, medication-related injury, eosinophilic
inflammation, radiation, and caustic ingestion. Symptoms include heartburn, odynophagia,
dysphagia, and chest pain.

\medterm{Oestrogen}-- The primary female sex hormone, produced mainly by the ovaries (granulosa cells of
developing follicles) and in smaller amounts by adipose tissue, the adrenal glands, and
the placenta during pregnancy.

\medterm{Oestrogen Replacement Therapy}-- The administration of exogenous estrogen (with or without progestogen) to relieve
symptoms of estrogen deficiency and prevent long-term health consequences in
postmenopausal women. See Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT).

\medterm{Off-Label Use}-- The use of a licensed medication for an indication, dose, route, or patient population
not included in its marketing authorisation (product licence).

\medterm{Ogilvie Syndrome}-- Acute colonic pseudo-obstruction characterized by massive colonic dilation in the
absence of mechanical obstruction typically occurring in hospitalized patients with
serious comorbidities including post-surgical states, severe infections, and medication
effects.

\medterm{Ogilvie’s syndrome}-- Acute colonic pseudo-obstruction characterized by massive dilation of the colon,
particularly the cecum and right colon, without mechanical obstruction. It occurs in
hospitalized patients with serious medical or surgical conditions, often
postoperatively, and results from autonomic nervous system imbalance.

\medterm{Ointment}-- A semi-solid, greasy topical preparation for application to the skin, mucous membranes,
or eyes. Ointments are typically composed of a hydrophobic base (petrolatum, lanolin,
paraffin, beeswax, vegetable oils) and may contain dissolved or suspended active
pharmaceutical ingredients.

\medterm{Okihiro Syndrome}-- A congenital disorder, usually caused by pathogenic variants in SALL4, combining
radial-ray limb abnormalities with Duane retraction anomaly or other eye-movement
defects. Hearing, cardiac, renal, and vertebral anomalies may also occur.

\medterm{Oleothorax}-- Historical surgical obliteration of the pleural space using infused oil to collapse the
lung, once used for tuberculosis treatment before effective chemotherapy, now largely
obsolete.

\medterm{Olfactory Nerve}-- Cranial nerve I (CN I), responsible for the sense of smell. Olfactory receptor neurons
in the olfactory epithelium of the nasal roof project axons through the cribriform plate
of the ethmoid bone to the olfactory bulb, then via the olfactory tract to the primary
olfactory cortex (piriform cortex, amygdala, entorhinal cortex).

\medterm{Oligoclonal Bands}-- Immunoglobulin G (IgG) proteins produced by a limited number of B-cell clones in the
cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), detected by isoelectric focusing of CSF and serum samples.

\medterm{Oligodendroglioma}-- A slow-growing glioma arising from oligodendrocytes, most often in the cerebral
hemispheres. Associated with IDH mutation and 1p/19q codeletion, which confer a better
prognosis and chemosensitivity.

\medterm{Oligohydramnios}-- Insufficient amniotic fluid volume, defined as amniotic fluid index less than 5 cm or
single deepest pocket less than 2 cm. Causes include fetal renal anomalies, post-term
pregnancy, premature rupture of membranes, maternal dehydration, and placental
insufficiency.

\medterm{Oligometastasis}-- A limited number of metastatic deposits, often defined as up to five, amenable to
aggressive local treatment (surgery, stereotactic radiation, or ablation) with the
potential for prolonged control.

\medterm{Oliguria}-- Reduced urine output, defined as <400 mL/day or <0.5 mL/kg/hour in adults. Oliguria is
an important sign of acute kidney injury (AKI) and prerenal azotaemia.

\medterm{Olive Pontocerebellar Atrophy}-- A group of neurodegenerative disorders characterized by progressive loss of neurons in
the inferior olives, pons, and cerebellum. They cause worsening ataxia, dysarthria,
incoordination, and sometimes pyramidal, autonomic, or cognitive features.

\medterm{Omadacycline}-- A tetracycline-related antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit. It is
used for selected bacterial respiratory and skin infections; resistance, adverse effects,
and local prescribing guidance influence its use.

\medterm{Omega-3}-- Polyunsaturated fatty acids (EPA, DHA, ALA). Anti-inflammatory, cardiovascular benefits.
Sources: fatty fish, flaxseed, walnuts. Supplementation common.

\medterm{Omega-6}-- Polyunsaturated fatty acids (linoleic acid). Pro-inflammatory in excess. Balance with
omega-3 important. Sources: vegetable oils, nuts, seeds.

\medterm{Omenn Syndrome}-- A severe combined immunodeficiency caused by hypomorphic defects affecting
lymphocyte-receptor gene rearrangement or related pathways. It presents in infancy with
erythroderma, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, diarrhea, failure to thrive, and
recurrent or opportunistic infections.

\medterm{Omentectomy}-- Removal of part or all of the greater omentum, commonly performed during staging and
cytoreduction for ovarian and other intra-abdominal cancers.

\medterm{Omentum}-- A double layer of peritoneum connecting the stomach to other abdominal organs. The
greater omentum (gastrocolic omentum) hangs from the greater curvature of the stomach,
covers the transverse colon and small intestine, and is rich in fat and immune cells
(milky spots—aggregates of macrophages and lymphocytes).

\medterm{Omeprazole}-- A proton pump inhibitor (PPI) that irreversibly inhibits the gastric H+/K+ ATPase
(proton pump) on parietal cells, profoundly reducing acid secretion. Used for GERD,
peptic ulcer disease, H. pylori eradication (triple therapy), Zollinger-Ellison
syndrome, and stress ulcer prophylaxis.

\medterm{Omphalitis}-- Infection of the umbilical stump in neonates, typically occurring within the first 1--2
weeks of life. Omphalitis is a potentially serious condition requiring prompt treatment
due to the risk of spread to the liver (portal vein thrombosis, portal hypertension) and
peritoneum via the umbilical vessels.

\medterm{Omphalocele}-- A congenital abdominal wall defect where abdominal organs herniate into the base of the
umbilical cord, covered by a peritoneal-amniotic membrane. Associated with chromosomal
abnormalities (trisomies 13, 18) and other anomalies. Differentiated from gastroschisis
by membrane coverage and midline location.

\medterm{Onchocerca volvulus}-- A filarial nematode that causes onchocerciasis (river blindness), transmitted by
repeated bites of infected blackflies (Simulium species) breeding near fast-flowing
rivers. Adult worms reside in subcutaneous nodules (onchocercomata), where they can
survive for years, producing microfilariae that migrate through the skin and eyes.

\medterm{Onchocerciasis}-- A parasitic disease caused by the filarial nematode Onchocerca volvulus, transmitted by
repeated bites of infected blackflies (Simulium species) breeding near fast-flowing
rivers. Adult worms form subcutaneous nodules (onchocercomata).

\medterm{Oncocytoma}-- A usually benign epithelial tumor, commonly arising in the kidney and composed of cells
with abundant eosinophilic mitochondria-rich cytoplasm.

\medterm{Oncogene}-- A gene that, when activated, overexpressed, or altered, can promote uncontrolled cell
proliferation or survival. Oncogenes commonly arise from normal proto-oncogenes through
mutation, amplification, translocation, or abnormal regulation.

\medterm{Oncogenes}-- Genes that, when activated or altered, can promote uncontrolled cell growth and survival. They often arise from changes in
normal proto-oncogenes involved in growth signaling.

\medterm{Oncogenic}-- Capable of promoting the development of cancer.

\medterm{Oncologic Emergency}-- A cancer-related condition that threatens life, organ function, or neurologic integrity
and requires urgent treatment. Examples include malignant spinal-cord compression,
superior vena cava syndrome, tumor lysis syndrome, hypercalcemia, airway obstruction,
and febrile neutropenia.

\medterm{Oncologist}-- A physician who specializes in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer. Includes medical
oncologists (systemic therapy), radiation oncologists (radiotherapy), and surgical
oncologists (operative management).

\medterm{Oncology PET}-- PET, often combined with CT, used in selected cancers to assess disease distribution, treatment response, or recurrence.

\medterm{Oncology Rehabilitation}-- Cancer rehabilitation: fatigue, pain, neuropathy, lymphedema, functional impairment.
Pre- and post-treatment support.

\medterm{Oncolytic Virus}-- A genetically modified virus that selectively replicates in and destroys cancer cells
while sparing normal tissue, e.g., talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC) for melanoma.

\medterm{Oncosis}-- A form of cell death characterized by cellular swelling, derived from the Greek word
onkos meaning swelling, representing a pre-lethal state that precedes necrosis. It is
typically caused by ischemic injury leading to ATP depletion, which impairs the
sodium-potassium pump and causes osmotic water influx.

\medterm{Oncotic Pressure}-- The osmotic pressure exerted by plasma proteins (predominantly albumin) that do not
freely cross the capillary wall. Normal plasma oncotic pressure is approximately 25-28
mmHg. It opposes the hydrostatic pressure that drives fluid out of capillaries, favoring
fluid reabsorption at the venular end.

\medterm{One Health}-- Approach recognizing health connections between humans, animals, environment. Examples:
zoonotic diseases, antibiotic resistance, food safety.

\medterm{One-Hour Glucose Challenge Test}-- A screening test for gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) performed at 24-28 weeks. The
patient drinks 50 g of glucose (regardless of fasting state), and plasma glucose is
measured at 1 hour. Thresholds vary by protocol; a result in the range of 130--140 mg/dL
positive, warranting diagnostic 3-hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT).

\medterm{Onycholysis}-- Separation of the nail plate from the underlying nail bed, beginning distally or
laterally. Causes include trauma, psoriasis, fungal infection, thyroid disease,
medications, and chemical exposure; the underlying cause should be identified.

\medterm{Onychomycosis}-- Fungal nail infection, most commonly caused by dermatophytes (Trichophyton rubrum),
affecting toenails more than fingernails. It presents as nail thickening, discoloration
(yellow-brown), subungual debris, and nail separation. Diagnosis is confirmed by KOH
preparation or fungal culture.

\medterm{Oocyte}-- The female gamete (egg cell) produced in the ovary through oogenesis. Oocytes develop
from primordial germ cells, which undergo mitosis to form oogonia, then enter meiosis I
before birth and arrest in prophase I until puberty.

\medterm{Oophorectomy}-- Surgical removal of one (unilateral) or both (bilateral) ovaries.

\medterm{Oophoritis}-- Inflammation of an ovary, usually associated with pelvic infection or, less commonly, autoimmune disease.
It may cause lower abdominal or pelvic pain, fever, abnormal discharge, or menstrual changes;
severe pain requires assessment to exclude ovarian torsion or abscess.


\medterm{Opacity}-- Lack of transparency of a normally transparent ocular structure (cornea, lens, vitreous,
or aqueous humour). Corneal opacity results from scarring (trauma, infection—herpes
simplex keratitis, trachoma), dystrophies (Fuchs endothelial dystrophy,
granular/lattice/macular dystrophies), or edema.

\medterm{Open Fractures}-- Fractures in which the broken bone or fracture hematoma communicates with an external wound. They have increased risks of
infection and soft-tissue injury and require urgent assessment and specialized treatment.

\medterm{Open Reduction and Internal Fixation}-- Operative exposure and realignment of a fracture followed by fixation with implants such
as plates, screws, wires, or nails. It is used when closed treatment cannot provide
acceptable alignment, stability, or joint restoration.

\medterm{Open Surgery}-- Traditional surgical approach with incision. Used for bypass, aneurysm repair,
thrombectomy.

\medterm{Open-book fracture}-- A pelvic ring disruption with diastasis of the pubic symphysis or sacroiliac joints,
often from high-energy trauma, carrying risk of massive hemorrhage and organ injury.

\medterm{Open-Heart Surgery}-- Any surgical procedure involving opening the chest wall (sternotomy or thoracotomy) and
operating on the heart, usually with cardiopulmonary bypass (the heart--lung machine).

\medterm{Operative Vaginal Delivery}-- Vaginal delivery assisted by vacuum or forceps. Indications: prolonged second stage,
suspected fetal compromise, need to shorten the second stage for maternal benefit
(exhaustion, cardiac disease, hypertensive crisis).

\medterm{Ophthalmic Emergency}-- An eye or visual condition requiring urgent evaluation to prevent irreversible vision
loss or severe ocular injury. Examples include acute angle closure, retinal detachment,
central retinal artery occlusion, endophthalmitis, chemical injury, orbital cellulitis,
and open-globe trauma.

\medterm{Ophthalmomyiasis}-- Infestation of the eye by fly larvae (most commonly Oestridae), which may be superficial
(conjunctival) or deep (intraocular), with the latter carrying a risk of retinal
detachment, endophthalmitis, and permanent vision loss.

\medterm{Ophthalmoplegia}-- Weakness or paralysis of one or more eye muscles, causing limited eye movement, double vision, or abnormal
eye position. Causes include nerve disorders, stroke, diabetes, thyroid disease, trauma, and
neuromuscular conditions.

\medterm{Ophthalmoscope}-- Instrument for examining retina and optic nerve. Direct: larger, brighter image.
Indirect: wider field. Essential for fundoscopic examination.

\medterm{Ophthalmoscopy}-- Examination of the interior of the eye, especially the retina, optic disc, and retinal
blood vessels, using an ophthalmoscope. It helps detect retinal disease, optic-nerve
abnormalities, glaucoma, and systemic vascular disease.

\medterm{Opiate}-- A natural alkaloid derived from the opium poppy (Papaver somniferum) or a semi-synthetic
derivative that binds to opioid receptors.

\medterm{Opioid Agonist Treatment}-- Treatment of opioid use disorder with a prescribed opioid agonist or partial agonist,
most commonly methadone or buprenorphine, together with psychosocial and medical
support. It reduces withdrawal, craving, illicit opioid use, overdose, and mortality.

\medterm{Opioid Analgesics}-- A class of drugs that bind to opioid receptors in the central and peripheral nervous
systems, producing analgesia and other effects.

\medterm{Opioid Antagonist}-- A medicine that blocks opioid receptors and can reverse opioid effects such as respiratory depression.
Naloxone is a commonly used example.

\medterm{Opioid Overdose}-- A medical emergency characterized by the classic triad of respiratory depression, miosis
(pinpoint pupils), and decreased level of consciousness. It can progress to respiratory
arrest, cardiovascular collapse, and death.

\medterm{Opioid Reversal}-- Emergency treatment of opioid toxicity with naloxone, a competitive opioid-receptor
antagonist. It is titrated to restore adequate breathing, with repeated doses or monitoring sometimes needed because
its effect may wear off before the opioid.

\medterm{Opioid Toxicity}-- Toxic effects of opioid agonists, classically respiratory depression, central nervous
system depression, and miosis. Management prioritizes ventilation and airway support;
naloxone is titrated to restore adequate breathing while recognizing the risk of
recurrent toxicity and withdrawal.

\medterm{Opioid Use Disorder}-- A problematic pattern of opioid use leading to significant impairment or distress.
Opioids include heroin, fentanyl, morphine, oxycodone, and hydrocodone. Criteria are the
same eleven as for substance use disorders generally. Overdose presents with respiratory
depression, miosis, and decreased level of consciousness.

\medterm{Opioids}-- A class of drugs that bind to opioid receptors in the central and peripheral nervous
systems, producing analgesia and other effects. See Opioid Analgesics.

\medterm{Opisthotonos}-- Severe backward arching of the head, neck, and spine caused by sustained extensor muscle
spasm. It is classically associated with tetanus but may also occur in meningitis,
seizures, or severe brain injury.

\medterm{Opportunistic Infection}-- An infection caused by organisms that ordinarily do not cause disease in immunocompetent
hosts but cause significant illness when host defences are impaired.

\medterm{Opposition}-- Movement of the thumb across the palm to contact the fingertips. It combines abduction,
flexion, and medial rotation at the first carpometacarpal joint.

\medterm{Oppositional Defiant Disorder}-- A childhood/adolescent disorder characterized by a persistent pattern (\(\geq 6\) months) of
angry/irritable mood, argumentative/defiant behavior, and vindictiveness.

\medterm{Opsoclonus}-- Rapid, involuntary, multidirectional, conjugate eye movements without an intersaccadic
interval. It may occur with myoclonus and ataxia as part of an autoimmune or
paraneoplastic syndrome, particularly in children with neuroblastoma.

\medterm{Opsonization}-- The process by which pathogens or particles are coated with opsonins (antibodies—IgG,
complement—C3b, or acute-phase proteins) that enhance recognition and phagocytosis by
phagocytes bearing corresponding receptors (Fc receptors, complement receptors).

\medterm{Optic Atrophy}-- End-stage degeneration of the optic nerve fibers resulting from any optic neuropathy.
Causes: glaucoma, ischemic optic neuropathy, multiple sclerosis, compressive lesions
(pituitary tumor, aneurysm), toxins (methanol, ethambutol), and hereditary (Leber
hereditary optic neuropathy).

\medterm{Optic Disc}-- The point on the retina where the optic nerve exits the eye, lacking photoreceptors and
creating the natural blind spot.

\medterm{Optic Disc Edema}-- Swelling of the optic-nerve head caused by axoplasmic-flow disruption. Bilateral
optic-disc edema from raised intracranial pressure is papilledema; unilateral or
non-pressure causes require separate evaluation for optic-nerve, retinal, inflammatory,
ischemic, or infiltrative disease.

\medterm{Optic Glioma}-- A usually slow-growing glioma of the optic nerve or optic pathway, occurring most often
in children. It may cause progressive visual loss, optic atrophy, proptosis, or
visual-field defects and is associated with neurofibromatosis type 1.

\medterm{Optic Nerve}-- CN II exiting through the optic disc (blind spot), passing through the optic canal, and
partially decussating at the optic chiasm. Technically a CNS tract with oligodendrocyte
myelination and meningeal coverings, making it susceptible to demyelinating disease
(optic neuritis in MS).

\medterm{Optic Nerve Atrophy}-- Pallor and degeneration of the optic nerve caused by loss of retinal ganglion-cell
axons. It produces permanent visual impairment and may result from ischemia,
inflammation, compression, glaucoma, trauma, or raised intracranial pressure.

\medterm{Optic Neuritis}-- Inflammation or demyelination of the optic nerve, often causing subacute vision loss, impaired color vision, and pain with
eye movement. It may be associated with multiple sclerosis or other inflammatory,
infectious, or immune-mediated disorders.

\medterm{Optical Coherence Tomography}-- A non-invasive imaging technique that uses low-coherence interferometry to produce
high-resolution, cross-sectional images of the retina and other ocular structures,
providing quantitative measurements of retinal layer thickness and detecting structural
abnormalities with micrometer-level resolution.

\medterm{Optometry}-- A healthcare profession concerned with examining the eyes and visual system for defects
and abnormalities, prescribing corrective lenses (spectacles, contact lenses),
diagnosing eye disease, and providing pre- and post-operative care for surgical eye
treatments.

\medterm{Oral Cancer}-- Malignancy of oral cavity, including lips, tongue, floor of mouth, buccal mucosa,
palate, tonsils, oropharynx. Major risk factors: tobacco use, alcohol consumption, human
papillomavirus (HPV, especially p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer). Presentation:
persistent ulcer, leukoplakia, erythroplakia, mass, pain, difficulty swallowing.

\medterm{Oral Candidiasis}-- Infection or overgrowth of Candida species on the oral mucosa, often presenting as
removable white plaques, erythematous patches, or angular cheilitis. Risk factors
include antibiotics, inhaled corticosteroids, immunosuppression, diabetes, xerostomia,
and dentures.

\medterm{Oral Cavity}-- The space extending from the lips to the oropharynx, bounded by the cheeks, palate, and
tongue. It receives food, begins mechanical and chemical digestion, and contributes to
speech.

\medterm{Oral Cavity Cancer}-- Squamous cell carcinoma of the lip, tongue, buccal mucosa, floor of mouth, or palate,
associated with tobacco, alcohol, and betel quid use. Treatment is primarily surgical
resection, with adjuvant radiation or chemoradiation for adverse features.

\medterm{Oral Contraceptive}-- Birth control pill: combined oral contraceptive (COC—ethinylestradiol + progestin) or
progestin-only pill (POP/minipill). Mechanisms: ovulation inhibition, cervical mucus
thickening, endometrial change.

\medterm{Oral Contraceptives}-- Hormonal medicines taken by mouth to prevent pregnancy. They may contain an estrogen and
progestogen or a progestogen alone; selection depends on health conditions, preferences, interactions, and reproductive
goals.

\medterm{Oral Glucose Tolerance Test}-- A diagnostic test measuring plasma glucose response after a standardized oral glucose
load. It is used in selected evaluations of diabetes, impaired glucose tolerance, and
gestational diabetes when fasting glucose or HbA1c is insufficient.

\medterm{Oral Hygiene}-- Practices maintaining oral health: brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste,
flossing daily, mouthwash (optional), regular dental visits. Proper brushing technique:
soft brush, 45-degree angle to gumline, gentle circular motions. Interdental cleaning
essential for plaque removal.

\medterm{Oral Hypoglycemic}-- A class of drugs used to treat type 2 diabetes (with lifestyle).

\medterm{Oral Leukoplakia}-- A white patch or plaque on the oral mucosa that cannot be scraped off and cannot be
characterized as any other diagnosable disease, considered a potentially malignant
disorder. Risk factors: tobacco use (most common), alcohol, and betel nut chewing. Most
common sites: buccal mucosa, alveolar ridge, and tongue.

\medterm{Oral Lichen Planus}-- Chronic inflammatory condition. Reticular (lace-like white lines), erosive (painful
ulcers). Associated with hepatitis C. Malignant transformation rare but possible.

\medterm{Oral Medicine}-- Specialty diagnosing oral mucosal diseases, dental causes of facial pain, oral
manifestations of systemic disease.

\medterm{Oral Mucosa}-- The moist stratified squamous epithelial lining of the oral cavity. It includes
masticatory, lining, and specialized mucosa and provides barrier protection, sensation,
lubrication, and sites for minor salivary glands.

\medterm{Oral Nutritional Supplement}-- A calorie-, protein-, or micronutrient-containing product consumed by mouth to
supplement, not automatically replace, usual food intake. The product and dose should
match the patient's needs, swallowing ability, disease restrictions, and tolerance.

\medterm{Oral Pathology}-- Study of diseases affecting oral tissues. Includes neoplasms, cysts, inflammatory
conditions, developmental anomalies.

\medterm{Oral Polio Vaccine}-- A live attenuated poliovirus vaccine administered by mouth. Its formulation and use
depend on the public-health program and circulating poliovirus strains; it has played an important role in polio control.

\medterm{Oral Radiology}-- Specialty of producing and interpreting images of oral and maxillofacial region.

\medterm{Oral Sex (Fellatio, Cunnilingus)}-- Oral stimulation of the genitals for sexual pleasure. Fellatio: oral stimulation of the
penis. Cunnilingus: oral stimulation of the vulva and clitoris. Analingus (rimming):
oral stimulation of the anus.

\medterm{Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma}-- A malignant neoplasm arising from oral stratified squamous epithelium. Persistent
induration, ulceration, red or white patches, unexplained bleeding, pain, or neck mass
requires evaluation and biopsy; staging incorporates tumor, nodal, and metastatic
findings.

\medterm{Oral Surgery}-- Surgical procedures in oral and maxillofacial region. Extractions, implant placement,
biopsies, cyst removal, corrective jaw surgery.

\medterm{Oral Toxicity}-- Harm caused by swallowing a toxic substance, medicine, or excessive dose.

\medterm{Oral Ulcer}-- Ulceration of oral mucosa. Aphthous ulcer (canker sore) most common. Traumatic, viral,
autoimmune causes.

\medterm{Orbital Cellulitis}-- A serious infection of the orbital tissues posterior to the orbital septum, potentially
sight-threatening and life-threatening due to the risk of optic nerve compression,
cavernous sinus thrombosis, and intracranial extension.

\medterm{Orbital metastasis}-- Secondary cancer deposits in the orbit, most commonly from breast, lung, or prostate
primaries. Symptoms include proptosis, diplopia, pain, and visual impairment.

\medterm{Orbital Pseudotumor}-- Noninfectious, idiopathic inflammation of orbital tissues, also called idiopathic
orbital inflammation. It can cause painful eye movement, proptosis, eyelid swelling,
diplopia, and restricted ocular motility.

\medterm{Orchiectomy}-- Surgical removal of one or both testicles. Radical inguinal orchiectomy is diagnostic
and therapeutic for testicular cancer; bilateral orchiectomy is a hormonal therapy for
prostate cancer.

\medterm{Orchitis}-- Inflammation of the testis, often associated with epididymitis (epididymo-orchitis).

\medterm{Orexin Receptor Antagonists}-- Medications that block orexin (hypocretin) receptors to promote sleep. Suvorexant and
lemborexant are approved for insomnia. They reduce wakefulness by blocking the arousal
system. They have lower abuse potential than benzodiazepines and do not cause
respiratory depression.

\medterm{Orf}-- A zoonotic viral skin infection caused by the parapoxvirus, transmitted to humans from
infected sheep and goats (direct contact with lesions, fomites—virus survives in scabs
for months). Orf is an occupational disease of farmers, butchers, and veterinarians.
Incubation: 3--7 days.

\medterm{Organ}-- A structure composed of two or more different types of tissues that has a recognizable
shape and performs specific functions.

\medterm{Organ System}-- A group of related organs that work together to perform major body functions.

\medterm{Organ Transplants}-- The surgical transfer of an organ from a donor to a recipient whose organ function is severely
impaired. Solid-organ transplantation may involve the kidney, liver, heart, lung, pancreas,
or intestine and requires donor assessment, recipient selection, and ongoing follow-up.

\medterm{Organophosphate}-- A class of chemicals that can inhibit acetylcholinesterase. Some are pesticides or nerve agents and can cause cholinergic poisoning.

\medterm{Organophosphate Poisoning}-- Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors. SLUDGE syndrome: salivation, lacrimation, urination,
defecation, GI upset, emesis. Miosis, bradycardia, bronchospasm, muscle fasciculations.
Treatment: atropine, pralidoxime, supportive care.

\medterm{Orogastric Tube}-- Tube through mouth to stomach. For infants, unconscious patients.

\medterm{Oropharyngeal Airway}-- A rigid airway adjunct inserted through the mouth to displace the tongue and help
maintain upper-airway patency in an unconscious patient without an intact gag reflex. It
does not protect against aspiration.

\medterm{Oropharyngeal Carcinoma}-- Malignancy arising in the oropharynx, including the tonsil, base of tongue, soft palate,
and posterior pharyngeal wall. HPV-associated tumors form an important subgroup;
evaluation includes endoscopy, biopsy, imaging, and cervical-node assessment.

\medterm{Oropharynx}-- The posterior portion of the mouth leading to the esophagus, containing the tonsils and
base of tongue. Area for swallowing and speech. Examination with tongue depressor and
light source. Pathology: pharyngitis, tonsillitis, malignancy.

\medterm{Orphan Drug}-- A pharmaceutical agent developed specifically to treat a rare medical condition
(affecting fewer than 200,000 people in the United States, as defined by the Orphan Drug
Act of 1983).

\medterm{Orphan Drugs}-- Medicinal products developed for the diagnosis, prevention, or treatment of rare
diseases (defined in the EU as affecting <5 per 10,000 people; in the US as affecting
<200,000 people).

\medterm{Orthodontic Bracket}-- A bonded or banded attachment that transmits forces from an orthodontic archwire to a
tooth. Bracket position and wire sequence guide controlled tooth movement.

\medterm{Orthodontic Retainer}-- A removable or fixed appliance used after active orthodontic treatment to maintain tooth
position while periodontal and alveolar tissues remodel. Retention is often needed long
term because relapse can occur.

\medterm{Orthodontics}-- Specialty of dental management of malocclusion and dental-facial discrepancies.
Appliances: fixed braces, removable appliances, clear aligners. Treatment indications:
crowding, spacing, overbite, underbite, crossbite, open bite, midline discrepancy.
Treatment duration: 12-24 months typically. Retention phase essential for stability.

\medterm{Orthognathic Surgery}-- Corrective jaw surgery for skeletal malocclusion. Le Fort I, Bilateral Sagittal Split
Osteotomy. Combined with orthodontics.

\medterm{Orthopedic Biologics}-- Biological or biomaterial products used to support bone or soft-tissue healing. Examples include autograft, allograft,
demineralized bone matrix, bone-marrow products, and synthetic graft substitutes; their
uses and evidence vary by procedure and condition.

\medterm{Orthopedic Implant}-- Devices for bone/joint reconstruction. Types: plates, screws, rods, hinges, prostheses.
Materials: titanium, cobalt-chrome, stainless steel, polyethylene, ceramic. Used for
fractures, arthritis, tumors, deformities. Complications: infection, loosening,
fracture, wear.

\medterm{Orthopedic Infections}-- Infections involving bones, joints, muscles, or orthopedic implants. Examples include osteomyelitis, septic arthritis,
prosthetic-joint infection, and some diabetic foot infections.

\medterm{Orthopedic Rehabilitation}-- Therapy that restores movement, strength, function, and independence after musculoskeletal
injury or surgery.

\medterm{Orthopedic Screws}-- Fixation devices for fractures. Materials: titanium, stainless steel. Types: cortical,
cancellous, interfragmentary. Used with plates, rods, nails. Complications: loosening,
breakage, infection.

\medterm{orthopedics}-- The branch of surgery concerned with the musculoskeletal system, including bones,
joints, ligaments, tendons, muscles, and nerves.

\medterm{Orthopnea}-- Dyspnea that occurs when lying flat and is relieved by sitting upright. It is measured
by the number of pillows the patient needs to sleep (two-pillow orthopnea). It is a
hallmark of left heart failure and is caused by redistribution of fluid from the lower
extremities to the lungs in the supine position.

\medterm{Orthopnoea}-- Shortness of breath that occurs when lying flat and is relieved by sitting or standing
upright. Orthopnoea is a classic symptom of left-sided heart failure (elevated pulmonary
venous pressure worsens pulmonary congestion in the supine position). Other causes:
severe COPD, asthma exacerbation, diaphragmatic paralysis, ascites, and obesity.

\medterm{Orthorexia}-- A term used for an excessive or rigid preoccupation with eating foods considered healthy. It is not a universally recognized formal psychiatric diagnosis but may cause nutritional or social impairment.

\medterm{Orthosis}-- An externally applied device that supports, aligns, prevents deformity, protects, or
assists movement of a body segment. It differs from a prosthesis, which replaces a
missing body part.

\medterm{Orthostatic Hypotension}-- Decrease in SBP >=20 mmHg or DBP >=10 mmHg within 3 minutes of standing. Causes:
dehydration, medications (antihypertensives, diuretics, alpha-blockers,
antidepressants), autonomic dysfunction (diabetes, Parkinson disease), and prolonged bed
rest. Symptoms: dizziness, lightheadedness, syncope, falls.

\medterm{Orthostatic Intolerance}-- Symptoms or signs that develop when standing because the cardiovascular and autonomic
systems do not maintain adequate cerebral perfusion. Manifestations include
light-headedness, palpitations, fatigue, visual dimming, presyncope, or syncope and may
occur with or without orthostatic tachycardia or hypotension.

\medterm{Orthotic Training}-- Training for brace users: donning/doffing, skin care, gait training with brace.

\medterm{Osborn wave}-- A positive deflection at the junction of the QRS complex and ST segment on
electrocardiography, also known as a J wave. It is classically associated with
hypothermia, where its amplitude correlates with the degree of cooling.

\medterm{OSFED}-- Other Specified Feeding or Eating Disorder, used when the clinical presentation causes
significant distress or impairment but does not meet full criteria for any of the
specific eating disorders.

\medterm{Osgood-Schlatter Disease}-- A traction apophysitis of the tibial tuberosity at the insertion of the patellar tendon,
caused by repetitive microtrauma during periods of rapid growth.

\medterm{Osler Nodes}-- Painful, erythematous nodules on the pads of fingers and toes, caused by immune complex
deposition in infective endocarditis. They are a minor criterion in the Duke criteria.
Osler nodes are tender and may last hours to days.

\medterm{Osmolality}-- The concentration of osmotically active particles per kilogram of solvent. Plasma
osmolality is determined mainly by sodium and its accompanying anions, glucose, and urea
and governs water movement across cell membranes.

\medterm{Osmosis}-- The net movement of water molecules across a semipermeable membrane from a region of
lower solute concentration (higher water concentration) to a region of higher solute
concentration (lower water concentration). Driven by the osmotic pressure difference,
which is proportional to the number of solute particles.

\medterm{Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome}-- Demyelinating process affecting the pons and extrapontine regions caused by overly rapid
correction of chronic hyponatremia. The brain adapts to chronic hyponatremia by
extruding osmoles and when sodium is corrected too quickly water shifts out of brain
cells causing osmotic injury to oligodendrocytes.

\medterm{Osmotic Diarrhea}-- Diarrhea caused by poorly absorbed solutes retaining water in the intestinal lumen. It
typically improves with fasting and may result from carbohydrate malabsorption,
laxatives, or other osmotically active substances.

\medterm{Osmotic Fragility Test}-- A test that measures the susceptibility of red blood cells to haemolysis when placed in
progressively more dilute saline solutions. Increased fragility supports hereditary
spherocytosis, although the result may also be affected by other haemolytic disorders
and recent transfusion.

\medterm{Osmotic Pressure}-- The pressure required to prevent the movement of water across a semipermeable membrane
when a solute concentration gradient exists, proportional to the number of dissolved
particles (van't Hoff law: pi = iCRT). It is a colligative property depending on
particle number, not particle identity.

\medterm{Osseointegration}-- Direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a
load-bearing implant under appropriate conditions. It depends on implant stability,
surface characteristics, bone quality, healing, and control of infection and occlusal
forces.

\medterm{Ossicles}-- Three smallest body bones forming the ossicular chain in the middle ear: malleus
(hammer, attached to tympanic membrane), incus (anvil), stapes (stirrup, footplate in
oval window).

\medterm{Osteoarthritis}-- The most common joint disease, characterized by progressive cartilage loss, subchondral
bone sclerosis, osteophyte formation, and synovial inflammation, affecting knees, hips,
hands (DIP/PIP Heberden/Bouchard nodes), spine. Risk factors: age, obesity, joint
injury, malalignment, genetics, female sex.

\medterm{Osteoarthritis (Geriatric)}-- Osteoarthritis occurring in later life, commonly affecting the knees, hips, hands, or spine
and causing joint pain, stiffness, and reduced movement. Management may include exercise,
weight management, medicines, assistive measures, or joint replacement when appropriate.

\medterm{Osteoblast}-- A bone-forming cell derived from mesenchymal stem cells in the bone marrow. Osteoblasts
synthesise and deposit the bone matrix (osteoid), composed primarily of type I collagen
and non-collagenous proteins (osteocalcin, osteonectin, alkaline phosphatase).

\medterm{Osteochondritis Dissecans}-- A condition characterized by separation of a fragment of articular cartilage and
subchondral bone from the joint surface, most commonly in the knee (lateral aspect of
the medial femoral condyle). Most common in adolescents and young adults. Etiology:
repetitive microtrauma, ischemia, or genetic predisposition.

\medterm{Osteochondroma}-- A usually benign cartilage-capped bony projection arising from the surface of a bone and continuous
with its cortex and medullary cavity. It may be solitary or occur in hereditary multiple
exostoses.

\medterm{Osteoclast}-- A large, multinucleated bone-resorbing cell derived from the monocyte--macrophage
lineage (hematopoietic stem cells).

\medterm{Osteocyte}-- The mature, terminally differentiated bone cell embedded within the bone matrix in
lacunae, derived from osteoblasts. Osteocytes are connected to each other and to bone
surface cells via canaliculi, forming a mechanosensory network that senses mechanical
loading and fluid flow.

\medterm{Osteogenesis Imperfecta}-- A connective tissue disorder caused by collagen type I mutations, ranging from mild to
lethal. Features: fractures with minimal trauma, blue sclerae, dental abnormalities, and
hearing loss. Severe forms (type II) cause multiple fractures in utero and perinatal
death. Diagnosis: clinical features, genetic testing, and bone biopsy.

\medterm{Osteoid Osteoma}-- A benign bone-forming tumor, usually in an adolescent or young adult, characterized by a small
vascular nidus surrounded by reactive sclerosis. It commonly causes localized pain that is
worse at night and may improve with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.

\medterm{Osteomalacia}-- Impaired mineralization of newly formed osteoid in adults resulting from vitamin D
deficiency, phosphate depletion, or defective mineralization.

\medterm{Osteomyelitis}-- An infection of bone and bone marrow, most commonly caused by bacterial pathogens, with
Staphylococcus aureus responsible for the majority of cases.

\medterm{Osteonecrosis (Avascular Necrosis)}-- Osteonecrosis (AVN) is bone death from vascular compromise, affecting femoral head (most
common), humeral head, femoral condyles, talus. Risk factors: glucocorticoids (high
dose, cumulative), alcohol, trauma, sickle cell disease, SLE, antiphospholipid syndrome,
pancreatitis, decompression sickness, radiation, organ transplantation.

\medterm{Osteopathy}-- A system of manual medicine and healthcare founded by Andrew Taylor Still (1828--1917)
based on the principle that the body's structure (musculoskeletal system) and function
are interrelated, and that the body has inherent self-regulatory and healing mechanisms.

\medterm{Osteopenia}-- Bone mineral density (BMD) that is lower than normal but not low enough to be classified
as osteoporosis. T-score between -1.0 and -2.5 on DXA scan. A risk factor for
osteoporosis and fractures.

\medterm{Osteopetrosis}-- A rare inherited bone disease characterized by defective osteoclast function, leading to
impaired bone resorption and abnormally dense, sclerotic bones. The bone is structurally
abnormal—brittle despite increased density—and prone to fracture.

\medterm{Osteoporosis}-- A skeletal disorder in which reduced bone strength increases the risk of fractures. Risk is influenced by age, hormones,
medicines, genetics, nutrition, smoking, alcohol, and other conditions; diagnosis and
management use clinical assessment and bone-density testing when appropriate.

\medterm{Osteoporosis Fracture Risk Assessment}-- Evaluation combining clinical risk factors, prior fragility fracture history, bone
mineral density, and tools such as FRAX to estimate future fracture probability and
guide treatment.

\medterm{Osteosarcoma}-- The most common primary malignant bone tumor in children and adolescents (excluding
leukemia), with a peak incidence during the adolescent growth spurt (ages 10-20 years),
and a second smaller peak in older adults (usually secondary to Paget disease or prior
radiation).

\medterm{Osteotomy}-- A surgical procedure involving cutting and repositioning of bone to correct deformity,
realign joint surfaces, alter weight-bearing forces, or change limb length.

\medterm{Ostomy}-- Surgical opening into bowel or urinary tract. Colostomy, ileostomy, urostomy. Appliances
for collection.

\medterm{Ostomy Appliance}-- Collection device for feces/urine after ostomy surgery. Types: urostomy (urine),
colostomy (feces), ileostomy (ileum). One-piece or two-piece systems. Open or closed
end. Skin care essential. Complications: skin irritation, hernia, obstruction, prolapse.

\medterm{Ostraceous psoriasis}-- A severe, chronic variant of plaque psoriasis in which the lesions become thick, hard,
and shell-like (ostraceous), with laminated, greyish-brown crusts resembling oyster
shells. It is often refractory to standard therapies and may require systemic agents,
biologics, or combination treatment.

\medterm{Other Pain Conditions}-- Pain conditions that do not fit neatly into a single category, including nociplastic pain, fibromyalgia, chronic
tension-type headache, temporomandibular pain, chronic pelvic pain, and complex regional
pain syndrome. Diagnosis and treatment depend on the specific condition.

\medterm{Other Specified Sexual Dysfunction}-- A category for sexual dysfunction symptoms that cause distress but do not meet full
criteria for any specific sexual dysfunction.

\medterm{Otic capsule}-- The otic capsule is the dense bony shell (otic capsule bone) that surrounds and protects
the inner ear structures, including the cochlea, vestibule, and semicircular canals,
forming the hardest bone in the human body.

\medterm{Otitis Externa}-- Inflammation or infection of the external ear canal, commonly causing ear pain, itching, tenderness, or discharge.

\medterm{Otitis media}-- A group of inflammatory conditions of the middle ear, most commonly caused by viral or
bacterial infection, with Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and
Moraxella catarrhalis being the most frequent bacterial pathogens.

\medterm{Otitis Media with Effusion}-- Nonpurulent fluid in the middle ear without acute signs of infection. It may cause
conductive hearing loss, aural fullness, or speech and learning effects in children;
many cases resolve spontaneously, but persistent or consequential effusion requires
hearing assessment and ENT review.

\medterm{Otocephaly}-- A severe congenital craniofacial malformation characterized by absent or markedly
underdeveloped mandible, ventral displacement or fusion of the ears, and associated
facial and airway abnormalities. It is usually lethal because of profound impairment of
breathing and feeding.

\medterm{Otolaryngology}-- The surgical specialty concerned with the diagnosis and treatment of disorders of the
ear, nose, throat, head, and neck. Otolaryngologists (ENT surgeons) manage conditions
ranging from otitis media and sinusitis to head and neck cancer, thyroid disease, and
voice disorders.

\medterm{Otology}-- The medical specialty concerned with disorders of the ear, including hearing loss, tinnitus, balance disorders, ear infections,
otosclerosis, cholesteatoma, and related conditions.

\medterm{Otorrhea}-- Discharge from the ear. Classification: acute otorrhea (<6 weeks) and chronic otorrhea
(>6 weeks).

\medterm{Otosclerosis}-- An abnormal bony growth in the middle ear that causes hearing loss.

\medterm{Otoscope}-- Instrument for examining ear canal and tympanic membrane. Light source, magnifying lens,
speculum. Essential for otologic evaluation.

\medterm{Otoscopy}-- A clinical examination technique used to visualize the external auditory canal and
tympanic membrane, and it is an essential component of the evaluation of ear complaints
including hearing loss, ear pain, otorrhea, and tinnitus.

\medterm{Outbreak}-- Synonymous with epidemic. Sometimes used to refer to a local epidemic as compared to a
larger, general epidemic.

\medterm{Outcome Measurement}-- The use of defined measures to assess a patient’s symptoms, function, quality of life, or other results before and after care.

\medterm{Outcome Research}-- Study of rehabilitation outcomes. Effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, patient-reported
outcomes.

\medterm{Outer Ear}-- The visible auricle and the external auditory canal, which collect and conduct sound toward the eardrum.

\medterm{Outpatient Rehabilitation}-- Rehabilitation provided without hospital admission to improve function after illness, injury,
surgery, or disability. The setting, frequency, and duration are individualized.

\medterm{Ovarian Cancer}-- A malignant tumor arising in the ovary or related tissues. It may cause abdominal or pelvic symptoms,
but early disease can be asymptomatic.

\medterm{Ovarian Cyst}-- A fluid-filled sac within or on the ovary, most commonly functional (follicular or
corpus luteum cysts) related to the menstrual cycle. Usually asymptomatic; can present
with pelvic pain, pressure, and bloating. Complications: rupture, hemorrhage, torsion
(sudden severe pain with nausea/vomiting).

\medterm{Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome}-- A potentially serious complication of ovarian stimulation in assisted reproduction
caused by increased vascular permeability and fluid shift. It produces enlarged ovaries,
abdominal distension, ascites, hemoconcentration, electrolyte abnormalities, thrombosis
risk, and, in severe cases, organ dysfunction.

\medterm{Ovarian Pregnancy}-- A rare ectopic pregnancy implanted in ovarian tissue rather than in the uterine cavity or fallopian
tube. Diagnosis uses clinical findings, serial human chorionic gonadotropin measurements,
ultrasound, and sometimes surgical or pathologic confirmation; treatment is individualized.

\medterm{Ovarian Torsion}-- Twisting of the ovary (and often the fallopian tube) on its vascular pedicle, causing
obstruction of blood flow—a gynecologic emergency. Risk: ovarian cysts/tumors
(especially >5 cm), longer utero-ovarian ligament, younger women, pregnancy.

\medterm{Ovariectomy}-- Surgical removal of one or both ovaries.

\medterm{Ovary}-- The female gonad, a paired almond-shaped organ located in the pelvic cavity on each side
of the uterus. Functions: production of oocytes (ova) and secretion of female sex
hormones (oestrogen, progesterone, and small amounts of androgens). Each ovary contains
follicles at various stages of development.

\medterm{Over-the-Counter}-- Medications available without a prescription, considered safe and effective for
self-care with appropriate labeling and low abuse potential. Examples: acetaminophen,
ibuprofen, antacids, antihistamines, decongestants, laxatives. Regulated in the US by
the FDA (OTC monograph system) and Rx-to-OTC switches (e.g., omeprazole, loratadine).

\medterm{Over-the-Counter Medications}-- Nonprescription drugs approved as safe and effective for self-management of common
symptoms, available at pharmacies and retail stores without a prescription.

\medterm{Over-the-Counter Drug}-- A medication that can be purchased without a prescription, deemed safe and effective for
self-medication when used according to label instructions. OTC drugs have established
safety profiles, low toxicity, and clear dosing guidelines suitable for consumer use.

\medterm{Overactive Bladder}-- A clinical syndrome of urinary urgency, usually with frequency and nocturia, with or
without urge incontinence, due to detrusor overactivity during filling. Causes:
idiopathic (most), neurogenic (stroke, spinal injury, MS, Parkinson), bladder outlet
obstruction, and aging; worsened by caffeine and diuretics.

\medterm{Overall Survival}-- Time from treatment to death from any cause.

\medterm{Overdenture}-- Removable denture supported by implants or natural teeth. Preservation of alveolar bone,
improved stability.

\medterm{Overdose}-- Exposure to a dose of a medicine or substance that is greater than intended or safe and may cause toxicity.

\medterm{Overflow Urinary Incontinence}-- Leakage of urine caused by incomplete bladder emptying and excessive bladder filling. It may occur with weak
urinary flow, dribbling, urgency, or urinary retention.

\medterm{Overgrowth Syndrome}-- A group of congenital disorders characterized by excessive growth of the body, a region,
or specific tissues, often with developmental or tumor-predisposition features. Causes
include genomic imprinting abnormalities, mosaic variants, and alterations in
growth-signaling pathways.

\medterm{Overlap Myositis}-- Patients with features of inflammatory myopathy (DM/PM/IMNM) plus another systemic
autoimmune disease (SLE, SSc, MCTD, Sjogren). Anti-PM-Scl (Scl/PM overlap, limited SSc +
myositis, good prognosis); anti-Ku (SLE/PM overlap); anti-U1-RNP (MCTD with myositis).

\medterm{Ovulation}-- The release of a mature egg from the ovary, at which time it is available to be
fertilized by sperm.

\medterm{Ovum}-- The female reproductive cell (egg) after ovulation and before fertilization. The ovum is
a large, non-motile cell (~100 \(\mu\mathrm{m}\) diameter) surrounded by the zona pellucida
(glycoprotein layer that mediates sperm binding and prevents polyspermy after
fertilization) and the corona radiata (follicular cells).

\medterm{Oxalate}-- A metabolic end product that can form calcium oxalate crystals in the kidneys,
contributing to nephrolithiasis and oxalate nephropathy, particularly in hyperoxaluric
states.

\medterm{Oxaloacetate}-- A four-carbon citric-acid-cycle intermediate that condenses with acetyl-CoA to form
citrate. It is also a precursor for gluconeogenesis and participates in amino-acid
transamination to aspartate.

\medterm{Oxidative Phosphorylation}-- Process generating ATP using energy released by electron transfer through the electron
transport chain. Protons pumped across inner mitochondrial membrane create
electrochemical gradient (proton motive force). ATP synthase (Complex V) uses proton
flow to phosphorylate ADP to ATP. P/O ratio: ~2.5 ATP per NADH, ~1.5 ATP per FADH2.

\medterm{Oxidative Stress}-- Imbalance between reactive oxygen species and antioxidants. DNA damage, protein
oxidation, lipid peroxidation.

\medterm{Oxygen Consumption}-- The volume of oxygen consumed by the body per unit time, reflecting overall metabolic
rate. Resting oxygen consumption (VO2) in a normal adult is approximately 250 mL/min
(about 3.5 mL/kg/min, 1 MET). It is calculated as cardiac output x (arterial O2 content
- venous O2 content) by the Fick principle.

\medterm{Oxygen Delivery}-- The rate at which oxygen is transported from the lungs to the tissues, equal to cardiac
output multiplied by arterial oxygen content: DO2 = CO x CaO2. Arterial oxygen content =
(1.34 x hemoglobin x SaO2) + (0.003 x PaO2). Normal oxygen delivery is approximately
1000 mL/min.

\medterm{Oxygen Enhancement Ratio}-- Ratio of dose under hypoxic vs. aerobic conditions. Higher for low LET radiation.

\medterm{Oxygen Mask}-- Delivers supplemental oxygen. Types: simple, nasal cannula, non-rebreather, Venturi.

\medterm{Oxygen Saturation}-- The percentage of hemoglobin carrying oxygen in blood. It may be estimated by pulse oximetry
(SpO2) or measured in an arterial blood sample by co-oximetry (SaO2). The appropriate
range depends on the person's condition, baseline oxygenation, and treatment goals.

\medterm{Oxygen-Hemoglobin Dissociation Curve}-- The sigmoidal relationship between partial pressure of oxygen (PO2) and hemoglobin
oxygen saturation (SO2). The curve reflects hemoglobin's cooperative binding: oxygen
binding to one subunit increases affinity for subsequent oxygen molecules. At PO2 of 100
mmHg (alveolar), hemoglobin is approximately 98 percent saturated.

\medterm{Oxytocics}-- A class of medications that stimulate uterine contractions, used for induction and
augmentation of labor and prevention/treatment of postpartum hemorrhage. Oxytocin
(Syntocinon) — synthetic analog of endogenous oxytocin, administered IV infusion for
labor induction or IM/IV for PPH prevention.

\medterm{Oxytocin Infusion}-- Intravenous synthetic oxytocin used to induce or augment labor or, in selected settings, to manage postpartum
bleeding. The dose is started and adjusted according to uterine activity, fetal status,
maternal condition, and local protocol; excessive uterine stimulation can be harmful.

\medterm{Ozaena}-- A chronic condition characterized by atrophic rhinitis with crusting, foul-smelling
nasal discharge (fetor), and a sensation of nasal obstruction despite a patent airway.
Ozaena is caused by progressive atrophy of the nasal mucosa and underlying bone, often
associated with Klebsiella ozaenae infection.

\medterm{Ondine's Curse}-- A rare disorder in which automatic breathing is impaired, especially during sleep; also called congenital central hypoventilation syndrome.

\medterm{Open Prostatectomy}-- Surgical removal of enlarged prostate tissue through an abdominal incision.

\medterm{Orbital Irradiation}-- Radiation treatment directed at tissues within the eye socket, sometimes used for severe thyroid eye disease.

\medterm{Overt Proteinuria}-- Abnormally high protein loss in the urine that is detectable by routine testing and may indicate kidney disease.

\medterm{Obstructive Hydrocephalus}-- A buildup of cerebrospinal fluid caused by a blockage in its normal flow through the brain's ventricles.

\medterm{Ocular Melanoma}-- A cancer that begins in pigment-producing cells of the eye.

\medterm{Oculomotor Nerve}-- The third cranial nerve, which controls most eye movements, raises the upper eyelid, and constricts the pupil.

\medterm{Odontogenic Cyst}-- A fluid-filled sac that develops from tissues involved in tooth formation, usually in the jaw.
\medterm{Ocular Rosacea}-- Inflammation affecting the eyes and eyelids in people with rosacea, causing irritation, redness, or dryness.
\medterm{Ophthalmia Neonatorum}-- Eye inflammation or infection in a newborn, often acquired during delivery.
\medterm{Optic Disk}-- The point where the optic nerve leaves the retina; it has no photoreceptors and forms the blind spot.
\medterm{Orthopneic Position}-- An upright sitting position that helps a person breathe more easily, often with the arms supported.
