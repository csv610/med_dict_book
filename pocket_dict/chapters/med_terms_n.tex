
\medterm{N-Acetylcysteine}-- An antidote that replenishes glutathione and enhances detoxification of the toxic
metabolite of acetaminophen. It is most effective when started early but may benefit
patients with delayed presentation or established liver injury under specialist
guidance.

\medterm{Na+/K+ ATPase}-- The sodium-potassium pump is a P-type ATPase found in the plasma membrane of all animal
cells, directly consuming approximately 20-30 percent of cellular ATP. It pumps 3 Na+
ions out of the cell and 2 K+ ions in per ATP hydrolyzed, establishing steep
electrochemical gradients: high Na+ outside, high K+ inside.

\medterm{Nabothian Cyst}-- A benign mucus-retention cyst of the uterine cervix caused by obstruction of an
endocervical gland. It is common, usually asymptomatic, and generally requires no
treatment.

\medterm{NAD+}-- Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide in its oxidized form, a coenzyme that carries electrons in cellular energy
metabolism.

\medterm{NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide)}-- Coenzyme involved in redox reactions, existing in oxidized (NAD+) and reduced (NADH)
forms. Accepts electrons and hydrogen (NAD+ + 2e- + H+ -> NADH). Key roles: glycolysis
(glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase), TCA cycle (isocitrate, alpha-ketoglutarate,
malate dehydrogenases), beta-oxidation.

\medterm{Nadir}-- The lowest point, particularly referring to the lowest white blood cell count after
chemotherapy. Bone marrow suppression typically reaches nadir 7-14 days post-treatment.
Important for infection risk assessment.

\medterm{Naegele Rule}-- A formula for estimating the due date (estimated date of delivery, EDD): add 7 days to
the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), then add 9 months (or subtract 3
months). Example: LMP January 1 yields EDD October 8. Assumes a 28-day cycle with
ovulation on day 14. The rule is most accurate with regular cycles and confirmed LMP.

\medterm{Naegleria Fowleri}-- A free-living amoeba that can cause primary amoebic meningoencephalitis after
contaminated water enters the nose. The infection is rare but rapidly progressive, with
fever, severe headache, meningism, altered consciousness, seizures, and high mortality.

\medterm{Naegleria Infection}-- A rare infection caused by the free-living amoeba Naegleria fowleri, usually acquired when contaminated warm
fresh water enters the nose. It can cause rapidly progressive primary amoebic meningoencephalitis with severe
headache, fever, vomiting, confusion, seizures, and coma. It is a medical emergency.

\medterm{Nail}-- A protective plate of hardened keratin covering the dorsal surface of the distal
phalanges of the fingers and toes.

\medterm{Nail Changes}-- Alterations in nail color, shape, texture, or attachment that may result from local injury, skin disease, infection,
medicines, nutritional deficiency, or systemic illness. Their significance depends on
the pattern and other clinical findings.

\medterm{Nail Clubbing}-- Abnormal enlargement of the distal digit with increased nail curvature and loss of the
normal nail-fold angle. It may be familial or associated with pulmonary, cardiovascular,
gastrointestinal, or malignant disease and should be interpreted with the clinical
context.

\medterm{Nail Infection}-- Infection of the nail or surrounding tissue, commonly caused by fungi or bacteria. It may cause thickening,
discoloration, separation of the nail, pain, swelling, or drainage; diabetes and poor circulation
increase the risk of complications.

\medterm{Nail Pits}-- Small, sharply circumscribed depressions in the nail plate caused by abnormal focal
keratinization of the proximal nail matrix. They are classically associated with
psoriasis but may also occur in alopecia areata, eczema, lichen planus, or other nail
disorders.

\medterm{Nail Psoriasis}-- Nail psoriasis affects 50 percent of patients with psoriasis and 80 percent with nail
involvement having joint disease. Features include pitting (most common), onycholysis
(nail separation), oil drop sign (salmon patch), subungual hyperkeratosis, and nail
plate crumbling. Nail psoriasis is associated with psoriatic arthritis.

\medterm{Naloxone}-- A competitive opioid-receptor antagonist used to reverse opioid-induced respiratory
depression and central nervous-system depression. Repeated dosing or continuous infusion
may be required because naloxone can have a shorter duration than the opioid involved.

\medterm{Nanocolloid}-- Tc-99m labeled colloidal particles for lymphatic imaging. Particle size 10-100 nm. Used
for sentinel node detection.

\medterm{Narcissistic Personality Disorder}-- A pattern of grandiosity, need for admiration, and lack of empathy.

\medterm{Narcolepsy}-- A chronic sleep disorder characterized by excessive daytime sleepiness and irresistible
sleep attacks, with or without cataplexy (sudden bilateral loss of muscle tone triggered
by strong emotions). Narcolepsy type 1: with cataplexy, caused by loss of
hypocretin/orexin neurons in the hypothalamus.

\medterm{Narrow angle glaucoma}-- Anatomical narrowing of the anterior chamber angle due to relative pupillary block or a
shallow anterior chamber, creating risk for angle closure and acute intraocular pressure
elevation. Identified on gonioscopy, it may be managed with laser peripheral iridotomy
to equalise pressure between the posterior and anterior chambers.

\medterm{Narrow Therapeutic Index}-- A drug characteristic where there is a small difference between the therapeutic dose and
the toxic dose, requiring careful dosing and therapeutic drug monitoring to ensure
efficacy while avoiding toxicity. Narrow therapeutic index drugs have a therapeutic
index <2-3 (the ratio of toxic dose to effective dose).

\medterm{Narrow-Complex Tachycardia}-- A rapid cardiac rhythm with QRS complexes of normal or near-normal width, usually arising
above the ventricles.

\medterm{Narrowband UVB Phototherapy}-- Controlled exposure to ultraviolet B light centered near 311--313 nm for inflammatory or
pigmentary skin disease. It is used for psoriasis, vitiligo, eczema, and other selected
conditions with eye protection, cumulative-dose monitoring, and skin-cancer risk
assessment.

\medterm{Nasal Cannula}-- A device with two small prongs placed in the nostrils to deliver supplemental oxygen. The flow rate is adjusted to the patient’s needs and the device used.

\medterm{Nasal Cavity}-- The paired space inside the nose, extending from the nostrils to the nasopharynx. It warms, humidifies, and filters inspired
air and contains the receptors responsible for smell.

\medterm{Nasal Congestion}-- A sensation of nasal stuffiness or obstruction from engorgement of the venous sinusoids
within the nasal mucosa, especially the inferior turbinates; a symptom shared by many
conditions.

\medterm{Nasal Endoscopy}-- Direct endoscopic inspection of the nasal passages, turbinates, septum, sinus drainage
pathways, and nasopharynx. It is useful for chronic rhinosinusitis, unilateral
obstruction, epistaxis, polyps, foreign bodies, and suspected neoplasia.

\medterm{Nasal Flaring}-- Widening and outward movement of the nostrils during breathing. It is a visible sign of
increased work of breathing, particularly in infants and children, and may accompany
airway obstruction, bronchiolitis, pneumonia, asthma, or severe systemic illness.

\medterm{Nasal Implant}-- A device placed in or near the nose to support nasal structures or improve airflow. Its purpose depends on the device and condition.

\medterm{Nasal Obstruction}-- Blocked airflow through one or both nasal passages. Causes include congestion, allergy, infection, a
deviated septum, polyps, trauma, or a mass. Persistent one-sided obstruction or bleeding requires
medical evaluation.

\medterm{Nasal Polyps}-- Benign, pedunculated, edematous outgrowths of the sinus and nasal mucosa that arise most
commonly from the ethmoidal sinuses and protrude into the nasal cavity through the
middle meatus, causing nasal obstruction, anosmia (loss of smell), and rhinorrhea.

\medterm{Nasal Septal Hematoma}-- A collection of blood between the nasal septal cartilage and its perichondrium, usually
after nasal trauma. It causes nasal obstruction and a soft fluctuant septal swelling and
requires urgent drainage to prevent cartilage necrosis and saddle-nose deformity.

\medterm{Nasal Septum}-- The wall that divides the nasal cavity into right and left sides. It consists mainly of cartilage in front and bone behind
and helps support the nose and direct airflow.

\medterm{Nasal Stent}-- Support for nasal septum/airway. Materials: silicone, metal. Indications: septal
hematoma, post-septoplasty support, nasal valve collapse. Complications: crusting,
infection, pressure necrosis.

\medterm{Nasogastric Tube}-- A tube passed through the nose into the stomach to remove contents or deliver nutrition, fluids, or medicines when clinically indicated.

\medterm{Nasopharyngeal Airway}-- A soft tube inserted through the nose to maintain a passage between the nares and
pharynx. It may be useful in a spontaneously breathing patient with partial upper-airway
obstruction but is relatively contraindicated in suspected basal skull fracture or
significant nasal trauma.

\medterm{Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma}-- A malignancy arising from the epithelial lining of the nasopharynx, and it has a unique
geographical and ethnic distribution, with highest incidence in southern China,
Southeast Asia, and North Africa.

\medterm{Natal Teeth}-- Teeth present at birth, most often involving the lower central incisors. They may be
loose, interfere with feeding, or injure the infant's tongue and require dental
assessment.

\medterm{Natural Killer Cell}-- A cytotoxic innate lymphoid cell that kills virus-infected and tumor cells without prior
sensitization or MHC restriction. Recognition: activating receptors (NKG2D) and
inhibitory receptors (KIR, NKG2A) for MHC class I—'missing self' (reduced MHC) triggers
killing.

\medterm{Natural Killer Cell}-- A type of innate lymphoid cell (lymphocyte) that provides early defense against
virus-infected cells and tumor cells without prior sensitization. NK cells recognize
stressed or missing-self cells (absent or altered MHC class I) and kill them by
releasing perforin and granzymes, or through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

\medterm{Nausea}-- An unpleasant sensation of an impending need to vomit, often accompanied by autonomic
symptoms such as salivation, sweating, pallor, or dizziness. Common causes include
gastrointestinal illness, pregnancy, medication effects, migraine, vestibular disorders,
metabolic disease, and serious abdominal or neurologic conditions.

\medterm{Navicular}-- Boat-shaped tarsal bone between talus and three cuneiforms. Navicular tuberosity is a
palpable medial prominence serving as posterior tibial tendon insertion, key medial
longitudinal arch stabilizer. Central third has a precarious blood supply, susceptible
to stress fractures and avascular necrosis (Kohler disease in children).

\medterm{NDM-1}-- New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase-1, an enzyme that allows certain bacteria to resist many beta-lactam antibiotics,
including carbapenems. NDM-1-producing organisms can cause difficult-to-treat urinary, wound, bloodstream, or lung
infections. Laboratory susceptibility testing and strict infection-control measures are essential.

\medterm{Near Miss}-- A patient-safety event that could have caused harm but did not, either by chance or
because it was intercepted. Reporting and analyzing near misses can identify system
vulnerabilities before harm occurs.

\medterm{Nearsightedness}-- A refractive error, also called myopia, in which distant objects appear blurred because
light focuses in front of the retina. It is corrected with diverging lenses, contact
lenses, or refractive surgery.

\medterm{Nebulizer}-- A device that converts liquid medications into a mist for inhalation. Used for
bronchodilators (albuterol, ipratropium) and corticosteroids in acute exacerbations.
More effective than MDI in patients with severe obstruction.

\medterm{Neck Cancer}-- Human papillomavirus, particularly HPV-16, is an increasingly important etiological
factor in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, particularly oropharyngeal cancer, and
it is responsible for the rising incidence of oropharyngeal cancer in younger patients
without traditional risk factors of tobacco and alcohol use.

\medterm{Neck Pain}-- Pain or stiffness in the cervical region, commonly caused by muscle strain, degenerative
disease, poor posture, or injury. Severe pain after trauma or pain with weakness,
numbness, fever, or neurologic deficits requires prompt assessment.

\medterm{Necrobiosis Lipoidica}-- A chronic inflammatory skin disorder characterized by yellow-brown, atrophic plaques,
usually on the shins. It is associated with diabetes but can occur without diabetes.

\medterm{Necrobiosis lipoidica diabeticorum}-- A chronic granulomatous skin disorder most commonly associated with diabetes mellitus,
presenting as well-demarcated, atrophic, yellow-brown plaques with telangiectasia,
typically on the pretibial area. The plaques have a waxy, glazed appearance with a
violaceous border. Ulceration occurs in approximately 35 percent of cases.

\medterm{Necrolytic migratory erythema}-- A recurrent, erosive, erythematous eruption that often affects periorificial and
intertriginous skin. It is classically associated with glucagonoma but may have other causes,
so evaluation should consider nutritional, hepatic, pancreatic, and metabolic disease.

\medterm{Necropsy}-- A post-mortem examination of an animal or human body to determine disease or cause of death.

\medterm{Necroptosis}-- A regulated, programmed form of necrosis that shares features of both classical necrosis
and apoptosis, serving as a backup cell death mechanism when apoptosis is inhibited. It
is particularly important during viral infections, as many viruses encode proteins that
block caspase-mediated apoptosis to ensure their own replication.

\medterm{Necrosis}-- Cell death caused by severe injury or disease, often with loss of membrane integrity and release of cellular contents.
It commonly provokes inflammation, although the appearance and inflammatory response vary
with the cause and tissue.

\medterm{Necrotizing anterior scleritis}-- A severe, sight-threatening form of scleral inflammation characterised by ischaemic
necrosis of the sclera with visible underlying uvea through thinned, grey-white scleral
patches.

\medterm{Necrotizing Enterocolitis}-- An acquired inflammatory disease that can cause intestinal necrosis, primarily affecting
premature infants. Risk factors include prematurity, enteral feeding, and bacterial
colonization. It may cause abdominal distension, bloody stools, bilious vomiting, and
feeding intolerance. Pneumatosis intestinalis on imaging strongly supports the diagnosis
but must be interpreted with the clinical findings.

\medterm{Necrotizing Fasciitis}-- A life-threatening soft tissue infection characterized by rapid necrosis of the
subcutaneous fascia and subcutaneous tissue, with relative sparing of the overlying skin
in early stages. It is a surgical emergency requiring immediate wide surgical
debridement and broad-spectrum antibiotics.

\medterm{Necrotizing Soft Tissue Infection}-- A rapidly progressive infection of fascia, subcutaneous tissue, or muscle causing severe
pain, swelling, systemic toxicity, and tissue necrosis. It is a surgical emergency
requiring immediate assessment, broad antimicrobial therapy, and aggressive debridement.

\medterm{Needle}-- Hollow metal tube for fluid withdrawal or injection. Gauge: larger number = smaller
diameter. Common gauges: 18G (blood draw), 22G (IV), 25-27G (injection).

\medterm{Needle Driver}-- Instrument for passing suture needle through tissue.

\medterm{Needle Driver (Perineal)}-- A hand instrument used to hold and guide a suture needle during a perineal repair.

\medterm{Needle Driver (Vascular)}-- A hand instrument used to hold and guide a suture needle during vascular repair.

\medterm{Needle Holder}-- A surgical instrument with jaws used to grasp and guide a suture needle during tissue repair.

\medterm{Negative Feedback}-- A regulatory mechanism in which the output of a system feeds back to decrease or reverse
the initiating stimulus, maintaining homeostasis around a set point.

\medterm{Negative Predictive Value}-- Probability that negative test indicates no disease. NPV = true negatives / all
negatives.

\medterm{Negative Predictive Value}-- The probability that a person with a negative test result truly does not have the target
condition. NPV varies with disease prevalence or pretest probability and is not an
intrinsic fixed property of a test.

\medterm{Negative Pressure Ventilation}-- Ventilation using negative pressure applied around the chest wall to expand the lungs,
as opposed to positive pressure applied to the airway. The ``iron lung'' is a historical
example. Modern negative pressure ventilators include the cuirass and wrap. They are
rarely used today but may be useful in neuromuscular disease.

\medterm{Negative Symptoms}-- Reduced or absent normal emotional, motivational, cognitive, or social functions,
including affective flattening, alogia, avolition, anhedonia, and asociality. Persistent
negative symptoms can substantially impair functional recovery.

\medterm{Neglect}-- A form of child maltreatment: the failure to provide for a child's basic needs—physical
(food, clothing, shelter, medical care), educational, or emotional—causing harm or risk
of harm. Features: failure to thrive, poor hygiene, untreated illness/injury, missed
healthcare appointments, inadequate supervision, and developmental delay.

\medterm{Neglect Syndrome}-- Hemispatial neglect (unilateral neglect, hemineglect) is a neurological condition
characterized by failure to attend to, respond to, or orient toward stimuli on the side
of space contralateral to the brain lesion, in the absence of primary sensory or motor
deficits sufficient to explain the neglect.

\medterm{Neisseria gonorrhoeae}-- A gram-negative diplococcus that causes gonorrhea, a sexually transmitted infection. The
organism has developed resistance to many antibiotics including fluoroquinolones and
macrolides.

\medterm{Neisseria meningitidis}-- A gram-negative diplococcus that causes meningitis and meningococcemia. The organism has
a polysaccharide capsule that is the target of conjugate vaccines. Serogroups A, B, C,
W, X, and Y cause most disease. Meningitis presents with fever, headache, neck
stiffness, and altered mental status.

\medterm{Nelson Syndrome}-- Enlargement and hypersecretion of an adrenocorticotropic-hormone-producing pituitary
tumour after bilateral adrenalectomy, usually for Cushing disease. It may cause rapid
skin hyperpigmentation, headache, visual-field loss, and other effects of an expanding
sellar mass.

\medterm{Neoadjuvant Therapy}-- Treatment before primary treatment to shrink tumor. Examples: chemotherapy before
surgery.

\medterm{Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome}-- A withdrawal syndrome in neonates following in utero exposure to opioids (most common)
or other substances (benzodiazepines, SSRIs, alcohol). Onset within 24-72 hours of birth
(varies by half-life of substance).

\medterm{Neonatal Cephalohematoma}-- A collection of blood beneath the periosteum of a newborn's skull, usually caused by
birth trauma. It is limited by skull sutures and generally resolves over time, although it requires assessment for
associated injury or jaundice.

\medterm{Neonatal Conjunctivitis}-- Inflammation of the conjunctiva during the first month of life, caused by chemical
irritation, bacteria, or viruses acquired during delivery. Purulent discharge, eyelid
swelling, and corneal involvement require urgent evaluation.

\medterm{Neonatal Diabetes Mellitus}-- Diabetes diagnosed within the first six months of life. Can be transient or permanent.
Transient neonatal diabetes mellitus is caused by abnormalities at chromosome 6q24 and
usually resolves but may relapse in adolescence.

\medterm{Neonatal Herpes Simplex}-- Herpes simplex virus infection in the first 28 days of life, usually HSV-2 from maternal
genital infection. Three forms: skin, eye, and mouth disease; CNS disease; and
disseminated disease. Disseminated form mimics sepsis with high mortality. Diagnosis by
viral PCR, culture, or Tzanck smear. Treatment: IV acyclovir for 14-21 days.

\medterm{Neonatal herpes simplex virus}-- A severe neonatal infection caused by herpes simplex virus, typically acquired during
passage through the birth canal, presenting within the first month of life as skin, eye,
and mouth disease, central nervous system disease, or disseminated disease with
multiorgan involvement.

\medterm{Neonatal hyperbilirubinemia}-- Elevated unconjugated bilirubin levels in newborns exceeding 5 mg per deciliter,
potentially causing jaundice and, if severe, kernicterus with neurological damage.
Phototherapy is the standard treatment.

\medterm{Neonatal Hypoglycemia}-- Blood glucose less than 40-45 mg/dL in term infants or less than 25 mg/dL in preterm
infants. Risk factors include prematurity, small for gestational age, large for
gestational age, and maternal diabetes. Presents with jitteriness, lethargy, poor
feeding, and seizures. Treatment: early feeding, IV dextrose infusion, and monitoring.

\medterm{Neonatal Hypothermia}-- Core body temperature below 36.5 degrees Celsius in a newborn. Classified as mild
(36-36.4), moderate (32-35.9), or severe (below 32). Occurs due to rapid heat loss
through evaporation, convection, conduction, and radiation. Prevention: skin-to-skin
contact, drying, warm environment, and delayed bathing.

\medterm{Neonatal Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy}-- Brain dysfunction in a newborn caused by inadequate oxygenation and/or cerebral blood
flow around the time of birth. Moderate or severe HIE may produce altered consciousness,
abnormal tone, seizures, and multiorgan injury; selected term or near-term infants may
benefit from early therapeutic hypothermia.

\medterm{Neonatal Infections}-- Early-onset sepsis (EOS, <72h): GBS, E. coli, Listeria. Risk factors: maternal GBS+,
chorioamnionitis, PROM >18h. Kaiser sepsis calculator for management. Late-onset sepsis
(LOS, >72h): CoNS, S. aureus, gram-negatives, Candida. NEC: prematurity, formula
feeding, ischemic gut. Pneumatosis intestinalis, portal venous gas.

\medterm{Neonatal Jaundice}-- Yellow discoloration of a newborn's skin or eyes caused by elevated bilirubin. It is common but may
require treatment or investigation when severe, early, or prolonged.

\medterm{Neonatal Jaundice (Jaundice of the Newborn)}-- Yellow discoloration of a newborn's skin or eyes caused by elevated
bilirubin. It is often physiologic but requires assessment when severe, early, rapidly increasing, or prolonged.

\medterm{Neonatal Resuscitation}-- The systematic approach to stabilizing a newborn after delivery, guided by the Neonatal
Resuscitation Program (NRP).

\medterm{Neonatal Sepsis}-- Systemic bacterial infection in the first 28 days of life, classified as early-onset
(within 72 hours, usually from vertical transmission) or late-onset (after 72 hours,
often nosocomial). Common organisms: Group B Streptococcus, E. coli, Listeria.

\medterm{Neonatal Skin Conditions}-- Common and usually benign. Erythema toxicum neonatorum (red papules and pustules on
erythematous base, appears day 2 to 3, resolves by 2 weeks). Milia (tiny white cysts on
nose and cheeks). Miliaria crystallina (clear vesicles from sweat duct obstruction).
Sebaceous hyperplasia (yellow papules on nose and cheeks).

\medterm{Neonatal thyrotoxicosis}-- Transient hyperthyroidism in newborns caused by transplacental transfer of maternal
thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins, presenting with tachycardia, irritability, and poor
weight gain in the first weeks of life.

\medterm{Neonatal Transition}-- The physiologic adaptation from placental gas exchange to pulmonary respiration after
birth. It includes lung aeration, falling pulmonary vascular resistance, closure of
fetal circulatory shunts, and changes in cardiac output and thermoregulation.

\medterm{Neonatologist}-- A pediatrician who specializes in the medical care of newborn infants, particularly
premature and critically ill neonates.

\medterm{Neoplasm}-- Abnormal growth of tissue, may be benign or malignant. Benign neoplasms: localized,
slow-growing, do not metastasize. Malignant neoplasms (cancer): invasive, metastasize,
can be fatal. Classification: carcinoma (epithelial origin), sarcoma (mesenchymal
origin), leukemia (hematopoietic), lymphoma (lymphoid).

\medterm{Nephrectomy}-- Surgical removal of a kidney. Radical nephrectomy: kidney + Gerota fascia + adrenal
gland (for renal cell carcinoma). Partial nephrectomy (nephron-sparing): for small renal
tumors (<7 cm, renal function preservation). Simple nephrectomy: for benign disease
(non-functioning kidney, chronic infection, massive hydronephrosis).

\medterm{Nephritic Syndrome}-- Characterized by hematuria with dysmorphic RBCs and RBC casts, oliguria, hypertension,
and mild proteinuria less than 3.5 g/day. Results from glomerular inflammation
increasing permeability to cells and reducing GFR. Hypertension from sodium and water
retention.

\medterm{Nephroblastoma}-- Wilms tumor, a malignant embryonal renal tumor of childhood that commonly presents as an
asymptomatic abdominal mass.

\medterm{Nephrocalcinosis}-- Deposition of calcium salts within the renal parenchyma, often caused by hypercalcemia,
hypercalciuria, distal renal tubular acidosis, prematurity, or inherited disorders. It
may impair renal concentrating ability and progress to chronic kidney disease.

\medterm{Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus}-- Inability of the renal collecting ducts to respond adequately to antidiuretic hormone,
causing excessive dilute urine production and intense thirst. Causes include inherited
defects, chronic kidney disease, electrolyte disorders, and medication exposure.

\medterm{Nephrogenic fibrosing dermopathy}-- A rare fibrosing disorder of the skin causing thickening and hardening, primarily of the
extremities and trunk, associated with kidney failure and gadolinium-based contrast
exposure.

\medterm{Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis}-- A rare fibrosing disorder associated mainly with exposure to certain gadolinium contrast
agents in people with severe kidney impairment.

\medterm{Nephrolithiasis}-- Formation of stones in the urinary tract. Calcium-containing stones are common, but stone
composition varies and may include uric acid, struvite, or cystine. Risk factors include dehydration, hypercalciuria,
hyperoxaluria, hypocitraturia, and low urine volume. Evaluation includes 24-hour urine
analysis.

\medterm{Nephron}-- Functional unit of the kidney comprising the glomerulus, proximal tubule, loop of Henle,
distal tubule, and collecting duct. Each kidney contains approximately one million
nephrons responsible for filtering blood, reabsorbing solutes and water, and excreting
waste to form urine.

\medterm{Nephron Function}-- The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney, with approximately 1 million per
kidney. Each nephron consists of a renal corpuscle (glomerulus and Bowman's capsule) and
a renal tubule (proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule, and
collecting duct).

\medterm{Nephronophthisis}-- An autosomal-recessive tubulointerstitial kidney disease causing progressive loss of
concentrating ability, polyuria, polydipsia, anemia, and chronic kidney failure. It may
be isolated or part of a ciliopathy with retinal, skeletal, hepatic, or neurological
manifestations.

\medterm{Nephropathy}-- Disease or damage of the kidneys, which may impair filtration, fluid balance, or other kidney functions.

\medterm{Nephrostomy}-- An artificial opening created between the kidney and the skin to drain urine directly
when the normal pathway is blocked. See urology.

\medterm{Nephrostomy Tube}-- A percutaneously placed catheter in the renal pelvis to drain urine, bypassing a
ureteral obstruction. Indications: ureteral obstruction (calculus, tumor, stricture)
causing hydronephrosis, pyonephrosis (infected, obstructed kidney — emergency drainage),
and as a temporary measure before definitive surgery.

\medterm{Nephrotic Range Proteinuria}-- Protein excretion greater than 3.5 g/day or urine protein-to-creatinine ratio greater
than 3.5 g/g. Reflects severe glomerular damage with significant protein loss. Degree
correlates with severity of injury and risk of complications including thromboembolism,
infection, and progression to ESRD.

\medterm{Nephrotic Syndrome}-- Clinical constellation of heavy proteinuria greater than 3.5 g/day, hypoalbuminemia less
than 3.0 g/dL, generalized edema, hyperlipidemia, and lipiduria. Results from increased
glomerular permeability to albumin. Reduced oncotic pressure causes fluid extravasation.
Hepatic lipoprotein synthesis increases causing hyperlipidemia.

\medterm{Nephrotoxicity}-- Kidney damage from toxins, drugs, contrast. Aminoglycosides, cisplatin, contrast media,
ethylene glycol. Monitoring: creatinine, urine output. Prevention: hydration, dose
adjustment.

\medterm{Nerve}-- A bundle of axons (nerve fibers) surrounded by connective tissue sheaths that transmits
electrical impulses between the central nervous system and the periphery. Structure:
epineurium (outer layer), perineurium (surrounds fascicles), endoneurium (surrounds
individual axons).

\medterm{Nerve Conduction Studies}-- Measurement of the speed and amplitude of electrical impulses along peripheral nerves.
Sensory NCS: stimulate at one point, record distally. Motor NCS: stimulate at two or
more points along the nerve, record from muscle. Parameters: conduction velocity (m/s),
amplitude (microvolts/millivolts), and distal latency.

\medterm{Nerve Conduction Study}-- A diagnostic test that measures the speed and strength of electrical signals traveling
through a nerve to detect nerve damage or dysfunction.

\medterm{Nervous Tissue}-- The tissue of the nervous system, composed of neurons and supporting cells, that detects
stimuli and transmits electrical signals.

\medterm{Nettle Rash}-- Urticaria, an itchy eruption of transient raised wheals caused by release of inflammatory mediators in the
skin.

\medterm{Neural Tube Defects}-- Congenital malformations resulting from failure of the neural tube to close during the
third to fourth week of gestation. Include anencephaly (incomplete cranial closure),
encephalocele (herniation through skull defect), and spina bifida (incomplete spinal
closure).

\medterm{Neuralgia}-- Pain caused by irritation or damage to a peripheral or cranial nerve, typically
described as sharp, burning, stabbing, or electric-shock-like and occurring along the
nerve’s distribution. It may be triggered by light touch or movement and can result from
compression, trauma, infection, metabolic disease, or demyelination.

\medterm{Neuraminidase Inhibitors}-- A class of antiviral drugs that inhibit the neuraminidase enzyme on the surface of
influenza viruses, preventing the release of newly formed viral particles from infected
cells and thereby limiting viral spread. The two available agents are oseltamivir,
administered orally, and zanamivir, administered by inhalation.

\medterm{Neuroblastoma}-- Childhood sympathetic nervous system tumor. MYCN amplification poor prognosis. Presents
as abdominal mass, opsoclonus-myoclonus syndrome. Spontaneous regression possible in
infants.

\medterm{Neurocognitive Disorder}-- A condition involving acquired decline in one or more cognitive abilities that affects daily
function. Causes include neurodegenerative disease, vascular disease, injury, infection, and medicines.

\medterm{Neurocysticercosis}-- The most common parasitic infection of the central nervous system worldwide and is
caused by the larval stage (cysticercus) of the pork tapeworm Taenia solium. It is
endemic in Latin America, Asia, and Africa, and is an important cause of epilepsy in
endemic regions.

\medterm{Neurodegeneration}-- The progressive loss of structure and function of neurons, culminating in their death,
and represents the underlying mechanism of many devastating neurological disorders.

\medterm{Neuroendocrine Tumor}-- A tumor arising from cells with both neural and hormone-producing features. These tumors can occur throughout the body and may produce hormones or be nonfunctioning.

\medterm{Neuroendocrine Tumors}-- Heterogeneous group of neoplasms arising from neuroendocrine cells throughout the
gastrointestinal tract and pancreas. May be functional producing hormones or
non-functional. Graded by Ki-67 index and mitotic count into well-differentiated
low-grade, intermediate-grade, and poorly differentiated high-grade tumors.

\medterm{Neuroendocrine Tumors Grading}-- Classification of neuroendocrine tumors by proliferative rate using Ki-67 index and
mitotic count. Well-differentiated low-grade tumors have Ki-67 less than three percent
and fewer than two mitoses per ten high-power fields. Intermediate-grade tumors have
Ki-67 of three to twenty percent.

\medterm{Neurofibroma}-- A benign peripheral nerve sheath tumor. Solitary neurofibromas are common and present as
soft, flesh-colored papules or nodules. Multiple neurofibromas are characteristic of
neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1).

\medterm{Neurofibromatosis}-- A group of autosomal dominant genetic disorders causing tumor growth along nerves. Type
1 (NF1, chromosome 17) causes café-au-lait spots, neurofibromas, optic gliomas, and
learning disabilities. Type 2 (NF2, chromosome 22) causes bilateral vestibular
schwannomas, meningiomas, and ependymomas.

\medterm{Neurofibromatosis Type 1}-- Autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutations in the NF1 gene encoding neurofibromin
on chromosome seventeen.

\medterm{Neurofibromatosis Type 2}-- An autosomal dominant disorder caused by NF2 gene mutation on chromosome 22.
Characterized by bilateral vestibular schwannomas (acoustic neuromas), meningiomas, and
ependymomas. Features: hearing loss, tinnitus, and balance problems. Diagnosis: MRI
showing bilateral vestibular schwannomas.

\medterm{Neurogenic Bladder}-- Lower urinary-tract dysfunction caused by a neurologic lesion affecting central or
peripheral control of storage and voiding. It may produce urgency, retention,
incontinence, high bladder pressures, recurrent infection, and upper-tract damage.

\medterm{Neurogenic megacolon}-- Massive dilation of the colon due to loss of autonomic innervation, most commonly from
Chagas disease, Hirschsprung disease, or diabetic autonomic neuropathy. It presents with
chronic constipation, abdominal distension, fecal impaction, and risk of toxic
megacolon.

\medterm{Neurogenic Shock}-- Distributive shock caused by loss of sympathetic vascular tone, usually after acute
spinal-cord injury above T6. It is characterized by hypotension with relative
bradycardia and warm skin, distinguishing it from hemorrhagic shock when the clinical
findings are complete.

\medterm{Neurography}-- MRI technique visualizing peripheral nerves. Used for nerve tumors, entrapment, trauma.

\medterm{Neuroleptic}-- An older term for antipsychotic medications, derived from their ability to produce a
state of mental indifference and motor slowing. The term is now largely replaced by
``antipsychotic,'' though it is still used in specific contexts such as neuroleptic
malignant syndrome. Typical neuroleptics are first-generation antipsychotics.

\medterm{Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome}-- A rare but life-threatening idiosyncratic reaction to dopamine receptor-blocking agents,
typically antipsychotics, characterized by hyperthermia, severe muscle rigidity, altered
mental status, and autonomic dysfunction.

\medterm{Neurologic Localization}-- The process of using the pattern of deficits in strength, sensation, reflexes,
coordination, cranial-nerve function, and cognition to identify the anatomic site of
nervous-system dysfunction. Localization guides differential diagnosis and selection of
investigations.

\medterm{Neurological Examination}-- A clinical assessment of mental status, cranial nerves, motor function, reflexes, coordination, sensation, and gait.
It helps localize nervous-system dysfunction and determine the need for further testing.

\medterm{Neurological Rehabilitation}-- Rehabilitation for stroke, spinal cord injury, traumatic brain injury, Parkinson
disease, multiple sclerosis. Goals: maximize function, independence, quality of life.
Multidisciplinary team: physiatrist, PT, OT, speech therapy, psychology.

\medterm{Neurology PET}-- PET imaging used in selected neurologic disorders to assess brain metabolism or receptor activity, such as in some dementias, epilepsy evaluations, and movement disorders.

\medterm{Neuromuscular Blocking Agents}-- Medications administered intraoperatively to induce skeletal muscle relaxation and
facilitate endotracheal intubation and surgical exposure.

\medterm{Neuromuscular Junction}-- The neuromuscular junction is the synapse between motor neuron and skeletal muscle
fiber, where acetylcholine released from presynaptic terminals binds to nicotinic
acetylcholine receptors on the postsynaptic motor end plate, causing depolarization and
muscle contraction.

\medterm{Neuromyelitis Optica}-- Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD), formerly known as Devic disease, is an
autoimmune, inflammatory demyelinating disorder of the central nervous system that
preferentially targets the optic nerves and spinal cord.

\medterm{Neuron}-- The fundamental excitable cell of the nervous system specialized for receiving,
integrating, and transmitting electrical and chemical signals. Structural components:
cell body (soma), dendrites (receptive), axon (conducting), and synaptic terminals
(releasing neurotransmitters).

\medterm{Neuropathic Pain}-- Chronic pain caused by damage or disease affecting the somatosensory nervous system,
including the peripheral nerves, dorsal root ganglia, spinal cord, or brain.

\medterm{Neuropathic Ulcer}-- A chronic wound of the foot in a patient with diabetes, resulting from peripheral
neuropathy, peripheral artery disease, and impaired wound healing. See diabetic foot
ulcer.

\medterm{Neuropathy}-- Disease or dysfunction of peripheral nerves causing pain, numbness, tingling, weakness,
or altered reflexes. Common causes include diabetes, alcohol, nutritional deficiency,
toxins, compression, autoimmune disease, and inherited disorders.

\medterm{Neuropathy (Peripheral Neuropathy)}-- Damage to peripheral nerves causing sensory, motor, or autonomic dysfunction. Common
causes: diabetes mellitus, alcohol use disorder, vitamin B12 deficiency, chemotherapy,
autoimmune diseases, and hereditary conditions (Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease). Presents
with numbness, tingling, burning pain, weakness, and gait instability.

\medterm{Neuroplasticity}-- The brain's ability to reorganize its structure, function, and connections in response
to experience, learning, and injury. It is relevant to psychiatric treatment because
antidepressants, psychotherapy, and ECT are thought to promote neuroplastic changes
including neurogenesis in the hippocampus and synaptic remodeling.

\medterm{Neurosarcoidosis}-- Neurological involvement by sarcoidosis, caused by non-caseating granulomatous
inflammation of the brain, meninges, cranial nerves, spinal cord, peripheral nerves, or
muscles. Presentations include cranial neuropathies, aseptic meningitis, seizures,
hypothalamic or pituitary dysfunction, myelopathy, and peripheral neuropathy.

\medterm{Neurosyphilis}-- Infection of the central or peripheral nervous system by Treponema pallidum. It may
occur at any stage of syphilis and cause meningitis, stroke, cranial-nerve dysfunction,
sensory ataxia, cognitive decline, or tabes dorsalis.

\medterm{Neurotoxin}-- Substance toxic to nervous system. Examples: lead, mercury, organophosphates, botulinum
toxin, tetrodotoxin, snake venoms.

\medterm{Neurotransmitter}-- Neurotransmitters are endogenous chemical messengers that transmit signals across
synapses from one neuron to another or to target cells such as muscle or gland cells.

\medterm{Neurovascular Examination}-- Assessment of distal motor function, sensation, pulses, capillary refill, skin
temperature, and color in an injured limb. Serial examination is essential for detecting
nerve injury, vascular compromise, compartment syndrome, and evolving ischemia.

\medterm{Neutropenia}-- A reduced neutrophil count ($<1.5\times10^9/\mathrm{L}$; severe $<0.5$), increasing risk of bacterial
and fungal infection, especially febrile neutropenia.

\medterm{Neutrophil Extracellular Traps}-- NETs: web-like structures of chromatin + granular proteins (MPO, NE, histone) released
by neutrophils (NETosis: suicidal vs vital).

\medterm{Neutrophil Recruitment}-- A multistep process essential for acute inflammation and host defense, involving
sequential adhesive interactions between neutrophils and endothelial cells. The process
begins with the rolling phase, where neutrophils loosely attach to endothelial cells
through selectin-mediated interactions.

\medterm{Neutrophilic dermatosis}-- A group of inflammatory skin conditions characterised by dense neutrophilic infiltrate
in the dermis without evidence of infection, which includes Sweet syndrome (acute
febrile neutrophilic dermatosis), pyoderma gangrenosum, and Behçet disease.

\medterm{Neutrophilic eccrine hidradenitis}-- A neutrophilic dermatosis characterized by neutrophilic infiltration of the eccrine
glands and surrounding dermis, typically occurring in patients receiving chemotherapy,
particularly cytarabine. It presents with painful, erythematous plaques or nodules, most
commonly on the trunk and extremities.

\medterm{Nevus}-- A benign hamartoma of the skin; most commonly melanocytic nevus (mole). Other nevi:
congenital, dysplastic/atypical, Spitz, blue nevus, halo nevus; non-melanocytic:
epidermal, sebaceous, vascular (strawberry hemangioma, port-wine stain).

\medterm{Nevus comedonicus}-- A congenital hamartoma of the pilosebaceous unit presenting as a localised cluster of
dilated follicular ostia filled with keratinous plugs, resembling closely grouped open
comedones. It typically appears at birth or in early childhood on the face, neck, or
trunk, and may be associated with other developmental anomalies.

\medterm{New Daily Persistent Headache}-- A headache that begins on a clearly remembered day and becomes continuous within 24 hours, persisting for
at least three months. It may resemble migraine or tension-type headache; a new persistent headache requires
medical evaluation to exclude secondary causes such as infection, pressure disorders, or vascular disease.

\medterm{Newborn Circumcision}-- Surgical removal of the foreskin from the penis, typically performed within the first
few days of life. Medical benefits include reduced risk of urinary tract infections,
sexually transmitted infections, and penile cancer. Complications include bleeding,
infection, and meatal stenosis.

\medterm{Newborn Hearing Screen}-- Universal screening for congenital hearing loss, performed before hospital discharge.
Two-step protocol: otoacoustic emissions (OAE: measures cochlear outer hair cell
response to click stimulus) and automated auditory brainstem response (AABR: measures
neural response from the cochlea to the brainstem). Pass/refer.

\medterm{Newborn Metabolic Screen}-- A dried blood spot test (heel stick) collected 24-48 hours after birth to screen for
congenital disorders before symptoms develop.

\medterm{Newborn Screening}-- A public health program testing newborns for treatable genetic, metabolic, and endocrine
disorders 24-48 hours after birth. Blood spot (heel stick) on filter paper (Guthrie
card). Conditions vary by state (Recommended Uniform Screening Panel: 35 core
conditions).

\medterm{Next-Generation Sequencing}-- Massively parallel sequencing technology that analyzes many DNA molecules
simultaneously. It supports gene panels, whole-exome sequencing, whole-genome
sequencing, and tumor sequencing, with limitations involving coverage, structural
variants, mosaicism, and interpretation.

\medterm{Niacin (Vitamin B3)}-- A water-soluble vitamin that forms NAD+ and NADP+, essential electron carriers in
oxidation-reduction reactions and biosynthetic pathways. Severe deficiency causes
pellagra, characterized by dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia.

\medterm{Nickel-Chromium}-- Base metal alloy for crowns. Less expensive than noble metals. Nickel allergy concern.

\medterm{Nicotine}-- An addictive stimulant found in tobacco and some vaping products that acts on neuronal nicotinic receptors.

\medterm{Niemann-Pick Disease}-- A group of autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorders caused by sphingomyelinase
deficiency (ASM, types A and B, SMPD1 mutations) or NPC1/NPC2 cholesterol transport
defects (type C).

\medterm{Night Blindness}-- Impaired vision in dim light (nyctalopia). Causes: vitamin A deficiency (most common
cause worldwide, leading to retinol deficiency and rod dysfunction), retinitis
pigmentosa (progressive rod-cone degeneration, genetic), congenital stationary night
blindness, advanced glaucoma, and cataract.

\medterm{Night Eating Syndrome}-- Delayed circadian pattern of eating. Evening hyperphagia, morning anorexia, insomnia.
Associated with obesity, depression.

\medterm{Night Sweats}-- Episodes of excessive sweating during sleep that soak clothing or bedding. They may occur with infection,
hormonal changes, cancer, medication effects, or other systemic illness; persistent unexplained
night sweats warrant assessment.

\medterm{NIH Stroke Scale}-- The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) is a standardized, validated
clinical assessment tool used to quantify the severity of acute ischemic stroke.

\medterm{Nikolsky Sign}-- A clinical dermatological sign in which light lateral pressure applied to
normal-appearing skin causes epidermal detachment or sloughing. Positive sign indicates
intraepidermal loss of cell-cell cohesion (acantholysis).

\medterm{Nipah Virus Infection}-- A zoonotic viral infection that may cause fever, respiratory illness, encephalitis, seizures, and coma. It can
spread from infected animals or people through close contact. There is no widely available licensed vaccine or
specific antiviral treatment; suspected infection requires urgent isolation and specialist care.

\medterm{Nitrates}-- Vasodilator drugs that release nitric oxide, causing relaxation of vascular smooth
muscle through activation of guanylyl cyclase and increased cyclic GMP. They primarily
cause venodilation at low doses, reducing preload and myocardial oxygen demand, and
arterial dilation at higher doses, reducing afterload.

\medterm{Nitrogen Balance}-- The difference between dietary nitrogen intake, estimated mainly from protein intake,
and nitrogen loss, primarily in urine. Positive balance supports growth or tissue
repair; negative balance occurs with inadequate intake, inflammation, trauma, or
catabolic disease.

\medterm{Nitroglycerin}-- An organic nitrate that releases nitric oxide, relaxing vascular smooth
muscle—predominantly venous. Used for acute angina (sublingual spray/tablet, IV for
ACS/heart failure), angina prophylaxis, and preload reduction in acute pulmonary edema.
Rapid onset (1-3 min sublingual).

\medterm{Nitroprusside}-- A direct-acting vasodilator (arterial and venous) releasing nitric oxide, used for
hypertensive emergencies and controlled hypotension during surgery. IV infusion with
rapid onset and offset; reduces both preload and afterload.

\medterm{Nitrous Oxide}-- An inhaled anesthetic gas with analgesic and anesthetic-sparing properties. It has rapid
onset and offset but relatively low potency, expands closed gas spaces, and may
contribute to diffusion hypoxia or postoperative nausea.

\medterm{Nitrous Oxide for Labor}-- A 50:50 mixture of nitrous oxide and oxygen (Entonox) inhaled by the mother during labor
for analgesia. Self-administered via a mask or mouthpiece; the woman controls her own
dosing, taking a breath 30 seconds before a contraction.

\medterm{Nocardia asteroides infection}-- Infection by Nocardia asteroides, an environmental bacterium that can cause lung, skin, or
brain infection, especially in immunocompromised people.

\medterm{Nocardiosis}-- A bacterial infection caused by Nocardia species (N. asteroides, N. brasiliensis), found
in soil. Most common in immunocompromised patients (transplant, HIV, chronic steroid
use). Pulmonary nocardiosis (most common): cough, fever, necrotizing pneumonia, cavitary
lesions.

\medterm{Nocebo}-- A harmful or unpleasant symptom or treatment effect caused or amplified by negative expectations rather than by the treatment’s specific pharmacologic action.

\medterm{Nociception}-- Neural processing of noxious thermal, mechanical, or chemical stimuli that may result in
the perception of pain. It includes transduction, transmission, modulation, and
projection; nociception and pain are related but not identical concepts.

\medterm{Nocturia}-- Complaint of waking one or more times during the night to void, with each void preceded
and followed by sleep. Highly prevalent in older adults and significantly impairs sleep
quality and quality of life.

\medterm{Nodular lymphoid hyperplasia}-- Benign aggregates of lymphoid tissue occurring in the gastrointestinal tract, most
commonly in the small intestine, presenting as multiple small nodules on endoscopy or
imaging. It may be associated with immunodeficiency states, particularly common variable
immunodeficiency, and Giardia lamblia infection.

\medterm{Nodule}-- Small, palpable mass <2 cm. Can be solid or cystic. Examples: thyroid nodule, rheumatoid
nodule, pulmonary nodule. Evaluation depends on location.

\medterm{Nomogram}-- A graphical tool that estimates a quantity or risk from related measurements. The Rumack–Matthew nomogram is used in selected cases of acute acetaminophen ingestion.

\medterm{Non communicating Hydrocephalus}-- Non-communicating (obstructive) hydrocephalus is a type of hydrocephalus in which there
is obstruction to the flow of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) within the ventricular system,
preventing CSF from reaching the arachnoid granulations for absorption.

\medterm{Non-Accidental Trauma}-- Injuries inflicted on a child, suspected when history and examination findings are
inconsistent with the reported mechanism. Red flags: multiple fractures at different
stages of healing, posterior rib fractures, metaphyseal corner fractures, and retinal
hemorrhages.

\medterm{Non-Adherence}-- Failure to follow an agreed treatment plan, which may result from access barriers, side effects, beliefs, misunderstanding, or other circumstances.

\medterm{Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease}-- Hepatic steatosis in the absence of significant alcohol consumption, strongly associated
with metabolic syndrome, obesity, and type 2 diabetes. NAFLD includes simple steatosis
and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) with inflammation and fibrosis. NASH can
progress to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.

\medterm{Non-Allergic Rhinitis}-- Nasal congestion, discharge, or sneezing not caused by an allergic immune response. Triggers may include changes in
temperature or humidity, smoke, odors, medicines, or hormonal changes.

\medterm{Non-Asthmatic Eosinophilic Bronchitis}-- A cause of chronic cough characterized by airway eosinophilia without the variable airflow
obstruction or bronchial hyperresponsiveness typical of asthma. Symptoms often improve
with inhaled corticosteroids, whereas bronchodilators alone may provide little benefit.

\medterm{Non-Celiac Gluten Sensitivity}-- Symptom improvement with gluten-free diet without celiac disease or wheat allergy.
Diagnosis of exclusion.

\medterm{Non-Communicable Disease}-- Disease not transmitted between persons. Examples: cancer, heart disease, diabetes.

\medterm{Non-Contact Tonometry}-- A method of estimating intraocular pressure with a brief puff of air directed at the cornea. It does not touch the
eye and is useful for screening, although abnormal or uncertain results may require
confirmation with another method.

\medterm{Non-Distensible Balloon (Vascular)}-- A catheter balloon designed to maintain its shape during high-pressure dilation of a
narrowed vessel.

\medterm{Non-Functioning Pituitary Adenoma}-- Pituitary adenomas that do not secrete clinically significant hormones. The most common
pituitary adenomas to present with mass effects.

\medterm{Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma}-- A group of malignant lymphoid tumors arising from B cells, T cells, or natural-killer
cells. Subtypes differ in biology, clinical behavior, affected organs, and treatment.

\medterm{Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing}-- Screening of cell-free placental DNA in maternal blood for selected fetal chromosomal
abnormalities, especially trisomies 21, 18, and 13. It is highly sensitive screening,
not a definitive diagnosis; positive results require confirmatory invasive testing.

\medterm{Non-Invasive Ventilation}-- Positive pressure ventilation delivered via a face mask or nasal mask without
endotracheal intubation. BiPAP (bilevel positive airway pressure) is most commonly used
for acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (COPD exacerbation) and cardiogenic pulmonary
edema. It reduces the need for intubation and improves outcomes in selected patients.

\medterm{Non-Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy}-- The early stage of diabetic retinopathy, characterized by structural abnormalities of
the retinal vasculature resulting from chronic hyperglycemia-induced damage to the
endothelial cells and pericytes of retinal capillaries, without neovascularization.

\medterm{Non-Radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis}-- Inflammatory arthritis affecting the spine and sacroiliac region without definite structural changes on plain
X-ray. It may cause chronic inflammatory back pain, morning stiffness, enthesitis, uveitis, psoriasis, or inflammatory
bowel disease. MRI, laboratory findings, and clinical assessment help establish the diagnosis.

\medterm{Non-Rebreather Mask}-- A face mask with a reservoir bag and one-way valves that can deliver a high concentration of supplemental oxygen when fitted and used correctly.

\medterm{Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer}-- Most common lung cancer type. Includes adenocarcinoma, squamous cell, large cell.

\medterm{Non-Stress Test}-- A fetal heart rate monitoring technique assessing fetal heart rate reactivity over 20-40
minutes. Normal (reactive) tracing shows at least 2 fetal heart rate accelerations of 15
beats per minute above baseline lasting at least 15 seconds within a 20-minute period.
Non-reactive tracings may indicate fetal hypoxia or sleep cycle.

\medterm{Non-Stress Test}-- Antepartum fetal monitoring assessing fetal heart rate reactivity. Reactive NST: \(\geq 2\)
accelerations of 15 bpm for 15 seconds (\(\geq 32\) weeks) or 10 bpm for 10 seconds (<32
weeks) within 20 minutes. Non-reactive: insufficient accelerations after 40 minutes (may
indicate fetal sleep, acidosis, or CNS depression). False positive rate high.

\medterm{Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria}-- NTM: environmental mycobacteria (M. avium complex - MAC, M. kansasii, M. abscessus).
Pulmonary NTM: ATS/IDSA criteria (clinical + radiographic + microbiologic: 2+ sputum or
1 BAL + nodules/cavities/bronchiectasis on CT). MAC: nodular bronchiectatic (middle-aged
women, "Lady Windermere syndrome") or fibrocavitary (older men, COPD).

\medterm{Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease}-- NAFLD: hepatic steatosis (>5 percent of hepatocytes) in the absence of significant
alcohol use, associated with the metabolic syndrome (obesity, type 2 diabetes,
dyslipidemia, hypertension).

\medterm{Nonalcoholic Steatohepatitis}-- Progressive form of NAFLD characterized by hepatic steatosis with lobular inflammation
and hepatocyte ballooning with significant risk of progression to fibrosis, cirrhosis,
and hepatocellular carcinoma. NASH affects approximately three to five percent of the
general population.

\medterm{Noncompetitive Antagonist}-- A noncompetitive antagonist is a drug that reduces the effect of an agonist by a
mechanism that cannot be overcome by increasing the agonist concentration.

\medterm{Noncompetitive Inhibition}-- A type of enzyme inhibition where the inhibitor binds at a site distinct from the active
site (allosteric site), and can bind to both the free enzyme (E) and the
enzyme-substrate complex (ES) with equal affinity. The inhibitor does not compete with
the substrate and cannot be overcome by increasing substrate concentration.

\medterm{Noninvasive Prenatal Testing}-- A maternal blood test that analyzes cell-free DNA to estimate the chance of selected
fetal chromosomal conditions. It is a screening test, not a definitive diagnosis.

\medterm{Noninvasive Ventilation}-- Positive-pressure ventilatory support delivered without an endotracheal tube, commonly
by a mask or helmet. CPAP provides continuous positive pressure; bilevel support
provides separate inspiratory and expiratory pressures. It is used selectively when the
patient can protect the airway and cooperate.

\medterm{Nonmelanoma Skin Cancer}-- A group of skin cancers that arise from the cells of the epidermis, excluding melanoma.
The two most common types are basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and squamous cell carcinoma
(SCC), which together account for the vast majority of all skin cancers diagnosed
globally.

\medterm{Nonselective Beta-Blockers}-- Nonselective beta-adrenergic blocking agents block both beta-1 and beta-2 adrenergic
receptors, reducing heart rate, myocardial contractility, and cardiac output through
beta-1 blockade, while also blocking beta-2 receptors in the bronchial smooth muscle and
peripheral vasculature.

\medterm{Nonsense Variant}-- A sequence variant that creates a premature stop codon, potentially producing a
truncated protein or triggering nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. The effect depends on the
gene, location, transcript, and residual protein production.

\medterm{Nonspecific Interstitial Pneumonia}-- A pattern with uniform, temporally homogeneous inflammation and/or fibrosis. Most common
ILD associated with connective tissue disease. HRCT shows bilateral ground-glass
opacities with subpleural sparing.

\medterm{Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug}-- A medicine that inhibits cyclooxygenase enzymes and reduces prostaglandin production, producing analgesic,
anti-inflammatory, and fever-reducing effects. NSAIDs can cause gastrointestinal,
kidney, cardiovascular, or other adverse effects.

\medterm{Nonsteroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs}-- Analgesic and anti-inflammatory drugs that inhibit cyclooxygenase enzymes and
prostaglandin synthesis. Perioperative use requires assessment of renal function,
gastrointestinal bleeding risk, platelet effects, cardiovascular risk, and surgical
bleeding considerations.

\medterm{Nontuberculous Mycobacterial Lung Disease}-- Chronic lung infection caused by environmental Mycobacterium species other than M. tuberculosis.
It may produce cough, fatigue, weight loss, breathlessness, or coughing blood, especially in people with structural
lung disease. Diagnosis requires compatible imaging and repeated microbiologic testing; treatment is prolonged and individualized.

\medterm{Nonunion and Malunion}-- Nonunion is failure of a fractured bone to heal after an adequate period (generally 6-9
months for long bones) without clinical or radiographic evidence of progression toward
union for 3 months. Classification: hypertrophic (viable ends, inadequate stability),
atrophic (devitalized ends, poor biology), oligotrophic (intermediate).

\medterm{Noonan Syndrome}-- An inherited disorder of the RAS--MAPK signaling pathway, characterized by distinctive facial
features, short stature, congenital heart disease, and variable developmental or bleeding problems.

\medterm{Norepinephrine}-- Sympathetic neurotransmitter. Vasoconstriction, increased heart rate.

\medterm{Normal Flora}-- The resident microorganisms (commensals) colonizing body surfaces—skin, oropharynx,
gastrointestinal tract, genitourinary tract—without causing disease under normal
conditions. They provide protection (colonization resistance, immune priming), synthesis
of vitamins, and nutrient metabolism.

\medterm{Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus}-- A disorder of cerebrospinal-fluid circulation causing enlarged ventricles and a
clinical pattern that may include gait impairment, cognitive decline, and urinary urgency
or incontinence.

\medterm{Normalization}-- Correction for detector efficiency variations. Performed daily with uniform source.

\medterm{Normocalcemic Hyperparathyroidism}-- Condition of elevated parathyroid hormone with persistently normal serum calcium and
normal vitamin D levels. Exclusion of secondary causes of elevated PTH including vitamin
D deficiency, renal insufficiency, and medications is required. Represents an early or
mild form of primary hyperparathyroidism. May progress to overt hypercalcemia.

\medterm{Norovirus}-- A single-stranded RNA virus that is the leading cause of gastroenteritis outbreaks
worldwide. The virus is highly contagious and can cause large outbreaks in closed
settings including hospitals, cruise ships, and nursing homes. Norovirus causes acute
onset of vomiting and diarrhea. Diagnosis is made by PCR or antigen detection in stool.

\medterm{Norovirus Infection}-- A highly contagious viral gastroenteritis causing sudden nausea, vomiting, watery diarrhea, abdominal cramps, and
sometimes fever. Illness is usually short but can cause dangerous dehydration, especially in infants, older adults,
or medically vulnerable people. Oral rehydration and careful hand hygiene are central to management and prevention.


\medterm{Norrie Disease}-- An X-linked retinal disorder, usually caused by pathogenic variants in NDP,
characterized by congenital or early-onset blindness from abnormal retinal development.
Hearing loss, developmental differences, and peripheral vascular or vitreoretinal
complications may occur.

\medterm{Nose}-- The external nasal structure and nasal cavities, the primary organ of olfaction and the
entry to the respiratory tract. External nose: bone (nasal bones, frontal process of
maxilla) and cartilage (septal, upper/lower lateral cartilages).

\medterm{Nosebleed}-- Bleeding from the nasal mucosa, also called epistaxis. Most cases arise from the
anterior nasal septum and result from local trauma, dryness, inflammation, or vascular
disease; persistent or severe bleeding may require medical intervention.

\medterm{Nosocomial}-- Hospital-acquired. Nosocomial infection: infection acquired in hospital.

\medterm{Nosocomial Infection}-- A hospital-acquired infection developing during or after hospitalization that was not
present or incubating at admission, classically defined by onset beyond 48 hours. Common
sites are bloodstream, urinary tract, lungs, and surgical wounds; causative organisms
are frequently multidrug-resistant, prevented by hand hygiene and stewardship.

\medterm{Notifiable Disease}-- Disease required by law to be reported to health authorities. Examples: tuberculosis,
measles, HIV, COVID-19.

\medterm{NSAIDs}-- Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are a class of drugs that inhibit cyclooxygenase
enzymes, COX-1 and COX-2, reducing the synthesis of prostaglandins and thromboxanes from
arachidonic acid. They produce analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, and
antiplatelet effects.

\medterm{NSAIDs (Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs)}-- A class of analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory drugs that inhibit
cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and/or COX-2), reducing prostaglandin and thromboxane synthesis.

\medterm{NSTEMI}-- Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction: acute myocardial necrosis (elevated cardiac
troponin) without persistent ST-segment elevation on ECG, caused by subtotal occlusion
or distal embolization of a coronary artery. Features: chest pain, ECG changes (ST
depression, T-wave inversion), elevated troponin.

\medterm{Nuchal Translucency Screening}-- An ultrasound measurement of the fluid-filled space at the posterior fetal nuchal
region, performed at 11-14 weeks (crown-rump length 45-84 mm). Increased NT (>95th
percentile or >3.0-3.5 mm) is associated with trisomies 21, 18, 13, Turner syndrome, and
cardiac defects.

\medterm{Nuclear Medicine}-- A medical specialty that uses small amounts of radioactive substances (radiotracers) to
diagnose, stage, and treat various diseases, including cancer and heart disease.

\medterm{Nuclear Medicine Documentation}-- Record keeping for radioactive materials, including inventory, disposal, administered
activity, and patient release.

\medterm{Nuclear Medicine Reporting}-- Structured interpretation of nuclear medicine studies using standardized terminology and
clinically relevant findings.

\medterm{Nuclear Medicine Resolution}-- Ability of a PET or SPECT system to distinguish small details, limited by factors such
as positron range, non-collinearity, and detector geometry.

\medterm{Nuclear Sclerosis}-- A common age-related cataract in which the central lens becomes increasingly yellow and hard. It can gradually reduce
vision, alter color perception, and sometimes temporarily improve near vision.

\medterm{Nuclear Stress Test}-- Myocardial perfusion imaging during stress. Detects coronary artery disease.

\medterm{Nucleoside Analogs}-- A class of antiviral drugs that structurally resemble natural nucleosides and inhibit
viral replication by interfering with DNA or RNA synthesis.

\medterm{Nucleus}-- The membrane-bound organelle containing the genetic material (DNA) in eukaryotic cells.
Surrounded by the nuclear envelope (double membrane with nuclear pores for transport).
Contains chromatin (DNA + histone proteins), nucleolus (ribosome biogenesis), and
nuclear matrix.

\medterm{Number Needed to Harm}-- The number of people exposed to an intervention for one additional person to experience
a specified adverse outcome compared with the control over a defined time. It is the
reciprocal of absolute risk increase.

\medterm{Number Needed to Treat}-- The number of people who must receive an intervention for one additional person to
experience the specified beneficial outcome compared with the control over a defined
time. It is the reciprocal of absolute risk reduction.

\medterm{Numbness and Tingling}-- Abnormal sensations, also called paresthesias, that may result from nerve compression,
peripheral neuropathy, spinal disease, vitamin deficiency, vascular problems, or
neurologic disorders. Sudden one-sided symptoms require urgent evaluation for stroke.

\medterm{Nummular eczema}-- A chronic inflammatory dermatosis presenting as coin-shaped, well-demarcated,
erythematous, vesicular, or crusted plaques, typically on the extremities. The lesions
are intensely pruritic and may be triggered by dry skin, skin trauma, or allergic
contact dermatitis. It is more common in adults with atopic predisposition.

\medterm{Nursing}-- A health profession focused on assessing patients, providing care, supporting recovery, preventing illness, and educating patients and families.

\medterm{Nursing Home}-- A residential facility that provides ongoing nursing care, assistance with daily activities, and medical or rehabilitation services for
people who cannot live independently.

\medterm{Nutcracker esophagus}-- Esophageal motility disorder characterized by high-amplitude peristaltic contractions
exceeding 180 mmHg in the distal esophagus on manometry, with preserved peristaltic
coordination. Presents with intermittent chest pain and dysphagia to solids and liquids.

\medterm{Nutcracker Syndrome}-- Compression of left renal vein between aorta and SMA causing hematuria and flank pain.
Managed conservatively. Stenting or surgery for severe cases.

\medterm{Nutrient Density}-- Amount of nutrients per calorie. Whole foods more nutrient-dense than processed foods.
Importance for weight management, health.

\medterm{Nutrition}-- The intake and use of food and nutrients needed for energy, growth, tissue maintenance, and health.

\medterm{Nutrition (Geriatric)}-- Nutritional assessment and intervention in elderly. Risks: malnutrition (undernutrition,
obesity), micronutrient deficiencies (vitamin D, B12, calcium), dehydration. Assessment:
weight, BMI, dietary intake, lab values. Interventions: supplementation, meal delivery,
appetite stimulants, feeding assistance.

\medterm{Nutrition (Oncology)}-- Nutritional care for people with cancer, addressing intake, symptoms, treatment effects, weight, muscle mass, and personal goals.

\medterm{Nutrition Education}-- Teaching patients about healthy eating. Cultural sensitivity, health literacy
appropriate. Motivational interviewing effective.

\medterm{Nutrition in Pregnancy}-- A balanced diet providing adequate calories, protein, vitamins, and minerals to support
maternal and fetal health. Additional caloric needs: ~300 kcal/day in the second and
third trimesters. Key nutrients: folic acid (400 mcg/day), iron (27 mg/day), calcium
(1000 mg/day), vitamin D (600 IU/day), iodine (220 mcg/day), DHA (200-300 mg/day).

\medterm{Nutrition Risk Screening 2002}-- A validated screening tool for hospitalized adults that considers nutritional status and
disease severity to identify patients likely to benefit from nutrition support.
Screening is not itself a complete malnutrition diagnosis.

\medterm{Nutrition-Focused Physical Examination}-- A structured examination for physical findings associated with malnutrition, including
loss of subcutaneous fat, muscle wasting, edema, ascites, reduced functional status, and
micronutrient-related signs. It complements dietary history, anthropometry, laboratory
data, and disease assessment.

\medterm{Nutritional Assessment}-- Comprehensive evaluation: dietary intake, anthropometrics, biochemical tests, clinical
examination. Dietitian involvement essential.

\medterm{Nutritional Dermatoses}-- Nutritional deficiencies cause distinctive skin findings. Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy):
perifollicular hemorrhage, corkscrew hairs, gingival bleeding. Niacin deficiency
(pellagra): photosensitive dermatitis in sun-exposed areas, diarrhea, dementia.

\medterm{Nutritional Screening}-- Rapid assessment for malnutrition risk. Tools: MST, MNA, MUST. Screening all elderly
patients recommended. Identifies need for comprehensive assessment.

\medterm{Nyctalopia}-- Difficulty seeing in dim light or darkness. See Night Blindness.

\medterm{NYHA Functional Classification}-- A four-class system grading heart failure severity based on symptoms and exercise
tolerance. Class I: no limitation of physical activity. Class II: slight limitation;
comfortable at rest but ordinary activity causes symptoms. Class III: marked limitation;
comfortable at rest but less than ordinary activity causes symptoms.

\medterm{Nystagmus}-- A rhythmic, involuntary oscillation of the eyes that can be horizontal, vertical,
rotational, or mixed in pattern, and it is classified based on the underlying cause as
infantile (developing in the first six months of life), acquired (developing later in
life), or periodic alternating.

\medterm{Natural Killer Cells}-- Immune cells that destroy infected or abnormal cells without prior exposure to a specific antigen.

\medterm{Nephritis}-- Inflammation of kidney tissue.

\medterm{Nerve Block}-- Injection of medicine near a nerve or nerve group to reduce pain or interrupt nerve signals.

\medterm{Nitric Oxide}-- A signaling molecule that relaxes blood-vessel muscle and promotes vasodilation.

\medterm{Nociceptors}-- Sensory nerve endings that detect potentially tissue-damaging stimuli and transmit pain signals.

\medterm{Non-HDL Cholesterol}-- Total blood cholesterol minus high-density lipoprotein cholesterol; it represents all cholesterol carried by potentially atherogenic particles.

\medterm{Nonketotic Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar Syndrome}-- A severe complication of diabetes marked by very high blood glucose, dehydration, and increased blood osmolality, usually without substantial ketoacidosis.

\medterm{Normal-Pressure Hydrocephalus}-- A disorder in which cerebrospinal fluid accumulates and causes gait difficulty, cognitive decline, and urinary problems despite near-normal measured pressure.

\medterm{Nucleus Accumbens}-- A brain region involved in reward, motivation, and reinforcement.

\medterm{Nephrosclerosis}-- Hardening and scarring of the kidneys caused by chronic vascular injury, often related to hypertension.

\medterm{Nocardia Infection}-- An infection caused by Nocardia bacteria, often affecting the lungs, skin, or brain.

\medterm{Noma}-- A rapidly progressive destructive infection of the mouth and face, usually associated with severe malnutrition and poor health conditions.

\medterm{Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma}-- A group of cancers arising from lymphocytes outside the Hodgkin lymphoma category.

\medterm{Non-Infectious Cystitis}-- Inflammation of the bladder that is not caused by an infectious organism.
\medterm{Nickel Allergy}-- An allergic skin reaction caused by contact with nickel-containing materials.
\medterm{Nightmare Disorder}-- Repeated disturbing dreams that cause significant distress or interfere with sleep and daytime function.
\medterm{Nonallergic Rhinitis}-- Nasal congestion, sneezing, or runny nose caused by irritants or other triggers rather than an allergy.
\medterm{Neuroglia}-- Supporting cells of the nervous system that nourish, protect, and maintain neurons.
