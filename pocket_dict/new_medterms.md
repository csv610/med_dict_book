# New Medical Terms: Childbirth

The following terms related to childbirth are missing from the dictionary. Definitions are written in simple, clear, and direct language suitable for a general medical reference.

---

### Amniotomy
Breaking the bag of waters during labor using a small plastic hook. A doctor or midwife does this through the cervix to help start or speed up labor, or to place an internal monitor on the baby.

---

### Bandl's Ring
An abnormal muscular ridge that forms across the middle of the womb during difficult labor. It shows that the baby is stuck in the birth canal, warning that the womb is under extreme strain and could tear if an emergency cesarean delivery is not done right away.

---

### Cardinal Movements of Labor
The natural series of turns and adjustments a baby makes to pass through the mother's pelvis during birth. These steps include entering the pelvis, moving downward, tucking the chin, turning, tilting the head back to emerge, turning to align the shoulders, and sliding out.

---

### Fetal Station
A measurement of how far down the baby's head has moved into the mother's pelvis during labor. It is measured in centimeters above or below the ischial spines, which are two bony points in the pelvis. A station of zero means the baby's head has reached these bones and is properly engaged.

---

### Bloody Show
A small amount of pink, red, or brownish mucus that passes from the vagina near the end of pregnancy or early in labor. It happens when the cervix begins to soften and stretch open, releasing the mucus plug that sealed the womb during pregnancy.

---

### Cervical Ripening
The natural process near the end of pregnancy where the cervix softens, thins out, and gets ready to open for labor. If labor needs to be started early, medicines or small inflatable balloons can be used to help ripen the cervix.

---

### Lower Segment Cesarean Section (LSCS)
A surgical operation to deliver a baby through a horizontal cut across the lower, thinner part of the womb. It is the standard type of cesarean delivery because it causes less bleeding, heals well, and is much less likely to tear open in a future pregnancy.

---

### Classical Cesarean Section
A cesarean delivery performed through a vertical cut down the upper, muscular part of the womb. It is reserved for rare emergencies because the resulting scar has a higher risk of tearing open during a future pregnancy.

---

### Vaginal Birth After Cesarean (VBAC)
Giving birth through the vagina after having had a cesarean section in an earlier pregnancy. Many women can safely have a VBAC, but labor must be monitored closely in a hospital in case complications arise.

---

### Peripartum Hysterectomy
An emergency surgery to remove the womb during or shortly after childbirth. It is performed as a life-saving measure to stop severe, uncontrollable bleeding that does not respond to medicines or other treatments.

---

### Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit Maneuver
A hands-on technique used by a doctor or midwife during a breech birth (where the baby comes out bottom-first) to keep the baby's chin tucked and safely guide the head out of the birth canal.

---

### Løvset's Maneuver
A technique used during a breech delivery to free the baby's arms and shoulders. The doctor or midwife gently rotates the baby's body halfway around in one direction to deliver one arm, and then rotates it back to deliver the other arm.

---

### Pinard's Maneuver
A technique used during a breech delivery when the baby's legs are straight up against the body. The doctor gently presses behind the baby's knee to bend the leg and bring the foot down so it can be delivered.

---

### Bakri Balloon
A soft medical balloon placed inside the womb and filled with warm sterile water to press against the bleeding walls of the uterus. It is used to quickly control severe bleeding after childbirth without needing immediate surgery.

---

### Subgaleal Hematoma
A dangerous pool of blood that forms under a newborn baby's scalp, usually caused by pulling forces during a difficult vacuum or forceps delivery. Because blood can spread freely across the entire scalp, it can cause severe blood loss and requires urgent medical attention.

---

### Fetal Scalp Blood Sampling
A test done during labor where a tiny drop of blood is taken from the baby's scalp through the birth canal. It measures oxygen and acid levels in the blood to check whether the baby is under stress and needs immediate delivery.

---

### Uterine Inertia
A condition where the contractions of the womb during labor are too weak, too short, or too far apart to open the cervix and push the baby down. It is often treated with medicines (such as oxytocin) to strengthen the contractions.

---

### Subinvolution of the Uterus
A condition where the womb fails to shrink back to its normal, pre-pregnancy size after childbirth. It is usually caused by an infection or small pieces of the placenta left behind inside the womb.

---

# New Medical Terms: Glands Diseases

The following terms related to gland diseases are missing from the dictionary. Definitions are written in simple, clear, and direct language suitable for a general medical reference.

---

### Toxic Multinodular Goiter (Plummer Disease)
An enlarged thyroid gland containing several lumps that produce too much thyroid hormone on their own. It is a common cause of hyperthyroidism in older adults and can cause rapid heartbeat, weight loss, heat intolerance, and shakiness.

---

### Toxic Adenoma
A single non-cancerous lump in the thyroid gland that works independently to release excess thyroid hormones. It causes symptoms of an overactive thyroid, while the rest of the thyroid gland shuts down and stops producing hormones.

---

### De Quervain's Thyroiditis (Subacute Granulomatous Thyroiditis)
A painful inflammation of the thyroid gland that usually develops after a viral infection. It causes severe tenderness in the front of the neck, fever, and a temporary phase of hyperthyroidism followed by a short period of low thyroid hormone levels before the gland heals.

---

### Wolff-Chaikoff Effect
A natural protective reflex of the thyroid gland where taking in very large amounts of iodine temporarily shuts down the production of thyroid hormones. Doctors use this effect to treat severe hyperthyroidism or to prepare the thyroid gland for surgery.

---

### Conn Syndrome
A condition where a benign tumor in one of the adrenal glands makes too much of the hormone aldosterone. This causes high blood pressure that is hard to control and low potassium levels in the blood, which can lead to muscle weakness and cramps.

---

### Adrenal Incidentaloma
An unexpected lump found in an adrenal gland during a CT or MRI scan that was done for an unrelated reason. Doctors run blood and urine tests to see if the lump is releasing excess hormones and check if it needs surgery or regular monitoring.

---

### VIPoma
A rare tumor of the pancreas that produces too much vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP). It causes severe, continuous watery diarrhea, dangerously low potassium levels, and dehydration that persists even when a person stops eating.

---

### Sialadenosis (Sialosis)
A painless, non-cancerous swelling of the salivary glands (most often the parotid glands near the cheeks) on both sides of the face. It is not caused by an infection or a stone, but is commonly linked to diabetes, heavy alcohol use, or eating disorders.

---

### Plunging Ranula
A large, fluid-filled cyst under the tongue that bursts through the floor of the mouth and spreads downward into the neck. It forms when a damaged salivary gland leaks saliva into the surrounding tissues, creating a soft swelling in the neck.

---

### Frey Syndrome
A condition where a person sweats and flushes on the side of the face while eating. It happens after surgery or injury to the parotid salivary gland, when severed nerve fibers mistakenly grow back into sweat glands in the skin instead of the salivary gland.

---

### Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD)
A common eye condition where the tiny oil glands along the edges of the eyelids become blocked or inflamed. Without enough healthy oil, tears evaporate too quickly, causing dry, gritty, red, and burning eyes.

---

### Fordyce Spots
Small, painless, yellowish-white bumps found on the lips, inside of the cheeks, or on the genitals. They are normal, harmless oil glands that do not have hair follicles, and they do not require any medical treatment.

---

# New Medical Terms: Stomach Disorders

The following terms related to stomach disorders are missing from the dictionary. Definitions are written in simple, clear, and direct language suitable for a general medical reference.

---

### Watermelon Stomach (Gastric Antral Vascular Ectasia / GAVE)
A condition where tiny blood vessels in the lower part of the stomach become swollen and form red stripes that look like the rind of a watermelon. These fragile vessels can slowly bleed over time, leading to low iron levels and anemia, especially in people with liver damage or kidney disease.

---

### Reactive Gastropathy (Chemical Gastropathy)
Irritation and damage to the stomach lining caused by harsh substances rather than an infection. It is most commonly caused by taking pain relievers like ibuprofen or aspirin for a long time, drinking too much alcohol, or having digestive bile wash backward into the stomach.

---

### Fundic Gland Polyp
A small, harmless bump that grows in the upper part of the stomach. It is the most common type of stomach polyp and is frequently found during an endoscopy in people who have been taking acid-reducing heartburn medications (proton pump inhibitors) for many years.

---

### Gastric Hyperplastic Polyp
A non-cancerous growth that develops in the stomach lining in response to long-lasting irritation or inflammation, such as an untreated stomach infection. While most are harmless, larger polyps can sometimes turn cancerous and are usually removed during an endoscopy.

---

### Perforated Peptic Ulcer
A medical emergency where an untreated stomach or intestinal ulcer burns a hole completely through the wall of the digestive tract. This allows stomach acid, digestive juices, and food to leak into the belly, causing sudden, extreme abdominal pain and a hard, board-like stomach that requires emergency surgery.

---

### Borchardt's Triad
A classic set of three warning signs that show the stomach has twisted upon itself in an emergency called gastric volvulus. The signs are: sudden severe pain in the upper abdomen, violent gagging or dry heaving without being able to vomit, and being unable to pass a stomach tube through the nose into the stomach.

---

### Gastric MALT Lymphoma
A slow-growing cancer of the white blood cells in the stomach lining that is triggered by a common bacterial infection (Helicobacter pylori). It is unique because treating and curing the bacterial infection with antibiotics can often make the cancer completely disappear without chemotherapy.

---

### Virchow's Node (Troisier's Sign)
A firm, painless, swollen lymph node located just above the left collarbone. It is an important physical sign during an exam because it often indicates that a cancer from inside the abdomen, especially stomach cancer, has spread through the body's lymph system.

---

### Ménétrier's Disease
A rare disorder where the folds of the stomach lining become massively enlarged and look like giant wrinkles. These overgrown folds produce large amounts of mucus while losing protein from the blood, causing swelling in the legs, stomach pain, weight loss, and low stomach acid.

---

### Postprandial Distress Syndrome (PDS)
A common type of indigestion where a person feels uncomfortably full for hours after eating a normal meal, or feels too full to finish after taking only a few bites. It occurs without any visible ulcer or blockage in the stomach.

---

### Epigastric Pain Syndrome (EPS)
A type of recurring indigestion where a person feels a burning or aching pain in the upper middle part of the belly between the ribs. The pain is not directly linked to meals and happens without an ulcer or infection.

---

### Gastric Atony
A condition where the stomach muscles completely lose their strength and stop churning or pushing food down into the intestines. It causes the stomach to swell up with fluids and gas, often happening temporarily after major abdominal surgery or as a complication of severe diabetes.

---

# New Medical Terms: Breast Conditions

The following terms related to breast conditions and procedures are missing from the dictionary. Definitions are written in simple, clear, and direct language suitable for a general medical reference.

---

### BI-RADS (Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System)
A standard scoring system used by radiologists worldwide to describe the results of mammograms, ultrasounds, and MRI scans of the breast. It gives a clear score from 0 to 6 to tell doctors whether the breast tissue is normal, needs a follow-up test, or needs a biopsy to check for cancer.

---

### Digital Breast Tomosynthesis (3D Mammography)
An advanced type of mammogram that takes multiple low-dose X-ray pictures from different angles to create thin, 3D cross-sectional images of the breast. It helps doctors see through dense breast tissue more clearly and find small cancers that might be hidden on regular 2D mammograms.

---

### Stereotactic Breast Biopsy
A minimally invasive procedure where a doctor uses a computer and special X-ray equipment to guide a hollow needle into a tiny, suspicious spot in the breast that cannot be felt by hand. The needle takes small tissue samples to check for cancer without needing open surgery.

---

### Atypical Ductal Hyperplasia (ADH)
A non-cancerous condition where unusual, abnormal cells grow inside the milk ducts of the breast. While it is not cancer itself, having this condition increases a woman's future risk of developing breast cancer, and doctors usually recommend minor surgery to remove the affected area.

---

### Fat Necrosis of the Breast
A harmless lump that forms when fatty breast tissue is injured, bruised, or damaged by radiation, surgery, or an accident. The healing fat cells can form a firm, painless knot that feels and looks very much like breast cancer on an exam and mammogram, but it is completely non-cancerous.

---

### Radial Scar
A non-cancerous breast growth that looks like a spiky star on a mammogram. Because its irregular shape closely resembles invasive breast cancer on imaging, doctors usually remove it or take a tissue sample to make sure no cancerous cells are hiding inside.

---

### Zuska's Disease (Subareolar Breast Abscess)
A painful, recurring pocket of infection that forms under the colored area around the nipple (the areola). It is caused by blocked milk ducts and is strongly linked to cigarette smoking, often requiring minor surgery to remove the damaged duct so the infection does not return.

---

### Axillary Tail of Spence
The normal part of the breast that extends outward into the armpit. Because more than half of the breast's milk-producing glands are located in this area, it is the most common place where both harmless lumps and breast cancers develop.

---

### Inverted Nipple
A condition where the nipple points inward into the breast instead of poking outward. While being born with inverted nipples is harmless, a nipple that suddenly turns inward in adulthood can be an important warning sign of an infection or an underlying breast cancer.

---

### Axillary Lymph Node Dissection (ALND)
A surgical operation to remove the lymph nodes from the armpit to check if breast cancer has spread and to remove cancerous nodes. Because it can affect lymph drainage, it sometimes leads to long-term swelling in the arm known as lymphedema.

---

### Modified Radical Mastectomy (MRM)
A major surgery used to treat breast cancer that removes the entire breast, the nipple, and the lymph nodes under the arm, while leaving the chest wall muscles intact. It allows doctors to remove all cancer tissue while preserving the underlying chest shape.

---

### Skin-Sparing Mastectomy
A type of surgery to treat or prevent breast cancer where all the breast tissue inside is carefully removed, but nearly all of the natural skin covering the breast is preserved. This allows a plastic surgeon to immediately rebuild a natural-looking breast in the same operation.

---

# New Medical Terms: Skin Diseases

The following terms related to skin diseases, clinical signs, and screening criteria are missing from the dictionary. Definitions are written in simple, clear, and direct language suitable for a general medical reference.

---

### Fixed Drug Eruption (FDE)
An allergic skin reaction to a medicine that causes round, red or purple patches to appear on the skin. The patches always return in the exact same spot on the body each time the person takes that specific medication, and they often leave behind a dark, stained mark as they heal.

---

### Pruritic Urticarial Papules and Plaques of Pregnancy (PUPPP)
A very common, harmless skin condition that happens in the late stages of pregnancy. It causes intensely itchy red bumps and hive-like patches that usually start inside stretch marks on the belly and spread to the thighs and buttocks, disappearing soon after the baby is born.

---

### Inverse Psoriasis (Flexural Psoriasis)
A type of psoriasis that develops in the warm, moist folds of the skin, such as the armpits, groin, under the breasts, and between the buttocks. Because of sweat and friction, the red patches look smooth, shiny, and raw without the thick, silvery scales seen in other types of psoriasis.

---

### Marjolin's Ulcer
A rare and aggressive type of skin cancer that grows inside an old, long-standing wound, burn scar, or chronic skin sore that has never completely healed. It often develops decades after the original injury and needs to be surgically removed.

---

### ABCDE Rule of Melanoma
A simple guide used by doctors and patients to check whether a mole on the skin might be cancerous (melanoma). It stands for: Asymmetry (the two halves do not match), Border (ragged or uneven edges), Color (multiple colors or shades), Diameter (larger than 6 millimeters, or a pencil eraser), and Evolving (changing in size, shape, color, or starting to itch or bleed).

---

### Breslow Depth
A measurement in millimeters of how deeply a melanoma skin cancer has grown into the layers of the skin. It is the single most important number doctors use to predict how aggressive the cancer is and to decide how much skin needs to be removed around it.

---

### Auspitz Sign
A classic sign of psoriasis where tiny pinpoint drops of blood appear when the thick, silvery scales on a psoriatic plaque are gently scraped or lifted off. It happens because the tiny blood vessels under the skin scales are unusually close to the surface.

---

### Herald Patch
A single, oval, pink or salmon-colored scaly patch that appears on the chest, back, or neck as the first sign of a common skin condition called pityriasis rosea. It usually appears one to two weeks before a widespread, smaller rash breaks out across the body in a pattern that looks like a pine tree.

---

### Wickham's Striae
A fine, white, lace-like pattern of lines seen on the surface of purple, itchy bumps in people with a skin condition called lichen planus. It is most easily seen when looking closely at the bumps on the wrists or inside the lining of the cheeks.

---

### Heliotrope Rash
A distinctive purple-red or violet rash that covers the upper eyelids, often accompanied by puffiness around the eyes. It is an important medical sign of dermatomyositis, an autoimmune condition that causes muscle weakness and skin inflammation.

---

### Pathergy
A condition where minor injuries to the skin—like a small needle prick or scratch—cause an exaggerated reaction, turning into a red, swollen pimple or sore within a day or two. It is a helpful diagnostic clue for doctors evaluating rare inflammatory disorders like Behçet's disease.

---

### Sign of Leser-Trélat
The sudden, rapid appearance of dozens of itchy, brown, warty skin growths (seborrheic keratoses) across the chest and back over a few weeks or months. It is an important warning sign that an undiscovered cancer may be growing inside the body, most commonly in the stomach or colon.

---

# New Medical Terms: USMLE High-Yield Concepts

The following high-yield medical terms, classic triads, and diagnostic physical signs are missing from the dictionary. Definitions are written in simple, clear, and direct language suitable for a general medical reference.

---

### Cushing's Triad
A dangerous set of three warning signs that show pressure is building up rapidly inside the skull, which can squeeze and damage the brain. The three signs are: very high blood pressure with a wide difference between the top and bottom numbers, an unusually slow heart rate, and irregular or slowed breathing.

---

### Samter's Triad
A medical condition made up of three related health problems: asthma, recurring non-cancerous growths inside the nose (nasal polyps), and a severe allergic reaction to aspirin and similar pain relievers (like ibuprofen). Taking aspirin triggers sudden, severe wheezing and trouble breathing in people with this condition.

---

### Unhappy Triad (Blown Knee)
A severe knee injury common in athletes that happens when the outside of a planted leg takes a hard hit while twisting. It tears three important parts of the knee at the same time: the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL), the medial collateral ligament (MCL), and the meniscus shock-absorber cartilage inside the joint.

---

### Hakim-Adams Triad
A set of three classic symptoms that point to normal pressure hydrocephalus, a condition where extra spinal fluid builds up inside the brain. The symptoms are often remembered as "wet, wobbly, and wacky": trouble walking with feet that feel glued to the floor, sudden loss of bladder control, and gradual memory loss or confusion.

---

### Hutchinson's Triad
A group of three physical signs seen in children who were born with an untreated syphilis infection passed from the mother during pregnancy. The signs are: notched, peg-shaped front teeth, clouding of the front of the eyes that can cause blindness, and nerve damage leading to deafness.

---

### Psoas Sign
A physical exam test used by doctors to check for appendicitis when the appendix is hidden behind the colon. The doctor has the patient lie on their left side and gently pulls the right leg backward; if this causes sharp pain in the lower right belly, it strongly suggests an inflamed appendix is rubbing against the deep hip muscle.

---

### Obturator Sign
A physical exam test used to check for appendicitis when the appendix hangs down into the pelvis. The doctor bends the patient's right knee and hip and gently turns the leg inward; sharp pain in the lower right belly suggests an inflamed appendix is irritating nearby pelvic muscles.

---

### Kehr's Sign
A sharp, unexpected pain felt at the tip of the left shoulder caused by blood leaking into the belly and irritating the underside of the diaphragm. It is a classic warning sign of a torn or ruptured spleen, usually following a car accident, a fall, or a sports collision.

---

### Battle's Sign
Dark purple bruising that appears on the skin behind the ear over the mastoid bone a day or two after a head injury. It is an important physical clue that the patient has suffered a broken bone at the base of the skull.

---

### Raccoon Eyes
Dark, circular bruising that appears around both eyes after a severe head injury, without any direct hit to the face or eyes themselves. It happens when blood from a skull fracture at the front of the head leaks forward into the soft tissues around the eyes.

---

### Lhermitte's Sign
A sudden, brief sensation like an electric shock that shoots down the neck, spine, and into the arms or legs whenever a person bends their head forward toward their chest. It is a classic sign of nerve damage in the spinal cord, most commonly seen in multiple sclerosis.

---

### Uhthoff's Phenomenon
A temporary worsening of vision problems, muscle weakness, or fatigue in people with multiple sclerosis whenever their body temperature rises. It can be triggered by a hot shower, fever, exercise, or hot weather, and symptoms improve once the body cools down.

---

### Marcus Gunn Pupil
An abnormal eye reaction where a person's pupils widen instead of shrinking when a bright light is shone into one eye. Doctors test this by swinging a flashlight back and forth between both eyes, and it indicates damage to the optic nerve carrying signals from the eye to the brain.

---

### Charcot-Bouchard Aneurysms
Microscopic, weak bulges that develop in the tiny blood vessels deep inside the brain in people who have had high blood pressure for many years. Because their walls are weak, these tiny bulges can suddenly burst, causing a major bleeding stroke inside the brain.
